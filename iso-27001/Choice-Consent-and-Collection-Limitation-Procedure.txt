# Choice, Consent, and Collection Limitation Procedure

**Document ID:** PRV-CON-035
**Version:** 3.0 (current) — **effective 2026-06-01**

**Revision note.** Version 3.0 separates the entity's two distinct privacy roles — controller for workforce
information, processor for personal information incidentally present in customer evidence — which version 2.x
conflated into a single set of undifferentiated commitments. It adds the documented basis for implicit
consent, the sensitive-information explicit-consent path, and the new-purpose notification process, none of
which version 2.x addressed. It supersedes version 2.3 of 2025-12-08.

**Owner:** Privacy Owner (Head of Engineering, acting)
**Approved by:** Head of Engineering 2026-05-28; reviewed by the Audit Committee 2026-05-29
**Next scheduled review:** Annually (next by 2026-12-31), and on any change to collection methods or purposes

**Scope.** All personal information the entity collects, in either of its two roles. The reference
implementation is AWS account **471885797689**, region **us-west-2**.

Companion documents: the retention and disposal standard; the data subject rights procedure; the third-party
disclosure procedure; the data quality and complaints procedure; the information requirements standard (which
classifies personal information as a data class).

---

## 1. The two roles, stated before anything else

Almost every defect in a privacy programme of this kind comes from answering a controller question with a
processor answer, or the reverse. So the roles are fixed here first:

| Role | Whose personal information | What the entity decides | Who the data subject deals with |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Controller** | Workforce: employees and contractors of the entity | Purposes, retention, lawful basis | The entity, directly |
| **Processor** | Any personal information incidentally present in customer-uploaded evidence documents, or in a customer's scanned account configuration | Nothing about purpose — the entity processes only on the customer's instruction | The **customer** (the data controller); the entity responds to the controller, not to the subject directly |

**The entity does not knowingly collect personal information from members of the public, and operates no
consumer-facing service.** It is a business-to-business compliance assessment service invoked by a scheduler,
never by a data subject.

**The processor role is the larger one by volume and the one most likely to be misread.** Where a requirement
below speaks of informing or obtaining consent from data subjects, the processor-role answer is that the
entity does so **through and on the instruction of the data controller**, under the terms of the service
agreement — and that is stated explicitly rather than left as an implication.

## 2. Limiting collection to what is necessary (P3.1-R1)

Collection is limited by design, not by policy alone. The complete inventory of personal information the
entity collects:

| # | Personal information | Role | Purpose | Why it is necessary | Alternative rejected |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Workforce name and corporate email | Controller | Authentication, access authorisation, accountability for control activities | An access record with no identity cannot support accountability | Pseudonymous accounts — rejected because they defeat the audit trail |
| 2 | Workforce role and reporting line | Controller | Least-privilege permission assignment; approval routing | Permissions are assigned by role | Per-person manual grants — rejected as unreviewable |
| 3 | Workforce federated identity subject identifier | Controller | Session establishment | Required by the identity protocol | n/a |
| 4 | Workforce training and attestation records | Controller | Competence evidence for control operation | The competence criterion requires it | n/a |
| 5 | Workforce on-call contact details | Controller | Incident paging | An unreachable responder is a control failure | Shared alias only — rejected because it delays paging |
| 6 | Names and email addresses of customer personnel appearing in customer-uploaded evidence (for example, a policy's named owner and approver) | **Processor** | None of the entity's own — the document is assessed as the customer submitted it | Not collected by choice; present because control evidence names its owners | Redaction on upload — **considered and rejected**, see §2.1 |
| 7 | Identifiers in a customer's scanned account configuration that may incidentally be personal, such as an IAM user name derived from a person's name | **Processor** | Evaluating the customer's access controls | The control being assessed is *about* those identities | Hashing before evaluation — rejected because the assessment must report the actual identity to be actionable |

**Nothing else is collected.** The entity collects no biometric, location, financial, health, or
device-telemetry information about any individual; it deploys no cookies, trackers, or analytics against
individuals; and it does not acquire personal information from data brokers or any other third-party source.

### 2.1 The one collection the entity would prefer not to make

Items 6 and 7 are collected **incidentally and unavoidably**, and this is stated plainly rather than
described as a deliberate minimised collection:

- Automatic redaction of names from uploaded evidence was evaluated in Q1 2026 and rejected: a control
  document's value as evidence depends substantially on naming its accountable owner, and redacting the
  owner would make the document fail the very criteria it is submitted against.
- The compensating limits are that this information is **never used for any purpose beyond assessing the
  document or configuration in which it appears**, is never aggregated into a profile of any individual, is
  never used to train or fine-tune any model, and is retained only as long as the evidence record itself
  (see the retention standard).
- **Registered limitation:** because the entity does not parse uploaded documents for personal information, it
  cannot enumerate which individuals are named across the evidence corpus without reading it. This limits the
  completeness of a subject-accounting response and is registered as such in §9 and in the data subject
  rights procedure.

## 3. Collection by fair and lawful means, reviewed before implementation (P3.1-R2, P3.1-R3)

Every collection method is reviewed **before** it is implemented, by the Privacy Owner jointly with the Head
of Security Engineering. The review is a gate in the change process, not a retrospective sign-off: a change
that introduces or alters a collection of personal information cannot merge without it.

| Collection method | Reviewed on | Fairness determination | Lawfulness determination |
|---|---|---|---|
| Workforce onboarding record | 2025-09-14, re-reviewed 2026-05-28 | Collected openly at onboarding, with notice; no deception or intimidation; providing it is a condition of employment and stated as such | Necessary for performance of the employment contract and for the entity's legitimate interest in securing its systems |
| Federated authentication | 2025-09-14 | Subject is authenticating knowingly and deliberately | Contract necessity |
| On-call contact collection | 2025-11-02 | Voluntary within the on-call rota; an individual may decline the rota | Legitimate interest; consent for the personal-device channel specifically |
| Customer evidence upload | 2026-01-20 | The uploading customer is informed in the service terms what happens to submitted content; the entity does not solicit personal information and asks for none | Processing on the controller's documented instruction |
| Customer account scan | 2026-01-20 | The customer authorises the scan by enabling the assessment and permitting credential vending; scope is read-only | Processing on the controller's documented instruction |

**Methods rejected at review** are recorded, because the rejections evidence that the review is real: an
early design that would have cached scanned IAM user listings across assessments for performance was rejected
in the 2026-01-20 review as retention beyond purpose; scans now always read fresh.

## 4. Third-party sources (P3.1-R4)

**The entity collects personal information from no third-party data source** — no brokers, no enrichment
services, no public-record aggregation. The only parties from whom personal information arrives are the
customer (as controller, on instruction) and the individual themselves (workforce).

The processors that *handle* personal information on the entity's behalf are assessed for reliability under
the third-party disclosure procedure, which covers the vendor set (cloud provider, identity provider, source
control, observability, ticketing, communication platform). That assessment includes whether each collects
fairly and lawfully to the extent it collects at all.

## 5. Additional information developed about an individual (P3.1-R5)

The entity **derives** a small amount of information about workforce individuals rather than collecting it:
control-activity attribution (who performed which activity, and whether within the deadline) and on-call
response latency.

Individuals are informed that this is derived, at onboarding and again in the annual privacy notice
refresh, because derived information is exactly the category people do not expect. The notice states what is
derived, that it is used for control-operation evidence and for on-call rota fairness, and that it is not used
for automated decisions about the individual. **No profiling and no automated decision-making about any
individual takes place.**

For the processor role, the entity derives nothing about customer personnel: an assessment's conclusions are
about the *customer's controls*, not about the people named in them.

## 6. Communicating choices, and the consequences of each (P2.1-R1, P2.1-R2, P2.1-R3)

### 6.1 What is communicated, to whom, and when

| Audience | Vehicle | Timing | Contents |
|---|---|---|---|
| Workforce | Privacy notice issued at onboarding; refreshed annually | At or before collection | The seven items in §2, purposes, retention, the choices in §6.2, the consequences in §6.3, the rights in the data subject rights procedure, and how to complain |
| Workforce | On-call rota briefing | Before joining the rota | The personal-device channel choice specifically |
| Customers (as controllers) | Service terms and the published privacy commitments | Before the service is enabled | What is processed, for what purpose, sub-processors, retention, breach notification, and how the entity supports the controller's own obligations to its data subjects |
| Data subjects in the processor role | **Via the controller**, whose notice governs | Controller's responsibility, supported by the entity | The entity supplies the controller with the processing description the controller needs in order to give notice |

**The notice states that consent is required where consent is the basis, and states where it is not the
basis.** Conflating the two is the common failure: telling someone their data is processed "with your consent"
when the real basis is contract necessity misrepresents a choice they do not have. The notice therefore names
the basis per item (§7.2).

### 6.2 The choices actually available

| Choice | Available to | Effect if exercised |
|---|---|---|
| Decline the on-call rota, or decline the personal-device paging channel and use a corporate device only | Workforce | No paging to a personal device; rota participation adjusted. **No employment consequence.** |
| Decline optional profile details (photograph, pronouns, displayed timezone) | Workforce | Omitted from internal directory; no other effect |
| Withdraw consent for optional items at any time | Workforce | Item deleted within 30 days; §6.3 consequences apply only to that item |
| Object to a specific processing on legitimate-interest grounds | Workforce | Balancing test performed and the outcome given in writing with reasons |
| Instruct deletion or return of submitted evidence | Customer (controller) | Executed per the retention and disposal standard |
| Decline to submit a document, or submit a redacted one | Customer (controller) | The affected requirement is assessed on scan evidence alone and may score lower — a substantive consequence, stated plainly |

### 6.3 Consequences of refusing or withdrawing — stated at collection time

Consequences are given **at the point of collection**, next to the request, not buried in a policy the person
reads later:

| If the individual declines… | Consequence |
|---|---|
| Name and corporate email | Access cannot be granted; the role cannot be performed. This is a genuine bar and is stated as one |
| Role and reporting line | Permissions cannot be assigned |
| Federated identity | Authentication is impossible; there is no alternative access path |
| Training and attestation records | The individual cannot be recorded as competent to operate controls, and so cannot hold control ownership |
| On-call contact details | Cannot join the on-call rota. **No other consequence, and no effect on standing or advancement** |
| Optional profile details | None beyond absence from the directory |

**Where the consequence is "you cannot use the service or hold the role", the notice says exactly that.** A
notice that implies a choice exists where it does not is a misrepresentation, and is treated here as a defect
rather than as reassuring language.

## 7. Obtaining consent, and the basis for implicit consent (P2.1-R4, P2.1-R5, P2.1-R10, P2.1-R11)

### 7.1 When and how consent is obtained (P2.1-R4)

Consent, where it is the basis, is obtained **at or before collection** — never retrospectively:

| Item | Mechanism | Record kept |
|---|---|---|
| On-call personal-device channel | Explicit opt-in at rota briefing | Dated acknowledgement, retained for the rota period plus 1 year |
| Optional profile details | Explicit act of supplying them, with the optional status shown at the point of entry | The record's existence is itself the consent record |
| Customer evidence submission | The controller's acceptance of the service terms, plus the deliberate act of upload with declared control references | Upload record with timestamp, submitter, and declared references |

### 7.2 Documented basis for implicit consent (P2.1-R10)

Most workforce processing does **not** rest on consent, and the basis is documented per item so that no
implicit consent is inferred where a different basis applies:

| Item | Basis | Why not consent |
|---|---|---|
| 1, 2, 3 (identity, role, federated identifier) | Contract necessity and the entity's legitimate interest in securing its systems | Consent would be unfree in an employment relationship, and withdrawal cannot be honoured without ending access — a consent that cannot be withdrawn is not consent |
| 4 (training records) | Legal and framework obligation; legitimate interest | Same |
| 5 (on-call contact) | Legitimate interest for the corporate channel; **explicit consent** for the personal-device channel | The personal device is genuinely optional, so consent is available and is used |
| Optional profile details | **Consent**, freely given and withdrawable | Genuinely optional |
| 6, 7 (customer evidence and configuration) | Not the entity's determination — processed on the controller's documented instruction | The entity is not the controller and must not purport to obtain consent for it |

**Implicit consent is relied on in exactly one place**: an individual who voluntarily supplies an optional
profile detail is treated as consenting to its display in the internal directory, because supplying it in
response to a request that states its purpose is an unambiguous affirmative act. Every other basis is either
explicit consent or a named non-consent basis. **No implicit consent is inferred from silence, from
inactivity, or from continued use of a system.**

### 7.3 Consent is confined to the stated purpose (P2.1-R11)

Consent, where obtained, is scoped to the purpose stated at the time and to that purpose only. The entity
does not use a consent given for one purpose to justify another — see §8 for what happens when a new purpose
arises. Consent text names the purpose, the retention period, and the withdrawal method in the same sentence
as the request, so the scope is not separable from the ask.

### 7.4 Confirming and implementing expressed preferences (P2.1-R5)

A preference is not merely recorded; it is **confirmed to the individual and then enforced**:

1. The preference is recorded against the individual with a timestamp.
2. A confirmation is sent to the individual restating the preference as recorded, so a mis-recorded preference
   is visible to the person best placed to notice it.
3. The system state is changed to match — for example, removing a personal-device paging target.
4. The change is verified within 5 business days, and the verification is recorded.
5. **Quarterly, recorded preferences are reconciled against actual system state.** The reconciliation exists
   because a preference honoured on paper and not in configuration is the failure mode that matters. The Q1
   2026 reconciliation covered all workforce preferences and found no discrepancy.

Withdrawal is implemented within **30 days**, and the individual is told when it has been completed.

## 8. New purposes for information already collected (P2.1-R6, P2.1-R7, P4.1-R1, P4.1-R2)

### 8.1 Use is confined to the purpose of collection (P4.1-R1, P4.1-R2)

Each item in §2 carries exactly one purpose column, and use outside it is prohibited. Four specific
prohibitions are stated because they are the plausible temptations for a system of this kind:

- **Customer evidence and scanned configuration are never used to train, fine-tune, or evaluate any model.**
  Inference is invoked per assessment with the customer's content in the request and nothing is retained by
  the entity for model improvement.
- **Assessment content is never used for the entity's own marketing, benchmarking, or cross-customer
  analysis.** No aggregate derived from one customer's evidence is shown to another.
- **Workforce control-activity attribution is never used for performance management**, only for control
  evidence and rota fairness.
- **No personal information is used for automated decision-making about the individual.**

Use is permitted without consent only where a law or regulation specifically requires it — for example, a
lawful order compelling disclosure — and any such instance is recorded in the disclosure record and, where
lawful to do so, reported to the affected controller.

### 8.2 Documenting and notifying a new purpose (P2.1-R6, P2.1-R7)

When a purpose not already in the privacy notice is proposed:

1. **The new purpose is documented** in the change record, naming the item, the proposed purpose, the basis,
   and whether it is compatible with the original purpose. This documentation is created **before** any use.
2. The Privacy Owner determines compatibility. Where the new purpose is incompatible, the original consent or
   basis does **not** carry over.
3. **Data subjects are notified, and implicit or explicit consent obtained, before the new use begins** — the
   privacy notice is updated and the update is actively communicated, not silently republished.
4. Where the basis is consent, the new use applies **only** to individuals who have consented; the entity
   does not proceed on the basis of non-response.
5. In the processor role, a new purpose is **not the entity's to adopt**: the entity asks the controller for
   an instruction, and does nothing until it arrives.

**No new purpose has been adopted for previously collected information in the current reporting period**, so
this process has been exercised only in a tabletop walkthrough (2026-04-15) rather than in production. That is
stated rather than implied, and is registered in §9.

## 9. Sensitive personal information, and endpoint transfers (P2.1-R8, P2.1-R9, P3.2)

### 9.1 The entity does not collect sensitive personal information

**No special-category or sensitive personal information is collected in the controller role**: no racial or
ethnic origin, political opinion, religious belief, trade union membership, genetic or biometric data, health
data, sex life or sexual orientation, precise geolocation, government identifier, or financial account data.

This is enforced rather than asserted: the seven items in §2 are the complete permitted set, and the
pre-implementation review in §3 is the gate through which any new item — sensitive or not — must pass.

### 9.2 The explicit-consent path, which exists and is tested (P3.2-R1, P3.2-R2, P3.2-R3, P2.1-R8)

A procedure that has nothing to apply to still has to work the first time it is needed, so the path is
defined and exercised:

1. **Determination.** The Privacy Owner determines at pre-implementation review whether a proposed collection
   includes sensitive personal information, or information that any applicable law requires explicit consent
   for. The determination is recorded either way.
2. **Communication of the need for consent, before collection** (P3.2-R1). The individual is told, in advance
   of any collection: that the item is sensitive, that **explicit consent is required and is the sole basis**,
   what the item is, the purpose, the retention period, who it may be disclosed to, and how to withdraw.
3. **Communication of the consequences of not consenting** (P3.2-R2). The individual is told what will and
   will not happen if they decline. Because no sensitive item is necessary to any role in the entity, the
   consequence is stated as: **the item is not collected and there is no effect on employment, standing,
   advancement, or system access.** A sensitive collection that carried an adverse consequence for refusal
   would not be freely consented and would be rejected at step 1.
4. **Explicit consent obtained before collection** (P3.2-R3, P2.1-R8). Consent is a separate, affirmative,
   unbundled act — never a pre-ticked box, never bundled with another agreement, never inferred from
   continued employment or continued use of a system, and **obtained directly from the data subject**, not
   from an employer, a colleague, or any other intermediary.
5. **No collection proceeds without a recorded consent.** Absence of a response is not consent.

### 9.3 Retention of explicit consent documentation (P3.2-R4)

Documentation of explicit consent is retained for **the lifetime of the processing plus 3 years**, so that the
lawfulness of a past processing can be demonstrated after it has ceased. Each record holds: the individual,
the exact consent text presented, the affirmative act and its timestamp, the version of the privacy notice in
force, the purpose consented to, and — where applicable — the withdrawal and its timestamp.

Consent records are held in the governance store, encrypted at rest, access-controlled to the Privacy Owner,
and are **exempt from routine deletion** while the underlying processing continues, because deleting the
consent record would destroy the evidence that the processing was lawful. Existing explicit consent records
for the personal-device paging channel are retained on this basis and were verified present at the 2026-04-08
evidence currency check.

### 9.4 Endpoint device transfers (P2.1-R9)

Personal information is transferred to or from an individual's endpoint device in exactly two circumstances,
each consented:

| Transfer | Consent |
|---|---|
| Paging notification to a personal mobile device | Explicit opt-in per §7.1; withdrawable, and withdrawal removes the target |
| Workforce access to entity systems from a managed endpoint | Consented at onboarding as part of the endpoint management terms, which state what the entity can and cannot see on the device |

**No personal information is transferred to a customer's or data subject's endpoint device**, because the
entity has no consumer-facing interface. No transfer occurs to an unmanaged personal endpoint other than the
paging channel above.
