# Physical Threat Protection and Secure-Area Working Standard

**Document ID:** SEC-PHY-044
**Version:** 2.0 — **effective 2026-03-02**, superseding v1.1 of 2025-12-03
**Owner:** Director, IT Operations (facilities lead reports to this role)
**Approved by:** CISO 2026-02-26, per the approval authority in GOV-POL-040 §3
**Next scheduled review:** 2026-12-31 (Annually, and **before beginning critical operations at any new physical site**, on)
any lease change, and on any material change to the local threat picture
**Scope:** Physical and environmental threat protection for the two entity-operated offices (HQ, 34 desks, with
one server closet holding network equipment only; EU subsidiary suite, 9 desks), the three provider-operated
co-working locations, the 42 primarily remote work locations, and — by reference and inheritance only — AWS
us-west-2, which holds every production information asset and whose physical and environmental controls the
entity monitors but does not operate. Also covers secure-area working practice and the clear desk / clear
screen rules for all locations.

**Related:** SEC-PRO-013 (physical access and facility security), OPS-EQP-045 (equipment siting, utilities,
cabling, maintenance), SEC-MED-046 (storage media, off-premises assets, equipment disposal), SEC-END-043
(endpoints and remote working), RSK-STD-008 (risk methodology), OPS-BCP-031 (continuity), TPR-PRO-016
(supplier assurance), GOV-PRO-007 (shared-responsibility allocation).

## 1. What the entity protects physically, and what it inherits

The entity operates **no data centre**. Every production information asset — customer evidence, assessment
results, logs, model artefacts — exists only in AWS account 471885797689 in us-west-2. The consequence for
this standard is precise and is stated rather than blurred:

| Asset class | Physical location | Threat protection operated by | How the entity obtains assurance |
|---|---|---|---|
| Production evidence, assessment results, logs, model artefacts | AWS us-west-2 only | **AWS** — an inherited control | Annual review of the provider's audit reports against the shared-responsibility allocation in GOV-PRO-007 §5; recorded as inherited and **not asserted as the entity's own** |
| Network equipment (switch, firewall, patch panel, UPS) | HQ server closet | Entity | Direct testing: environmental alarm, quarterly inspection, monthly access-log review |
| Endpoint devices | With 48 individuals across five geographies | The individual under SEC-END-043, with technical compensation | Posture enforcement, encryption, remote wipe |
| Signed contracts and statutory registers | Locked cabinets, HQ and EU suite | Entity | Key custody list, quarterly review |
| Log copies | Managed SIEM provider's colocation facility | **Provider** | Annual provider assessment including physical and environmental controls |
| Removable media | **None exists** — eliminated by design (SEC-MED-046 §3) | — | Device-policy compliance report; the absence of an approved media register is the evidence |

**The honest consequence:** the entity's most effective physical control is that the valuable asset is not in
any building it controls. Several requirements below are therefore satisfied for the entity's own premises at
office scale and satisfied for production by inheritance, and each is labelled as one or the other. An
inherited control asserted as an operated control would be a misstatement, and a requirement left blank
because it is inherited would be a gap; neither is done here.

## 2. Designing and implementing protection against physical and environmental threats (7.5-R1)

Protection against physical and environmental threats — natural disasters and other intentional or
unintentional physical threats to infrastructure — is designed and implemented at each site in scope, sized to
what the site actually holds.

| Site | Threats designed against | Controls implemented | Type |
|---|---|---|---|
| **HQ office and server closet** | Fire; water ingress; power failure; electrical surge; theft and burglary; vandalism; civil disruption; earthquake (moderate regional exposure); extreme heat | Landlord fire detection and suppression with entity-verified coverage of the suite; **no water-bearing services routed through the server closet** (verified at fit-out and re-verified at each landlord works notification); UPS with clean shutdown for closet equipment; surge protection at the closet distribution board; badge-controlled suite entry with prop-open alarm; laminated glazing on ground-floor aspects; seismic restraint straps on the closet rack; temperature and humidity alarm to a monitored channel; no signage identifying the entity as a technology tenant at street level | Preventive + detective |
| **EU subsidiary suite** | Fire; water ingress; power failure; theft; civil disruption; flood (site is 400 m from a watercourse — assessed, above the modelled 1-in-200 level) | Landlord fire detection and suppression; entity-controlled suite access; no server room and therefore no closet-specific controls; flood exposure assessed and recorded as acceptable with no basement storage | Preventive |
| **Co-working locations (3)** | All of the above, none of it under entity control | **Risk removed rather than managed**: no entity information asset above Internal may be stored on site; no entity equipment is left overnight; provider assessment on file; operator badge list reconciled monthly | Avoidance |
| **Remote locations (42)** | Fire, flood, theft, power and connectivity loss at individual homes | Risk removed by the no-local-copy control; devices encrypted and remotely wipeable; continuity handled by role handover rather than by site resilience (OPS-BCP-031 §5) | Avoidance + compensating |
| **AWS us-west-2 (production)** | Fire, flood, seismic, power, physical intrusion at data-centre scale | **Inherited.** Multi-AZ deployment within the region is the entity's own design decision and its own control; the physical protection of each facility is the provider's | Inherited + entity architecture |
| Managed SIEM provider facility | As above, at the provider's scale | Provider-operated; assessed annually | Inherited, assessed |

**A limitation recorded rather than implied.** The entity's production deployment is **single-region**
(us-west-2, multi-AZ). A regional physical event is therefore an availability risk that the current
architecture does not eliminate. It is Board-accepted as risk **R-022**, conditional on the quarterly restore
test passing, and it is disclosed in the customer-facing description. Multi-AZ addresses facility-level and
campus-level physical events; it does not address a regional one, and the standard says so rather than
implying resilience the entity does not have.

## 3. Risk assessments for physical and environmental threats (7.5-R2, 7.5-R3, 7.5-R4, 7.5-R5)

### 3.1 When assessments are performed (7.5-R2)

A physical and environmental threat assessment is performed **prior to beginning critical operations at each
physical site** and **at regular intervals thereafter**:

| Assessment | Trigger / interval | Performed by | Output |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pre-occupancy site assessment | **Before any critical operation begins at a new site** — before the first desk is used, not after fit-out | Facilities lead with the CISO, using the RSK-STD-008 methodology | Site risk record with controls required as a condition of occupancy |
| Periodic site reassessment | **Annually** per site, and within 30 days of a material change | Facilities lead | Updated site risk record; findings into the deficiency register |
| Lease or landlord change | On event | Facilities lead with the General Counsel | Reassessment plus lease-clause review |
| Provider (co-working, SIEM, cloud) physical assessment | Annually, on the TPR-PRO-016 cycle | Third-Party Risk Manager | Provider assessment record |
| Threat-picture monitoring | **Continuous, reviewed quarterly** | Facilities lead, with specialist input (§3.4) | Change log; out-of-cycle reassessment where warranted |

**Performed this period:** annual reassessment of HQ (2026-01-14) and of the EU suite (2026-01-21); a
pre-occupancy assessment of the EU suite was performed in 2025-08 before occupancy, and its conditions —
lockable statutory-records cabinet, entity-controlled suite reader, no basement storage — were met before the
first working day; annual provider assessments for the three co-working operators and the SIEM provider; four
quarterly threat-picture reviews.

### 3.2 Location and construction: topography and urban threats (7.5-R3)

Site selection and construction were assessed against local topography and urban threat, and the assessment is
recorded per site rather than assumed:

| Factor assessed | HQ | EU suite |
|---|---|---|
| **Elevation and flood exposure** | Floor 4 of 9; site elevation above the modelled 1-in-1000 fluvial and pluvial extent; no basement occupancy | Floor 2 of 5; 400 m from a watercourse, above the modelled 1-in-200 level; **no basement storage permitted**, recorded as a condition of occupancy |
| **Bodies of water** | Nearest significant watercourse 2.1 km; coastal surge not applicable | Assessed as above; the condition above is the mitigation |
| **Tectonic fault lines** | Moderate regional seismic exposure; building constructed to current seismic code; **rack seismic restraint** fitted in the closet; assessment concluded no additional structural measure is available to a tenant | Low seismic exposure |
| **High-profile location attracting political unrest, criminal activity or terrorist attack** | Assessed as **low-to-moderate**: a mixed commercial district with no government, diplomatic or high-symbolism co-tenant; two peaceful demonstrations passed the building this period with no impact. Mitigations: no street-level entity signage, no publication of the office address on the website, reception-controlled entry, and a documented close-and-work-remotely trigger | Assessed as **low**: suburban business park, no notable co-tenants; the same no-signage rule applies |
| Adjacent hazard | No fuel storage, chemical processing or high-hazard occupancy within the assessed radius; a ground-floor restaurant at HQ is the material adjacency and is the reason fire-detection coverage of the suite was independently verified rather than accepted from the landlord's certificate | Ground-floor retail; same verification applied |
| **Criminal activity** | Local commercial burglary rate reviewed against police open data; laminated glazing and the no-overnight-equipment rule for street-facing desks follow from it | Low; no additional measure |

Because the entity is a tenant in both cases, "construction" is largely a **selection** criterion rather than a
design one: the assessment records what the building provides, what the lease permits the entity to add, and —
where a threat cannot be addressed at tenant level — the residual risk and the compensating measure. This is
recorded explicitly, because a tenant claiming to have designed a building's flood resilience would not be
credible.

### 3.3 Controls implemented against the identified threats (7.5-R4)

Controls follow from the assessment results rather than from a generic list. Where a named threat is not
applicable to a site, that is recorded with the reason:

| Threat named in 7.5-R4 | HQ / EU controls | Production (inherited) |
|---|---|---|
| **Fire** | Landlord detection and suppression, coverage of the suite independently verified; extinguishers serviced annually; evacuation drills twice yearly with 46 of 48 participation across the two sites; the server closet has smoke detection reporting to the monitored channel; no combustible storage in the closet; charging of lithium batteries restricted to the designated bench with a metal enclosure | AWS facility fire protection |
| **Flooding and water** | No water-bearing services through the closet, re-verified at each landlord works notification; leak-detection puck under the closet rack (added this period); no basement storage; no equipment on the floor; EU condition of occupancy as above | AWS facility |
| **Electrical surges and power failure** | Surge protection at the closet distribution board; UPS providing clean shutdown for closet equipment (the closet holds no production data, so ride-through capacity is deliberately not purchased — the requirement is orderly shutdown, not continuity); details in OPS-EQP-045 §3 | AWS facility power; multi-AZ deployment |
| **Explosives and weapons** | Assessed as **not a design threat** for either site given the location assessment in §3.2: no screening or blast measure is implemented, and this is a recorded, reasoned decision rather than an omission. The mitigations that do apply are reception-controlled entry, no street signage, the challenge-any-unbadged-individual instruction, and the close-and-work-remotely trigger | AWS facility |
| Vandalism, theft, burglary | Badge-controlled suite entry with prop-open alarm; laminated glazing on ground-floor aspects; no equipment visible from the street; overnight clear-desk enforcement; locked device store | AWS facility |
| Dust, vibration, chemical, electromagnetic, temperature, humidity | Covered for equipment in OPS-EQP-045 §2 and §5; closet temperature and humidity alarmed | AWS facility |
| Civil disruption / site inaccessibility | Documented trigger to close a site and work remotely — invoked **once** this period for a half day during a transport strike at the HQ city, with no operational impact because the workforce is already remote-capable | AWS facility; R-022 stated for the regional case |

### 3.4 Monitoring changes, and obtaining specialist advice (7.5-R5)

**Changes to physical and environmental threats are monitored**, and specialist advice is obtained where the
entity's own competence ends:

| Input monitored | Source | Cadence | Acted on this period |
|---|---|---|---|
| Severe-weather and natural-hazard advisories for both office cities | National meteorological and hazard services; alerting to the facilities lead | Continuous, alert-driven | Two heat advisories; closet temperature monitored more frequently, no exceedance |
| Flood-map and seismic-code updates | Public hazard mapping | Annually | No change to either site's conclusion |
| Local crime and public-order picture | Police open data; landlord tenant bulletins; building management notices | Quarterly | The two demonstrations; no control change |
| Landlord works and building-system changes | Contractual notification from building management | Per event | Three works notifications; the water-services re-verification was performed each time, which is how the leak-detection puck came to be added after a chilled-water pipe was rerouted on the floor above |
| Provider physical-security posture | AWS audit reports; co-working and SIEM provider assessments | Annually | No adverse finding; the SIEM provider's move to Confidential handling triggered a re-executed annex (HR-PRO-042 §4.4) |
| Insurance surveyor recommendations | Insurer survey | On renewal | Two recommendations: extinguisher signage and the lithium-battery charging enclosure — both implemented |
| **Specialist advice** | Fire risk assessor (statutory, annual, both sites); insurer's surveyor; the landlord's structural and M&E engineers; a seismic-restraint specialist consulted at HQ fit-out; the entity's own competence is deliberately not stretched to structural, fire-engineering or utility design | Annual and event-driven | Fire risk assessments completed 2026-01 for both sites, no material finding; seismic restraint specification confirmed |

**Findings are handled like any other deficiency**, entering the register with an owner and a date rather than
sitting in a facilities file: **D-2026-015** (Low) recorded the absence of leak detection under the closet rack
after the landlord's pipe reroute, closed on installation; **D-2026-016** (Low) recorded that the EU suite's
evacuation drill had slipped past its due date by 6 weeks, closed after the drill on 2026-02-11.

## 4. Working in secure areas (7.6)

### 4.1 What constitutes a secure area here (7.6-R1)

**Security measures for working in secure areas are designed and implemented, applying to all personnel and
covering all activities taking place in the secure area.** The entity has exactly one secure area of its own,
and identifying it narrowly is what makes the measures enforceable:

| Secure area | Why it is one | Population with access |
|---|---|---|
| **HQ server closet** | Holds the network equipment on which office connectivity depends, the patch panel, and the only physical infrastructure the entity operates | A **named list**, currently 5 individuals (6 at period start), each requiring dual authorization from the AI Security Lead and the Director IT Operations |
| Locked records cabinets (HQ and EU) | Hold signed contracts and statutory registers | Key custody: General Counsel (records), IT Operations (device store) |
| **AWS data centres** | Genuinely secure areas, but **not the entity's** — no entity personnel has or seeks physical access | None; recorded as inherited |

Measures applying to all activities in the server closet: entry only against a change record or an incident
record; entry logged by the badge reader and **reviewed monthly against work records**, with any unmatched
entry investigated; out-of-hours entry generating an **alert** to the AI Security Lead rather than waiting for
the monthly review; work performed to the equipment-maintenance rules in OPS-EQP-045 §5; and no material
removed from the closet without an asset-register update.

### 4.2 Need-to-know about the area and its activities (7.6-R2)

Personnel are made aware of the existence of, and activities within, the secure area **on a need-to-know
basis**. Practically: the closet's location is not marked with signage identifying its contents; the named
access list is not published; planned work in the closet is communicated to the change approvers and the
affected service owners, not broadcast; and incident work in the closet is described in the incident record by
function rather than by physical detail. The 42 remote personnel have no need to know and are not told, which
is not secrecy for its own sake but the removal of an unnecessary reconnaissance surface.

### 4.3 Avoiding unsupervised work (7.6-R3)

**Measures are in place to avoid unsupervised work in secure areas**, for safety as well as to reduce the
opportunity for malicious activity:

1. Work in the server closet requires **two people present** where the work involves opening the rack,
   handling power, or physically altering connectivity. This is a written rule with a technical backstop: the
   badge log shows single-badge entries, and a single-badge entry coinciding with a rack-opening change record
   is a finding.
2. A **single-person entry is permitted only** for non-invasive activity — reading a status light, retrieving
   a labelled spare, photographing a serial number — and the change or incident record must say so.
3. **External technicians are never unsupervised**: an entity escort from the named list is present for the
   whole visit, and the escort is recorded by name in the visit record (OPS-EQP-045 §5).
4. **Vacant means locked** (§4.4), so "unsupervised" cannot arise by drift.
5. Lone working outside business hours triggers a check-in with the on-call IT Operations engineer at entry
   and exit, for safety.

**This period:** 7 closet entries. 5 were two-person; 2 were single-person non-invasive with the reason
recorded. One external technician visit (a landlord electrician working on the floor distribution board) was
escorted throughout. The monthly log review found **one entry with no corresponding work record** — an
engineer retrieving a spare cable — recorded as **D-2026-017** (Low) with the correction that the change tool
now offers a lightweight "closet access" record type, because requiring a full change record for a 90-second
task was the reason the record was skipped.

### 4.4 Vacant secure areas locked and periodically inspected (7.6-R4)

The server closet is **locked whenever unoccupied** — the door is self-closing and self-locking on a separate
entity-keyed lock that is not part of the building master suite, with a prop-open alarm at 60 seconds. It is
**periodically inspected**: a documented quarterly inspection by the facilities lead covering physical
integrity of the door and lock, rack security, cable tidiness and labelling, absence of unauthorised devices,
environmental readings, UPS status, extinguisher currency, and absence of stored material that should not be
there. The records cabinets are similarly locked when unattended and inspected at the same cadence.

**Inspections this period:** 4 of 4 completed. Findings: one unlabelled patch lead (corrected at the
inspection); a cardboard box of packaging material stored in the closet (removed, and a no-combustible-storage
sign fitted); and the pipe reroute observation that produced D-2026-015.

### 4.5 Endpoint devices in secure areas (7.6-R5)

The carrying and use of user endpoint devices in the server closet is **appropriately controlled**: a laptop
may be taken in only where the work requires it (console access to network equipment), it must be an
entity-issued, in-posture device, it must not be left in the closet, and it must not be connected to a
management port without a change record naming that action. Personal devices other than a phone are not taken
in. Where console access is needed, the preferred path is the out-of-band management interface from the desk
rather than a laptop in the closet, because it produces a logged session instead of a physical presence.

### 4.6 Recording equipment in secure areas (7.6-R6)

**Photographic, video, audio and other recording equipment — including cameras in user endpoint devices — is
not permitted to be used in the server closet unless authorized.** Authorization is granted for a specific,
recorded purpose: photographing a serial number for a warranty claim, documenting a cabling change for the
as-built record, or evidencing a fault for a vendor. The authorization is recorded in the same work record,
the images are stored in the asset or change record rather than on the device, and images showing rack layout
or credentials are classified Confidential. Phones are not banned from the closet — a blanket ban that
everyone would breach is worse than a use rule that is followed — but **using** a camera without authorization
is a policy breach. Three authorized photographs were taken this period, all attached to change records.

The entity **operates no camera inside its own suite**, a deliberate decision recorded on privacy grounds with
badge logging and the monthly log review as the compensating controls (SEC-PRO-013 §5). Building-perimeter and
lobby video is the landlord's.

### 4.7 Emergency procedures posted (7.6-R7)

**Emergency procedures are posted in the secure area in a readily visible and accessible manner**: on the
inside face of the closet door, at eye height, laminated, a single page carrying the evacuation route and
assembly point; the location of the nearest extinguisher and its class; the electrical isolation point for the
closet and the instruction not to operate it except in an emergency; the emergency contact list (on-call IT
Operations, facilities lead, building management, emergency services) with numbers rather than names alone;
and the instruction that life safety takes precedence over equipment in every case. The same information, in
its site-appropriate form, is posted at the office entrances and in the EU suite. Currency of the posted sheet
is a line item on the quarterly inspection (§4.4) — an out-of-date emergency contact list is the failure mode
that a posted procedure most often has, and it was corrected once this period after an on-call number changed.

## 5. Clear desk and clear screen (7.7)

### 5.1 The topic-specific policy, defined and communicated (7.7-R1, 7.7-R3)

**A topic-specific policy on clear desk and clear screen is established and communicated to all relevant
interested parties**, and the rules are **defined** rather than left to interpretation. It applies at HQ, at
the EU suite, at co-working locations and at home, because 42 of 48 personnel work primarily remotely and a
rule that stops at the office door would not protect anything.

| Rule | Applies to | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Desks are cleared of all papers and removable storage media whenever the desk is left for an extended period and **always at end of day** | All locations | 7.7-R1 |
| No material classified Confidential or above is left on a desk unattended at any time | All locations | 7.7-R1 |
| Screens are locked whenever the desk is left, however briefly | All locations | 7.7-R1 |
| Screens are positioned so they are not readable from a doorway, window, or adjacent seat; a privacy filter is used in transit and at co-working locations | All locations | 7.7-R1 |
| Whiteboards and flip charts are cleared of Confidential or critical information when no longer required | Offices and home | 7.7-R10 |
| Nothing is left behind when a facility or room is vacated | All locations | 7.7-R11 |

Communication: published on the policy portal as part of this standard; acknowledged annually with the code of
conduct (**48 of 48**); included in the onboarding module; reinforced in the quarterly security briefing;
issued to contractors and the 11 named vendor individuals as a contractual annex; and communicated to the
landlord's cleaning contractor through building management, because a cleaner is a relevant interested party
who works in the suite unsupervised outside hours.

### 5.2 Enforcement (7.7-R2)

The rules are **appropriately enforced**, by technical means where possible and by observation where not:

| Rule | Enforcement mechanism |
|---|---|
| Screen lock | **Technical**: 5-minute automatic lock, immediate on lid close, non-disableable by the user (SEC-END-043 §6) |
| Timeout / automatic logout | **Technical**: 15-minute re-authentication for production tooling, 8-hour maximum session, idle console sessions terminated at 15 minutes |
| Print restrictions | **Technical**: Confidential printing disabled at the device; badge-release printing at HQ |
| Removable media | **Technical**: USB mass storage blocked, so there is no removable media to leave on a desk |
| Local storage of Confidential material | **Technical**: the no-local-copy control means a Confidential document generally cannot be on the desk in the first place |
| Paper, whiteboards, positioning, end-of-day state | **Observational**: unannounced clear-desk walkthroughs; end-of-day sweeps by the last person out at each office, recorded on a rota; internal audit's annual observation pass |
| Remote locations | **Self-assessment plus provision**: the location self-assessment in SEC-END-043 §4 asks specifically about screen visibility and lockable storage, and a filter or lock is issued in response |

**Enforcement results this period, including the failures.** Six unannounced walkthroughs (4 HQ, 2 EU) found:
two desks with Internal papers left out overnight (individuals reminded; both were the same team, which
received a targeted briefing); **one Confidential printout left at the HQ printer overnight** — a customer's
own report, printed for a call, in breach of both the print rule and the collection rule — recorded as
deficiency **D-2026-018** (Moderate), which produced the change to badge-release printing at HQ described in
§5.5; one whiteboard left with a customer name and finding summary (cleared; a "clear before you leave" sticker
now sits on every meeting-room whiteboard); and one unlocked, unattended screen (the case that also drove the
lock-timer reduction from 10 to 5 minutes in SEC-END-043 §11). Internal audit's annual pass found the clear
desk rules substantially followed and reported the printer case independently.

### 5.3 Sensitive information locked away in secure furniture (7.7-R4)

Sensitive or critical business information on paper or on electronic storage media is **locked away in secure
furniture when not required, and especially when the office is vacated**:

- HQ: keyed cabinets for signed contracts and statutory records (key custody with the General Counsel), and a
  keyed device store for spare laptops (custody with IT Operations). Key holders are a named list reviewed
  quarterly.
- EU suite: its own keyed statutory-records cabinet, a condition of occupancy from the pre-occupancy
  assessment.
- Remote locations: lockable drawer units or cabinets **provided on request** (11 issued); an individual with
  no lockable storage may not hold physical material above Internal at all, which converts a provisioning gap
  into a handling restriction rather than into an unenforced rule.
- Co-working locations: nothing above Internal may be held on site, and nothing is left overnight.
- **Electronic storage media**: none exists to lock away, by design.

### 5.4 Protecting unattended devices, and locking mechanisms (7.7-R5, 7.7-R6)

| Requirement | Implementation |
|---|---|
| Devices protected by key locks or other security means when not in use or unattended (7.7-R5) | Cable locks available at HQ and EU desks and used for desk-side docked laptops; spare and unissued devices held in the keyed device store; devices never left in a vehicle or in checked baggage; a device left overnight at a co-working location is a policy breach, not a risk to be assessed |
| Logged off or protected by a screen and keyboard locking mechanism controlled by user authentication when unattended (7.7-R6) | Lock requires password or biometric; **screen and keyboard both locked**; privileged sessions must be **logged off**, not merely locked, when the task ends — locking a session that holds a just-in-time production grant leaves the grant live, so the rule distinguishes the two |
| Configured with a timeout or automatic logout (7.7-R6) | 5-minute device lock; 15-minute production re-authentication; 8-hour session ceiling; idle SSH/console termination at 15 minutes; all MDM- and identity-provider-enforced, none user-adjustable |

### 5.5 Printer and multi-function device output (7.7-R7)

Output from printers and multi-function devices is collected by the originator **immediately**, and since
**2026-02** HQ printing uses **badge-release ("pull") printing**, so a job is not rendered until the
originator authenticates at the device — meaning an uncollected job never becomes an uncollected printout. The
change was made in response to D-2026-018 rather than in the abstract. Alongside it: Confidential printing is
disabled at the device entirely; the EU suite's single device is badge-release-capable and was enabled at the
same time; jobs unclaimed after 4 hours are deleted; and no home or co-working printer is registered for
entity material above Internal.

### 5.6 Secure storage and disposal of documents and removable media (7.7-R8)

Documents and removable storage media containing sensitive information are securely stored (§5.3) and, when no
longer required, **discarded using secure disposal mechanisms**: cross-cut shredders at both offices and
provided on request to remote personnel (9 issued); a locked confidential-waste consignment at HQ collected by
a certificated destruction contractor with a certificate of destruction retained against the consignment
record; **never household or general office waste** for anything above Internal. Removable media disposal is
governed by SEC-MED-046 §6 and is currently a null case because no removable media exists. Retention periods
that determine *when* material is no longer required come from PRV-RET-036, so that "discard when no longer
needed" has a defined meaning rather than a discretionary one.

### 5.7 Pop-ups, screen sharing and presentation (7.7-R9)

**Rules and guidance are established and communicated for the configuration of pop-ups on screens**, to prevent
inadvertent disclosure during presentations, screen sharing, or work in public areas:

- Notification previews are **suppressed by MDM policy** on the lock screen and, for mail and chat, reduced to
  sender-only with content hidden — configuration, not instruction.
- A **presentation / focus mode** must be engaged before screen sharing or presenting; the collaboration
  platform is configured to suppress notifications automatically when sharing is detected, and the manual step
  exists as a backstop for tools that are not integrated.
- **Share a window, not a desktop**, is the default rule; sharing a whole desktop requires a deliberate choice
  and is discouraged in customer-facing calls.
- Browser tabs, bookmark bars and open documents are reviewed before sharing; a dedicated demonstration profile
  with no customer material is provided for customer-facing sessions.
- Credentials are never entered while sharing; the platform's screen-share indicator is the prompt to stop.
- In public areas, a privacy filter is mandatory and Confidential material is not opened at all.
- Guidance is delivered in the onboarding module and refreshed quarterly, with a worked example drawn from a
  real near miss: a notification preview showing a customer name appeared during an internal demonstration in
  2025-11, which is why preview suppression became a configuration rather than a recommendation.

### 5.8 Whiteboards and displays (7.7-R10)

Sensitive or critical information on whiteboards, flip charts and wall displays is **cleared when no longer
required**: meeting rooms carry a "clear before you leave" prompt, the end-of-day sweep rota includes meeting
rooms, digital displays in common areas show only non-sensitive dashboards with no customer identifiers, and
persistent architecture material on walls is reviewed at the quarterly inspection for anything that has become
Confidential over time. Remote personnel are covered by the same rule for home whiteboards, on the same
reasoning as §5.1 — a household member or a video-call participant is an unauthorised viewer.

### 5.9 Vacating facilities and the final sweep (7.7-R11)

**Procedures are in place for vacating facilities, including a final sweep prior to leaving to ensure the
entity's assets are not left behind.** Three cases are distinguished, because they fail differently:

| Case | Procedure | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| **End of day / last person out** | Rota-recorded sweep: desks clear, whiteboards cleared, meeting rooms checked, printer tray empty, cabinets locked, closet door closed and locked, suite door closed | Sweep rota record |
| **Vacating a co-working location or a meeting room off-site** | Checklist before leaving: all papers collected, no device left charging, no material in the booth, whiteboard cleared, and — added after the two visitor-badge cases in SEC-PRO-013 §3 — a deliberate look at the seat and the floor. Nothing is stored on site overnight in any circumstance | Individual attestation for the location, spot-checked |
| **Permanently vacating a leased facility** | A documented decommissioning procedure: full inventory reconciliation against the asset register; removal of all entity equipment including network equipment, patch leads and the UPS; removal of entity labels, signage and markings; secure disposal or return of all physical records per PRV-RET-036 with a certificate; **removal or handover decision for entity-installed security controls** recorded per SEC-MED-046 §7; final sweep of every room including storage and above ceiling tiles; formal handover to the landlord with a signed inventory; and confirmation to the CISO that no entity information asset remains | Decommissioning record; not exercised this period (no facility vacated), and tested by walkthrough of the procedure during the annual site reassessment |
