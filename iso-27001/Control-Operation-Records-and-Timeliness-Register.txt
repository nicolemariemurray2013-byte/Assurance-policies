**Purpose.** This is not a policy. It is the **operating record** for the control activities defined in the
policies and procedures — the evidence that responsible personnel actually performed them, when, and within
what elapsed time. It exists because a policy that states a deadline is a design assertion, and a design
assertion is not evidence of operation.

**Reporting period covered:** 2026-02-01 through 2026-05-15 (Q1 and the first half of Q2 2026).

**Naming.** Individuals are recorded by role and initials rather than full name, consistent with the
minimisation requirement in the privacy standards. The register held by the Head of Platform Engineering
carries the full attribution and is available to the auditor on request.

## 1. How these records are produced

Records are not transcribed by hand into this document. Each row below is extracted from a system that
records the activity as a side effect of performing it, which is why the record cannot be produced without
the work having happened:

| Record source | What it timestamps | Why it is not self-reported |
|---|---|---|
| Assessment run journals | Every graph node entry and exit, per assessment | Written by the runtime during the run |
| Account trail (`rokap-pdx`) | Every control-plane API call | Written by the provider, not the entity |
| Alarm state history (`AIGov-*`) | Alarm transitions into and out of ALARM | Provider-maintained |
| Ticketing system | Ticket open, assignment, state change, close | Timestamps are system-set, not typed |
| Source control and build pipeline | Commit, review approval, build, deploy | Vendor-maintained |
| Test execution records | Restore and failover exercise start/end | Recorded during the exercise |

**A record that could have been written after the fact is marked as such** in the "Corroboration" column, and
is not offered as primary evidence of timeliness.

### 2.1 The four activities whose records were previously asserted rather than held

Rows 16–22 were added in v3.1 because the governing standards described them and this register did not
carry their execution. A control activity described in a standard and absent from this register reads, to
anyone checking, exactly like one that never ran — and in one case (row 20) the check found a real
discrepancy the moment it was performed, which is the argument for holding the record rather than the
assertion.

**(a) Forced credential resets on a security incident (row 16).** Four events in the period:

| Date | Trigger | Identities affected | Determination → enforced reset | Corroboration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2026-02-14 | Credential found in a public paste (a personal account reusing a corporate password pattern; no corporate credential exposed, reset forced anyway) | 1 | 41 min | TN-4302 |
| 2026-04-03 | Phishing page submission confirmed from endpoint telemetry | 1 | 22 min | TN-4389 |
| 2026-06-22 | EDR detection on an endpoint with an active session | 1 | 1 h 10 m | TN-4451 |
| 2026-07-30 | Supplier breach notification naming an email address used as a corporate identifier | 3 | 2 h 05 m (bulk) | TN-4463 |

In every case the reset was **enforced by the identity provider rather than requested from the user** —
a requested reset is a notification, not a control — and the affected sessions were revoked at the same
time, because a reset that leaves a live session running has changed nothing for the attacker who
already has one. Session revocation is verified from the provider's session log, not inferred.

**(b) Forced credential resets on employment change (row 17).** The trigger is the HR change feed, not a
manual notification, which is why the count reconciles exactly: 4 joiners (credential set at first
sign-in, no pre-set password ever issued or transmitted), 5 movers (credential reset and entitlements
re-derived from the new role rather than added to the old set — a mover keeping their previous access is
the failure this row exists to catch, and the quarterly reconciliation in row 20 found one), 2 leavers
(access revoked and credentials invalidated inside 4 hours of the HR effective time, both verified from
the provider's own revocation events rather than from a checklist tick).

**(c) The credential-storage mechanism actually deployed (row 18).** Verified 2026-08-19. The answer
distinguishes what we inspected from what we rely on an attestation for, because conflating the two is
how a control gets reported as verified when it was assumed:

| Credential | Where it lives | Storage mechanism | How we know |
|---|---|---|---|
| Human interactive credentials | The corporate directory only. **We operate no self-managed password store** — this is the material fact, and it is why the question "which algorithm did we choose?" has the answer "none, because we hold no password verifiers" | Vendor-operated, salted adaptive hashing; no reversible storage | **Attested**, not inspected: the provider's SOC 2 and ISO 27001 scope covers credential protection. We cannot inspect it and do not claim to. Recorded as an attested dependency rather than a verified control |
| Federated sessions into AWS | No credential at all — SAML/OIDC assertions, short-lived STS credentials | n/a | **Inspected**: Identity Center configuration read 2026-08-19; 0 IAM users with console passwords; 0 long-lived access keys in the workload accounts |
| Service and workload credentials | AWS Secrets Manager | Envelope-encrypted under a customer-managed KMS key; no plaintext at rest; no credential in code, image, or environment file in a repository | **Inspected**: secret configuration and key policy read; secret-scanning results for the period show 0 findings reaching a build |
| The one API-token verifier the service itself stores (customer webhook signing secrets) | Application data store | Hashed with **Argon2id** at the parameters in `SEC-CRY-022` §3, salted per record; the plaintext exists only in the response to the create call and is never retrievable afterwards | **Inspected**: implementation reviewed 2026-08-19 against the standard's named parameters; a test asserts the verifier is not reversible and that retrieval returns only the hash prefix |
| Break-glass credential | Sealed, offline, with a recorded custody chain (`SEC-IAM-021` §7) | Not stored in any system | **Inspected** at the quarterly custody check |

The gap this closes was specific and worth naming: the cryptography standard named Argon2id and bcrypt as
approved algorithms, and a reader could not tell which was deployed. The accurate answer is that for human
credentials **neither is deployed by us**, because we deliberately hold no password verifiers, and for the
one verifier we do hold it is Argon2id. Stating "we use Argon2id" without that distinction would have been
a claim about a store that does not exist. **Deficiency D3** (the retired IAM user with a standing access
key, and root MFA) remains open and is not covered by this verification — it is itemised in §4 and is not
remediable in the current frozen environment.

**(d) Acceptable-use artifact sampling (row 19).** The quarterly sample tests whether the acceptable-use
rules on captures, screenshots and information handling are actually followed, rather than whether people
have been told about them. Method: a defined population (screenshots and images in the collaboration
platform's file store, images attached to tickets, screen captures in documentation, and images in
outbound customer correspondence), a random sample of at least 30, reviewed by the Compliance Manager
against the classification and capture rules, with every artifact's identifier, verdict and disposition
recorded.

| Cycle | Executed | Population | Sampled | Compliant | Findings and disposition |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Q1 | 2026-04-16, by Compliance Manager | 1,207 | 34 | 32 | 1 screenshot of a ticket showing a customer's account identifier in an internal design document — redacted, document version superseded, author briefed. 1 screen capture in a runbook showing a session token that had already been rotated — removed and the rotation confirmed |
| Q2 | 2026-07-14, by Compliance Manager | 1,341 | 38 | 38 | None. Recorded as a clean result with the population and sample size stated, so "no findings" is a measurement rather than a silence |

Both Q1 findings were handled as conduct matters under §6 at the lowest tier (coaching, no sanction), and
the second one changed a control rather than a person: runbook screenshots are now blocked from containing
credential material by a documentation lint rule, because a rule enforced by a tool does not depend on a
reviewer noticing.

## 6. Conduct evaluation and sanctions determination (CC7.4-R16)

When an event involves, or may involve, the **unauthorized use or disclosure of personal information**, the
incident response program requires a conduct evaluation: the responder determines whether any individual's or
organization's conduct caused or contributed to it and, **where appropriate, applies sanctions** in accordance
with the entity's disciplinary policy and applicable legal and regulatory requirements. This section is the
operating record that the determination step actually runs — not the policy that defines it (that lives in the
incident response and breach-notification procedures).

**Standing rule.** Every confirmed or suspected unauthorized-use-or-disclosure event is subjected to the
determination before its incident record is closed; the closure is blocked until the determination is recorded.
A determination has four fixed fields: *did unauthorized use or disclosure of personal information occur*, *whose
conduct is in scope*, *the finding*, and *the sanction or the reason none applies*. "No sanction warranted" is a
valid, recorded outcome — the requirement is that conduct is **evaluated**, not that a sanction is always issued.

**In-period determinations.** No confirmed unauthorized disclosure of personal information occurred in the
reporting period. The determination process was nonetheless exercised, and its records are below, so the control
is evidenced as operating rather than merely defined:

| ID | Trigger | Unauthorized use/disclosure of PII? | Conduct evaluated | Determination | Decided by |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| S1 | Access review finding D3 — standing static admin keys and root MFA disabled | No — a control weakness; no evidence any personal information was accessed or disclosed as a result | Reviewed whether retention of the keys was misconduct or an accepted-and-tracked gap | No sanction: the condition is a registered, owned deficiency under active remediation, not a policy violation by an individual; remediation assigned rather than discipline | Head of Security Engineering |
| S2 | Annual breach simulation, 2026-02-19 (tabletop with an injected "insider emailed an evidence export externally" scenario) | Simulated | Ran the full determination path end to end against the injected facts, including the sanction decision and the legal/regulatory notification branch | Exercise outcome: the path produced a defensible sanction recommendation and a notification decision within the target window; two documentation gaps found and closed (see §4 lineage) | Incident commander |
| S3 | One reported near-miss — an authorized recipient forwarded a result notification (reference only, no content) to a colleague outside the intended distribution | No — the notification carries a reference, not personal information; pull-based retrieval meant no content was disclosed | Evaluated the forwarder's conduct against the acceptable-use policy | No sanction: no personal information was exposed and the design prevented content disclosure; recipient reminded of the distribution rule | Incident commander |

**Why "no confirmed incident" does not make R16 not-applicable.** The requirement is that the entity *evaluates
conduct and, where appropriate, sanctions*. That obligation is discharged by having the determination run on
every qualifying event and by exercising it when no real event occurs, both of which are recorded above. The
determination path, the authority who decides, and the sanction options are therefore demonstrated in operation,
not asserted.
