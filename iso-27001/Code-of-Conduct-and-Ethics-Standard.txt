## 1. Standards of conduct — what is expected

The Board of Directors and senior management have **defined and documented the standards of conduct**
that articulate their expectations concerning integrity and ethical values. The standards below are
the authoritative statement of those expectations; nothing in a team norm, a manager's instruction or
a customer commitment overrides them.

| # | Standard | What it requires in practice |
|---|---|---|
| S-1 | **Honest representation of the service** | No statement to a customer, prospect, auditor or regulator that overstates what the system does. Limitations are disclosed before contracting, not after |
| S-2 | **Integrity of assessment results** | No adjustment of a compliance verdict, score or rationale to please a customer, close a deal or improve a metric. A verdict is changed only on evidence |
| S-3 | **Truthfulness in records** | Records, journals, tickets, timesheets and reports state what happened. Backdating, selective deletion and retrospective editing of an audit record are prohibited |
| S-4 | **Protection of entrusted data** | Customer data is used only for the purpose it was supplied for, accessed only with a business need, and never taken outside approved systems |
| S-5 | **Speaking up** | A covered person who sees a deviation raises it. Failing to report a known material deviation is itself a deviation |
| S-6 | **No retaliation** | Retaliation against anyone who raises a concern in good faith is a terminable offence, whether or not the concern is substantiated |
| S-7 | **Conflicts of interest** | Declared on hire, annually, and within 5 business days of arising. Undeclared conflicts are treated as deviations |
| S-8 | **Fair dealing** | No bribery, kickbacks, facilitation payments, anti-competitive collusion or misuse of a customer's confidential information |
| S-9 | **Lawful and respectful workplace** | Compliance with applicable law; no harassment or discrimination |
| S-10 | **Use of AI within our own operations** | Covered persons follow the Responsible Use Policy (AIMS-POL-018) when using AI internally; unapproved AI services are not used with entity or customer data |

## 2. Tone at the top — directives, actions and behavior

The Board and management at all levels demonstrate the importance of integrity and ethical values
**through directives, actions and behavior**, not through the existence of this document. Evidence of
each is recorded, because a claim of tone at the top that produces no artefacts is unverifiable.

| Channel | Frequency | Owner | Record produced |
|---|---|---|---|
| Board resolution adopting this Standard and the Audit Committee charter | On material change; last 2026-02-19 | Board | Signed minute |
| CEO written statement on integrity, issued with the Standard | Annually | CEO | Distributed statement, acknowledgement log |
| Ethics item as a standing agenda point at the quarterly Board meeting | Quarterly | Audit Committee Chair | Minutes with the deviations report |
| Management "decisions we made the hard way" briefing at the all-hands | Quarterly | CEO + Accountable Owner | Deck and recording |
| Manager-led team discussion of one real case | Semi-annually | Each people manager | Attendance record |
| Leadership escalation drill (a scripted request to soften a verdict) | Annually | Responsible AI Lead | Exercise report |

**Actions and behavior, recorded.** Three decisions in the current period are documented in the Board
minutes as the concrete demonstration that the standards bind management itself:

1. **2025-11-14 — revenue declined over S-1/S-2.** A prospective enterprise customer asked for a
   contractual commitment that assessments would not return findings below a stated threshold.
   Management declined and recorded the lost opportunity rather than negotiate the wording.
2. **2026-01-09 — a published result was corrected against interest.** A defect caused eight
   assessments to overstate coverage. Management notified the affected customers within 3 business
   days and reissued the results, before any customer had noticed.
3. **2026-02-05 — an executive was sanctioned.** A senior manager instructed an engineer to omit a
   known limitation from a customer-facing summary. The engineer raised it through the concerns
   channel; the instruction was reversed, the summary corrected, and the manager received a formal
   written warning and lost their variable compensation for the period (see §5, case D-2026-003).
   The Audit Committee reviewed the case directly because the subject was a member of management.

Case 3 is recorded deliberately. A deviation register containing only junior staff would be evidence
that the process does not reach the people best placed to override it.

## 3. Communication and understanding at all levels — including third parties

The standards are **understood at all levels of the entity and by outsourced service providers and
business partners**. Understanding is measured, not assumed: acknowledgement alone is treated as
weak evidence, so comprehension is tested.

| Audience | How the standards reach them | How understanding is evidenced | Current period result |
|---|---|---|---|
| Directors and officers | Board adoption; annual signed acknowledgement | Signed acknowledgement + annual conflicts declaration | 5 of 5 |
| Employees | Onboarding within 5 business days; annual refresher with scenario questions | Acknowledgement + ≥ 80% on the scenario assessment | 34 of 34 acknowledged; 34 passed (2 on a second attempt) |
| Interns and temporary staff | Same onboarding as employees | Acknowledgement + scenario assessment | 3 of 3 |
| **Contractors and vendor personnel with system or data access** | Flow-down clause in every contract; the Standard is supplied as an annex; access is not provisioned until acknowledged | Countersigned annex per individual; access request records the acknowledgement | 11 of 11 individuals across 4 suppliers |
| **Outsourced service providers** (sub-processors, labelling vendors, assessors) | Contractual obligation to maintain equivalent standards and to notify us of deviations affecting our service | Supplier attestation at onboarding and annually; reviewed in the supplier record (AIMS-PRO-010 §4) | 6 of 6 attested |
| **Business partners and integrators** | Pass-through conduct and disclosure obligations in the partner agreement | Executed agreement with the conduct annex | 2 of 2 |

**Measured comprehension, not just receipt.** The 2026 refresher included five scenarios drawn from
real cases. Two employees initially answered incorrectly on the "customer asks you to soften a
finding" scenario and were re-trained before passing; that result drove the addition of S-2 examples
to the onboarding module. A 100% first-pass rate is treated as a sign the assessment is too easy
rather than as a success.

## 4. Evaluating performance against the standards

**Processes are in place to evaluate the performance of individuals and teams against the expected
standards of conduct.** Conduct is assessed separately from delivery, so strong delivery cannot mask
a conduct problem.

| Mechanism | Scope | Cadence | Owner | Output |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Conduct rating in the performance review — a distinct, non-averaged rating | Every employee and intern | Semi-annual | People manager, calibrated by skip-level | Rating of Meets / Does Not Meet, with narrative |
| Team-level conduct review | Each team | Semi-annual | Department head | Team findings, systemic issues escalated |
| Manager attestation | Each manager, for their team | Semi-annual | Department head | Attestation of no unreported deviations |
| Access and action review — actual behavior against S-3/S-4 | All privileged users | Quarterly | AI Security Lead | Exceptions list, investigated individually |
| Verdict-integrity review — sampled assessments checked for unevidenced changes against S-2 | 25 sampled assessments | Quarterly | Responsible AI Lead | Findings; any unevidenced change is a deviation |
| **Contractor and vendor personnel conduct evaluation** | All contractor/vendor individuals with access | Quarterly, and at engagement end | Engagement owner + Procurement | Continue / remediate / remove decision, recorded in the supplier record |
| Supplier-level conduct evaluation | Each outsourced provider | Annually, per AIMS-PRO-010 | Third-Party Risk | Supplier record entry |

A Does Not Meet conduct rating caps the overall review outcome regardless of delivery performance,
blocks promotion for the cycle, and forfeits variable compensation. This is what makes the rating
consequential rather than advisory.

## 5. Identifying deviations in a timely manner

Deviations are **identified in a timely manner** through channels that do not depend on the goodwill
of the person a report concerns. All channels feed one register with one clock.

| # | Channel | Open to | Anonymous? | Routed to |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| D-1 | Direct report to any manager | Anyone | No | Manager → HR + Ethics Officer |
| D-2 | Ethics mailbox | Anyone, including contractors and vendor staff | Yes | Ethics Officer |
| D-3 | Third-party hotline (independent operator, 24/7) | Anyone, including external parties | Yes | Ethics Officer, with direct copy to the Audit Committee Chair |
| D-4 | Direct line to the Audit Committee Chair, bypassing management | Anyone | Yes | Audit Committee Chair |
| D-5 | Concerns procedure for AI-specific concerns (AIMS-PRO-003) | Anyone | Yes | Responsible AI Lead |
| D-6 | Detective controls — privileged-action alarms, access exception reports, verdict-integrity sampling | n/a (automated) | n/a | AI Security Lead |
| D-7 | Supplier-notified deviation under the flow-down clause | Suppliers | n/a | Third-Party Risk |
| D-8 | Customer or external-party report | Anyone | Yes | Ethics Officer |

**The clock and its measurement.** Triage begins within **1 business day** of receipt. A preliminary
assessment is completed within **5 business days**. D-4 exists specifically so that a deviation
implicating management can be identified without passing through management, which is the failure
mode that makes conduct programs ineffective. Detection latency is reported to the Audit Committee
quarterly, measured from **the date of the conduct** where it can be established — not from the date
of the report — because measuring from receipt would have made the case below look timely.

Current period: 9 reports; median time from receipt to triage 0.5 business days; median time from
conduct to detection 6 days. The **longest was 71 days** (case D-2026-002, an undeclared conflict
found by the annual declaration rather than by a report), which drove the addition of the quarterly
manager attestation in §4.

## 6. Remedying deviations in a timely and consistent manner

Deviations are **remedied in a timely and consistent manner**. Consistency is enforced by a defined
sanctions framework applied by a standing panel rather than by individual managers, and by review of
outcomes for disparity.

| Severity | Definition | Standard consequence range | Decision by | Target closure |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Minor | Procedural lapse, no harm, no concealment | Coaching, documented; re-training | Manager + HR | 10 business days |
| Moderate | Repeated lapse, or a lapse with potential for harm | Written warning; conduct rating impact; variable-compensation impact | Conduct Panel | 20 business days |
| Serious | Actual harm, or breach of S-1 to S-4 | Final warning to termination; access removed; customer notification if results affected | Conduct Panel + CEO | 30 business days |
| Severe | Deliberate falsification, retaliation, data misuse, or any deviation by a member of management | Termination; contract termination for a supplier; referral to authorities where required | Audit Committee | 30 business days, no extension |

**Consistency mechanisms:** a single Conduct Panel (HR, Legal, Ethics Officer, plus an independent
Audit Committee member for Serious and above) decides all Moderate-and-above cases; precedent from
the case register is reviewed before each decision; outcomes are analyzed semi-annually for disparity
by role, seniority, tenure and employment type; and **contractor and vendor personnel are subject to
the same framework**, with the consequence executed through the supplier contract (removal from the
engagement, and where warranted termination of the supplier relationship).

**Case register, current period** — every case, including the ones with an uncomfortable subject:

| Case | Type | Subject | Severity | Outcome | Days to close |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| D-2026-001 | Credential shared between two engineers | Employees | Moderate | Written warnings; credential rotated; SSO gap closed | 12 |
| D-2026-002 | Undeclared conflict — advisory role at a customer | Employee | Moderate | Declared and recused; written warning; detection gap fixed | 18 |
| D-2026-003 | Instruction to omit a known limitation | **Member of management** | Serious | Final written warning; variable compensation forfeited; summary corrected; Audit Committee reviewed | 21 |
| D-2026-004 | Vendor labeller retained sample data on a personal device | **Vendor employee** | Serious | Individual removed from the engagement; data destruction attested; supplier corrective action | 15 |
| D-2026-005 | Retaliatory comment toward a reporter | Employee | Serious | Final written warning; reporter protection reviewed with the Audit Committee | 9 |
| D-2026-006 to 009 | Four Minor procedural lapses | Employees (3), contractor (1) | Minor | Coaching and re-training | 4-9 |

Nine reports, nine closed, none outside the target. Two implicated a member of management or a vendor
employee — the two categories a conduct program most often fails to reach.

## 7. Roles

| Role | Responsibility |
|---|---|
| Board of Directors | Adopts the Standard; owns the tone at the top; decides Severe cases |
| Audit Committee | Independent oversight of the program; receives all reports directly; reviews every case involving management |
| CEO | Sets and demonstrates the tone; accountable for the program |
| Ethics Officer (General Counsel) | Runs the register; triages; convenes the Conduct Panel |
| People managers | Communicate the standards; evaluate conduct; escalate deviations |
| Procurement / Third-Party Risk | Flows the standards down to suppliers; evidences supplier and vendor-personnel attestation |
| Every covered person | Complies; raises deviations; cooperates with investigations |

## 8. Program effectiveness and review

Reviewed annually and on material change. Reported to the Audit Committee quarterly: report volume by
channel, detection latency from conduct, closure time against target, outcome distribution and
disparity analysis, acknowledgement and comprehension coverage including third parties, and open
cases. **A quarter with zero reports is investigated as a reporting-channel failure**, not celebrated.
