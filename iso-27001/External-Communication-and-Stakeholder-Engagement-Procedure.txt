## 1. Processes to communicate relevant and timely information to external parties

**Processes are in place to communicate relevant and timely information to external parties**,
including shareholders, partners, owners, regulators, customers, financial analysts and others. Each
process below has a named owner, a defined trigger or cadence, and a retained record — an outbound
communication that is not recorded cannot be shown to have happened.

| # | External party | Information communicated | Owner | Trigger / cadence | Record retained |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|

| E-2 | Customers — incident notification | Nature of the incident, systems and data affected, actions taken, customer actions required, remediation | Incident Commander with the CEO | **Severity 1: within 24 hours of confirmation**; Severity 2: within 72 hours | Incident record with timestamps of each notice |
| E-3 | Customers — availability and SLA | Live status, planned maintenance, degradation, SLA attainment | VP Engineering | Status page real-time; maintenance ≥ 10 business days ahead; SLA quarterly | Status page history; SLA statements |

| E-5 | **Regulators and supervisory authorities** | Registrations and notifications; personal data breach notification; responses to inquiries; DPO contact details | General Counsel with the DPO | On statutory trigger — **breach notification within 72 hours of becoming aware**; otherwise per the applicable deadline | Regulatory correspondence file with dispatch evidence |

security posture or compliance status requires review by the accountable owner before release, and a
statement about compliance status additionally requires Compliance Manager sign-off. This exists
because misrepresenting the service externally is treated as a Severe conduct deviation
(GOV-STD-001 S-4); the recorded 2026-01-09 correction of published results (GOV-STD-001 §2) is the
instance where this process was used against the entity's own interest.

## 2. Open channels enabling inbound communication

**Open communication channels allow input from customers, consumers, suppliers, external auditors,
regulators, financial analysts and others, providing management and the Board with relevant
information.** Inbound is treated as a control input, not as correspondence: every channel routes to a
named owner, carries a response commitment, and is reported upward.

| Inbound channel | Open to | Routed to | Response commitment | Reported to |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Customer support and escalation | Customers, authorized users | Customer Operations | Acknowledged within 1 business day; Sev-1 immediately | Monthly management review |
| **Customer complaint register** | Customers, users | Director, Customer Operations | Logged same day; substantive response within 10 business days | Quarterly to management **and the Board** |
| Assessment accuracy / dispute channel | Customers, users | Responsible AI Lead | Triage within 2 business days; re-assessment where warranted | AI Governance Oversight Committee, quarterly |
| **Data subject request channel** (web form, email, postal) | Data subjects, incl. non-customers | DPO | Acknowledged within 5 days; fulfilled within the statutory period | Quarterly privacy report to the Board |
| Privacy inquiries and objections | Data subjects | DPO | Within 30 days | Quarterly privacy report |
| **Vulnerability disclosure** | Security researchers, anyone | AI Security Lead | Acknowledged within 3 business days; triaged within 5 | Quarterly security report to the Board |
| Supplier and partner channel | Suppliers, sub-processors, partners | Third-Party Risk Manager | Within 5 business days | Semi-annual third-party report |
| Regulator inquiries | Supervisory authorities, regulators | General Counsel | Per the deadline stated; acknowledged within 2 business days | **Immediately to the Board Chair** |
| External auditor requests | Auditors | Compliance Manager | Per the engagement plan | Audit Committee each meeting |
| Investor and analyst inquiries | Shareholders, analysts | CEO / CFO | Within 5 business days | Board |
| **Third-party ethics hotline** (see §4) | Anyone external, including anonymously | Ethics Officer **and** Audit Committee Chair | Triage within 1 business day | Audit Committee, quarterly and immediately for Severe |
| Public contact address and trust page form | Anyone | Triaged by Customer Operations to the right owner | Within 5 business days | Monthly management review |

Channels are **published where the party will look**: in the customer agreement and in-product for
customers, in the privacy notice for data subjects, on the public trust page and `security.txt` for
researchers, in supplier contracts for suppliers, and on the public website for everyone else.

Inbound information is analysed, not merely answered: complaint and dispute themes are reviewed
quarterly against the risk register, and two changes in the current period originated externally — a
customer's report of an ambiguous assessment rationale changed the output template, and a researcher's
disclosure changed the transport-security configuration standard.

## 3. Communicating the results of external assessments to the Board

**Relevant information resulting from assessments conducted by external parties is communicated to the
Board of Directors.** External assessment results go to the Board directly and are not summarized by
the management being assessed; where an assessment concerns management itself, the provider reports to
the Audit Committee in camera.

| External assessment | Provider | Result reported to | Route and timing |
|---|---|---|---|
| SOC 2 examination | Independent service auditor | Audit Committee, then the full Board | Auditor presents **directly**, in camera without management present for part of the session; on issuance |
| Internal audit engagements | Outsourced internal audit provider | Audit Committee | Provider presents directly each quarter; reports to the Committee, not to management (GOV-STR-003 §2) |
| Penetration test | External security testing firm | Security & Privacy Committee | Full report plus remediation status, quarterly |
| ISO/IEC 42001 certification audit and surveillance | Certification body | AI Governance Oversight Committee and the Board | On issuance, with all findings including minor nonconformities |
| Third-party risk assessments of the entity by customers | Customer security teams | Board, in aggregate | Semi-annual, with any adverse finding reported immediately |
| Privacy assessment / DPIA review by external counsel | Retained privacy counsel | Security & Privacy Committee | On completion; counsel reports to the Committee |
| AI assurance advisory review | Independent AI assurance advisor | AI Governance Oversight Committee | Semi-annual |
| Financial audit | External accountants | Audit Committee | Annual, in camera |
| Regulatory examination or inquiry outcome | Authority | **Board Chair immediately**, then the Board | Within 24 hours of receipt |

**All findings are reported, including those management disputes** — a disputed finding is presented
with both the provider's position and management's response, so the Board is not given a
pre-reconciled view. In the current period the Board received: the internal audit provider's four
findings, the penetration test's two high findings, the certification body's two minor
nonconformities, and one adverse customer assessment finding on boundary protection which is tracked
in the CAP register.

## 4. Separate external communication lines as fail-safe mechanisms

**Separate communication channels, such as whistle-blower hotlines, are in place and serve as
fail-safe mechanisms to enable anonymous or confidential communication when normal channels are
inoperative or ineffective.** The normal external channel is customer support or the account owner;
each channel below bypasses a specific failure mode of that path.

| Channel | Open to | Anonymous | Bypasses | Routed to |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Third-party ethics hotline** — independent operator, 24/7, phone and web, published on the public website | Customers, suppliers, partners, vendor staff, anyone | **Yes** | The entire management chain, including the CEO | Ethics Officer **and** the Audit Committee Chair simultaneously |
| **Direct line to the Audit Committee Chair**, published on the public governance page | Anyone external | **Yes** | Management entirely | Audit Committee Chair |
| Confidential customer escalation to an executive sponsor | Customers | No, but confidential | The account team, when the account team is the problem | Named executive sponsor |
| **Responsible-AI and assessment-integrity concerns procedure** | Customers, users, researchers, the public | **Yes** | The delivery chain that owns the model | Responsible AI Lead, with a copy to the Committee |
| Vulnerability disclosure with safe harbour | Security researchers | Pseudonymous accepted | Ordinary support triage and its queue | Security on-call, paged |
| Privacy channel direct to the DPO, published in the notice | Data subjects, anyone | Optional | The operational chain, so a privacy matter is not triaged as a support ticket | DPO |
| Supplier escalation to the Third-Party Risk Manager and, above that, to the Audit Committee | Suppliers, sub-processors | Optional | The engagement owner who may be applying the pressure | Third-Party Risk Manager / Audit Committee |

**The fail-safe property is tested, and protection is stated publicly.** The hotline is tested
semi-annually with a seeded external report — 2 tests in the current period, both reaching the Audit
Committee Chair within one business day without passing through management. Non-retaliation extends
explicitly to external reporters: the entity commits publicly not to pursue researchers acting within
the disclosure policy, and not to penalize a supplier or customer for raising a concern. Volumes are
reported to the Audit Committee quarterly, and **a quarter with zero external reports is investigated
as a possible channel failure rather than accepted as good news**.

## 5. Selecting the method of external communication

**The method of communication considers the timing, audience and nature of the communication and
legal, regulatory and fiduciary requirements and expectations.** Method is prescribed per class, and
the legal, regulatory and fiduciary constraints are named alongside it because those constraints — not
convenience — determine the choice.

| Nature of communication | Timing | Audience | Method | Legal / regulatory / fiduciary driver |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Personal data breach notification to a supervisory authority | **Within 72 hours of becoming aware** | Authority | The authority's prescribed form or portal, signed by the DPO; General Counsel reviews | Statutory deadline and prescribed form; a non-conforming method does not discharge the obligation |
| Breach notification to data subjects | **Without undue delay** where the risk threshold is met | Affected data subjects | Direct written notice; public notice where direct contact is disproportionate | Statutory; content elements are prescribed |
| Security incident notice to customers | Within 24h of confirmation (Sev-1) | Affected customers | Direct notice to the named security contact + status page + a call for material impact | Contractual notification clauses; the duty not to mislead |
| Regulatory inquiry response | By the stated deadline | Authority | Written, through General Counsel, legally reviewed, with privilege considered | Legal; a single controlled channel prevents inconsistent statements |
| Material matter for owners | Without delay | Shareholders, owners | Written report; board-authorized statement | **Fiduciary duty**; equal and simultaneous disclosure so no owner is advantaged |
| Financial and operating results | Quarterly | Shareholders | Reporting pack + meeting | Fiduciary; constitutional documents |
| Change to a customer commitment or the system boundary | **≥ 30 days before effect** | Customers, partners | Written notice to the contract contact + portal + release notes | Contractual change-notice terms |
| Change to the privacy notice | Before the change takes effect; **prior consent where the new purpose requires it** | Data subjects, customers | Updated notice with a version history and change summary; direct notice for a material change | Statutory transparency; consent cannot be retrospective |
| Change to confidentiality objectives or requirements | ≥ 30 days, or immediately if it raises protection | Customers, vendors, partners | Written notice + updated requirements annex | Contract and NDA terms |
| Sub-processor addition or change | **Per the DPA notice period, before the change** | Customers acting as controllers | Written notice + sub-processor list update, preserving the customer's objection right | DPA obligation; the objection right is meaningless if notice is late |
| Planned maintenance | ≥ 10 business days | Customers | Status page + email | SLA terms |
| Assurance report issuance | Annually, on issuance | Customers, prospects under NDA | Report delivered under NDA through the portal | Confidentiality of the report; auditor's distribution restrictions |
| Public security-posture information | On publication | Public | Public trust page, reviewed by General Counsel and Compliance | Duty not to misrepresent; avoid disclosing exploitable detail |
| System design and operational detail | On change | **Authorized** external users only | Access-controlled documentation portal | Confidentiality commitments; a boundary description is not public information |
| Vulnerability disclosure response | Acknowledge in 3 business days | Researcher | Direct, coordinated; public detail only after remediation | Safe-harbour terms; the duty not to expose customers before a fix |

Three standing rules resolve conflicts: **a statutory deadline overrides internal convenience and
overrides an incomplete investigation** (notify on what is known, then supplement); **fiduciary equality
overrides selective briefing** (no owner or analyst is briefed ahead of the others); and
**confidentiality constrains breadth, never truthfulness** — where detail cannot be shared, the entity
says that it cannot be shared rather than giving a partial impression.

## 6. Communicating confidentiality objectives and changes to them

**The entity communicates, to external users, vendors, business partners and others whose products or
services are part of the system, its objectives related to confidentiality and the protection of
confidential information, as well as changes to those objectives.**

**The confidentiality objectives communicated externally.** Information designated Confidential or
Restricted — customer assessment inputs, findings, evidence documents, the resulting compliance
verdicts, and customer environment configuration data — is (a) used only to deliver and support the
service and for no other purpose, in particular not for model training or for benchmarking that could
identify a customer; (b) disclosed only to those with a defined need, to named sub-processors under
equivalent obligations, and where legally compelled, with notice to the customer where lawful;
(c) protected in transit and at rest by encryption; (d) retained only for the defined period and then
destroyed per C1.2; and (e) returned or destroyed on termination on the customer's instruction. Each
objective is stated as an obligation the entity can be held to, not as an aspiration.

| Audience | Where the confidentiality objectives are stated | Change communication |
|---|---|---|
| **Customers and authorized external users** | The customer agreement and its confidentiality and security annexes; the public trust page; the system description; in-product notices on classification and sharing; the onboarding pack | ≥ 30 days written notice before an objective changes; immediately where a change increases protection; annual reaffirmation with the assurance report |
| **Vendors, sub-processors, outsourced providers** | Contract confidentiality clauses; the security requirements annex flowing these objectives down; the DPA; the vendor onboarding briefing; the Code of Conduct annex | Written notice with a countersignature required before the change takes effect; annual reaffirmation and attestation |
| **Business partners and integrators** | Partner agreement; the interface specification stating what may and may not be transmitted, logged or retained | ≥ 30 days notice; the interface specification is versioned |
| **External auditors and consultants** | Engagement letter with confidentiality terms; NDA | On engagement and on change |
| Prospective customers and the public | Public trust page; the confidentiality summary in the service description | On publication, with version history |
| Individual vendor personnel with access | Personal NDA plus the confidentiality module of the entity's training, completed before access | On change, with re-acknowledgement |

**Changes communicated in the current period:** the addition of a sub-processor with its
confidentiality terms (notified 2025-11-24 with the objection right preserved); a tightening of the
retention objective for evidence documents (notified 2026-01-20 — a change increasing protection, so
communicated immediately and applied); and a revision to the requirements annex flowed down to all
seven outsourced providers, with countersignature obtained from all seven before it took effect.

## 7. Communicating privacy objectives and changes to them

**The entity communicates, to external users, vendors, business partners and others whose products or
services are part of the system, its objectives related to privacy and the protection of personal
information, as well as changes to those objectives.**

**The privacy objectives communicated externally.** Personal information is collected only for the
identified, disclosed purposes and limited to what those purposes require; processed on a stated lawful
basis; not used for a new, incompatible purpose without a lawful basis and, where required, consent;
disclosed only to identified categories of recipients and to named sub-processors under equivalent
obligations; transferred across borders only under a lawful mechanism; retained only for the defined
period and then deleted or de-identified; kept accurate and correctable; protected by defined security
measures; and subject to the data subject's rights of access, correction, deletion, objection,
restriction and portability. The entity does not sell personal information and does not use customer
personal data to train models.

| Audience | Where stated | Change communication |
|---|---|---|
| **Data subjects** | The privacy notice — layered, plain-language, versioned, with an effective date and a change summary; collection-point notices; the rights page | Notice updated before the change takes effect; **direct notice for a material change**; **prior consent obtained where a new purpose requires it** — never applied retrospectively |
| **Customers / user entities** (as controllers) | The DPA and its annexes: purposes, categories, sub-processors, transfer mechanism, retention, security measures, assistance with rights; the trust page | ≥ 30 days written notice; sub-processor changes notified per the DPA with the objection right preserved |
| **Vendors, sub-processors, providers** | DPA and standard contractual clauses; the privacy requirements annex flowing these objectives down; instruction-only processing terms; the vendor privacy briefing | Written notice and countersignature before effect; annual attestation |
| **Business partners** | Partner agreement privacy terms; the interface specification stating what personal data may cross the interface | ≥ 30 days notice |
| Regulators and supervisory authorities | Registrations; DPO contact details published in the notice and filed with the authority | On change, filed within the statutory period |
| Public | Public trust and privacy pages, with the notice archive so a prior version can be retrieved | On publication |

**Changes in the current period:** notice v4.0 (2025-11-20) added the EU subsidiary as a joint
establishment and the transfer mechanism, communicated by direct notice to all data subjects and to
customers 34 days before effect; notice v4.1 (2026-01-20) narrowed a retention period, communicated
with the notice update; and one proposed change — a new analytics purpose — was **not made**, because
the DPO concluded it required consent that had not been obtained, and the decision was recorded rather
than the purpose being quietly added.

## 8. Communicating the privacy incident reporting process to external parties

**The entity communicates to user entities, third parties, data subjects and others the process used to
report a suspected privacy incident.**

| Audience | Where the process is communicated | The process communicated |
|---|---|---|
| **Data subjects** | The privacy notice, in a section headed how to report a privacy concern or incident; the rights page; the DPO contact block | Email the DPO or use the web form; report on suspicion without needing to confirm anything; DPO acknowledges within 1 business day; report anonymously if preferred; the DPO's identity and address are given, as is the right to complain to a supervisory authority instead of or in addition to the entity |
| **User entities / customers** | The DPA incident annex; the onboarding pack; the in-product support menu; the named security and privacy contact list exchanged at onboarding | Report to the 24/7 security channel and to the DPO; report suspected incidents affecting personal data even where the cause may lie with the customer; the entity acknowledges within 1 hour and provides the information the customer needs to meet its own controller obligations |
| **Third parties, vendors, sub-processors** | Contract incident clause; the vendor onboarding briefing | **Notify the entity within 24 hours of becoming aware of a suspected incident**, with the prescribed content, to the named channel; do not delay to complete an internal investigation; a late notification is a contractual breach and is recorded in the supplier record |
| **Business partners** | Partner agreement; interface specification | Same 24-hour channel and content |
| **Security researchers** | The public vulnerability disclosure policy and `security.txt` | Report through the disclosure channel; if personal data was accessible, say so explicitly so the DPO is engaged; safe harbour applies; do not download or retain personal data encountered |
| **Anyone, including anonymously** | The public trust page, the ethics hotline, and the direct line to the Audit Committee Chair | Any of these channels reaches the DPO; anonymity is preserved; the entity commits not to retaliate |

**What is published for every audience:** a single named recipient (the DPO) with direct contact
details; the instruction to report on suspicion rather than after confirmation; the acknowledgement
commitment; the availability of anonymous reporting; the commitment not to retaliate; and the fact that
the entity will notify authorities and affected individuals where the legal thresholds are met, so a
reporter knows the report leads somewhere. One external report was received in the current period,
through the hotline, and was routed to the DPO within one business day.

## 9. Communicating system design, operation and boundaries to authorized external users

**The entity prepares and communicates information about the design and operation of the system and its
boundaries to authorized external users to permit them to understand their role in the system and the
results of system operation.**

| Artefact | Content | Audience | Access | Maintained |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **System description** | Purpose; the five-node assessment pipeline; components; data flows; **what is inside the boundary** (the evaluation service, its data stores and pipeline in account 471885797689 / us-west-2) and **what is outside and therefore not the entity's responsibility** (the customer's own AWS environments under assessment, AWS-managed infrastructure, the customer's identity provider, corporate IT) | Customers, authorized users, auditors | Customer portal, authenticated | Reviewed quarterly and on architectural change |

| Integration and interface specification | APIs, authentication, data formats, what may be transmitted | Partners, integrators | Access-controlled | Versioned |
| **Results of system operation** | Live status and incident history; SLA attainment; assessment completion and quality metrics; the customer's own assessment history and evidence coverage; post-incident reviews for incidents that affected them | Customers, authorized users | Status page (public) + in-product dashboard (authenticated) | Continuous |

**Boundary and authorization discipline.** Detailed design information is provided to **authorized**

never published is the detail that would help an attacker without helping a user understand their role.

## 10. Communicating system objectives to external users

**The entity communicates its system objectives to appropriate external users.** The objectives are
stated as commitments with measures attached, so an external user can tell whether they were met.

| Objective category | Communicated as | Where | To whom |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Security** | The entity's security commitments: access is restricted to authorized users and processes; data is encrypted in transit and at rest; vulnerabilities are remediated within defined SLAs; incidents are detected, responded to and notified | System description §3; agreement security annex; trust page | Customers, users, partners, vendors |
| **Availability** | The availability commitment and its measurement window; the recovery time and recovery point objectives; the maintenance-window terms | SLA; system description; status page | Customers, users |
| **Processing integrity** | Assessments are complete, valid, accurate, timely and authorized; the evaluation logic and its inputs are controlled and change-managed; results are traceable to the evidence and the scan data behind them; the stated limitations of automated assessment | System description; the service documentation; the limitations statement in the product | Customers, users |
| **Confidentiality** | The objectives in §6 | Agreement confidentiality annex; trust page | Customers, vendors, partners |
| **Privacy** | The objectives in §7 | Privacy notice; DPA | Data subjects, customers, vendors |
| Objectives the entity does **not** undertake | Explicit non-objectives: the service does not certify compliance, does not replace an independent audit, and does not assess resources outside the granted scope | The limitations statement in the system description and in-product | Customers, users, prospects |

Stating the non-objectives is part of communicating the objectives: an external user who believes the
service certifies compliance has misunderstood their own role in it. Objectives are communicated at
onboarding, restated with each annual assurance report, and re-communicated within 30 days of any
change; the availability commitment change of 2026-01-20 followed that path.

including to someone who cannot log in.

| Billing or contractual issue | Account manager; finance mailbox | Finance | 5 business days |
| Anything, from someone with no account | The public contact form and the published address on the trust page | Triaged to the right owner within 1 business day | 5 business days |

Every entry states **who receives it, what happens next and what protection applies**, and each channel
is reachable without a login for at least one route, because an external user locked out by the very
failure they want to report must still be able to report it. Escalation paths are published too: if a
report is not acknowledged within the commitment, the next contact is named, and above that the
executive sponsor and the Audit Committee line.

## 13. Contact with special interest groups, security forums and professional associations

Everything above concerns parties the entity has a commercial, legal or contractual relationship with.
This section concerns a different kind of contact, and it exists for a different reason: a small entity
cannot generate enough of its own threat experience to stay current. Membership of specialist
communities is how the entity learns about an attack technique before it meets one, receives an
advisory before an exploit is public, and gets a second opinion from someone with no stake in the
answer.

**Ownership.** The CISO owns the entity's participation in these communities and is accountable for
keeping it live. The AI Security Lead owns the AI-specific communities. Membership is reviewed at each
annual review of this procedure, and a lapsed membership is a deficiency, not a dormant subscription —
because a membership nobody attends produces none of the benefit above while still appearing on a list.
