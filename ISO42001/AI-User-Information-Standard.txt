## 1. Determination of what information users need

The organization **determines and provides the information necessary to users of the AI system.**
Determination is deliberate and recorded: §2 documents the criteria applied, §3 the resulting
information set per user category, and §4 how it is kept accessible, complete, current and accurate.

### 1.1 Who counts as a user

| User category | Relationship to the system | Expertise assumed |
|---|---|---|
| **Compliance analysts and auditors** (primary users, and the human oversight point) | Read, interrogate, override and act on verdicts | Qualified in the framework assessed; not necessarily AI-literate |
| **Assessed organizations' staff** (recipients of findings) | Receive and act on findings and remediation tasks | Domain-competent, variable AI literacy |
| **Operators / administrators** | Configure environments, scope, evidence and model configuration; suspend or roll back | Technically expert |
| **External auditors and certification bodies** | Rely on results as assurance input | Expert in audit, not in this system |
| **Internal management** | Rely on aggregate posture | Non-specialist |

## 2. Documented criteria for determining whether and what information is provided

The following criteria are applied to every candidate item of user information. An item is provided when
any criterion triggers it; the criteria also determine the **form and depth**, so information is
appropriate to context and user need rather than uniform.

| # | Criterion | How it is applied | Example of what it produced |
|---|---|---|---|
| C-1 | **Intended use** — the user must know what the system is for and what its output means | Anything a user must know to rely correctly on a verdict is provided | The statement that a result is an assessment aid and **not** a certification or attestation |
| C-2 | **Reasonably foreseeable misuse** — where a plausible misuse exists, information is provided specifically to counter it | Each foreseeable misuse in AIMS-REC-005 §2 is checked for a corresponding user-facing statement | The prohibition on using verdicts to evaluate individual employees; the warning that a passing score is not evidence of certification |
| C-3 | **User expertise** — depth and vocabulary are set by the least AI-literate user in the category, not by the most expert | Analyst-facing material assumes framework competence but not AI competence; operator material assumes technical depth | Rationale-interpretation guidance for analysts; configuration reference for operators only |
| C-4 | **Specific impacts of the system** — where an identified impact falls on a user or on someone they affect, the user is told | Each impact in AIMS-REC-005 §5 and §7 is checked for a disclosure | Disclosure that a false gap can create unnecessary remediation work; the English-language domain limitation |
| C-5 | **Awareness that the output is AI-generated** | Always provided, unconditionally | Every verdict is labelled as machine-generated and pending human review |
| C-6 | **Ability to intervene** — if a user can influence or reverse an outcome, they must be told how | Override, halt, re-scope, annotate and suspend are all documented with instructions | Override procedure in the operator guide |
| C-7 | **Legal, contractual or regulatory obligation** | Legal & Privacy identify mandatory disclosures per jurisdiction | Data-processing and retention disclosures |
| C-8 | **Accuracy limits and domain of use** — a user must be able to tell when they are outside the characterized domain | Limitations are published, not buried | Supported frameworks; English-language limitation; point-in-time nature of a result |
| C-9 | **Proportionality** — information must not be so voluminous that the important parts are lost | Layered presentation: essential statements in the interface, detail in documentation | The non-certification statement appears on the result itself, not only in the manual |
| C-10 | **Withheld where justified, and the withholding recorded** | Intellectual property and security-sensitive detail may be withheld, but never in a way that prevents a user judging the output | Prompts and rubrics are withheld; **every verdict's rationale and cited evidence are always disclosed** |

## 3. Information provided

### 3.1 Provided to all users, unconditionally

| Information | Where it appears | Criterion |
|---|---|---|
| **That this is an AI system and the verdicts are machine-generated** | On every result and in the interface, not only in documentation | C-5 |
| **Intended purpose** — automated, evidence-cited compliance assessment to support qualified human judgement | Product documentation; result header | C-1 |
| **That the result is an assessment aid, not a certification or attestation** | On every result and in every export | C-1, C-2 |
| **That a qualified human must review consequential verdicts, and that override is available** | Result presentation; operator guide | C-1, C-6 |
| **Technical limitations** — probabilistic judgement, point-in-time observation, API-observability limits, English-language domain, supported frameworks only | Published limitations page (AIMS-DOC-012 §2) | C-4, C-8 |
| **How to interpret an evidence gap versus a failure** | Shown distinctly in the output; explained in the interpretation guide | C-1, C-4 |
| **What the score does and does not mean**, including that unreadable requirements are excluded rather than failed | Scoring explanation | C-1, C-8 |
| **How data is used** — no customer data used for training; retention; tenant isolation | Privacy and data-handling notice | C-7 |
| **How to report a problem or an adverse impact**, including anonymously | In-product help; support portal (AIMS-PRO-016) | C-6, C-7 |
| Prohibited uses | Intended-use statement | C-2 |

### 3.2 How to interact with the system, and how and when to override

| Topic | Information provided |
|---|---|
| **How to interact** | The system is machine-invoked and asynchronous — users do not converse with it. Users act by: registering an environment and authorizing read-only access; creating a project and selecting a framework; uploading evidence **and linking it to the specific controls it supports** (an unlinked document is invisible to a control — stated explicitly, because it is the most common user error); running an assessment; then reviewing results |
| **How to read a result** | Per-requirement status, rationale and cited evidence; the control summary and score; the assessment journal for the full phase and tool-call trace |
| **How to override** | Any verdict can be overridden by a qualified analyst through the result view; the override is recorded with actor and timestamp and retained with the assessment |
| **When to override** | Guidance provided: when the rationale does not follow from the cited evidence; when evidence exists that the system did not have or could not read; when the requirement is not applicable to the organization; when the analyst's framework judgement differs on interpretation. Guidance is explicit that override is **expected in normal use**, not an exception — a zero override rate is itself a signal of insufficient review |
| **When not to override** | When the disagreement is with the requirement itself rather than the verdict (that is a framework-scoping decision); when no additional evidence supports the change. Overrides must be justified in the record |
| **Other controls** | Halt or re-scope an assessment; add or replace evidence and re-run; annotate; suspend the system (operator/AIGB authority) |
| **What users cannot influence** | The scoring formula, requirement text, and the rubric — stated so users do not expect configuration that does not exist |

| Category | Additional information | Form |
|---|---|---|
| Compliance analysts / auditors | Rationale-interpretation guide with worked examples; evidence-citation model; override procedure and expectations; journal walkthrough; known failure modes to look for | Operator guide + in-product help; worked examples rather than abstract description |

## 4. Accessibility, completeness, currency and accuracy — and their validation

Information provided to users is **made accessible and is validated to be complete, up to date and
accurate.** Validation is a defined activity with an owner and a record, not an assumption.

### 4.1 Accessibility

| Aspect | Measure |
|---|---|
| Assistive technology | Output and documentation are compatible with screen readers; semantic structure rather than visual-only layout |
| No colour-only encoding | Verdict status is conveyed in text as well as colour (IC-8) |
| Plain language | Customer-facing material avoids internal identifiers and jargon; reviewed for readability |
| Findability | Essential statements (AI-generated, not-a-certification, human-review-required) appear **at the point of use**, not only in a manual — a document nobody opens is not information provided |
| Availability | Documentation published alongside the release it describes, never after |
| Reviewed by | Accessibility specialist, most recently 2026-03-03 |

### 4.2 Validation

| Validation | Method | Frequency | Owner | Last performed |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Completeness** | Each criterion in §2 is walked against the current information set; each foreseeable misuse (AIMS-REC-005 §2) and each identified impact (§5, §7) must have a corresponding disclosure or a recorded reason for none | Every release; full review semi-annually | AI System Accountable Owner | 2026-03-06 |
| **Currency** | Documentation is updated in the same change as the system change it describes; documentation currency (RC-10) blocks release | Every release | AI Development Lead | 2026-03-06 |
| **Accuracy** | Stated behaviour is checked against actual behaviour on the release candidate; a discrepancy is a defect, not a documentation nicety. Analysts confirm the interpretation guide matches what the system actually produces (release criterion 5) | Every release | Compliance analysts + AI Development Lead | 2026-03-04 |
| **Comprehensibility** | Analyst comprehension review — can a qualified reader determine, unaided, why a verdict was reached? (IC-11) | Quarterly, sampled | Responsible AI Lead | 2026-02-25 |
| Accessibility | Specialist review | Semi-annually and on interface change | Responsible AI Lead | 2026-03-03 |

**Findings from the last validation (2026-03-06):** two gaps were found and closed — the evidence-linkage
requirement was not stated prominently enough at the point of upload (now surfaced in the upload flow
and in §3.2), and the override guidance did not say that override is expected rather than exceptional
(now stated in §3.2). Recording closed findings is deliberate: a validation that never finds anything is
not a validation.

## 5. Change and communication

User-visible changes are communicated per AIMS-PRO-007 §5.2: verdict-affecting changes at least 10
business days ahead with their effective date and expected effect; new framework support on release;
withdrawal or re-issue of a published result immediately on decision. **Documentation is published
before or with the change, never after.**
