## 1. Allocation of responsibilities within the organization and with other parties

Responsibilities are **allocated between the organization, its partners, suppliers, customers and
relevant third parties**, and the allocation is documented rather than assumed. An unallocated
responsibility is the failure mode this document exists to prevent: the parts of an AI life cycle most
likely to be neglected are the ones each party assumes the other is covering.

### 1.1 The organization's roles, and why it holds more than one

The organization is simultaneously:

| Role held | In relation to | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| **Provider / developer** | The compliance-evaluation system it builds and offers | Owns design, V&V, documentation, limitations disclosure |
| **User / deployer** | Its own use of that same system | Subject to AIMS-POL-018 as a user, not exempt from it |
| **Customer** | Its suppliers (cloud, foundation models) | Must exercise supplier requirements and monitoring (AIMS-PRO-010) |
| **Processor** | Assessed organizations' personal data | Acts only on the controller's instruction |

Holding several roles is recorded explicitly because each carries different duties, and conflating them
is how a provider ends up exempting itself from the obligations it places on its own users.

### 1.2 Allocation across the life cycle

Legend: **A** accountable (one only) · **R** responsible · **C** consulted · **I** informed · **—** no role

| Life-cycle stage | Organization (provider) | Cloud & model suppliers | Assessed organization (customer) | Partners / integrators | Third parties (auditors, regulators, affected individuals) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Inception — decide to build/use** | **A/R** — use-case approval, prohibited-use screen, impact and risk assessment | I | C — needs and expectations gathered (AIMS-PRO-026) | C | C — affected groups consulted in the impact assessment |
| **Requirements and design** | **A/R** — intended purpose, valid domain, failure-mode choices, oversight design | C — platform and model capability and constraints | C — requirements and usability input | C — integration requirements | C — analyst and auditor expectations |
| **Data** | **A/R** — quality, provenance, preparation, corpus governance | R — model training data (**their** responsibility, not visible to us) | **R — supplies its own evidence and is responsible for its accuracy and completeness, and for linking it to controls** | — | C — data subjects' rights |
| **Development** | **A/R** | R — platform and model availability and behaviour | — | R — integration code they own | — |
| **V&V** | **A/R** — evaluation against qualified human labels, adversarial and fairness testing, release criteria | C — model behaviour characterization | C — participates in acceptance where contracted | R — tests their own integration | C — external audit of the process |
| **Release / deployment** | **A/R** — deployment as code, pinned image and model, documentation currency | R — service availability | **R — registers the environment and creates the read-only role, i.e. grants and bounds our access** | R — deploys their own integration | I |
| **Operation** | **A/R** — monitoring, alerting, coverage, incident response | R — service and model operation, deprecation notice | **R — uses within the valid domain; provides human review of consequential verdicts; keeps evidence current** | R — operates their integration | — |
| **Human oversight of individual verdicts** | R — provides the capability, guidance and record; verifies it is exercised | — | **A — the reviewing analyst is accountable for the verdict relied upon** | — | — |
| **Monitoring for adverse impacts** | **A/R** — register, triage, remediation | I | R — reports what it observes | R | **R — anyone may report; we must provide the channel** |

| **Decommissioning** | **A/R** — AIGB decision, record retention, customer notice | R — service withdrawal notice | R — exports its records | R | I |

### 1.3 Boundaries that are stated explicitly because they are commonly misread

| Boundary | Ours | Not ours |
|---|---|---|
| Correctness of the *evidence* | We assess and cite what we are given, and report unreadable evidence as unable-to-assess rather than non-compliance | The truthfulness and completeness of what the customer supplies |
| The compliance *conclusion* | The assessment aid, its rationale and its citations | The certification, attestation or assurance opinion — always the customer's or its auditor's, with qualified human sign-off |
| Access to the customer's account | Using only what the customer granted, read-only, in scope | Creating or widening that access; the role is the customer's to create, bound and revoke |
| Remediation of findings | Reporting them clearly and actionably | Performing remediation in the customer's environment |
| Foundation model behaviour | Selecting, pinning, testing, monitoring, and disclosing limitations | The model's weights and its training data |
| Personal data in evidence | Processing on instruction, minimizing, securing, deleting on request | Determining the purpose of that processing — the customer is the controller |

## 2. Parties intervening in the life cycle, and their roles

All parties are documented, internal and external. "Intervening" is read broadly: anyone whose action or
inaction can change an outcome.

### 2.1 Internal

| Role | Responsibility in the life cycle |
|---|---|
| **Top management** | Provides resources; owns the AIMS; approves the AI policy; management review |
| **Chief AI Officer** | Accountable executive for AI governance; approves Severity 1 external communication |
| **AI Governance Board** | Approves use cases, authorization to operate, residual-risk acceptance; may suspend the system |
| **AI System Accountable Owner** | Single accountable owner for this system end to end; decides withdrawal, correction, re-issue |
| **Responsible AI Lead** | Impact assessment; fairness; adverse-impact handling; **veto on prohibited uses** |
| **AI Development Lead** | Design, build, V&V, prompts and rubrics, documentation currency |
| **AI Operations Lead** | Deployment, monitoring, alerting, incident response, logging and retention |
| **AI Security Lead** | Threat modelling, adversarial testing, credential and access design, supplier security |
| **Data Steward** | Data acquisition, selection, quality, provenance; admits or rejects data |
| **Compliance analysts** | Define correct behaviour; label ground truth; **exercise human oversight of verdicts** |
| **Legal & Privacy (incl. DPO)** | Legal and regulatory obligations; privacy roles; DPAs; breach notification |
| **Procurement / Third-Party Risk** | Approved sourcing; supplier contracts |
| **Internal Audit** | Independent audit of the AIMS; nonconformity reporting |
| **HR / L&D** | Competence and awareness |
| **Finance** | Funds ongoing monitoring and maintenance — a named condition of use approval |

### 2.2 External

| Party | Role in the life cycle | Governing instrument |
|---|---|---|
| **Cloud provider (AWS)** | Infrastructure, managed runtime, logging, identity services | Customer agreement; shared-responsibility model |
| **Foundation model supplier (via Amazon Bedrock)** | Model capability, availability, deprecation notice; training-data provenance is theirs and is not disclosed to us | Service terms; AIMS-PRO-010 |
| **Assessed organizations (customers)** | Grant read-only access; supply and link evidence; exercise human review; decide reliance | Contract, DPA, usage agreement (AIMS-PRO-026) |
| **Customers' analysts / auditors** | Users, and the human oversight point | Usage agreement; AIMS-STD-015 |
| **Partners and integrators** | Consume or embed results | Partner agreement with pass-through obligations |
| **Sub-processors** | Discrete processing functions | DPA; 30-day change notice with objection window |
| **External auditors / certification bodies** | Rely on results as assurance input; audit our AIMS | NDA; scheme rules |
| **Regulators and supervisory authorities** | Oversight; recipients of notifications | AIMS-REG-017 |
| **Data subjects** | Individuals named incidentally in evidence — **no role, but rights** | Data-protection law |
| **Affected groups and society** | No contractual role; impacts assessed and reportable by anyone | AIMS-REC-005; AIMS-PLAN-016 §A |
| Contractors / labellers | Corpus labelling under supervision | Contract with confidentiality and de-identification obligations |

## 3. Responsible approach and documentation when supplying to a third party

Where the organization supplies an AI system, or its outputs, to a third party:

| Obligation | How it is met |
|---|---|
| Responsible approach applied | The supplied system has an accountable owner, a completed impact assessment, V&V evidence, and disclosed limitations before any third party receives it |
| **Documentation provided with the system** | Intended purpose and valid domain; capabilities and **limitations**; required resources and required human oversight; input-data expectations; interpretation guidance; incident and support routes; data-handling and retention terms — the set in AIMS-DOC-012 §1 |
| Limits of the valid domain stated | Published and contractual, not merely in a manual (AIMS-REG-017 O-16) |
| Responsibility allocation shared | The recipient receives §1.2 and §1.3 so the split is agreed in advance, not discovered during an incident |
| Pass-through obligations | Partners must not present output as certification, must preserve the AI-generated disclosure and the human-review requirement, and must pass both to their own customers |
| No responsibility transfer by silence | If a recipient assumes a duty we have not accepted, it is corrected in writing at contracting and at each renewal |
| Change and deprecation notice | ≥ 10 business days for verdict-affecting change; documented deprecation and end-of-support commitments |
| Feedback route | Recipients get the same adverse-impact channels as anyone else |

## 4. PII privacy roles, responsibilities and applicable privacy controls

### 4.1 Privacy roles

| Party | Privacy role | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| **Assessed organization** | **Controller** — determines the purpose and means; decides what evidence to supply | It chooses to run the assessment and what to submit |
| **Our organization** | **Processor** — processes only on the controller's instruction, for the assessment purpose only | DPA |
| Sub-processors | Sub-processors, under flow-down terms | DPA; 30-day change notice |
| Model supplier | Sub-processor for inference; **contractually no training use** | Service terms; AIMS-REC-021 §4.1 |
| Cloud provider | Sub-processor / infrastructure | Customer agreement |
| **Data subjects** | Individuals whose personal data may appear incidentally in evidence — typically policy owners, approvers and signatories | Data-protection law |
| **DPO / Legal & Privacy** | Oversight, advice, breach notification, rights handling | Statutory and internal |
| Data Steward | Operational data governance, including de-identification gates | AIMS-PRO-024 |

### 4.2 Applicable privacy controls

| Control | Implementation |
|---|---|
| **Purpose limitation** | Evidence is processed only for the assessment it was supplied for. **No secondary use, no cross-tenant aggregation, and no use for training or corpus assembly** — the default position on customer data is prohibition |
| **Minimization** | Personal data is neither solicited nor extracted; special-category data is not solicited and never extracted; evidence content and customer personal data are **excluded from logs and telemetry, verified quarterly** |
| De-identification | Mandatory before any corpus entry; entries failing review are **rejected, not repaired** |
| Storage limitation | Defined retention with automatic expiry; deletion recorded as a provenance event |
| **Data-subject rights** | Access, correction, erasure and restriction supported via the controller, within statutory timelines (AIMS-REG-017 O-7) |
| Security | Encryption in transit and at rest; per-tenant isolation; least-privilege, short-lived, read-only credentials; access logging, including logging of log access |
| Transparency | Privacy and data-handling notice; disclosure of sub-processors, transfer mechanisms and processing locations |
| Cross-border transfers | Processing locations and transfer mechanisms disclosed; 30 days' notice before change (O-9) |
| **Breach notification** | Processor→controller within 24 hours of confirmation; 72-hour supervisory-authority clock handled by the controller with our support (O-1…O-3) |
| Privacy by design | Privacy assessed at design; no personal-data-dependent feature; **no demographic attributes held or inferred**, which is also why no demographic breakdown of the corpus exists |
| DPIA support | Information provided to controllers for their own DPIA on request |
| Accountability | Records of processing; DPA in place with every customer and sub-processor |

## 5. Verification that the allocation holds

An allocation nobody checks decays. Verified as follows: contract and DPA review at onboarding and
renewal (Legal & Privacy); confirmation at each incident post-mortem that every party performed its
allocated part, with gaps raised as nonconformities; **monthly verification that customer-side human
oversight is actually being exercised** (override rate and review coverage), since it is the single
allocated responsibility whose failure is least visible to us; quarterly supplier review; annual
review of this document against the parties actually involved. The 2026-03-06 review found no unallocated
responsibility and one clarification needed — added at §1.3 — on who owns remediation in the customer's
environment.
