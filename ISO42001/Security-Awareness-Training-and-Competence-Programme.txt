# Security Awareness, Training and Competence Programme

**Document ID:** SEC-AWR-041
**Version:** 2.0
**Owner:** CISO
**Approved by:** CISO and Head of People Operations
**Approval date:** 2026-08-14
**Next scheduled review:** 2026-12-31
**Scope:** All personnel — 48 employees, 11 individuals engaged through 4 contracting vendors, and 6
non-employee directors and advisors — across all locations and all information classifications. The
programme covers awareness (what everyone must know), role-specific training (what a role requires),
and competence (evidence that a role-holder can actually perform the control they own).

---

## 1. Why this programme exists, and what it is accountable for

Every control in the ISMS is performed by a person or watched by one. A control that nobody has been
told about is a control that operates by luck. This programme is the entity's mechanism for making
each obligation **known, understood, practised and evidenced** — and for producing the records that
show it, because an unrecorded briefing is indistinguishable from one that never happened.

The programme owns four outcomes:

| Outcome | What it means | How it is evidenced |
|---|---|---|
| **Awareness** | Every person knows the rules that apply to them and the channels available to them | Completion records per module per person, with dates |
| **Understanding** | They can apply the rules, not just recite them | Comprehension assessment with a pass threshold, plus simulation results |
| **Competence** | A person who OWNS a control can perform it | Role competence matrix, assessment against a named skills profile, development plan for any gap |
| **Currency** | Both the content and the people stay current as threats, laws and policies change | Content review triggers, annual refresh, continuing-education obligation and its hours record |

**Nobody is exempt.** Directors, advisors, contractors, vendor personnel with access, and the CEO
complete the same core curriculum as employees. Where a contracting vendor runs equivalent training,
the entity accepts it only against a mapped-content review recorded in the vendor file — and the
entity's own acceptable-use, classification and reporting modules are always taken directly, because
those are the entity's rules and no third party teaches them.

## 3. Acceptable use — what the programme communicates, specifically

C2 is the entity's mechanism for communicating the acceptable-use rules that the Information Asset
Inventory and Data Classification Standard and the Responsible Use Policy (AIMS-POL-018) establish.
Communication is not a link to a policy: the module states each rule, gives a worked example of
compliance and of breach, and tests it.

| Topic communicated in C2 | The rule as taught |
|---|---|
| **Permitted use by classification** | Public / Internal / Confidential / Restricted — for each level, where it may be stored, who may see it, whether it may leave a managed device, and whether it may be printed (it may not) |
| **Customer evidence** | Restricted. Never copied to an endpoint, never pasted into a chat or a model prompt outside the product path, never used for testing or demonstration |
| **Prohibited use** | Personal cloud storage, personal email, unapproved SaaS, unapproved AI tools, personal removable media, credential sharing, security-control bypass tooling |
| **Assets** | Devices are the entity's, are managed, and are returned on exit; the acceptable-use terms survive the device |
| **Monitoring** | What is logged, why, the legal basis, and the explicit statement that logs are not used for performance or attendance management |
| **Consequence** | Breach is handled under the disciplinary process; the module states this plainly rather than implying it |

**Comprehension is measured, not assumed.** C2's scenario items are scored separately from the recall
items, and the scenario sub-score is reported per cohort, because a person can pass a recall test on
rules they cannot apply. Current period: **48/48 personnel and 11/11 vendor individuals completed C2**;
mean scenario sub-score 91%; **2 people scored below 70% on the scenario half, were re-briefed 1:1 by
the CISO and re-tested (both passed)**, recorded as **D-2026-016**.

## 4. Classification and labelling training

C3 exists because labelling fails in practice more often than it fails in policy. The module teaches
the four levels, the label mechanics for each medium (document header, repository topic, S3 object
tag, evidence record field, email marking), the rule that **an unlabelled artifact is treated as
Confidential until labelled**, and the escalation path when the correct level is unclear.

The exercise half requires the learner to label six real (redacted) artifacts drawn from the entity's
own corpus and to justify two of the six. **Completion this period: 48/48 employees, 11/11 vendor
individuals, 6/6 directors.** Mislabelling found in the quarterly artifact sample is fed back into the
module's exercise set — the July sample found 3 mislabelled artifacts out of 40 (**D-2026-018**), and
two of the three are now exercise items.

## 5. Screens, transit and conversation

C6 covers the exposure paths that no technical control fully closes:

| Path | What personnel are told |
|---|---|
| **Screens in public** | No work on Confidential or Restricted material in a public place without a privacy filter; no seat with a sightline to a screen; screen lock within 60 seconds |
| **Screenshots and photographs of screens** | **Capturing a screen showing Confidential or Restricted information — by screenshot, phone camera or screen-share recording — is prohibited except through an approved support workflow that redacts before capture.** The prohibition is stated in the acceptable-use terms, taught here with two worked examples (a support ticket and a demo recording), and audited: the quarterly artifact sample includes shared screenshots and recordings, and the DLP rules alert on image attachments in support channels |
| **Screen sharing** | Share a window, never a desktop; close evidence viewers before joining; no recording of a session showing customer evidence |
| **Conversations** | **Confidential and Restricted matters are not discussed in public places, in transit, on public transport, in shared or co-working space, or on any consumer voice or messaging channel.** The reminder is issued at onboarding, in the annual refresh, and as a standing line in the meeting-room booking confirmation for rooms booked as Confidential |
| **Transfer channels** | The approved transfer mechanisms and the rule that an unapproved channel is a reportable event, not a shortcut |

## 6. Malware, phishing and social engineering

C4 is the entity's user-awareness control for malicious software, and it teaches recognition rather
than mechanism:

- **Identifying a malware-carrying email, file or program**: the indicator set (unexpected attachment,
  mismatched sender domain, macro or script prompt, archive-in-archive, executable disguised by a
  double extension, an installer offered outside the software catalogue, a link to a look-alike
  authentication page), each with a real example drawn from what the entity has actually received.
- **What to do and what NOT to do**: do not open, do not forward, do not "test it in a VM", do not
  reply, do not attempt to determine whether it is genuinely malicious. Report it, and let the
  responders handle it. **Personnel are explicitly told not to attempt to prove or investigate a
  suspected weakness or malware sample themselves** — the module says so in those terms, because a
  well-meant investigation destroys evidence, can execute the payload, and can look
  indistinguishable from an attack in the logs.
- **Endpoint protection**: what the EDR agent does, what the user sees when it acts, and that
  disabling or interfering with it is prohibited and detected.
- **Phishing simulation**: monthly, all personnel. Anyone who submits credentials or opens a payload
  receives a **targeted refresher within 5 business days**. Current period: 3 of 48 failed the June
  simulation, all three completed the refresher, and none failed the July or August simulation.
  Reporting rate — the metric that matters more than the failure rate — is **71% of simulated
  phishing reported through the C4 channel within 4 hours**.

## 7. Reporting: making the obligation and the channels known

Awareness of *how to report* is the deliverable here; the reporting procedures themselves live in the
incident and concerns procedures.

| What personnel are told | Detail |
|---|---|
| **That reporting is an obligation** | Every person is responsible for reporting an observed or suspected information security event, weakness, or policy breach — promptly, and without waiting to be certain. The obligation is stated in the employment terms, in C1, in C4, and in the annual acknowledgement |
| **What to report** | The full event range, with examples for each: ineffective control, access or authentication anomaly, suspected malware, phishing, lost or stolen device, mis-sent information, unapproved tool or channel, physical security event, supplier security concern, suspected privacy breach, AI-system harm or unexpected behaviour, and any suspected weakness |
| **The designated point of contact** | The security channel and its single escalation number, both memorised in C4's exercise; the named duty responder rota; and the out-of-hours path |
| **How** | Four channels, no manager permission required: the security channel, the security mailbox, the duty responder number, and the **confidential ethics line — anonymous, externally operated, available to all personnel, and covered by a stated anti-retaliation protection with an investigation and feedback commitment** |
| **That no investigation is expected of them** | Personnel must not attempt to prove, exploit or investigate a suspected weakness or vulnerability. Reporting is complete when it is reported |
| **What happens next** | Acknowledgement targets, that the reporter is told the outcome, and that a report made in good faith is never a mark against the reporter even when the concern turns out to be nothing |

The **anti-retaliation** statement is not decorative: retaliation against a reporter is itself a
disciplinary matter, the ethics line's operator reports attempted identification of a reporter
directly to the Board's chair, and the annual acknowledgement asks each person to confirm they know
the channel exists and is protected. **48/48 confirmed for the current period.**

## 8. Competence — for the people who own controls

Awareness is for everyone; competence is for role-holders, and it is assessed rather than assumed.

| Element | How it works |
|---|---|
| **Skills profile per security role** | Each named security role (CISO, AI Security Lead, AI Operations Lead, incident responders, deployment approvers, evidence reviewers) has a written profile listing the knowledge, technical skills and judgement the role requires, and the artifacts that demonstrate each |
| **Assessment** | Annual assessment of each role-holder against their profile by the role's manager, plus a second opinion from outside the reporting line for the CISO and the AI Security Lead |
| **Gap handling** | Any gap produces a dated development plan with a named action, an owner and a target date; the plan is reviewed at the next quarterly ISMS review, not deferred to the next annual cycle |
| **Independence of the evaluator** | A person does not assess their own competence, and no person is the sole assessor of the control they themselves depend on |
| **Records** | Profile, assessment, gap, plan, and closure — retained 7 years |

### 8.1 Keeping security role-holders current — the continuing-education obligation

This is a standing obligation on **all** personnel, not only the board:

| Population | Obligation | Current-period record |
|---|---|---|
| **All personnel** | **8 hours of recorded security or privacy continuing education per year**, over and above the core curriculum | Mean 12.4 h; 46/48 met the 8-hour minimum; **2 personnel short (5.5 h and 6 h) with a dated catch-up plan — D-2026-020** |
| **Named security role-holders** | **24 hours per year**, of which at least 8 must be external (conference, course, certification maintenance, or a security community forum), plus documented tracking of threat-landscape developments relevant to the role | CISO 41 h; AI Security Lead 33 h; AI Operations Lead 27 h; all met |
| **Engineers** | 16 hours per year including at least one secure-development course or exercise | Mean 21 h; 9/9 met |
| **Directors and advisors** | Semi-annual briefing plus 6 hours per year of governance-level security education | 6/6 met |
| **Currency mechanism** | Role-holders subscribe to and are expected to read the named advisory sources; participation in the security community groups the entity belongs to is part of the 8 external hours; a monthly 30-minute internal threat-landscape briefing is delivered by the CISO and recorded for those who cannot attend | 12 briefings delivered; mean attendance 39/48 live, 48/48 including recording |
| **Certification maintenance** | Where a role-holder holds a certification the entity relies on, its maintenance requirement is tracked as part of the 24 hours and its lapse is a deficiency | No lapse this period |

Hours are recorded per person in the learning record with the activity, date and duration, and the
record is what the annual competence assessment reads — an unrecorded hour does not count.

## 9. Keeping the content current

| Trigger | Response | Deadline |
|---|---|---|
| A policy or standard in the ISMS is revised | The owning module is reviewed against the change and updated where the change affects what personnel must do | 30 days |
| **A law or regulation applicable to the entity changes** | Legal and the CISO jointly review affected modules; a change to an obligation personnel act on triggers a targeted communication, not only a module edit | 30 days |
| A significant incident, near-miss or deficiency | The lesson becomes module content or an exercise item | Next quarterly refresh |
| A phishing-simulation theme succeeds | That theme becomes an exercise item and a targeted briefing | 30 days |
| A new threat pattern reported by the community groups the entity participates in | Assessed for relevance; where relevant, a briefing note and a module update | 60 days |
| Annual scheduled review | Full curriculum review against the risk register, the control set and the year's incidents | Annually |

**Content-review record for the current period:** 7 module updates (C2 twice, C4 three times, C6, R3),
each with a dated reason recorded in the learning platform's version history.

**What this programme does not do**, stated so a reader does not infer more than is here: it does not
run technical control validation (that is the monitoring procedure), it does not itself operate the
reporting channels (that is the incident and concerns procedures), and it does not replace the
disciplinary process. Its deliverable is that the people who operate this ISMS know what they are
responsible for, can demonstrate they understand it, and are kept current — and that there is a record
proving it.
