# AI System Impact Assessment Process

**Document ID:** AIMS-PRO-005
**Version:** 3.2
**Owner:** Responsible AI Lead
**Approved by:** AI Governance Board (AIGB)
**Approval date:** 2026-02-18
**Next scheduled review:** 2026-12-31
**Scope:** All AI systems developed, deployed, provided or used by the organization, across the entire
AI system life cycle — including the compliance-evaluation AI system in AWS account 471885797689.

---

## 1. Purpose: a process to assess consequences for individuals, groups and societies

The organization **establishes and maintains this process to assess the potential consequences for
individuals, for groups of individuals, and for societies that can result from an AI system throughout
its life cycle** — not only at launch. The process is mandatory: no AI system passes its design gate,
first release, or any material change without a current impact assessment (AIMS-POL-006 §2.4).

The assessment is distinct from, but linked to, AI risk assessment: risk assessment asks what could go
wrong for *the organization*; impact assessment asks what could happen *to people and to society*. Both
run, and findings cross-feed — an impact finding is registered as a risk and treated in the AI Risk
Register.

## 2. Circumstances in which an impact assessment is performed

An impact assessment is required in the following circumstances, and the trigger criteria are
themselves the factors that make impact likely.

### 2.1 Life-cycle trigger points

| Trigger | Type |
|---|---|
| Inception, before design is committed | Initial |
| Before first release | Full |
| Before any **significant change** to the factors in §2.2 | Re-assessment |
| Annually, regardless of change | Periodic review |
| After a Severity 1 incident, a substantiated concern raised under AIMS-PRO-004, or an external finding | Event-driven |
| Before a new deployment context (new customer segment, new jurisdiction, new intended use) | Re-assessment |
| Before decommissioning, where withdrawal itself affects users | Targeted |

### 2.2 Factors determining whether, and how deeply, an assessment is performed

The depth of assessment is proportionate to these factors, each of which is scored and recorded:

| Factor | What is considered | Effect on this system |
|---|---|---|
| **Criticality of the intended purpose and context of use** | Whether output influences decisions affecting rights, access, safety, employment, credit or legal standing; whether use is in a regulated context; how reversible a wrong output is | **Medium-high.** Output drives compliance decisions and remediation spend for organizations, and may be relied on in audit. It does not decide anything about an individual, which bounds criticality |
| **Complexity and degree of automation of the AI system** | Number of interacting models and tools, agentic autonomy, whether a human is in the loop before consequence, explainability of the mechanism | **Medium-high.** A five-node graph with tool use and autonomous scanning is complex; automation is bounded because verdicts are advisory and a qualified human reviews and can override before reliance |
| **Sensitivity of data types and sources processed** | Personal data, special-category data, children's data, confidential business data, customer-supplied versus organization-owned, provenance quality | **Medium.** Primarily confidential business and configuration data. Personal data may appear incidentally in customer documents (names, approvers) — a real sensitivity, handled by minimization and access control |
| Scale and reach | Number of affected organizations and individuals, geographic spread | Medium and growing |
| Vulnerability of those affected | Presence of groups with specific protection needs | Low direct — affected parties are organizations; workers are considered in §A.5.4 analysis |
| **Significant change to any of the above** | A change to purpose, autonomy, oversight point, data category, model family, or scale | Any such change is itself a trigger under §2.1 |

A system scoring high on criticality **or** on automation-without-oversight is classified
**high-impact**, which additionally requires AIGB approval of the assessment and of release.

## 3. Elements of the impact assessment

Every assessment follows these defined elements in order, and each produces a recorded output.

| # | Element | What is done | Recorded output |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Identification** | Identify potentially impacted individuals, groups and societies (§4), the intended purpose, reasonably foreseeable misuse, and the impact areas in scope | Impacted-party map; misuse list |
| 2 | **Analysis** | For each impacted party and impact area, analyse plausible positive and negative impacts, the causal mechanism, predictable failure modes, likelihood and severity, and who bears the consequence | Impact analysis table with likelihood × severity |
| 3 | **Evaluation** | Compare analysed impacts against the organization's AI policy principles, AI objectives, trustworthiness expectations, legal obligations and defined acceptance thresholds; decide acceptable / acceptable-with-treatment / unacceptable | Evaluated impact register with a stated decision per impact |
| 4 | **Treatment** | Determine and assign treatment for anything not acceptable as-is: design change, technical control, human oversight, documented limitation, scope reduction, disclosure, or do-not-proceed. Each treatment has an owner and a date | Treatment plan; residual impact after treatment |
| 5 | **Documentation** | Record the assessment per AIMS-REC-005 (results, method, participants, date, version of the system assessed, residual impacts, decision) | Impact assessment record, retained per §6 |
| 6 | **Reporting** | Report results to the accountable roles and to the AIGB; high-impact systems require AIGB decision | AIGB minutes; release-gate record |
| 7 | **Communication** | Communicate relevant results to users and other interested parties — intended purpose, limitations, human oversight point, and known residual impacts — in customer-facing documentation | Published limitations and disclosures (AIMS-DOC-012 §2) |
| 8 | **Monitoring and review** | Monitor the treated impacts in operation; re-open on trigger (§2.1) | Monitoring signals in AIMS-PRO-007 §2; review record |

An unacceptable impact that cannot be treated is grounds to **not proceed**; that decision rests with
the AIGB and is recorded.

## 4. Identifying potentially impacted individuals, groups and societies

Identification is driven from the system's **intended purpose, actual use and characteristics**, not
from a generic checklist. The method: trace every output and every data flow to the parties it reaches,
then ask who is affected without having chosen to be.

| Category | Identified parties for this system | Basis of identification |
|---|---|---|
| **Individuals — direct users** | Compliance analysts, auditors and operators who read, rely on and override verdicts | They act on the output; they carry the oversight duty |
| **Individuals — data subjects** | People named incidentally in customer evidence (policy owners, approvers, reviewers) and in event logs (operators) | Their personal data is processed even though they are not users |
| **Individuals — indirectly affected** | Employees of assessed organizations whose work is redirected by a finding | A false gap creates work; a missed gap leaves them exposed |
| **Groups** | Compliance and audit professionals as an occupational group (task displacement and de-skilling); teams in assessed organizations; **workers** whose processes are audited | Considered explicitly under A.5.4 |
| **Groups with specific protection needs** | Assessed and recorded: children, impaired persons, elderly persons — **no direct exposure**, since the system assesses organizations and is not consumer-facing; **workers** — in scope and analysed; **persons with disabilities** — in scope through accessibility of the output | Recorded as considered, with the basis stated, rather than left unexamined |
| **Societies** | The assurance and audit ecosystem; regulators and the public who rely on AI governance being real; the environment (computational cost); the wider market for compliance labour | Considered explicitly under A.5.5 |
| **Organizations (context, not "individuals")** | Assessed organizations; their customers | Carried for completeness of the causal chain |

Where the organization's own knowledge is insufficient, **experts are consulted** (§5.3).

## 5. Who performs the assessment, and how results are used

### 5.1 Who performs it

| Role | Part in the assessment |
|---|---|
| **Responsible AI Lead** | **Owns and facilitates** the assessment; signs the record; cannot be the same person as the system's development lead |
| AI System Accountable Owner | Provides purpose, context and use; accountable for acting on the outcome |
| AI Development Lead | Provides architecture, data, automation and failure-mode input |
| Data Steward | Provides data categories, provenance, sensitivity and lawful-basis input |
| AI Security Lead | Provides threat and misuse input |
| Legal and Privacy | Assess legal, data-protection and human-rights aspects |
| Compliance analysts (users) | Consulted as the affected user group and the oversight point |
| **AIGB** | Reviews and, for high-impact systems, **approves**; may require treatment or refuse the system |
| External experts | Engaged per §5.3 |

Segregation is deliberate: the assessment is **not** performed solely by the team that wants to ship
the system.

### 5.2 How results inform design, use, review and approval

| Use of results | Mechanism | Example from this system |
|---|---|---|
| **Design** | Findings become design requirements before design is committed; the design gate checks them | The distinct read-failure state exists because an assessment found that reporting unreadable evidence as customer non-compliance mis-attributes fault |
| **Use** | Findings set the documented intended purpose, the out-of-purpose list, and the mandatory human oversight point | The prohibition on using verdicts to evaluate individual employees, and the "not a certification" disclosure |
| **Review** | Impact criteria (IC-1 … IC-12) become release criteria evaluated at every release and quarterly | AIMS-PLAN-010 §4 and §6 |
| **Approval** | Release authorization requires impact sign-off; the AIGB may refuse or impose conditions | RC-8 blocks release without Responsible AI Lead sign-off |
| **Communication** | Residual impacts and limitations are published to users | AIMS-DOC-012 §2 limitations |
| **Change control** | A material change re-triggers the assessment before deployment | AIMS-POL-006 §4 |

### 5.3 Consulting experts, researchers and users

Where a fuller understanding is needed, the organization **consults**: internal subject-matter experts
(Legal, Privacy, Security, Accessibility); the compliance analysts and auditors who use the system;
customers, through quarterly advisory sessions and structured feedback on verdict quality; external
responsible-AI researchers, engaged for the fairness and evasion review; and an accessibility
specialist for output presentation. Consultation, its date, its participants and its effect on the
assessment are recorded in the impact assessment record. Consultation is mandatory where the system is
classified high-impact, where a group with specific protection needs is in scope, or where the
organization has no internal competence in an identified impact area.

## 6. Aspects of the AI system the assessment must take into account

Every assessment covers, at minimum, these aspects of the system itself — impact cannot be judged from
the intended purpose alone:

| Aspect | What is examined |
|---|---|
| **Data used for development** | Categories, provenance, lawfulness, licensing, de-identification, labelling quality, representativeness and known biases of the evaluation corpus; whether customer data is used (it is not, for training); data-quality assumptions and their failure modes (AIMS-REG-006 §3) |
| **AI technologies used** | That pre-trained foundation LLMs are used by inference only, with no training or fine-tuning; the implications of that choice — no customer data in weights, but dependence on a supplier's model and its opacity, and susceptibility to prompt injection |
| **Functionality of the overall system** | The end-to-end graph, not the model in isolation: scope resolution, autonomous read-only scanning of a customer environment, document analysis, model-authored judgement, deterministic scoring, published verdicts and remediation tasks, and the human oversight point — because impact arises from the *system's* behaviour, including its non-model parts |
| Degree of autonomy and human oversight | Where a human can and must intervene, and what happens if they do not |
| Deployment and operating context | Cloud-only, machine-invoked, multi-tenant, cross-account read access |
| Interfaces and output presentation | How a verdict is presented, and whether presentation could mislead |
| Life-cycle stage under assessment | Which stage triggered this assessment and what is still undecided |

## 7. Retention, documentation and review of assessments

Assessment results are documented and retained per **AIMS-REC-005** and the retention schedule in
AIMS-STD-013 §4.2 (impact assessment records: 7 years, and for the life of the system plus 3 years,
whichever is longer). Records are updated — not replaced — as the elements in §3 are revisited, so the
history of what was known and decided at each point remains reconstructable.
