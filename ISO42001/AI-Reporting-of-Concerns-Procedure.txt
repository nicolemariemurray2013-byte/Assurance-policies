# AI Reporting of Concerns Procedure

**Version:** 2.0
**Owner:** General Counsel (process owner); AI Governance Board (AIGB) escalation authority
**Approved by:** AI Governance Board
**Approval date:** 2026-01-22

**Scope:** Concerns about the organization's role with respect to any AI system, at **any stage of its
life cycle** — inception, design, development, evaluation, release, deployment, operation, change,
or decommissioning — including the Bedrock agents and AgentCore runtimes in AWS account

## 1. Purpose

This procedure establishes the organization's process for **reporting concerns about the
organization's role with respect to an AI system throughout its life cycle**. It exists so that any
person who believes an AI system is being built, sold, operated or used in a way that conflicts with
the AI Policy (AIMS-POL-001), the law, or our stated responsible-AI principles has a defined,
protected route to raise it and receive an answer.

Concerns in scope include, but are not limited to: harmful, biased or discriminatory output; use of
a system outside its documented intended use; inadequate human oversight; unlawful or unlicensed
training data; undisclosed automated decision-making; unsafe deployment; suppression of evaluation
results; and pressure to bypass a release gate or an AIMS control.

## 2. Who may report

The process is **available to employed and contracted persons alike**: permanent and fixed-term
employees, interns, contractors, contingent workers, agency staff, secondees and vendor personnel
working on or with our AI systems. Customers and members of the public may raise AI concerns through
the external channel in section 3.4, which feeds the same triage queue.

No person needs permission from their manager to report, and no reporter is required to establish
that the concern is well-founded before reporting — a good-faith concern is enough.

## 3. Reporting channels

| # | Channel | Confidential | Anonymous | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 3.1 | AI Concerns intake form on the AIMS portal | Yes | Optional | Reporter chooses "named", "confidential" or "anonymous" at submission |
| 3.2 | Independent third-party ethics hotline (24/7, telephone and web, operated by an external provider) | Yes | Yes | The provider forwards the report without reporter identity unless the reporter consents |
| 3.3 | Direct to the Responsible AI Lead, AI Risk Manager, General Counsel or any AIGB member | Yes | No | For reporters who prefer a named person |
| 3.4 | External AI concerns address published in the Responsible AI Statement | Yes | Yes | Customers, users and the public |

**Confidentiality and anonymity.** Concerns may be raised **confidentially, anonymously, or both**.
For confidential reports, the reporter's identity is known only to the Investigation Lead and the
General Counsel and is not disclosed to the reported party or to line management. For anonymous
reports, no identifying metadata is retained; the hotline provides a reference code so an anonymous
reporter can follow up and receive the outcome without ever identifying themselves.

## 4. Protection from reprisals

Retaliation against a person who raises an AI concern in good faith, or who participates in an
investigation as a witness, subject-matter expert or investigator, is a **disciplinary offence up to
and including dismissal**, and is treated as a nonconformity in the AIMS.

- Protection extends to **all reporting and investigation participants**, not only the reporter.
- Protection applies even where the concern is ultimately not substantiated, provided it was raised
  in good faith.
- For 12 months after a report closes, any adverse employment action affecting a participant
  (performance rating, role change, termination, compensation decision) requires prior review and
  written clearance by HR and the General Counsel.
- Reporters may raise a retaliation concern through any channel in section 3; retaliation concerns
  are escalated directly to the AIGB.

## 5. Qualified personnel with authority

Concerns are handled by personnel who are **qualified and hold the authority to investigate, resolve
and escalate**:

| Role | Qualification | Authority |
|---|---|---|
| Triage Officer (General Counsel's office) | Trained in whistleblowing intake and AI risk taxonomy | Classifies severity; assigns Investigation Lead within 3 business days |
| Investigation Lead | Independent of the reported team; trained investigator | Compels access to systems, logs, evaluation records and personnel interviews |
| Responsible AI Lead | Responsible-AI subject-matter expert | Assesses fairness, transparency and oversight concerns on the merits |
| AI Security Lead | Security and adversarial-testing expert | Assesses security and model-abuse concerns |
| AI Governance Board | Management body owning the AIMS | **Authority to halt or suspend any AI system deployment or operation**, order corrective action, and escalate to the Executive Leadership Team |

Investigators are always independent of the AI system and team that the concern is about. Where a
concern implicates a member of the AIGB, that member is recused and the Executive Leadership Team
appoints the Investigation Lead.

## 6. Response time frames and outcomes

The process includes defined mechanisms to **respond within an appropriate time frame and to provide
reporting or escalation outcomes**:

| Stage | Target | Severity 1 (safety, legal, or live harm) |
|---|---|---|
| Acknowledgement to reporter | 2 business days | Same business day |
| Severity classification and Investigation Lead assigned | 3 business days | 24 hours |
| Interim risk decision (continue / restrict / suspend the AI system) | 10 business days | 48 hours, by the AIGB |
| Investigation concluded and findings issued | 30 business days | 10 business days |
| Outcome communicated to reporter | 5 business days after conclusion | 2 business days after conclusion |
| Corrective actions verified closed | 90 days | 30 days |

**Outcome communication.** Every reporter — including anonymous reporters, via the hotline reference
code — receives the outcome: whether the concern was substantiated, what corrective action was taken
or why none was required, and their route to escalate if dissatisfied. Escalation outcomes are
reported to the AIGB quarterly in aggregate, and immediately for Severity 1.

**Maintaining confidentiality while reporting outcomes.** Outcome communications are written to
preserve the reporter's confidentiality or anonymity, the privacy of individuals named in the
concern, and **business confidentiality** — commercially sensitive detail, customer identities and
security-sensitive specifics are summarized or withheld, and the reason for withholding is stated so
the reporter knows the omission is deliberate rather than an incomplete answer.

## 7. Records and continual improvement

Every concern is recorded in the AI Concerns Register (AIMS-REG-005) with severity, dates against
each time frame in section 6, outcome and corrective actions; records are retained for 7 years.
Anonymous reports are recorded without identifying metadata. The AIGB reviews register trends
quarterly, and themes feed the AI Risk Register (AIMS-REG-001) and the annual review of the AI Policy.
