**Approved by:** AI Governance Board
**Approval date:** 2026-03-06
**Next scheduled review:** 2026-12-31
**Scope:** Every AI system the organization uses, whether developed internally or obtained from a third

which is both developed and used by the organization and is therefore subject to this policy as a user

**Alignment with existing policy.** This policy is a **domain-specific extension of the organization's
existing technology-use policies** — the Acceptable Use Policy, Information Security Policy, Third-Party
Risk Management Policy, Procurement Policy, Change Management Policy and Data Protection Policy. It does

is specific to AI. Where a term is defined in those policies it carries the same meaning here. Conflicts
resolve in favour of the stricter requirement, and any residual conflict is decided by the AIGB with the
policy owner concerned.

# Part 1 — Processes for the responsible use of AI systems (A.9.2)

The organization **defines and documents the processes for the responsible use of AI systems.** Four
processes apply, in order: U-1 decision to use, U-2 sourcing and approval, U-3 authorization to operate,
U-4 ongoing responsible use.

## U-1 — Deciding whether to use an AI system at all

Applies identically to systems **we develop** and systems **obtained from a third party**; the
difference is only in which evidence satisfies each step.

| Step | What is determined | Recorded | Decision authority |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Problem statement | What outcome is sought, and for whom | Use-case record | Requesting business owner |
| 2. **Is AI the right instrument?** | Whether a deterministic, rules-based or human process would meet the need with less risk. **AI is not the default.** For our own system: deterministic rule-checking was evaluated and rejected only for the natural-language evidence-judgement portion, and the **scoring aggregation was deliberately kept as deterministic code, not model judgement** (AIMS-PRO-023 §3.1) | Rejected-alternatives record | AI Governance Board |
| 3. Prohibited-use screen | Whether the use falls in a prohibited class (§P below). A prohibited use ends the process | Screening record | Responsible AI Lead — veto right |
| 4. Impact assessment | Impact on individuals, groups and societies, proportionate to the use (AIMS-PRO-005) | AIMS-REC-005 record | Responsible AI Lead |
| 5. Risk assessment | AI risks, including the security and privacy risks that the Information Security Policy already requires | AI risk register | AI Security Lead + Risk |
| 6. **Cost of use, including ongoing cost** | Not only licence or build cost, but the **full cost of responsible operation**: inference and infrastructure cost; **monitoring**; **maintenance**; evaluation-corpus upkeep and re-labelling; human oversight effort; incident response; documentation currency; re-validation on supplier model change; and eventual decommissioning. A use case is **not approved without a funded owner for the ongoing monitoring and maintenance cost** — an unfunded monitoring commitment is treated as a decision not to monitor | Total-cost-of-use record, reviewed annually | Finance + Accountable Owner |
| 7. Legal and regulatory requirements | Applicable AI, data-protection, sectoral and contractual requirements per jurisdiction (AIMS-REG-017) | Legal determination | Legal & Privacy |
| 8. Objectives and success measures | The responsible-use objectives from Part 2 that apply, with measures | Objectives record | Accountable Owner |
| 9. Decision | Approve, approve with conditions, defer, or reject — with reasons | Minuted decision | AI Governance Board |

**Rejections are recorded with reasons.** Three internal proposals have been rejected under U-1:
automated evaluation of employee compliance performance (prohibited use), automated generation of
customer-facing attestations without human sign-off (unacceptable residual risk), and fine-tuning on
customer-derived data (rights basis absent — AIMS-REC-021 §5). A process that has never rejected anything
is not a control.

## U-2 — Sourcing requirements (approved sourcing of AI systems)

AI systems and components are obtained only through **approved sourcing**, which extends the existing
Procurement and Third-Party Risk policies with AI-specific requirements:

| Requirement | Applies to |
|---|---|
| Sourced only from suppliers assessed and approved under AIMS-PRO-010, through the standard procurement route — **no shadow procurement, no free-tier or personal-account use of AI services for organizational work** | All third-party AI |
| Supplier must provide adequate documentation: intended purpose, capabilities, limitations, evaluation results, data-handling terms | All third-party AI |
| Contractual terms required: **no use of our or our customers' data for training**; data location and retention; security and breach-notification obligations; deprecation notice; audit or attestation rights | All third-party AI |
| Model access via approved managed services only (Amazon Bedrock), with the model identifier and region **pinned and recorded**, never floating | Foundation models |
| Open-source or freely available components subject to the same assessment; "free" does not mean unapproved-use-permitted | All |
| Internally developed systems must satisfy AIMS-POL-006 (life cycle) before use, including V&V and release criteria | Internal |
| Where a supplier cannot evidence a requirement, **compensating measures are defined and recorded, or the sourcing is rejected**. The foundation-model training-data opacity is handled this way (AIMS-PRO-024 §3.2, D-9) | All |

## U-3 — Authorization to operate

No AI system enters organizational use without a recorded authorization naming: the accountable owner;
the approved intended purpose and the **valid domain of use**; prohibited uses; the required human
oversight (Part 2, §M-4); the monitoring measures and their funded owner; the review date; and the
conditions under which authorization is suspended. Authorization is **time-bounded** and lapses if not
renewed — the current authorization for the compliance-evaluation system expires 2026-09-06.

## U-4 — Ongoing responsible use

| Activity | Frequency | Owner |
|---|---|---|
| Use within intended purpose and valid domain confirmed | Continuous, with the checks in AIMS-REC-019 | AI Operations Lead |
| Monitoring measures reviewed against thresholds | Monthly | AI Operations Lead |
| Human oversight actually exercised — override rate, review coverage — verified rather than assumed | Monthly | Accountable Owner |
| Adverse-impact reports and concerns reviewed | Monthly | Responsible AI Lead |
| Total cost of use, including monitoring and maintenance, reviewed against the approved envelope | Annually | Finance + Accountable Owner |
| Impact and risk assessments refreshed | Annually, and on significant change | Responsible AI Lead |
| Supplier performance and model currency reviewed | Quarterly | AI Security Lead |
| Authorization renewed or withdrawn | Semi-annually | AI Governance Board |

## P — Prohibited uses

Absolute, and screened for at U-1 step 3: evaluating or making decisions about **individual persons**
(including using compliance verdicts to assess an employee's performance); producing a certification,
attestation or legal opinion **without qualified human sign-off**; use outside the published valid domain
of use presented as though inside it; use on personal or special-category data beyond what the approved
purpose requires; granting an AI system **write, delete or configuration-change authority** in a customer
or production environment (our own system holds **read-only** credentials by design); circumventing an
approval, security or privacy control; and any use whose adverse impacts have not been assessed.

# Part 2 — Objectives for the responsible use of AI systems (A.9.3)

The organization **defines and documents objectives for the responsible use of AI systems**, and the
mechanisms by which they are achieved. The objectives below were selected against the organization's
context — the system renders judgements that organizations and their auditors rely on, so
**explainability, reliability and accountability dominate**, while some aspects common to other AI uses
(physical safety, environmental control) are less material and are recorded as such rather than padded.

| # | Objective | Why it applies in this context | Mechanisms that achieve it | Measure |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **O-1 Fairness** | Equivalent evidence must receive equivalent verdicts, whatever the size, sector, maturity or documentation style of the organization assessed | Rubrics score the **presence of required elements, not polish or length**; terse-compliant and verbose-empty strata are permanent release gates; fairness measured across strata (M-7); disparity investigated as a defect; no demographic attributes held or inferred | Disparity across strata; presentation-bias gate pass |
| **O-2 Accountability** | A machine verdict must never be an unowned verdict | A **named accountable owner** for the system; per-decision human accountability through recorded, attributable overrides; RACI in AIMS-PRO-003; the system is authoritatively **not** the decision-maker of record | Every published result has an accountable reviewer |
| **O-3 Transparency** | Users and assessed organizations must know they are relying on AI output and what its limits are | Machine-generated labelling on every verdict; published limitations and valid domain of use; disclosed scoring behaviour; no covert operation | Disclosure present on 100% of results |
| **O-4 Explainability** | The **primary** objective here: a compliance finding that cannot be explained is worthless to an auditor | Every requirement verdict carries a **rationale and citations to the specific evidence relied on**; coverage validation refuses to publish an uncited verdict; the full phase and tool-call journal is retained and disclosable; **traceable rationale is why fine-tuning was rejected** (AIMS-DOC-009 §3.1) | 100% of verdicts cited; analyst comprehension review ≥ threshold |
| **O-5 Reliability** | Results feed audits and regulatory filings; instability destroys their value | Deterministic scoring in code; pinned model identifier; quarterly no-change regression to detect supplier drift; held-out test split never used for tuning; release criteria gate accuracy | Agreement with qualified human labels; no-change regression stability |
| **O-6 Safety** | **Low materiality, recorded rather than inflated:** the system is read-only, has no physical actuation and no safety-of-life function. The residual safety-relevant path is indirect — a wrong verdict contributing to a false assurance conclusion | Read-only credentials with no write path; human sign-off before any assurance claim; withdrawal and correction process for published results | Zero write attempts; zero unreviewed published assurance claims |
| **O-7 Robustness and redundancy** | Evidence is customer-controlled and therefore an attack surface; a silent failure would be worse than a loud one | Structural trust-boundary encoding so customer content is never instructions; adversarial release suite (injection, contradiction, overstatement); **fail-loud design — unreadable evidence yields unable-to-assess, never non-compliance**; retries with idempotency; graceful degradation with recorded coverage; human review as the redundant path rather than a second model | Adversarial suite pass; zero read-failures scored as non-compliance (Q-15) |
| **O-8 Privacy and security** | Evidence may incidentally contain personal data and always contains confidential material | Processor role under DPA; encryption in transit and at rest; per-tenant isolation; least-privilege short-lived read-only credentials; evidence content excluded from logs and telemetry, verified quarterly; **no customer data used for training**; defined retention and honoured deletion | Zero cross-tenant findings; zero minimization violations |
| **O-9 Accessibility** | Users include people using assistive technology; an inaccessible finding is an unusable finding | Screen-reader-compatible output; **status conveyed in text as well as colour**; plain-language customer material; specialist review semi-annually | Accessibility review closed with no open high findings |
| **O-10 Environmental responsibility** | Lower materiality than the objectives above, but not nil, and recorded honestly | Inference-only (no training runs); prompt and context efficiency; right-sized models per node with only the auditor node on the largest model; scheduled rather than continuous operation; managed-service efficiency | Inference volume per assessment, trended |

## M — Mechanisms, including where human oversight belongs

Objectives are achieved through the mechanisms in the table above plus the following cross-cutting ones.

| # | Mechanism | Achieves |
|---|---|---|
| M-1 | Governance: AIGB approval, time-bounded authorization to operate, quarterly review | O-2, and all |
| M-2 | Life-cycle control: AIMS-POL-006 gates, V&V, release criteria, change classification | O-5, O-7 |
| M-3 | Data governance: quality requirements, provenance, preparation criteria (AIMS-STD-022, AIMS-PRO-024, AIMS-PRO-023) | O-1, O-5, O-8 |
| **M-4** | **Human oversight — see below** | O-2, O-4, O-6 |
| M-5 | Monitoring and measurement against thresholds (AIMS-PRO-007) | O-5, O-7 |
| M-6 | Adverse-impact reporting from anyone, handled in the same register as failures (AIMS-PLAN-016) | O-1, O-2, O-3 |
| M-7 | Fairness and accuracy evaluation on our own task across strata | O-1, O-5 |
| M-8 | Competence: analysts qualified in the framework they review; operators trained on limitations and failure modes | O-2, O-4 |
| M-9 | Transparency artefacts: user information, limitations, annual performance reporting | O-3 |
| M-10 | Supplier management, model pinning and drift detection | O-5, O-7 |

### M-4 — Where meaningful human oversight is applied across the life cycle

Oversight is placed where a human can actually change the outcome, and the authority to stop is real at
every point — oversight that cannot halt anything is not oversight.

| Life-cycle stage | Human oversight | Authority held | Why here |
|---|---|---|---|
| Inception / use decision | AIGB approves or rejects the use case; Responsible AI Lead holds a **veto** on prohibited uses | Reject; approve with conditions | The cheapest point to prevent harm is before building |
| Requirements and design | Compliance analysts define correct behaviour; **failure modes chosen deliberately** (unable-to-assess over guessing) | Block a design | Design decisions determine which errors are even possible |
| Data | Data Steward admits or rejects data; labelling by qualified analysts with adjudication | Reject data | Ground truth is a human product |
| Prompts and rubrics | Two-approval peer review by practising analysts | Block | Rubrics are where interpretation lives |
| V&V | Analyst review of sampled outputs; release criteria signed by named roles | **Block release** | Last point before customers rely on it |
| **Operation — per assessment** | **A qualified analyst reviews and may override any verdict before it is relied on. Override is expected in normal use; a zero override rate is investigated as a sign of insufficient review, not celebrated as accuracy** | Override; annotate; re-scope; halt | Where the individual consequential decision actually happens |
| Operation — aggregate | Monthly review of monitoring measures, override rates, coverage and concerns | Suspend the system | Patterns are invisible from a single assessment |
| Publication of assurance claims | **No certification, attestation or assurance conclusion is issued on machine output alone** | Refuse to sign | The highest-consequence output must carry a human signature |
| Change | Change classification, approval, and rollback authority with Operations | Reject or roll back a change | Changes silently move verdicts |
| Incident | Incident commander; Accountable Owner may withdraw published results | Withdraw, correct, suspend | Harm in progress needs someone empowered to stop it |
| Decommissioning | AIGB decision; record retention confirmed | Decide | Ending use is also a use decision |

**Conditions that make oversight meaningful, not nominal:** reviewers are competent in the framework
being assessed (M-8); they see the rationale and the cited evidence, not just the verdict; they have
time and are not measured on throughput alone; **disagreeing with the system is the expected behaviour
and carries no penalty**; overrides are recorded and analysed as product feedback; and no automated path
exists that publishes a consequential result without a human in it.
