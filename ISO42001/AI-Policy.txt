## 1. Purpose and direction for AI

This is the organization's documented policy for the development and use of AI systems. It
establishes the organization's overall direction for AI and is appropriate to the organization's
purpose: we build and operate AI systems that automate compliance evaluation and decision support
for regulated environments. Because our AI systems inform governance and audit outcomes, accuracy,
traceability and human accountability are the qualities we optimize for ahead of speed or coverage.

This policy provides the **framework for setting AI objectives**. Every AI objective in the AI
Objectives Register (AIMS-REG-002) must trace to one of the guiding principles in section 3, must be
measurable, and must name one accountable role from the AI Roles and Responsibilities Policy
(AIMS-POL-003).

## 2. Commitments

The organization commits to:

1. **Meeting applicable requirements** — all legal, regulatory, statutory and contractual
   requirements applicable to our AI systems, including ISO/IEC 42001, applicable data-protection
   law, and customer contractual terms governing automated processing.
2. **Continual improvement of the AI management system (AIMS)** — the AIMS is measured against the
   AI objectives, reviewed in management review, and improved through corrective action; nonconformities
   are tracked to closure in the AIMS corrective-action log.
3. **Human accountability** — no AI system operates without a named Accountable Owner, and no
   consequential decision is made by an AI system without a defined human oversight point.

## 3. Guiding principles

| # | Principle | What it means in practice |
|---|---|---|
| P1 | Human oversight and accountability | Every AI system has a named Accountable Owner and a documented human review point for consequential outputs. |
| P2 | Transparency and explainability | Intended use, limitations and valid domain of use are documented and disclosed to users. |
| P3 | Fairness | Systems are evaluated for disparate performance across relevant demographic groups before release and on material change. |
| P4 | Safety and robustness | Systems are threat-modelled, adversarially tested, and monitored for drift outside intended conditions. |
| P5 | Privacy and data protection | Personal data used by AI systems is lawfully acquired, minimized, and governed under the Data Governance Policy. |
| P6 | Security by design | AI systems inherit the organization's security controls and are subject to prompt-injection and access-control testing. |
| P7 | Traceability | AI system operation produces event logs sufficient to reconstruct what the system did and on what basis. |

## 4. Handling deviations and exceptions

Deviations from this policy are permitted only as **documented, time-bound exceptions**:

1. The requester raises an exception request naming the policy clause, the business justification,
   the compensating control, and a proposed expiry date (maximum 12 months).
2. The AI Risk Manager assesses the residual risk against the approved AI risk appetite.
3. The **AIGB approves or rejects**; exceptions exceeding the risk appetite may be approved only by
   the Executive Leadership Team.
4. Approved exceptions are recorded in the AI Exceptions Register (AIMS-REG-003) with owner, expiry
   and compensating control, and are reviewed at every AIGB meeting. An expired exception is a
   nonconformity.

Undocumented deviation is not permitted. There are no standing or open-ended exceptions.

## 5. Business and risk context informing this policy

This policy is informed by, and re-derived from, the organization's documented context:

- **Business strategy.** Our strategy is to deliver automated assurance products to regulated
  customers. This makes correctness and auditability of AI output a direct commercial requirement,
  not only an ethical one — reflected in P2, P3 and P7.
- **Organizational values.** Our stated values place customer trust ahead of growth; this is why P1
  (human oversight) is non-negotiable and why fully autonomous consequential decisions are outside
  our risk appetite.
- **Risk appetite.** The AIGB has approved an appetite that is **low** for privacy, security and
  fairness risk, **moderate** for availability risk, and **zero** for undisclosed automated
  decision-making affecting an individual's rights.
- **Identified AI-related risk.** The ten risks in the AI Risk Register (AIMS-REG-001), R-01 to R-10,
  each map to a principle above and to an accountable role in AIMS-POL-003.
- **Legal and contractual requirements.** ISO/IEC 42001; applicable data-protection law for personal
  data processed by AI systems; customer contracts requiring disclosure of automated processing and
  retention of decision records.
- **Impacts to interested parties.** Assessed for customers, end users whose compliance posture is
  evaluated, employees, regulators and auditors — recorded per system in the AI system impact
  assessment (AIMS-PRO-005).

## 6. Responsible AI topics — intersections with other organizational policies

This policy identifies where other organizational policies apply to, or are affected by, our AI
objectives and practices, and aligns AI governance with each domain. Where an intersection exists it
has been addressed either by updating the affected policy or by incorporating the necessary
provisions directly into this policy.

| Policy domain | Instrument | Intersection with AI | How the intersection was addressed |
|---|---|---|---|
| Information security | Information Security Policy (SEC-POL-001) | AI systems are in-scope assets; prompt injection and model abuse are new threat classes | SEC-POL-001 updated (v6.2) to add AI systems as an asset class and require AI threat modelling |
| Privacy | Privacy Policy (PRIV-POL-001) | Personal data in training, tuning, prompts and logs | PRIV-POL-001 updated (v4.1) to cover AI processing, inference-time personal data, and log minimization |
| Safety | Product Safety Standard (SAF-STD-002) | Consequential AI output requires a human review point | Provision incorporated into this policy as P1 and referenced from SAF-STD-002 |
| Quality | Quality Management Policy (QMS-POL-001) | AI evaluation and validation form part of release quality gates | QMS-POL-001 release checklist extended with AI verification and validation evidence |
| Compliance / legal | Compliance Policy (CMP-POL-001) | Regulatory obligations for automated decision-making and disclosure | Referenced here in section 5; CMP-POL-001 register extended with ISO/IEC 42001 |
| Human resources | HR Policy and Code of Conduct | Competence requirements and the duty to report AI concerns without reprisal | Code of Conduct updated to protect AI concern reporters; competences recorded in AIMS-REG-004 |
| Procurement | Procurement Standard (PRC-STD-001) | Supplier-provided models and AI components carry inherited risk | PRC-STD-001 updated with AI supplier assessment gate; see AIMS-PRO-010 |
| Data governance | Data Governance Policy (DG-POL-001) | Provenance, lineage, quality and retention of AI data | DG-POL-001 updated to add AI training-data provenance and lineage duties to the Data Steward role |

No identified intersection is left unaddressed; the map above is reviewed with this policy.

## 7. Communication and availability

This policy is **communicated within the organization** by publication on the internal AIMS portal,
mandatory acknowledgement during onboarding for all engineering, data and product personnel,
annual refresher training, and an all-hands briefing on each major version. It is
**made available to interested parties as appropriate**: a public Responsible AI Statement
summarizes it for customers and the public, the full policy is provided to customers and auditors
under NDA on request, and it is supplied to regulators on request.

## 8. Review of this policy

The CAIO — a management-approved role — is responsible for the **development, review and
evaluation** of this policy. Review occurs **at planned annual intervals and additionally as
needed**, and explicitly takes into account:

- opportunities for improvement identified since the last review;
- changes in the **organizational environment** (structure, AI system inventory, personnel);
- changes in **business circumstances** (new products, new customer commitments, new markets);
- changes in **legal conditions** (new or amended AI, privacy or sector regulation);
- changes in the **technical environment** (new model providers, new agent architectures, newly
  identified attack classes);
- the **results of management reviews**, including AIMS performance against the AI objectives,
  audit findings, nonconformities and the status of the AI Exceptions Register.

Each review concludes with a documented determination of the policy's continuing **suitability,
adequacy and effectiveness**, recorded below, and either reaffirms or amends the policy.
