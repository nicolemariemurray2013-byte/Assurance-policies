## 1. Why this is a topic-specific policy in its own right

The organization runs entirely on cloud services. There is no data centre and no on-premises
production estate. That makes the cloud provider relationship structurally different from every other
supplier relationship: the provider does not deliver something to us that we then operate, it operates
the substrate our controls run on. A generic supplier policy cannot carry that.

This policy therefore exists separately from `SEC-PRO-020` (Third-Party and Vendor Risk Management),
which continues to govern supplier assessment, contracting and monitoring generally. Where the two
overlap, `SEC-PRO-020` sets the process and this policy sets the cloud-specific requirements the
process must apply.

Three positions shape everything below:

- **A shared responsibility model that is not written down is not shared, it is assumed.** The most
  common cloud failure is not a provider failure; it is a control that both parties believed the other
  was operating. §4 makes the boundary explicit per service.
- **A cloud service is acquired, not merely enabled.** Enabling a managed service in an existing
  account takes one API call and no procurement event. §3 makes that an authorisation decision anyway,
  because the ease of the action has no relationship to the risk of it.
- **A service we cannot leave is a service that governs us.** Exit is designed at acquisition, when we
  still have leverage, not at termination, when we have none. §8.

## 2. Scope and the definition this policy uses

"Cloud service" means any service where a third party operates the compute, storage, network or
application layer on our behalf, regardless of the commercial model or how it was acquired. That
includes:

| In scope | Examples in our estate |
|---|---|
| Infrastructure and platform services from the primary provider | AWS compute, storage, database, network, identity, key management, AI/ML services |
| Managed application services we depend on operationally | Source hosting and CI, ticketing, monitoring/SIEM, endpoint management, corporate productivity and mail, e-signature |
| Services consumed as an API rather than provisioned | Model inference endpoints, third-party enrichment and threat-intelligence APIs |
| Free-tier, trial and unpaid services | In scope on identical terms. A trial that touches in-scope information is an acquisition |
| Services embedded in a supplied product | In scope; the supplier's sub-processors are assessed through `SEC-PRO-020` and the transfer terms in `SEC-REG-047` §6 |

**Explicitly in scope and easily missed:** browser extensions and IDE plugins that transmit content to
a hosted service; AI assistants and code-generation services; anything a personal account could be
used to reach. `SEC-STD-027` prohibits processing in-scope information through a personally held
account, and this policy is the reason.

**Not in scope:** services that neither receive in-scope information nor form part of an in-scope
system's operation — a public documentation site read anonymously, for example. The determination is
recorded rather than assumed, because "it does not touch our data" is a claim that ages badly as a
product adds features.

## 3. Acquisition — the roles and the authorisation path

Cloud services are acquired against a defined set of requirements, assessed before use, and recorded.
No in-scope information may be placed into a cloud service before the assessment concludes.

| Stage | What is required | Who |
|---|---|---|
| Request | The business purpose, the information classification involved, the personal data categories if any, the expected volume, and the alternatives considered | Requesting team lead |
| Security assessment | The requirements in §5, the provider's attestations under §6, and a threat assessment where the service is internet-facing or holds Confidential information | Head of Security Engineering |
| Privacy assessment | Lawful basis, transfer mechanism, sub-processor position, retention, and a DPIA where `SEC-REG-047` §5 requires one | DPO |
| Legal and contractual | The terms in §7, the DPA, and the exit terms in §8 | General Counsel |
| Architecture fit | Whether an already-approved service covers the need — the first question, not the last | Head of Platform Engineering |
| Authorisation | **Confidential information or personal data, or any new provider: CISO.** Internal information on an already-approved provider: Head of Platform Engineering. Public information only: team lead, recorded | Per classification |
| Recording | Entry in the cloud service register: service, provider, purpose, classification, owner, authorisation, assessment date, exit assessment, review date | Compliance Manager |

**Responsibilities that are held permanently, not per acquisition:**

| Role | Responsibility |
|---|---|
| CISO | Owns this policy; authorises Confidential and new-provider acquisitions; owns the residual risk position |
| Head of Platform Engineering | Owns the technical requirements in §5, the configuration baselines the provider's services are held to (`SEC-CFG-025`), and the architecture-fit judgement |
| Service owner (named per entry in the register) | Owns the service day to day: its configuration, its access list, its cost, its review, and its continued necessity. **Every register entry has one named human owner; a service owned by "the platform team" is a service nobody reviews** |
| Head of Procurement | Owns acquisition and exit as commercial processes, and the supplier relationship |
| DPO | Owns the personal-data position, transfers, and sub-processor changes |
| General Counsel | Owns contractual adequacy, including the exit and audit terms |
| Compliance Manager | Owns the register's completeness and the review calendar |
| All personnel | Must not introduce a cloud service outside this path. The path is deliberately fast for low-classification cases so that compliance is easier than circumvention |

**Unsanctioned use.** Discovery runs monthly: egress destination review against the register, expense
and card-transaction review with Finance, browser-extension and plugin inventory from endpoint
management, and identity-provider application discovery. Findings are treated as a control deficiency,
not a disciplinary matter in the first instance — the team wanted something and the path did not serve
them, which is a signal about the path. This period: 3 services found unregistered (2 subsequently
assessed and approved, 1 removed with its data deleted and deletion confirmed).

## 4. The shared responsibility position — stated per service class

The division of responsibility is documented for every service class we use, and the documentation is
ours rather than a link to the provider's page, because the provider's page describes what they do and
we need a record of what **we** must therefore do. The controls below are the ones most often assumed.

| Control area | Provider responsibility | Our responsibility | Where ours is implemented |
|---|---|---|---|
| Physical and environmental security | Entirely the provider's | Verify through attestation; do not attempt to control | `SEC-PRO-013` §1 states this explicitly, so the absence of our own data-centre controls is a recorded position rather than a gap |
| Hypervisor, host OS, managed-service patching | Provider | Verify patch position through attestation; patch anything we run | `SEC-CFG-025` |
| Guest OS, runtime and application patching | Ours, for anything we deploy | Baselines, scanning, deadlines | `SEC-CFG-025` §5 |
| Identity and access | Provider operates the mechanism | **Every policy, role, permission and review is ours.** The provider will faithfully enforce a policy that grants the world access | `SEC-IAM-021` |
| Network configuration | Provider operates the fabric | Security groups, NACLs, endpoints, exposure | `SEC-CFG-025`, `SEC-NET-023` |
| Encryption | Provider supplies the mechanism and key custody | **Enabling it, choosing the key, controlling the key policy, and rotation** | `SEC-CRY-022` |
| Data classification and handling | Provider is agnostic to what we store | Entirely ours | `SEC-STD-024`, `SEC-CLS-017` |
| Backup and recovery | Provider offers durable storage and snapshot mechanisms | **Configuring backups, testing restores, and retention.** Durability is not backup: durable storage faithfully preserves a deletion | `SEC-BCK-018` |
| Logging | Provider emits and stores control-plane and service logs | Enabling them, retaining them, protecting them, and reading them | `SEC-MON-026`, `SEC-LOG-015` |
| Monitoring and detection | Provider supplies primitives and some managed detections | Deciding what constitutes an alert and who responds | `SEC-MON-026` |
| Availability architecture | Provider supplies zones, regions and an SLA | **Choosing a multi-AZ or multi-region architecture and validating it.** An SLA is a commercial remedy, not an availability control | `BCP-PLN-020`, and stated in `SEC-CRY-022` §4C |
| Vulnerability disclosure about the platform | Provider publishes bulletins | Consuming the feed and acting | `SEC-CFG-025` §5.5, `SEC-MON-026` §5 |
| Incident response | Provider handles platform incidents | Everything above the platform, plus deciding when a provider incident becomes our incident | `SEC-IRP-012` |

**The rule this table exists to enforce:** where a control appears in the "ours" column, it must exist
in a named document with a named owner. The 2026-08 reconciliation confirmed all 13 rows trace to a
control; one row (availability architecture) previously traced only to an implicit assumption that the
provider's SLA was sufficient, which is the finding that produced the explicit statement in
`SEC-CRY-022` §4C.

## 5. Security requirements a cloud service must meet

| # | Requirement | Applies to |
|---|---|---|
| C1 | Encryption in transit using current protocols and cipher suites per `SEC-CRY-022` | All |
| C2 | Encryption at rest, with customer-managed keys where the service supports them and Confidential information is held | All holding Internal or above |
| C3 | Federated authentication from our directory with MFA; no local service accounts; no shared credentials | All supporting it. Where a service cannot federate, it is a recorded exception with a compensating control and a migration intent — currently 1 |
| C4 | Role-based authorisation granular enough to express least privilege, and an access review capability | All |
| C5 | Administrative and data-access audit logging, exportable to our own retention | All |
| C6 | Data residency controllable to the regions permitted by `SEC-REG-047` §5 | All holding personal data |
| C7 | Tenant isolation with a stated mechanism | Multi-tenant services |
| C8 | Backup or export capability sufficient to reconstruct our data independently of the provider | All holding data we could not recreate |
| C9 | A published vulnerability-handling and notification practice meeting `SEC-CFG-025` §5.5 | All |
| C10 | Sub-processor transparency with advance notice of change and a right to object | All processing personal data |
| C11 | Configuration that can be expressed and enforced as code, or in its absence a documented and reviewed manual baseline | All |
| C12 | No use of our data to train models, other than under a specifically approved and contracted arrangement | All, and specifically assessed for every AI or inference service |
| C13 | Availability characteristics adequate for the dependent business activity's RTO, per the BIA in `BCP-PLN-020` §2 | All in a recovery path |

Where a requirement cannot be met, the service may still be approved by the CISO with a compensating
control, a residual-risk statement and a review date. Requirements are not silently waived: the
register records which of C1–C13 are met, which are compensated, and which are open. Current
position: 21 registered services; 19 meeting all applicable requirements; 2 with recorded compensations
(one for C3, one for C7 where the provider's isolation mechanism is attested but not independently
verifiable by us).

## 6. Provider architecture, controls and the standards we require them to be assessed against

We do not build the provider's controls and cannot audit their data centres. Assurance therefore comes
from independent assessment against **named** standards, and "named" is the operative word — a claim of
"industry-standard security" is not assurance, because it identifies no scope, no auditor and no
period.

| Standard or framework | What we require | Applied to |
|---|---|---|
| **ISO/IEC 27001** with a current certificate | Certificate, scope statement covering the services we use, and the Statement of Applicability's exclusions reviewed. **The scope check matters more than the certificate**: a certificate scoped to a different service line is not assurance for ours | All providers holding Internal or above |
| **ISO/IEC 27017** — cloud-specific controls | Preferred; assessed where available, as it addresses the shared-responsibility controls that 27001 alone does not | Cloud providers |
| **ISO/IEC 27018** — personal data in public cloud | Preferred where the provider processes personal data | Providers processing personal data |
| **ISO/IEC 27701** — privacy information management | Considered, and required of processors holding special-category or high-volume personal data | Where applicable |
| **SOC 2 Type II** (SSAE 18 / ISAE 3402) | Report obtained and **read**, not merely collected: the exceptions section, the complementary user-entity controls (which are our obligations, and are reconciled into §4), the period covered, and any bridging letter for the gap between period end and now | All providers holding Internal or above |
| **ISO/IEC 22301** or an equivalent continuity attestation | Assessed for providers in a recovery path | Recovery-path providers |
| **NIST SP 800-53** / FedRAMP authorisation where held | Accepted as additional evidence of control depth | Where held |
| **CIS Benchmarks** | Applied by **us** to the provider's services — this is our configuration obligation, not the provider's attestation. 368 AWS Config rules derive from it | Our configuration of every service |
| **PCI DSS**, **HIPAA** | Recorded as not applicable to our processing, per the completeness test in `SEC-REG-047` §2, rather than omitted |  |
| **ISO/IEC 42001** | Required consideration for providers of AI services, aligned to our own management system | AI service providers |
| **FIPS 140-3** validated modules | Required for cryptographic services, satisfied through provider validation per `SEC-CRY-022` §4B.1 | Cryptographic services |

**Architecture assessment.** Beyond attestations, the assessment records: the multi-tenancy and
isolation model, the regions and availability zones in use and the failure domain each represents, the
key management design and who can access key material, the network path from us to the service and
whether it traverses the public internet, the sub-processor chain, and the provider's own change and
incident practices as far as they are disclosed. Where a provider will not disclose enough to assess
these, that is recorded as a limitation on the assurance rather than passed over.

**Audit rights.** We hold no right to physically audit the hyperscale provider; assurance is by
attestation. This is the recorded deviation `D-2026-028` — one deviation, referenced consistently
here, in `SEC-PRO-020` and in `SEC-CRY-022` §4C, rather than restated as several. Smaller providers do
grant audit and questionnaire rights, and 6 were exercised this period.

**Refresh.** Attestations are re-obtained on their own cycle (annually at minimum), tracked with
expiry dates, and an expired attestation raises a deficiency. Verification 2026-08: 21 services, 21
current attestations, 0 expired, 2 bridging letters obtained.

## 7. Contractual and use requirements

Terms are set through `SEC-PRO-020` and `SEC-REG-047` §6. The cloud-specific requirements are: a DPA
with the transfer mechanism named; sub-processor notice and objection; the security commitments in §5
reflected contractually rather than only in marketing material; incident notification within 24 hours
for anything affecting our data; the vulnerability terms in `SEC-CFG-025` §5.5; data ownership stated
unambiguously as ours; no use of our content for the provider's own purposes including model training
unless specifically approved; return and certified destruction on termination; and the exit terms in
§8. Clickwrap acceptance of standard terms is a legal act and is not performed by an engineer — where
a service offers only non-negotiable standard terms, General Counsel reviews them and the acceptance
is recorded.

**Cost and commitment** are governed too, because a commitment is a lock-in mechanism: multi-year
reserved commitments require the same exit assessment as a technical dependency.

## 8. Exit, portability and continuity of the service

An exit position is required **at acquisition**, recorded in the register, and reviewed annually. The
questions it must answer:

| Question | Why it is asked at acquisition |
|---|---|
| How do we get our data out, in what format, and how long does it take at current volume? | An export capability that exists but takes six weeks is not an exit path for a 30-day termination notice |
| What is the alternative — a named second provider, a self-hosted option, or an accepted decision that there is none? | "There is no alternative" is an acceptable answer only if it is written down and risk-accepted |
| What breaks operationally the day the service stops? | Identifies the dependency in the recovery path |
| How is our data destroyed, and how is the destruction evidenced? | Governed by `SEC-STD-024` §5; the deletion certificate requirement is contractual, not aspirational |
| What happens if the provider exits, is acquired, changes terms materially, suffers a sustained outage, or fails? | Provider failure is a continuity scenario in `BCP-PLN-020`, not only a commercial one |
| Are we depending on a proprietary interface where a portable one exists? | A recorded architectural choice, made deliberately if made at all |

**Concentration.** The overwhelming majority of our processing sits with one provider. This is a
recorded, accepted concentration risk (`R-2026-030`) with a stated rationale — the security and
operational benefit of a single consistently managed estate, per `OPS-STD-049` §6, exceeds the
diversification benefit at our scale — and with the mitigations that are actually available: multi-AZ
and multi-region capability, infrastructure defined as portable code where practical, data exportable
in open formats, and backups readable without the provider's tooling. The risk is reviewed annually by
the Security Governance Board rather than treated as settled.

**Exit tested, not assumed:** one service exit was executed this period (the discovery-phase tooling
replaced in 2026-Q2) — data exported, verified readable outside the provider, deletion certificate
obtained in 11 days against a contractual 30. An exit path is credible once it has been walked.

## 9. Communication of this policy

A policy nobody has been told about is not a control, so communication is a requirement of the policy
rather than an activity that follows it.

| Audience | How it is communicated | Record |
|---|---|---|
| All personnel | On publication, through the all-hands channel and the monthly security note, with the one-paragraph summary of what an individual must do (do not introduce a service; the path is here; it is fast) | Publication notice 2026-08-21; read receipt not required, but the discovery controls in §3 test whether the message landed |
| New joiners | In the security induction, alongside `SEC-STD-027`; covered in the awareness programme `SEC-AWR-041` §2.1 | Induction completion records — 48/48 |
| Engineering and platform teams | Walked through in the engineering forum, with the §4 responsibility table as the working artifact | Forum record 2026-08-20, 22 attendees |
| Service owners (21 named) | Directly, with their register entry and their review obligations | Acknowledged individually — 21/21 |
| Procurement, Legal, DPO | Through the acquisition-path change; the assessment gates are in their workflow, not in a document they must remember | Process update record |
| Suppliers and interested parties | The policy is not published externally; the commitments it produces appear in customer trust documentation and in supplier terms | Trust documentation revision |

Communication is repeated, not one-shot: the acquisition path appears in the quarterly security note,
and the discovery result in §3 is reported alongside it so that personnel see the policy is
operating.
