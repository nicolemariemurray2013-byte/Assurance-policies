## 1. Policies and practices reflecting competency expectations

The entity has **established policies and practices that reflect the competency expectations necessary
to support the achievement of objectives**. Competence is defined per role in a role profile, and the
profile is derived from the objectives and commitments the role must deliver — not written
generically — so that a competence shortfall can be identified against something specific.

| Role | Competency expectations (from the role profile) | Objective / commitment it supports |
|---|---|---|
| Assessment / AI engineer | Model evaluation methodology; prompt engineering; the frameworks being assessed; statistical reasoning about output quality; secure coding | Processing integrity of assessment results |
| Platform engineer | AWS architecture; IaC; secure SDLC; change management; on-call operations | Availability and security commitments |
| Security engineer | Threat modelling; IAM and cryptography; detection engineering; vulnerability management; incident response | Security commitments |
| Site reliability / on-call | Incident command; the recovery runbooks; capacity analysis | Availability commitments and the recovery objectives |
| Compliance analyst | The trust services criteria and ISO/IEC 42001; evidence sufficiency; control testing | Control operation and the assertion |
| Privacy / legal counsel | Applicable privacy law; DPIA methodology; contract drafting | Privacy and confidentiality commitments |
| People manager | Performance and conduct evaluation; the escalation duties in GOV-STD-001 | The control environment itself |
| Customer operations | The service's actual limitations; incident communication; confidentiality handling | Honest representation; confidentiality |
| Board / committee member | The skills matrix in GOV-CHT-002 §3 | Oversight |

**Practices that give the expectations effect:** a role profile is mandatory before a requisition
opens; interview loops are structured against the profile with a written scorecard per competency and a
designated bar raiser; a hiring decision requires the security-competency scorecard for any role with
production access; competency expectations appear in the job description, the offer and the performance
review; and access to production is provisioned only after role-specific training is complete
(enforced in the access workflow, not by reminder).

## 2. Background of personnel, contractors and vendor employees

The entity **addresses the background of potential and existing personnel, contractors and vendor
employees when determining whether to employ and retain the individuals.**

| Population | Pre-engagement screening | Re-screening | Enforcement point |
|---|---|---|---|
| Employees and interns | Identity and right-to-work verification; employment-history verification for the last 7 years; education verification where claimed; criminal-records check where lawful; two professional references | Every 3 years for privileged-access roles; on promotion into a privileged role | Start date does not proceed without a cleared result; **production access is not provisioned until screening clears** |
| **Contractors** | Same standard as employees, performed by the entity or evidenced by the supplier to the entity's standard | Every 3 years, or at contract renewal if sooner | Access request is rejected without a screening attestation on file |
| **Vendor employees** with system or data access | Supplier must attest per named individual that screening equivalent to this policy was completed, and retain the evidence for audit | Annually per individual, plus at each engagement renewal | **No named-individual attestation, no access** — a hard gate in the access workflow |
| Elevated-privilege roles (break-glass, identity administration, production data access) | Enhanced screening: the standard set plus a financial-probity check where lawful and an additional reference | Every 2 years | Elevated role is removed if re-screening lapses |
| Outsourced service providers (organizational) | Corporate due diligence: SOC 2 or equivalent, security questionnaire, financial stability, sanctions screening | Annually per CC9 | No production data before DPA and diligence completion |

Screening covers **existing** individuals, not only candidates, which is why re-screening cadences and
lapse consequences are defined. Local law limits what can be checked in some jurisdictions; where a
check is unlawful the limitation is recorded in the individual's screening record with the
compensating control applied (additional reference and a 90-day supervised-access period).

**Current period:** 11 employees and 3 interns screened before start; 11 vendor/contractor individuals
attested; 1 case where employment history could not be verified for a 5-month gap, escalated to the
Head of People and the hiring manager, resolved with a documented explanation and an additional
reference; 2 re-screenings overdue at the 2026-Q1 review, both privileged users, whose elevated roles
were **removed** until the re-screening completed. Recording the removal is the point — a screening
policy with no consequence on lapse would not be a control.

## 3. Technical competency of personnel, contractors and vendor employees

The entity **addresses the technical competency of potential and existing personnel, contractors and
vendor employees when determining whether to employ and retain the individuals.**

| Determination point | How technical competency is assessed | Evidence produced |
|---|---|---|
| Hiring | Structured technical interview against the role profile; hands-on exercise (a real defect or a real architecture problem); security-competency scorecard for production roles | Scorecards per competency; written hiring decision citing them |
| Probation / first 90 days | Manager assessment against the role profile at 30/60/90 days | Probation decision record; 1 employee did not pass probation in the current period on the assessment-quality competency |
| Ongoing (existing personnel) | Semi-annual competency assessment separate from the delivery rating; on-call readiness sign-off; annual security-competency refresh with a pass threshold | Competency ratings; on-call certification list |
| Privileged access | Role-specific competency verification before provisioning, re-verified annually | Access record references the verification |
| **Contractors** | Same technical interview and scorecard as employees for equivalent work | Scorecards on file |
| **Vendor employees** | Supplier proposes named individuals; the entity **reviews and may reject** each individual's competency; the entity interviews for any role touching assessment logic | Individual acceptance record; 2 proposed individuals rejected in the current period |
| **Outsourced service providers** (organizational competence) | Diligence includes the provider's own competence practices and certifications; verified annually | Supplier record entry |

Retention decisions use the same standard: the semi-annual competency assessment can conclude that an
individual no longer meets the profile, which triggers §5.

## 4. Evaluating competence across the entity and in outsourced service providers

The Board and management **evaluate competence across the entity and in outsourced service providers
in relation to the established policies and practices.**

| Evaluation | Scope | Cadence | Performed by | Output |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Individual competency assessment | Every employee, intern, contractor | Semi-annual | Manager, calibrated across managers | Rating per competency against the profile |
| Role-coverage analysis | Every critical role and control | Semi-annual | Head of People + control owners | Single-point-of-knowledge list; feeds §6 |
| Entity-wide competence review | All operating units | Annual | CEO + Head of People, presented to the Board | Competence report with gaps and actions |
| On-call readiness review | On-call roster | Quarterly | VP Engineering | Certified / not certified per engineer |
| Security and privacy competency test | All personnel, contractors, vendor staff with access | Annual | AI Security Lead / DPO | Pass rate; individual results |
| **Outsourced provider competence evaluation** | Every provider in GOV-STR-003 §1.4 | Annual, per CC9 | Third-Party Risk + named owner | Provider competence conclusion in the supplier record |
| **Vendor personnel competence evaluation** | Each named individual with access | Quarterly and at renewal | Engagement owner | Continue / remediate / remove |
| **Board competence evaluation** | Directors | Annual (February) | Chair, per GOV-CHT-002 §3 | Skills matrix with identified gaps |

**2026 entity-wide review — what it actually found.** The review, presented to the Board on
2026-02-19, concluded competence was sufficient in six of eight operating units and identified three
shortcomings: (a) privacy-law depth at Board level and one gap in Legal & Privacy; (b) detection
engineering depth in Security — one engineer held effectively all of it; (c) incident-command
competence thin outside two individuals. A review that identifies nothing is treated as evidence the
review was not performed properly.

## 5. Acting to address identified competence shortcomings

The Board and management **act to address identified competence shortcomings.** Every shortcoming
above carries a named owner, an action and a date, and closure is verified rather than assumed.

| Shortcoming identified | Action taken | Owner | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| Privacy-law depth at Board level | External privacy counsel retained 2025-10-01; privacy-law depth added as a required attribute for the next director; four director-education sessions delivered | Board Chair | **Closed** — verified by GOV-CHT-002 §4 |
| Privacy-law gap in Legal & Privacy | Senior privacy counsel requisition opened 2026-02-24; counsel retainer covers the interim | General Counsel | **Open** — interim cover in place, hire targeted 2026-Q3 |
| Detection engineering concentrated in one engineer | Second engineer trained and certified; managed SIEM provider retained for depth; runbooks written so the knowledge is not personal | AI Security Lead | **Closed** — verified by the 2026-Q2 on-call review |
| Incident command thin beyond two individuals | Four additional engineers trained as Incident Commander; two led a live incident and two led a game-day exercise | VP Engineering | **Closed** — 6 certified ICs |
| 1 employee below profile on assessment quality after probation | Did not pass probation; engagement ended | Hiring manager + Head of People | **Closed** |
| 2 privileged users overdue for re-screening | Elevated roles removed until cleared; both cleared and restored | AI Security Lead | **Closed** |
| Vendor supplier's proposed individuals below the competency bar | Both rejected; supplier proposed alternates who were accepted | VP Engineering | **Closed** |
| Vendor supplier weak on secure-coding competence overall | Corrective action plan required; supplier's staff completed the entity's secure-coding module; scope restricted to non-production until verified | Third-Party Risk | **Closed** with scope restriction retained |
| AI assurance depth at Board level | Independent AI assurance advisor retained 2025-11-15 | Board Chair | **Closed** |

Action includes acting on **outsourced providers** (two entries above) and, where competence cannot be
brought to standard, restricting scope — which is a control decision, not an HR one.

## 6. Mentoring and training to attract, develop and retain personnel and providers

The entity **provides the mentoring and training needed to attract, develop and retain sufficient and
competent personnel and outsourced service providers.**

| Mechanism | Population | Cadence |
|---|---|---|
| Assigned onboarding buddy plus a 90-day structured plan | Every new joiner, including contractors | On joining |
| Named mentor outside the direct reporting line | Every employee who wants one; mandatory for anyone in the first year and for new managers | Continuous; 22 active pairings |
| Manager 1:1s covering development, not only delivery | All employees | Weekly or biweekly |
| Individual development plan with a funded learning budget | All employees | Set annually, reviewed semi-annually |
| Conference, certification and continuing-education funding | All employees; contractors where role-relevant | On request, per the budget |
| Internal technical deep-dive sessions | All engineering | Biweekly |
| New-manager training including conduct and escalation duties | New people managers | On appointment |
| Retention: compensation benchmarking, career-path framework, promotion cycle | All employees | Annual benchmark; semi-annual promotion cycle |
| Attraction: published role profiles, structured and consistent interview loops, candidate-experience feedback | Candidates | Continuous |

**Mentoring and training extended to outsourced providers and their personnel:** vendor and contractor
individuals receive the entity's onboarding, the Code of Conduct annex, the secure-coding module and
the annual security and privacy training; the entity delivered a targeted secure-coding program to a
supplier's staff as the corrective action in §5; and provider-facing runbooks and standards are shared
so the provider can meet the entity's bar rather than guess at it.

Retention outcome for the current period: voluntary attrition 6%, no departures from a
single-point-of-knowledge role, and 5 internal promotions. Attrition is reported to the Board because
losing a competent individual in a control role is a control risk.

## 7. Training programs, continuing education and technical competency maintenance

The entity **provides training programs, including continuing education and training, to develop and
maintain the skill sets and technical competency of existing personnel, contractors and vendor
employees.**

| Program | Audience | Frequency | Completion requirement | Current period |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Security awareness and secure development | All personnel, contractors, vendor staff with access | Annual + on joining | Mandatory; pass threshold; access suspended if overdue by 30 days | 48 of 48 complete |
| Privacy and data protection training | All personnel; enhanced module for those handling personal data | Annual | Mandatory | 48 of 48 |
| Code of Conduct with scenario assessment | All covered persons | Annual | Mandatory; ≥ 80% | Per GOV-STD-001 §3 |
| Responsible AI and AI risk | Engineering, product, assessment, customer ops | Annual | Mandatory | 31 of 31 |
| Role-specific technical training per the role profile | By role | Annual, in the IDP | Manager-verified | 34 IDPs current |
| Incident response and incident command certification | On-call roster and ICs | Annual + quarterly game day | Certification required to hold on-call | 6 ICs, 12 on-call certified |
| Change management and secure SDLC | All engineers | Annual | Mandatory before merge rights | 19 of 19 |
| Framework and criteria training (trust services criteria, ISO/IEC 42001) | Compliance, assessment engineering, customer ops | Annual, refreshed on framework changes | Mandatory | 14 of 14 |
| Continuing education / certification maintenance (CISSP, CIPP, cloud certifications) | Individuals holding them | Per certifying body's CPE cycle | Funded; CPE evidence retained | 7 individuals current |
| Phishing simulation with targeted follow-up training | All personnel | Quarterly | Follow-up mandatory on failure | 4 campaigns; 3 individuals re-trained |
| Vendor-personnel technical onboarding | Vendor employees | On engagement + annually | Access gated on completion | 11 of 11 |

Overdue training is a tracked exception with a 30-day access-suspension consequence; three suspensions
were applied in the current period and all three cleared within a week. Completion is reported to the
Board quarterly alongside the competence metrics.

## 8. Succession and contingency planning for internal-control responsibilities

Senior management and the Board **develop contingency plans for assignments of responsibility
important for internal control.** Every control-critical role has a named deputy who is trained and
has performed the duty, not merely nominated. Planned and unplanned absence are treated differently
because they need different mechanisms.

| Control-critical role | Primary | Named deputy | Deputy exercised? | Emergency succession |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Accountable Owner for the AIMS / internal control | CEO | VP Engineering | Yes — chaired 2026-Q1 governance review | Board appoints an interim within 5 business days |
| Security control ownership | AI Security Lead | Senior security engineer (2nd, per §5) | Yes — ran the 2026-Q2 access review | Board Security & Privacy Committee appoints interim; break-glass re-keyed |
| Incident Commander | Rotating roster | 6 certified ICs | Yes — 4 led an incident or a game day | Roster escalation, always ≥ 2 available |
| Privacy / DPO | General Counsel | External privacy counsel (retained) | Yes — handled 2 DSRs during leave | Retained counsel acts on an interim basis; regulator notified where required |
| Processing-integrity ownership | Chief AI Officer | Responsible AI Lead | Yes — approved 2 model changes | CEO assumes pending appointment |
| Compliance / evidence ownership | Compliance Manager | Senior compliance analyst | Yes — ran the 2026-Q1 evidence cycle | General Counsel assumes |
| Change approval | Engineering managers (pool of 4) | Any other approver in the pool | Continuous | Pool design means no single point |
| Deployment pipeline ownership | Platform lead | Second platform engineer | Yes | Documented break-glass deploy procedure, two-person |
| Board oversight roles | Committee chairs | Deputy chair named per committee | Yes — deputy chaired 1 meeting | Board appoints from the independent directors |

**Contingency plans are tested.** Deputies operate the control at least annually — the "Deputy
exercised?" column is the evidence, and an untested deputy is treated as an open gap. Two structural
contingencies were added after the 2026 role-coverage analysis found single points of knowledge:
detection engineering (second engineer trained) and incident command (four additional ICs). Emergency
succession for the Board-level roles is a Board responsibility and is recorded in the Board's own
minutes, since management cannot plan its own oversight.
