# Third-Party and Vendor Risk Management Procedure

**Document ID:** TPR-PRO-016
**Version:** 4.1
**Owner:** Third-Party Risk Manager (within Legal & Privacy), accountable to the General Counsel; each
engagement has a named entity-side **engagement owner** who is accountable for the outcome of the service
**Approved by:** CEO 2026-02-19; reviewed by the Audit Committee 2026-02-19
**Next scheduled review:** 2026-12-31 (Annually, on the addition or removal of any Tier 1 or Tier 2 provider, and on any)
change to a provider's scope, sub-processors or control environment
**Scope:** All vendors, service providers, business partners, sub-processors and contracted individuals whose
products or services could affect the entity's security, availability, processing integrity, confidentiality
or privacy objectives — currently **7 outsourced providers** and **11 named vendor individuals**, plus the
cloud provider, across both legal entities and five geographies.

**Standing principle:** accountability for a control does not transfer with the work. Where a provider
operates a control, the entity remains accountable for the outcome, and the provider's performance of that
control is **monitored rather than assumed**.

---

## 1. Requirements established for every engagement

**The entity establishes specific requirements for vendor and business partner engagements covering scope of
services and product specifications, roles and responsibilities, compliance requirements, and service
levels.** No engagement proceeds to contract without all four recorded, and for Tier 1 and Tier 2 they are
captured in a **requirements annex** attached to the contract, control by control.

| Requirement category | What must be specified | Where it is recorded | Enforcement |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Scope of services and product specifications** | The precise service; the systems and data it touches; the **data categories and classifications** the provider may access; the environments (production / non-production); named permitted sub-processors; permitted processing locations; **what the provider is expressly not permitted to do** — including using entity or customer data to train models, which is prohibited in every agreement that touches data | Statement of work plus the DPA's processing schedule | Access is provisioned only to the recorded scope; a scope change requires a contract change, and **one provider's scope was restricted to non-production** during the period as a corrective action |
| **Roles and responsibilities** | A control-by-control allocation in the requirements annex — which controls the provider operates, which the entity operates, and which are shared with the split stated; the provider's named operational and security contacts; the entity's named engagement owner; escalation paths on both sides; the provider's obligation to notify before a material change | Requirements annex | The annex is the document tested at each assessment; a control claimed by neither party is a gap raised before signature |
| **Compliance requirements** | Confidentiality obligations at least equivalent to the entity's own (§11); privacy obligations and the DPA (§13); security requirements (encryption in transit and at rest, MFA, least privilege, logging, vulnerability management, secure development); **screening attestations for named individuals**; incident notification with a **maximum notification time**; audit and evidence rights (the right to receive audit reports and, for Tier 1, the right to audit); sub-processor approval and flow-down; data return and deletion on exit; insurance minima and indemnity; applicable law and cross-border transfer mechanism | Requirements annex, DPA and the master agreement | Screening attestation is a **hard gate on access** for named individuals; the notification time is tested at assessment |
| **Service levels** | Availability, response and resolution times, notification times, recovery objectives, accuracy or quality measures where applicable, and **the consequence of a miss** — service credits, corrective action, scope restriction, or termination | Service level schedule | Measured in the performance assessment (§8); a miss triggers §9's issue procedure |

**Where a provider will not accept a requirement**, that is recorded as a decision with the residual risk
named, not quietly dropped. Two instances this period: one provider would not grant an on-site audit right
(accepted, with its independent audit report plus an expanded evidence request as the compensating measure),
and one small provider could not meet the encryption-key-management requirement (**the engagement was
refused** by the Third-Party Risk Manager exercising the negative authority in GOV-STR-003, and an
alternative was selected).

## 2. Evaluating vulnerabilities arising from the relationship, including third-party access and network connections

**The entity evaluates vulnerabilities arising from vendor and business partner relationships, including
third-party access to the entity's IT systems and connections with third-party networks.** The evaluation is
about the *path the relationship opens*, which is distinct from the provider's own control maturity.

| Vulnerability class | How it is evaluated | Current position |
|---|---|---|
| **Human access to entity systems** | Every provider individual with access is named, screened (attestation held), competence-reviewed with a right of rejection, granted least-privilege scoped roles with a **hard expiry at the engagement end date**, and reviewed quarterly | 11 named individuals; 4 hold unescorted physical access; **2 individuals were rejected on competence** and one start was delayed 9 days pending a screening attestation |
| **Privileged access held by a provider** | Assessed specifically: what could the provider reach beyond its stated scope? Break-glass and administrative paths are enumerated | The managed SIEM provider holds read access to log data only; no provider holds administrative access to the production account. The **administrative-access breadth finding** concerns entity identities, not providers, and is recorded as open |
| **Network connections and integrations** | Every inbound and outbound connection is inventoried with its protocol, direction, authentication and data flow; connections are point-to-point and authenticated, with no flat network trust | Log egress to the SIEM provider; model-provider API calls outbound; identity federation inbound; payments and payroll outbound. **No site-to-site VPN or flat network extension to any provider exists** — a deliberate design choice recorded as such |
| **API and credential exposure** | Provider credentials held in central secret management, scoped, rotated, and never embedded in code (SAST blocks credentials in code); a provider's compromise is assumed and the blast radius bounded | **R-009** in the register; per-provider credential scope reviewed at each assessment |
| **Data-flow vulnerability** | What data leaves, to where, under what mechanism, and whether it could include personal data or customer confidential evidence | Data map maintained per provider; the SIEM log flow assessed for incidental personal data and configured to minimize it |
| **Sub-processor / fourth-party exposure** | Named sub-processors reviewed; flow-down of obligations required; the provider must notify before adding one | **R-006** — a sub-processor breach is a register risk with its own incident runbook; one provider's proposed new sub-processor was reviewed and approved with a transfer mechanism check |
| **Software supply chain** | Open-source and third-party components scanned continuously (SCA); provenance and known-vulnerability status assessed; a component is a vendor relationship even without a contract | Continuous scanning; severity-based SLAs |
| **Provider's own supply chain and concentration** | Whether several providers depend on the same underlying provider — the concentration is evaluated at the portfolio level, not per engagement | **The dominant concentration is the cloud provider itself, which is not diversifiable and is recorded as R-022 (single-region) and its sibling single-provider dependency, Board-accepted** |
| **Model provider specifically** | Assessed as a processing-integrity vulnerability as well as a security one: model behaviour change, deprecation, availability, and data-use terms | Mitigated by entity ownership of prompts, logic and the regression suite, so substitution does not require rebuilding the product; a substitute must pass the accuracy floor or the verdict class is suspended |
| **Physical access by provider personnel** | Escorted by default; unescorted only on separate justification; server closet excluded from landlord access | SEC-PRO-013 §2 and §5 |
| **Insider risk within the provider** | Addressed through the screening attestation per named individual, least privilege, access logging, and the provider's own conduct obligations | Attestation is a hard access gate — the control that delayed one start by 9 days |

Vulnerabilities identified here feed the entity's own risk register rather than living in a separate list, so
a third-party vulnerability competes for remediation attention on the same scale as an internal one.

## 3. Inventory and risk tiering

**The entity inventories and tiers vendors and business partners by risk.** The inventory is maintained by the
Third-Party Risk Manager and is reconciled quarterly against **accounts payable and the identity provider**,
so a provider engaged without going through the process is discovered rather than invisible. Two such
providers were found this period by that reconciliation and brought into the process retrospectively; that is
recorded as a deficiency in the intake control, not as a success of the reconciliation alone.

**Tiering criteria** — a provider's tier is the highest that any single criterion warrants:

| Tier | Criteria | Diligence | Assessment cadence | Population |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Tier 1 — Critical** | Access to customer evidence, assessment results or production systems; or a service whose failure breaches an availability or processing-integrity commitment; or processing of personal data at scale | Full assessment: independent audit report reviewed with **CUECs and exceptions read, not just the opinion**; security questionnaire; architecture and data-flow review; DPA and requirements annex; named-individual screening; financial check; insurance certificates; exit plan; **right to audit** | **Annually, plus within 30 days of any material change; quarterly performance monitoring** | 3 — the cloud provider, the model provider, the managed SIEM provider |
| **Tier 2 — Significant** | Access to internal or confidential data, or to identity, or a service whose failure degrades operations materially | Assessment: audit report or equivalent evidence; questionnaire; DPA where personal data is involved; requirements annex; insurance; exit plan | Annually; semi-annual performance review | 4 — identity provider, payroll/HR provider, contracted security engineering firm, payments provider |
| **Tier 3 — Limited** | No access to confidential data or production systems; failure is inconvenient | Lightweight review: security posture attestation; standard terms; confidentiality clause | Every 2 years, or on change | 9 corporate tools and services |
| **Named individuals** (contractors and vendor personnel, 11) | Tiered by the access they hold, **not by their employer's tier** | Screening attestation; competence review with a right of rejection; confidentiality undertaking; scoped access with hard expiry | Quarterly access and status review | 11 |
| **Business partners** (auditors, assessors, resellers, integration partners) | Tiered by data access and by whether they represent the entity to customers | NDA; scope and purpose recorded; where they access confidential material, Tier 2 treatment | Annually | 4 |

Each inventory record holds: the service, tier, engagement owner, contract and DPA references, the
requirements annex, data categories and classifications accessed, systems and network connections, named
individuals, named sub-processors and processing locations, assessment history and due date, performance
results, open issues, insurance and indemnity position, notice period, and the **exit plan**.

## 4. Periodic assessment of threats — including fourth parties, financial failure, security, disruption and regulatory failure

**The entity periodically assesses threats arising from relationships with vendors and business partners (and
those entities' vendors and business partners), covering financial failure, security vulnerabilities,
operational disruption, and failure to meet business or regulatory requirements**, and the vulnerability of
its own objectives to those threats.

| Threat class | How it is assessed | Frequency | What was found this period |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Financial failure** | Financial statements or credit assessment for Tier 1 and 2; funding and ownership changes tracked; concentration of the entity's spend; for small providers, the risk that the entity is a material portion of their revenue | Annually and on any adverse signal | One Tier 3 provider showed distress signals; its data was minimized and an alternative identified before any disruption occurred |
| **Security vulnerabilities** | Independent audit reports read in full including **exceptions and CUECs**; questionnaires; penetration-test summaries; vulnerability-management evidence; public breach history; the vulnerability path analysis in §2 | Annually for Tier 1/2; on any reported incident | One provider's report contained an exception relevant to secure development → corrective action plan and **scope restriction to non-production** until closed |
| **Operational disruption** | The provider's own resilience: RTO/RPO, redundancy, its dependency on *its* providers; against the entity's RTO/RPO in BCM-STD-015 §1; substitution time estimated and the exit plan tested for feasibility | Annually and in the annual continuity exercise | Provider-originated disruption tabletop held 2026-01, producing the requirement that every annex name a notification contact and a **maximum notification time** |
| **Failure to meet business or regulatory requirements** | Compliance obligations mapped per provider (privacy, transfer mechanisms, sectoral requirements, sanctions); the provider's certifications' currency and scope checked — **scope, because a certificate covering a different service is not evidence**; regulatory actions against the provider tracked | Annually and on any legal change (feeds **R-047**) | One certificate found to cover a different product line than the service consumed → additional evidence requested and the finding recorded |
| **Fourth-party / sub-processor threats** | Named sub-processors of each Tier 1/2 provider are reviewed for the same four threat classes, at a depth proportionate to their role; flow-down of obligations verified in the provider's contract text rather than accepted as asserted | Annually, and before any new sub-processor is approved | One new sub-processor approved after a transfer-mechanism check; **R-006** carried in the register with a dedicated runbook |
| **Concentration and correlation** | Portfolio-level: do multiple providers share an underlying dependency? Would one event affect several? | Annually | The **cloud-provider concentration is confirmed as not diversifiable**, recorded as an accepted risk with the rebuild-from-IaC and restore-test mitigations rather than presented as managed away |
| **Insider and personnel threats within providers** | Screening attestations; competence reviews; access reviews; the provider's own conduct obligations | Quarterly for access; annually for the programme | 2 individuals rejected on competence; 1 start delayed pending screening |
| **AI-specific threats** | For the model provider: model deprecation and behaviour change, data-use terms, and whether entity or customer data could be used for training (prohibited contractually) | Annually and on any provider announcement | Terms re-verified at renewal; substitution path exercised in the annual exercise |

**Assessment results flow into the entity's own risk register** — **R-006** (provider/sub-processor breach)
and **R-024** (critical provider failure) are scored from these assessments — and into the Audit Committee's
quarterly reporting.

## 5. Assigned responsibility and accountability for managing vendors and changes to their services

**The entity assigns responsibility and accountability for the management of risks and changes to services
associated with vendors and business partners.**

| Role | Responsibility | Named accountability |
|---|---|---|
| **Engagement owner** (a named individual per provider, never a team mailbox) | Day-to-day relationship; service-level monitoring; raising issues; approving or refusing a scope change; **accountable for the outcome of the service to the entity's customers** | Named in the inventory record per provider; a hand-off notice is required on any change of owner so an engagement is never silently un-owned |
| **Third-Party Risk Manager** | The programme: intake, tiering, assessment, the register, the annexes, monitoring cadence, exit plans; **holds the record independently of the engagement owner** so the person who wants the service is not the person who certifies it; holds a **negative authority to refuse an engagement** | Third-Party Risk Manager, reporting to the General Counsel |
| **General Counsel / DPO** | Contract and DPA terms; transfer mechanisms; privacy commitments; the DPO independently assesses any provider processing personal data and holds a **launch veto** | General Counsel; DPO for privacy |
| **AI Security Lead** | Security requirements, the vulnerability path analysis, provider security incidents, and a **block on any security-relevant change** to a provider's integration | AI Security Lead |
| **Control owners** | Where a provider operates a control on the entity's behalf, the entity-side control owner remains the accountable owner in the control register and must obtain the evidence | Named per control in CTL-POL-012 §8 |
| **CFO** | Spend, financial-failure assessment, insurance and indemnity adequacy | CFO |
| **Audit Committee** | Oversight of the programme; reviews Tier 1 assessments, material issues and terminations quarterly | Committee chair |

**Changes to provider services are governed as changes, not as account management.** Every Tier 1/2 contract
obliges the provider to notify before a material change — scope, sub-processor, processing location,
architecture, security control, or ownership. On notification: the engagement owner logs it; the change is
assessed within **30 days** under RSK-PRO-009 §14's change-assessment procedure; the DPO assesses any privacy
dimension and may veto; the AI Security Lead may block a security-relevant change; the requirements annex and
inventory record are updated; and where the change reduces the provider's control coverage, the entity's own
control register is updated so a control does not silently become unowned. A provider change discovered
*after* the fact rather than notified is a service-level failure and is recorded as such — which is what
happened with the late incident notification that moved one provider to quarterly assessment.

## 6. Communication and resolution protocols

**The entity establishes communication and resolution protocols for service or product issues related to
vendors and business partners.** Protocols are agreed at contracting so that a live issue does not begin with
finding out who to call.

| Element | What is defined | Recorded where |
|---|---|---|
| **Named contacts on both sides** | Provider operational, security and executive contacts; entity engagement owner and escalation chain — **individuals with a phone route, not a support portal alone** | Requirements annex and the inventory record; verified at each annual assessment because contacts go stale |
| **Routine communication** | Cadence per tier: monthly service review for Tier 1, quarterly for Tier 2, annual for Tier 3; agenda includes service levels, open issues, planned changes and security posture | Annex |
| **Issue raising** | Any entity personnel may raise a provider issue through the engagement owner or directly to Third-Party Risk; provider issues are logged in the same issue register as internal ones so they are not tracked more leniently | Procedure |
| **Severity and response protocol** | Issue severities with the provider's committed response and resolution times, mirroring the entity's own incident severities so an event does not translate badly at the boundary | Service level schedule |
| **Security incident notification** | The provider's obligation to notify within a **maximum notification time**, to a named entity contact, with defined minimum content; the entity's obligation to engage the provider on its own incidents where relevant; **integration of the provider's alerts into the entity's paging system rather than a portal someone must check** | Annex; SEC-PRO-014 §3 |
| **Escalation ladder** | Engagement owner → Third-Party Risk Manager → General Counsel / accountable officer → executive-to-executive; with defined trigger points rather than discretion | Annex |
| **Resolution and closure** | Root cause required for significant issues; corrective action plan with dates; **closure verified by the entity, not asserted by the provider** | Issue register |
| **Dispute resolution** | Contractual mechanism, governing law, and the commercial levers available (service credits, scope restriction, withholding, termination) | Master agreement |
| **Customer-facing coordination** | Where a provider issue affects customers, the entity communicates to its own customers under its own name and timers (24 hours for Sev-1) — **the entity does not wait for the provider's public statement**, and does not pass the provider's language through unexamined | SEC-PRO-014 §3 |

**Exercised in practice this period:** the provider that notified an incident later than its annex required
was escalated to the General Counsel level, its supplier record annotated, its assessment cadence raised to
quarterly, and its annex amended to name a contact and a maximum time. The protocol worked in the sense that
the escalation happened; it also revealed that the original annex had not named a notification time at all,
which is recorded as a defect in the entity's own contracting standard.

## 7. Exception handling

**The entity establishes exception handling procedures for service or product issues related to vendors and
business partners.** An exception is any departure from the agreed requirements — a missed service level, a
requirement the provider cannot meet, an assessment finding, an unapproved change, or a request to operate
outside the recorded scope.

| Exception type | Handling |
|---|---|
| **A requirement a provider cannot meet at contracting** | Recorded before signature with the residual risk named, a compensating measure identified, and authorization at the tier appropriate to the risk — Tier 1 exceptions require the accountable officer, and any High+ residual requires the Board under RSK-PRO-009. Two this period: the refused on-site audit right (accepted with expanded evidence) and the key-management shortfall (**engagement refused**) |
| **A missed service level** | Logged against the provider; root cause requested; service credits or remedies applied; repeat misses raise the assessment cadence and can trigger scope restriction |
| **An assessment finding** | Corrective action plan with dates, tracked in the supplier record and in the entity's deficiency register; **interim compensating controls applied on the entity's side**, since the entity cannot make a provider move faster than it will. The secure-development exception led to a **non-production scope restriction** until closure |
| **A request to operate outside the recorded scope** | Refused by default; requires a contract and annex change with the same authorization as the original engagement — never granted informally by the engagement owner |
| **A temporary exception with a time limit** | Recorded with an expiry date and an owner; **it expires rather than lapsing quietly**, and expiry without closure escalates to the accountable officer |
| **An emergency engagement** (a provider needed faster than diligence allows — e.g. incident response support) | Permitted with the accountable officer's authorization and a **restricted, time-boxed scope**, followed by full diligence within 30 days; used once this period for specialist support, with diligence completed retrospectively and the record noting it |
| **An unapproved provider discovered in the reconciliation** | Brought into the process retrospectively, with the intake failure recorded as a deficiency; two instances this period |
| **A provider's own exception affecting the entity** (an exception in its audit report, or its own sub-processor issue) | Assessed for entity impact; the entity's control register updated if a relied-upon control is not operating; **a CUEC the entity is not meeting is a finding against the entity, not the provider** — this is checked explicitly at each Tier 1 assessment |

Exceptions are reported quarterly to the Audit Committee with their age, and an exception past its expiry is
reported as overdue in the same way as any overdue remediation.

## 8. Performance assessment, at a frequency warranted by risk

**The entity assesses the performance of vendors and business partners at a frequency commensurate with the
risk associated with each.** Performance assessment is distinct from the control assessment in §4 — one asks
whether the service is being delivered, the other whether the controls are operating.

| Tier | Performance assessment | Frequency | What is measured |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Tier 1** | Formal quarterly performance review plus a monthly service review | **Quarterly formal, monthly operational** | Availability against the service level; incident count, severity and **notification timeliness**; response and resolution times; change notification compliance; open corrective actions; evidence delivery on request; sub-processor changes; for the model provider, **accuracy contribution measured through the entity's own regression suite and expert-agreement sampling** |
| Tier 2 | Formal semi-annual review | Semi-annually | Service levels; incidents; responsiveness; corrective actions; certification currency |
| Tier 3 | Reviewed at renewal | Every 2 years or on change | Whether the service is still needed; any incidents; posture attestation |
| **Risk-adjusted uplift** | A provider with an open finding, a missed notification, a distress signal or a scope restriction moves to a **higher cadence irrespective of tier** | Immediately on the trigger | The specific issue plus the standard measures |
| Named individuals | Competence and conduct reviewed with the engagement owner; access reviewed | Quarterly | Performance, competence, access appropriateness, screening currency |

**Results this period:** 7 of 7 providers assessed on schedule; **one provider moved from annual to quarterly**
after the late incident notification; one Tier 1 provider met all service levels; one Tier 2 provider had a
secure-development exception with a corrective action plan and a **non-production scope restriction**; one
Tier 3 provider showed financial distress signals and had its data minimized with an alternative identified;
two named individuals were **rejected on competence** and one start delayed 9 days pending a screening
attestation. The model provider's contribution to assessment accuracy was measured through the entity's own
regression suite rather than accepted from the provider's claims — which is the only performance measure the
entity considers reliable for that engagement.

## 9. Addressing issues identified during assessments

**The entity implements procedures for addressing issues identified with vendor and business partner
relationships.** An assessment finding is not a filed observation; it enters the same deficiency register as
an internal finding, with the same 2-business-day capture rule, the same severity scale and the same
requirement that closure be **verified by someone other than the owner**.

| Step | Action | Timing |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Record | Finding logged in the supplier record **and** the entity's deficiency register, with severity | Within 2 business days |
| 2. Communicate | Raised formally with the provider's named contact and, for significant findings, at the escalation level warranted | Within 5 business days |
| 3. **Protect the entity's own position first** | Interim compensating controls applied on the entity's side — reduced scope, additional monitoring, data minimization, or restricting the provider to non-production — because the entity cannot make the provider move at the entity's pace | Immediately, before the provider's plan is agreed |
| 4. Corrective action plan | Obtained from the provider with dated milestones and a named provider owner; recorded in the supplier record | Within 30 days |
| 5. Track | Monitored at the (risk-uplifted) assessment cadence; overdue items escalate on the ladder | Continuous |
| 6. Escalate | Escalation ladder to executive-to-executive; commercial levers where progress stalls: service credits, withholding, **scope restriction**, cadence increase, or termination | On the trigger points in the annex |
| 7. **Verify closure** | The entity verifies closure with evidence — a provider's assertion of closure is not closure | Before the finding is closed |
| 8. Reassess the risk | The register entries (R-006, R-024) and the entity's own control register are updated; where a relied-upon provider control was not operating, the entity's assertion is corrected | At the next quarterly review, or immediately if material |
| 9. Feed back into contracting | Where the finding reveals a defect in the entity's own standard, the standard changes — as with the annex that had not named a notification time | On identification |

**Live examples:** the secure-development exception (corrective action plan + non-production scope
restriction, tracked to closure); the late notification (escalated, cadence raised, annex amended, recorded in
the supplier record and reported to the Audit Committee); the mis-scoped certificate (additional evidence
obtained, and the assessment checklist changed to check certificate **scope** rather than existence); the
distressed Tier 3 provider (data minimized, alternative identified); the two unapproved providers found in
reconciliation (brought into process, with the intake control recorded as deficient).

## 10. Terminating relationships, on predefined considerations

**The entity implements procedures for terminating vendor and business partner relationships based on
predefined considerations, including safe return of data and its removal from the provider's systems.**

**Predefined termination considerations — decided in advance so a termination decision is not improvised:**

| Consideration | Threshold |
|---|---|
| Material breach of security, confidentiality or privacy obligations | Immediate termination right; exercised at the entity's discretion after impact assessment |
| A security incident revealing systemic control failure | Grounds for termination; at minimum a scope restriction pending remediation |
| Repeated service-level failure | Defined miss counts in the schedule trigger a right to terminate |
| Failure to remediate an assessment finding within the agreed plan | Escalation, then termination right |
| A change the entity will not accept — an unapproved sub-processor, a new processing location without a lawful transfer mechanism, ownership change to an unacceptable party, or a change in data-use terms | Grounds for termination; the DPO's veto applies to the privacy dimension |
| Financial failure or insolvency | Immediate; the exit plan is activated on the distress signal, not on the insolvency |
| Sanctions or legal disqualification | Immediate |
| Loss of the business need, or consolidation | Ordinary notice per the contract |
| **The entity's inability to obtain the evidence it needs to support its own assertions** | Grounds for termination — an unverifiable provider cannot support an assurance service |

**Exit procedure**, with the data steps as hard requirements rather than best endeavours:

1. **Decision and authorization** — engagement owner recommends; the accountable officer authorizes; Tier 1
   terminations are reported to the Audit Committee.
2. **Activate the exit plan** held in the inventory record: the named alternative, the migration steps, the
   estimated substitution time, and — for Tier 1 — the tested feasibility of the plan.
3. **Transition** with an agreed plan and, where needed, a transition-services period; the entity's control
   register is updated so no control becomes unowned during the transition.
4. **Access removal** — all provider access, all named individuals, all credentials and API keys, and all
   physical access, removed **within 1 business day** of the effective date, with removal timestamps
   verified by internal audit against the supplier record.
5. **Safe return of data** — the entity's and customers' data returned in an agreed, usable format,
   integrity-verified on receipt, before deletion is requested.
6. **Deletion from the provider's systems** — **required in writing, with a defined deadline, covering backups
   and any sub-processors**, and evidenced by a certificate of deletion or equivalent written confirmation.
   The obligation and the deadline are in the contract at signature, because they cannot be negotiated
   afterwards. Where a provider's backup retention prevents immediate deletion, the retention period and its
   expiry date are recorded and tracked to expiry rather than accepted as complete.
7. **Sub-processor deletion** — confirmed through the provider, since the entity has no direct relationship;
   this is the step most likely to be skipped and is therefore a required checklist item.
8. **Asset recovery** — any entity devices or credentials held by provider individuals recovered per
   SEC-PRO-013 §6, with the provider confirming the return in writing.
9. **Records** — the contract, DPA, assessments, findings and the deletion evidence are retained per the
   entity's retention schedule (a legal and evidential need that survives the relationship).
10. **Post-exit review** — did the exit plan work? Was the substitution time as estimated? The answer updates
    the remaining providers' exit plans.

**This period:** no Tier 1 or Tier 2 termination occurred. Three named individuals' engagements ended — access
removed same-day, one entity laptop returned in 3 business days with the provider's written confirmation. One
Tier 3 provider was replaced (the key-management refusal), with data returned and deletion confirmed in
writing. The distressed Tier 3 provider's exit plan is activated to the point of having an alternative
identified and data minimized, without termination — recorded so that the state of readiness is visible.

## 11. Confidentiality commitments from providers, consistent with the entity's own

**The entity obtains confidentiality commitments from vendors and business partners who have access to
confidential information, consistent with the entity's own confidentiality commitments and requirements.**
This is a flow-down requirement: the entity's commitments to its customers set the floor, and no provider
agreement may fall below it.

| Element | Requirement | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Written confidentiality obligation | In every agreement where the provider may access Confidential information; **at least equivalent to the entity's commitments to its customers**, checked clause by clause against the customer template rather than accepted as "standard" | In place for all 7 providers and all 4 business partners |
| Definition of confidential information | Broad enough to cover customer evidence, assessment results, and the fact of an assessment's outcome | Verified at contracting |
| Permitted purpose | Use strictly for delivering the contracted service; **no use for product improvement, benchmarking, publication, or model training** | Explicit prohibition in every data-touching agreement |
| Onward disclosure | Only to approved sub-processors under equivalent written terms; **flow-down verified in the sub-processor contract text**, not accepted as asserted | Verified for each named sub-processor |
| Personnel obligations | Each named individual under a personal confidentiality undertaking, in addition to the corporate obligation | 11 of 11 held |
| Survival | Obligations survive termination, without a time limit for customer evidence | Verified in each agreement |
| Return and destruction | On termination or on request, per §10 | Contractual, with the deadline stated |
| Security requirements supporting confidentiality | Encryption, access control, logging, segregation of the entity's data from other clients' | In the requirements annex per provider |
| Breach notification | Within the maximum notification time, with defined content | In place; **one late notification recorded** and the annex amended |
| Remedies | Injunctive relief, indemnity, and insurance minima | In place for Tier 1/2 |
| Business partners (auditors, assessors) | NDA before any access; purpose and date range recorded; **the auditors' own confidentiality obligations checked** rather than assumed from their profession | 4 of 4 |

## 12. Assessing providers' compliance with confidentiality commitments

**On a periodic and as-needed basis, the entity assesses compliance by vendors and business partners with the
entity's confidentiality commitments and requirements.** Holding a commitment is not evidence of compliance,
so compliance is assessed separately from contracting.

| Assessment method | Applied to | Frequency | Result this period |
|---|---|---|---|
| Independent audit report review with **confidentiality-relevant controls, exceptions and CUECs read specifically** | Tier 1/2 | Annually, on report issue | Exceptions reviewed; the secure-development exception addressed as above |
| Requirements annex walkthrough — each confidentiality control confirmed as operating, with evidence | Tier 1/2 | Annually | Evidence obtained for all; one provider's evidence required a second request |

| Named-individual access and status review | 11 individuals | Quarterly | Scope confirmed or reduced; one reverted from unescorted to escorted after a scope change |

| Confidentiality incident review | All | On any incident | The late-notification case; no confidentiality breach identified |

| **The entity's own DLP and egress monitoring** | Provider integrations | Continuous | Anomalous egress alerting covers provider paths as well as internal ones |

**Corrective action is taken where compliance is not demonstrated** — the scope restriction, the cadence
increase, and the additional evidence requests above are all instances. Where the entity cannot obtain
sufficient evidence, the position is recorded as an unverified reliance in the control register rather than
credited; the rule that **an untested control cannot be credited** (RSK-PRO-009 §13) applies to provider
controls too.

## 13. Privacy commitments from providers, consistent with the entity's own

**The entity obtains privacy commitments from vendors and business partners who have access to personal
information, consistent with the entity's own privacy commitments and requirements.** The DPO — reporting to a
Board committee rather than to management, and holding a launch veto — assesses every engagement involving
personal data independently of the engagement owner.

| Element | Requirement | Status |
|---|---|---|
| **Data processing agreement** | Required before any personal data is shared; terms at least equivalent to the entity's obligations to its own data subjects and to its customers' DPAs | In place for all 5 providers processing personal data |
| Role clarity | Controller / processor / joint controller determined and stated in writing | Determined per engagement; recorded |
| Documented instructions | Processing only on the entity's documented instructions; the processing schedule states purposes, categories, data subjects and duration | In every DPA |
| **Purpose limitation and no secondary use** | No use for the provider's own purposes; **explicit prohibition on training models on personal data** | Explicit in all 5 |
| Security of processing | Technical and organizational measures specified, not left generic | Requirements annex per provider |
| **Sub-processor control** | Prior approval; equivalent obligations flowed down in writing; the provider remains liable | In place; verified in contract text |
| **Cross-border transfers** | A lawful transfer mechanism for each processing location, recorded per provider; **no new processing location without a DPO check** — the no-new-region-without-DPO gate | Verified; one new sub-processor's location checked before approval |
| Data subject rights assistance | Obligation to assist within a timeframe that lets the entity meet the statutory period (the internal target is 60% of it) | In every DPA |
| Breach notification | **Without undue delay and within the annex's maximum notification time**, so the entity can meet its own 72-hour obligation with the clock started at discovery | In place; the one late notification is recorded as a failure of this commitment |

| Liability and indemnity | Privacy-specific indemnity; insurance minima | Tier 1/2 |

## 14. Assessing providers' compliance with privacy commitments, and corrective action

**On a periodic and as-needed basis, the entity assesses compliance by vendors and business partners with the
entity's privacy commitments and requirements and takes corrective action as necessary.** The DPO owns this
assessment, which is deliberately separate from the security assessment because a provider can be secure and
still non-compliant.

| Assessment activity | Frequency | Method | Result this period |
|---|---|---|---|
| **DPA and processing-schedule currency review** — does the recorded processing match what actually happens? | Annually and on any change | Comparison of the schedule to the data map and to the actual integration | One schedule updated where a data category had drifted from the record |
| Sub-processor list verification and transfer-mechanism check | Annually and on notification | The provider's current list against the approved list; mechanism per location | One new sub-processor approved after the check; **no unapproved sub-processor found** |
| **Deletion and retention conformance, including at sub-processors** | Quarterly retention conformance check | Deletion instructions issued and **written evidence obtained**, not assumed | Findings on data held past its period → deleted, deletion evidenced including at sub-processors, and enforcement tightened (**R-045**) |
| Data subject rights assistance test | Annually | A live or simulated request routed through the provider, measuring its response time against the DPA | Providers met the timeframe; all entity DSRs completed within the statutory period |
| Breach-notification obligation test | Annually, plus after any real event | The provider-originated incident tabletop; and review of any actual notification against the annex | **The late notification is recorded as non-compliance with the commitment** — escalated, cadence raised to quarterly, annex amended to name a contact and a maximum time |
| Independent audit report review for privacy-relevant controls and CUECs | Annually | Report read in full | Reviewed; the CUECs the entity must meet were checked against the entity's own controls |
| Cross-border transfer review | Annually and on any legal change (**R-047**) | Obligation inventory per jurisdiction, across two legal entities and five geographies | Mechanisms current; one review triggered by a legal change and completed |
| DPIA review where the provider's processing changed | On change | DPO assessment, with a **launch veto** available | Exercised as a veto once in the period on an unrelated internal launch; no provider change required a veto |
| Personnel screening and training attestations | Annually, and per individual at onboarding | Attestation held per named individual as a **hard access gate** | 11 of 11 held; one start delayed 9 days pending the attestation |
| **Corrective action tracking** | Continuous | Findings in the entity's deficiency register with owners, dates and independent closure verification | The late-notification corrective actions, the schedule update, and the deletion-evidence findings are all tracked to closure; **the deletion enforcement item remains open with a named owner and date** |

**Corrective actions taken this period, in summary:** an assessment cadence raised to quarterly; an annex
amended to name a notification contact and maximum time; a scope restriction to non-production pending
remediation of a secure-development exception; two named individuals rejected on competence; one start
delayed pending screening; additional evidence obtained where a certificate's scope was wrong; a processing
schedule corrected; deletion evidence obtained including at a sub-processor; a Tier 3 provider's data
minimized on distress signals; an engagement refused outright on key management; and the entity's own
contracting standard and intake control corrected where the assessments revealed defects in them rather than
