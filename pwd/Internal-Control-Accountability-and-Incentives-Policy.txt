## 1. Mechanisms to communicate and hold individuals accountable, and to implement corrective action

Management and the Board **have established mechanisms to communicate and hold individuals accountable
for the performance of internal control responsibilities across the entity, and implement corrective
action as necessary.** Accountability starts from a named owner: every control in the control set has
exactly one accountable individual, recorded in the control register, and that assignment is what the
mechanisms below act on.

| Mechanism | What it holds people accountable for | Applies to | Cadence | Corrective action it can trigger |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Control register with a single named owner per control | Operating the control as designed | All control owners | Reviewed quarterly | Reassignment; escalation to the owner's manager |
| Internal control responsibilities written into the role profile, offer letter and performance review | The individual's specific control duties | All employees, contractors | Annual review; semi-annual rating | Rating impact; development plan |
| **Quarterly control-owner attestation** — each owner attests the control operated as designed and reports any failure or exception | Honest self-reporting of control performance | 24 control owners | Quarterly | A false attestation is a Serious conduct matter under GOV-STD-001 |
| Independent testing by the internal audit provider | Whether the control actually operated, irrespective of the attestation | All controls on a rotating plan | Semi-annual | Corrective action plan with an owner and a date |
| Corrective action plan (CAP) register | Closing identified control deficiencies | Deficiency owners | Reviewed monthly by management, quarterly by the Audit Committee | Escalation on overdue; individual accountability for repeated slippage |
| Management review of control performance | Aggregate effectiveness | Management team | Monthly | Resource reallocation; design change |
| Board / Audit Committee review | Management's own accountability | CEO and officers | Quarterly | Direct instruction to management; compensation consequence; §7 action |
| Conduct and ethics program | Adherence to the standards of conduct | All covered persons | Continuous | The GOV-STD-001 §6 sanctions framework |
| Vendor and provider accountability through contract | Control responsibilities allocated to third parties | Outsourced providers, vendor personnel | Per CC9 review cycle | Corrective action; scope restriction; removal; termination |

**Communication of the responsibilities** — accountability requires that people know what they are
accountable for. Control responsibilities are communicated on appointment (the control register entry
is acknowledged by the owner in writing), at onboarding, in the annual policy acknowledgement, in the
quarterly attestation cycle itself (which restates the control's design), and at the quarterly
all-hands where control performance is reported to the whole entity.

**Corrective action, in the current period** — the mechanisms produced consequences rather than
reports:

| Trigger | Corrective action implemented |
|---|---|
| Access review not completed by the due date in 2025-Q4 | Control reassigned to a second owner with the original owner retained as backup; a calendar-driven ticket now opens automatically; the miss was recorded in the owner's performance review |
| Internal audit found evidence retention was not being verified | CAP opened; verification step added and tested; closed and re-tested in the following cycle |
| Control-owner attestation reported an exception in change approval (two emergency changes lacked post-hoc review) | Both reviewed retrospectively; the pipeline now blocks the next deploy until a prior emergency change has a completed review |
| Repeated slippage on a CAP by the same owner | Escalated to the owner's manager; workload reallocated after the root cause proved to be capacity, not diligence — the corrective action addressed the cause, not the person |
| Vendor personnel control failure (GOV-STD-001 case D-2026-004) | Individual removed; supplier CAP; scope restriction retained |

## 2. Performance measures, incentives and rewards appropriate for responsibilities at all levels

Management and the Board **have established performance measures, incentives and other rewards
appropriate for responsibilities at all levels of the entity, reflecting appropriate dimensions of
performance and expected standards of conduct, and considering the achievement of both short-term and
longer-term objectives.**

| Level | Performance measures | Dimensions reflected | Short vs longer term | Incentive / reward form |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Board / committee members** | Meeting attendance and preparation; completion of the skills evaluation and director education; discharge of the oversight responsibilities in GOV-CHT-002 §1 | Oversight quality; independence; conduct | Longer term — no short-term financial measure by design | Fixed fees only. **No variable pay, no share options tied to short-term results**, so oversight is not exposed to the outcome being overseen |
| **CEO and officers** | Balanced scorecard: customer and revenue outcomes; **security and availability commitments met**; **processing-integrity metrics**; privacy and compliance outcomes; conduct; people outcomes | Financial, operational, control, conduct, people | Short-term annual bonus **plus** multi-year equity vesting | Annual variable pay with a control-and-conduct gate (§3); equity vesting over 4 years |
| **People managers** | Team delivery; team control-ownership performance; conduct evaluation of the team; competence development of the team | Delivery, control, conduct, people | Annual variable pay; promotion (longer term) | Variable pay; promotion; recognition |
| **Control owners** | Control operated as designed; attestation timeliness and accuracy; independent test results; CAP closure on time | Control performance and honesty | Annual | Explicit line in the performance review; recognition award; eligibility gate for promotion |
| **Engineers and analysts** | Delivery quality; change-failure rate; security and quality gate adherence; on-call performance; contribution to reliability and technical health | Delivery, quality, control | Annual variable pay; multi-year equity | Variable pay; equity; promotion; spot recognition |
| **Contractors** | Statement-of-work deliverables; adherence to the entity's standards and conduct | Delivery, conduct | Contract term | Renewal decision; rate review |
| **Outsourced providers** | Contractual SLAs; control obligations; incident notification timeliness | Service and control | Contract term | Renewal; scope expansion or restriction |

**Rewards that specifically recognize internal control behavior**, because measures without a matching
reward do not change behavior: a quarterly recognition award for the strongest control or reliability
contribution (4 awarded in the current period, one to an engineer who self-reported a control failure
that nobody had detected); a formal "raise-it" recognition for surfacing a control weakness or a
conduct concern; and a resilience-engineering award tied to the game-day program.

## 3. Incentives and rewards aligned with the fulfillment of internal control responsibilities

Management and the Board **align incentives and rewards with the fulfillment of internal control
responsibilities in the achievement of objectives.** Alignment is structural — gates and weightings —
rather than an expectation that people will balance the two themselves.

**Structural alignment mechanisms**

1. **Control-and-conduct gate on all variable pay.** No individual receives variable compensation if
   they hold a Does Not Meet conduct rating, an unremediated control failure attributable to their own
   omission, or overdue mandatory training. The gate is binary and applied before any performance
   calculation. Applied in the current period to 2 individuals (case D-2026-003, a member of
   management, forfeited variable pay in full; one engineer forfeited 25% for an unremediated control
   failure).
2. **Control measures carry explicit weight in the officer scorecard** — 30% of the CEO and officer
   scorecard is security, availability, processing integrity, privacy and compliance outcomes. The
   weighting is set by the Board, not by management.
3. **No incentive is tied to a compliance verdict, score or finding count.** This is the alignment
   decision that matters most for this entity: paying anyone on the outcome of an assessment would
   create a direct incentive to bias the product. Sales compensation is tied to bookings and
   retention, explicitly **not** to assessment outcomes, and the sales plan is reviewed by the General
   Counsel each year to confirm it.
4. **No incentive is tied to a low count of reported incidents, vulnerabilities or ethics reports.**
   Suppressing reports would be the cheapest way to hit such a target. Metrics used are
   time-to-detect, time-to-remediate and closure rate — measures that improve when reporting
   increases.
5. **Longer-term alignment through equity vesting** over four years, so decisions that trade
   durable control quality for a short-term result reduce the decision-maker's own outcome.
6. **Clawback.** Variable compensation is recoverable where a subsequent finding shows the result it
   rewarded was misstated or was achieved through a control breach. Board-approved provision; not
   invoked in the current period.
7. **Provider incentives aligned the same way**: supplier contracts tie renewal to control obligations
   and incident-notification timeliness, and no supplier is paid on assessment volume in a way that
   would reward speed over accuracy.

## 4. Evaluating incentives and rewards for ongoing relevance

Management and the Board **evaluate incentives and rewards for ongoing relevance.** The evaluation is
performed each December, before compensation decisions are made, by the CEO and Head of People with
the Board approving any change to officer or Board arrangements. It asks three questions: do the
measures still correspond to the entity's current objectives and commitments; is any measure now
gameable or driving an unintended behavior; and is the reward still competitive enough to retain the
competence the control environment depends on.

| Evaluation date | Finding | Change made |
|---|---|---|
| 2024-12 | Officer scorecard was almost entirely financial and operational; control outcomes were mentioned but unweighted | Introduced the explicit 30% control weighting |
| 2025-12 | The support incentive included a "tickets closed" measure, which was **driving premature closure** of customer issues, including two that concerned assessment accuracy | Measure removed and replaced with resolution quality and reopen rate |
| 2025-12 | A proposed sales accelerator would have paid on customers achieving a compliance milestone — a direct incentive to bias verdicts | **Rejected before implementation** by the General Counsel and the Board |
| 2025-12 | Vulnerability metric was expressed as open-finding count, which discouraged reporting | Replaced with time-to-remediate by severity and SLA attainment |
| 2026-02 | Security engineering compensation was below market, creating retention risk in a role the control environment depends on (linked to the competence finding in HR-POL-004 §4) | Off-cycle adjustment for two roles; benchmark cadence moved to semi-annual for control-critical roles |
| 2026-02 | Board fees confirmed as fixed-only; a proposal to grant options to the independent directors was declined to preserve objectivity | No change — decision recorded |

The 2025-12 cycle produced two rejections and two removals. An evaluation that never changes anything
would not be evidence that the incentives are relevant, only that nobody looked.

## 5. Evaluating and adjusting excessive pressures

Management and the Board **evaluate and adjust the pressures associated with the achievement of
objectives as they assign responsibilities, develop performance measures and evaluate performance.**
Excessive pressure is treated as a cause of control failure and of fraud risk, and it is measured
rather than sensed.

| How pressure is evaluated | Indicators used | Cadence |
|---|---|---|
| Pressure assessment at the point of assigning responsibilities | Is the scope achievable with the resource and time available? Is the deadline externally fixed? Does one person hold too many control duties? | At every assignment and reorganization |
| Pressure review during the performance cycle | Were targets set at a level that required shortcuts? Did anyone raise a concern about a target? | Semi-annual |
| Operational pressure indicators | On-call page volume and out-of-hours pages; change-failure rate; overtime; unplanned-work ratio; PTO taken | Monthly |
| Workforce sentiment | Anonymous survey with specific items on pressure to cut corners and on speaking up | Semi-annual |
| Ethics channel signal | Any report citing pressure, deadlines or a request to bypass a control | Continuous |
| Fraud-risk linkage | Pressure is an explicit input to the fraud risk assessment (CC3) | Annual |

**Adjustments actually made in the current period** — the response is to change the pressure, not to
counsel the person feeling it:

| Pressure identified | Adjustment |
|---|---|
| A customer-committed launch date was driving pressure to skip the pre-launch security review | **The date was moved.** Management informed the customer rather than compress the review. Recorded in the Board minutes as a tone-at-the-top demonstration |
| One engineer held 7 control ownerships plus on-call | Redistributed to 4; a second owner added for two controls |
| Out-of-hours page volume concentrated on two engineers | Roster expanded to 12 certified on-call engineers; noisy alerts fixed rather than tolerated |
| Quarter-end commercial pressure coincided with the release freeze, creating pressure to use the emergency change path | Release calendar decoupled from quarter-end; emergency path now blocks the next deploy until reviewed (§1) |
| Survey showed 18% did not feel able to raise a concern about a deadline | Managers briefed; the "raise-it" recognition introduced; item re-measured at 6% in the following survey |
| Compliance function under pressure during the examination window | Temporary analyst engaged; the internal audit provider's scope extended |

Two of these adjustments cost the entity something — a moved launch date and additional headcount.
Pressure management that is free is usually not real.

## 6. Evaluating performance of internal control responsibilities, adherence to conduct standards and competence

Management and the Board **evaluate the performance of internal control responsibilities, including
adherence to the standards of conduct and expected levels of competence.** The three dimensions are
evaluated **separately and are not averaged together**, so that strong delivery cannot conceal a
control or conduct failure.

| Dimension | Evaluated by | Instrument | Cadence | Result form |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Internal control responsibilities** | Manager, informed by the control register, the quarterly attestations, independent test results and CAP closure | Control-performance section of the review | Semi-annual | Meets / Does Not Meet, with the specific control cited |
| **Adherence to standards of conduct** | Manager, calibrated by skip-level; the Conduct Panel where a case exists | Conduct rating (GOV-STD-001 §4) | Semi-annual | Meets / Does Not Meet |
| **Expected levels of competence** | Manager, against the role profile | Competency assessment (HR-POL-004 §4) | Semi-annual | Rating per competency |
| All three for **officers** | Board / Audit Committee | Officer evaluation against the scorecard, including the control and conduct components | Annual, plus quarterly control review | Board-recorded evaluation |
| All three for **the CEO** | Board, in camera | CEO evaluation | Annual | Board minute |
| All three for **directors** | Chair; Chair evaluated by the independent directors | Board effectiveness and skills review (GOV-CHT-002 §3) | Annual | Recorded evaluation with actions |
| Equivalent for **contractors and vendor personnel** | Engagement owner | Quarterly continue/remediate/remove decision | Quarterly | Recorded decision |
| Equivalent for **outsourced providers** | Third-Party Risk | Annual provider evaluation covering control obligations and conduct | Annual | Supplier record conclusion |

Managers cannot mark their own team's control dimension without input: the control-performance section
is pre-populated from the control register, the attestation record and the independent test results, so
the evaluation is anchored to evidence rather than to impression. Officers and directors are evaluated
too — an accountability process that stops below the management line is the one auditors find fails.

## 7. Rewards and disciplinary action based on that evaluation

Management and the Board **provide rewards or exercise disciplinary action based on the evaluation of
performance of internal control responsibilities.** The link from evaluation to consequence is defined,
so the outcome does not depend on the manager's discretion.

| Evaluation outcome | Consequence — reward | Consequence — disciplinary | Decided by |
|---|---|---|---|
| Meets on all three dimensions, strong control performance | Full variable pay eligibility; promotion eligibility; recognition award; equity refresh | — | Manager, calibrated |
| Exceptional control contribution (self-reported failure, prevented incident, materially improved a control) | Recognition award; cited in the promotion case; spot award | — | Manager + control-owner community |
| Does Not Meet on **control responsibilities** | Ineligible for variable pay (the §3 gate); promotion blocked for the cycle | Documented improvement plan with a control-specific objective; control may be reassigned | Manager + HR |
| Does Not Meet on **conduct** | Ineligible for variable pay; promotion blocked | GOV-STD-001 §6 sanctions framework, applied by the Conduct Panel | Conduct Panel |
| Does Not Meet on **competence** | Promotion blocked | Development plan; role change or exit if unresolved (HR-POL-004 §5) | Manager + Head of People |
| Deliberate control circumvention, falsified attestation, or falsified record | None | Termination; access revoked immediately; customer notification if results were affected | Audit Committee |
| Officer or director failing the control or conduct dimension | None | Variable pay forfeiture; formal warning; removal from the role | Board / Audit Committee |
| Contractor or vendor individual | Renewal; rate review | Removal from the engagement; supplier CAP; supplier termination | Engagement owner + Procurement |

**Applied in the current period:** 4 recognition awards; 2 individuals gated out of variable pay in
full or in part; 1 member of management forfeited variable pay and received a final written warning
(D-2026-003); 1 probation failure ended an engagement; 2 elevated roles removed for lapsed screening;
1 vendor individual removed; 5 promotions granted, each with the control dimension confirmed as Meets.
Outcomes are analyzed semi-annually for consistency by role, seniority, tenure and employment type.

## 8. Sanctions for privacy policy violations and privacy incidents caused by negligence

The entity **has defined a sanctions process that is applied when an employee violates the entity's
privacy policies, or when an employee's negligent behavior causes a privacy incident.** The process is
part of the conduct framework but is set out explicitly here because privacy violations are handled on
a defined scale regardless of whether harm resulted.

**Scope.** Applies to employees, interns, contractors and vendor personnel. For contractors and vendor
personnel the sanction is executed through the supplier contract (removal from the engagement, supplier
corrective action, or termination of the relationship).

**Triggering events.** Accessing personal data without a business need; using personal data for a
purpose outside the notice or the contract; disclosing personal or confidential data without
authorization; moving personal data outside approved systems or regions; failing to honor a
data-subject request within the statutory deadline; retaining personal data past its retention period;
bypassing a required DPIA; failing to report a suspected privacy incident promptly; and negligent
behavior — misconfiguration, misdirected communication, lost device, weak handling — that causes or
could have caused a privacy incident.

**Process.** (1) The DPO is notified and is a mandatory participant. (2) The privacy incident process
runs in parallel and takes precedence — containment, assessment and any statutory notification are
never delayed by the disciplinary process. (3) The DPO and HR investigate jointly; the individual is
heard. (4) The Conduct Panel determines the sanction, with the DPO advising on severity. (5) Cases
involving a member of management, or any case requiring regulatory notification, go to the Audit
Committee. (6) The outcome is recorded in the conduct register and reported to the Board.

| Severity | Example | Sanction range |
|---|---|---|
| Minor | A single negligent act with no exposure — e.g. personal data pasted into an internal channel with restricted membership, self-reported immediately | Documented coaching; mandatory privacy re-training; access review |
| Moderate | Repeated negligence, or a negligent act creating real exposure — e.g. a misdirected email containing personal data | Written warning; conduct rating Does Not Meet; variable pay impact; re-training; access restriction |
| Serious | Accessing personal data without a business need; negligence causing a reportable privacy incident; failure to report a suspected incident | Final written warning to termination; access revoked; role change; notification to the affected customer where required |
| Severe | Deliberate unauthorized disclosure; using personal data for personal benefit; concealing a privacy incident; obstructing a data-subject right | **Termination**; referral to authorities where legally required; contract termination for a supplier |

**Aggravating factors:** concealment, repetition, seniority, obstruction of the investigation, and
whether the individual had completed the relevant training. **Mitigating factors:** prompt
self-reporting, cooperation, and whether a control the entity should have provided was absent — where
the root cause is a missing control, the corrective action is applied to the control and the individual
sanction is reduced accordingly. Sanctioning an individual for a systemic failure would suppress
reporting and leave the actual cause in place.

**Current period:** one privacy-related case (GOV-STD-001 case D-2026-004 — a vendor employee retained
sample data containing personal data on a personal device). Assessed Serious; the individual was
removed from the engagement; destruction was attested; the supplier completed corrective action; the
DPO assessed notification obligations and concluded none were triggered; and the root-cause control gap
(the sample-data handling standard did not technically prevent local copies) was closed with a CAP. No
employee privacy sanctions were required.
