# Control Monitoring, Evaluation and Deficiency Management Procedure

**Document ID:** MON-PRO-011
**Version:** 3.2
**Owner:** Compliance Manager, reporting to the CEO for operation and to the **Audit Committee** for the
evaluation results — the Committee, not management, receives the conclusion on whether the control
components are present and functioning
**Approved by:** Audit Committee 2026-02-19
**Next scheduled review:** 2026-12-31 (Annually, and on any change to the control set, the risk profile or the rate)
of change in the business
**Scope:** All components of internal control — control environment, risk assessment, control activities,
information and communication, and monitoring — across the entity, both legal entities, all eight
operating units, the seven outsourced providers, and the compliance-evaluation service in AWS account
471885797689, us-west-2.

## 1. A mix of ongoing and separate evaluations

**The entity selects and develops a mix of ongoing and separate evaluations to ascertain whether the
components of internal control are present and functioning.** The mix is deliberate: ongoing evaluations
give continuous coverage but are performed by the people who operate the controls, and separate
evaluations give objectivity but only at points in time. Neither alone is sufficient, so every control
component is covered by at least one of each.

| Internal control component | **Ongoing** evaluation covering it | **Separate** evaluation covering it |
|---|---|---|
| **Control environment** (CC1) | Quarterly control-owner attestation; the culture survey; conduct-case and detection-latency tracking; management's own control decisions reported quarterly | Annual internal audit of the control environment; the Audit Committee's in-camera sessions with the assurance functions; the annual Board and committee self-evaluation; the annual tone assessment for any new officer |
| **Risk assessment** (CC3) | Quarterly risk register review; continuous threat intelligence, vulnerability and configuration scanning; the mandatory quarterly challenge question | Annual entity risk assessment workshop challenged by the Board; internal audit review of the risk process; annual fraud risk assessment presented to the Audit Committee |
| **Control activities** (CC5–CC8, A1, C1, PI1, P-series) | Automated configuration and compliance scanning of the account; control-owner self-testing at the control's own frequency; exception and alert monitoring; access review; change-record review; backup and restore verification | Internal audit control testing on a risk-based plan; the SOC 2 examination; ISO/IEC 42001 certification and surveillance audits; quarterly penetration testing; annual DPIA reviews; provider assessments |
| **Information and communication** (CC2) | Acknowledgement and completion tracking on every required communication; the semi-annual hotline fail-safe test; complaint and inbound-channel volume review | Internal audit of the communication process; the culture survey's questions on whether people know how to report; the auditor's assessment of the system description |
| **Monitoring** (CC4 — this procedure itself) | Deficiency register ageing; CAP overdue tracking; evaluation-coverage tracking against the plan | **Internal audit's review of the monitoring function**, reported to the Audit Committee without management present; the external auditor's assessment of monitoring |

**Balance is measured, not assumed.** The annual monitoring plan records, per component, how much
assurance comes from ongoing versus separate evaluation, and the Audit Committee approves the balance.
The plan explicitly rejects two failure modes: relying on self-attestation alone for a component (which
is why every component has a separate evaluation), and relying on an annual audit alone for a fast-moving
component (which is why change-heavy areas carry continuous ongoing evaluation, per §2).

## 2. Considering the rate of change

**The entity addresses the rate of change in business and business processes when selecting and
developing ongoing and separate evaluations.** Evaluation frequency is set from the rate of change of the
thing being evaluated, not from a uniform calendar — a control over a process that changes weekly cannot
be assured by an annual test.

| Area | Observed rate of change | Evaluation design that follows from it |
|---|---|---|
| **Assessment logic, prompts and models** | Highest in the entity — changes multiple times per month, and the underlying models change outside the entity's control | **Evaluation is per change, not periodic**: the accuracy regression suite and adversarial evaluation run as a release gate on every change; drift monitored continuously; quarterly expert-agreement sampling as the separate evaluation |
| Infrastructure configuration | Continuous, via pipeline deploys and provider-side change | Continuous configuration and compliance scanning; drift detection; quarterly penetration test as the separate evaluation |
| Application code | Weekly releases | Automated checks on every commit and merge; change-record review monthly; annual internal audit of the change process |
| Access and identity | High — joiners, leavers, role changes, 11 vendor individuals | Event-driven checks at provisioning and removal; **quarterly** access review rather than annual; internal audit tests removals against HR records |
| Third-party relationships | Moderate — 7 providers, sub-processor changes | Continuous monitoring of provider performance against the requirements annex; annual provider assessment; reassessment within 30 days of any change |
| Legal and regulatory obligations | Moderate but consequential | Quarterly obligation-inventory review; assessment within 30 days of an identified change; DPIA per new processing |
| Financial processes | Monthly close cycle, stable process | Monthly close controls with review; annual external audit |
| Control environment and governance | Low rate of change | Annual evaluation, with event-driven reassessment on a leadership change |
| **Business model and growth** | Currently inside the growth tolerance band; growth above 60% is itself a trigger | A control-capacity review is triggered by growth above tolerance; new business lines require a control-impact assessment before Board approval |

The rate of change is re-examined when the monitoring plan is set each year, and mid-year if a change
trigger in RSK-PRO-009 §14 fires. In the current period the plan increased access-review frequency from
semi-annual to quarterly (driven by vendor-personnel churn) and moved model evaluation from a monthly
cadence to a per-change gate.

## 3. Baseline understanding of the control system's design and current state

**The entity uses the design and current state of the internal control system to establish a baseline for
ongoing and separate evaluations.** Evaluation is performed against a recorded baseline, so that
"functioning" means functioning as designed rather than as remembered.

| Baseline artefact | What it records | Maintained by | Refreshed |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Control register** | Every control: its identifier, the criteria and sub-objectives it addresses, its design, its owner, its frequency, the evidence it produces, the system or process it operates in, and whether it is automated or manual | Compliance Manager | On any control change, same day; fully reviewed annually |
| **System description** | The service's purpose, the five-node pipeline, components, data flows, the trust boundary and what is outside it, and the shared-responsibility split | VP Engineering | Quarterly and on architectural change |
| **Risk register with control mapping** | Each risk, its inherent and residual score, and the specific controls credited with the reduction | Compliance Manager | Quarterly |
| **Criteria-to-control mapping** | Every trust services criterion and ISO/IEC 42001 clause mapped to the control(s) that address it, with gaps stated as gaps | Compliance Manager | On framework or control change |
| **Current-state assessment** | For each control: last tested, result, open deficiencies, compensating controls, and whether the control has *ever* been tested | Compliance Manager | Continuously; reported quarterly |
| **Configuration baseline** | The expected configuration of the account and its resources, expressed as code and as compliance rules | VP Engineering + AI Security Lead | On change; drift detected continuously |
| **Accuracy baseline** | The regression suite and the expert-agreement result that a change must not degrade | Chief AI Officer | Per release; the suite itself reviewed quarterly |
| **Evidence inventory** | Which documents and system evidence support which control, and their currency | Compliance Manager | Continuously |

Two rules keep the baseline honest. First, **the baseline records current state including its known
defects** — the open findings on administrative access, MFA, log immutability, network ingress and the
`Demo` table configuration are part of the recorded current state, not omitted from it, so an evaluation
cannot report a clean result by comparing against an idealized design. Second, **a control that has never
been tested is flagged as such** and cannot be credited with reducing a residual risk (RSK-PRO-009 §13);
this rule identified two over-credited controls in 2026-Q1.

## 4. Evaluators with sufficient knowledge

**The entity ensures that evaluators performing ongoing and separate evaluations have sufficient
knowledge to understand what is being evaluated.** Competence is determined before the evaluation, per
evaluator type, and is evidenced.

| Evaluator | Evaluations performed | Knowledge required | How sufficiency is established |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Control owners** (24 named) | Ongoing self-testing and the quarterly attestation | The control's design, its evidence, and what failure looks like | The control assignment notice with written acknowledgement (GOV-PRO-006 §5); framework and criteria training (14 of 14 complete); the attestation itself asks whether they have the information they need, and a "no" raises an action |
| **Compliance Manager and compliance analyst** | Second-line testing, coverage and deficiency management | The trust services criteria, ISO/IEC 42001, evidence sufficiency, control testing methodology | Role profile in HR-POL-004 §1; annual criteria training refreshed on framework change; professional certification funded and maintained |
| **AI Security Lead and security engineers** | Vulnerability scanning, configuration review, access review, detection coverage review | Threat modelling, IAM, cryptography, detection engineering, the AWS control surface | Role profile; security-competency scorecard; annual competency test with a pass threshold; the detection-depth gap in HR-POL-004 §5 was closed by training a second engineer specifically so evaluation did not depend on one person |
| **Chief AI Officer and Responsible AI Lead** | Model, prompt and accuracy evaluation | Model evaluation methodology, statistical reasoning about output quality, the frameworks being assessed | Role profile; responsible-AI training (31 of 31); the independent AI assurance advisor provides external challenge |
| **DPO and General Counsel** | DPIAs, privacy control evaluation, obligation review | Applicable privacy law, DPIA methodology | Role profile; retained external privacy counsel supplements depth; the identified privacy-law gap is covered by the retainer while the senior counsel hire completes |
| **Outsourced internal audit provider** | Separate control testing on the approved plan | Control testing, the criteria, IT general controls, cloud environments | Engagement letter states required qualifications; competence evaluated annually as an outsourced provider (HR-POL-004 §4); reports to the Audit Committee |
| **External service auditor** | The SOC 2 examination | Attestation standards, the trust services criteria | Firm selected and retained by the **Audit Committee**, not management; independence confirmed annually |
| **Certification body** | ISO/IEC 42001 audits | The standard; accreditation | Accredited body |
| **External penetration testing firm** | Technical security testing | Offensive security for cloud and AI systems | Scope and tester qualifications agreed in the rules of engagement |
| **Audit Committee and Board** | Assessment of evaluation results (§8) | Enough to challenge, per the skills matrix | Skills matrix with **two identified gaps** (privacy law, AI assurance) and the commensurate action — retained privacy counsel and an independent AI assurance advisor, both reporting to committees rather than to management (GOV-CHT-002 §3) |

**Independence is part of sufficiency.** An evaluator does not evaluate their own work: control owners
self-test, but their controls are also tested by Compliance (second line) and by internal audit (third
line); Compliance's own function is evaluated by internal audit; and internal audit's work and the
monitoring function itself are reviewed with the Audit Committee without management present.

## 5. Ongoing evaluations built into business processes and adjusted to changing conditions

**The entity builds ongoing evaluations into its business processes and adjusts them to changing
conditions.** These are not separate exercises added on top of the process; the evaluation is a step of
the process, which is why it happens every time the process runs.

| Business process | Evaluation built into it | Where it sits in the process | Adjusts to changing conditions how |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Deploy / release** | Automated static analysis, dependency and secret scanning, the test suite, and — for anything touching evaluation logic — the **accuracy regression gate and adversarial evaluation**. A failing check blocks the deploy | A gate in the pipeline; deploy is impossible without it | New checks are added when a new failure mode is found; the regression suite grows with every accuracy defect; two checks were added this period from penetration test findings |
| **Change approval** | Independent review, self-approval blocked, security review for security-relevant change, CAB for significant change | Before merge and before deploy | The significance threshold is re-tuned quarterly from change-failure data; emergency change gained a **blocking** post-hoc review after a gap was found |
| **Access provisioning** | Screening attestation, competence verification and training completion checked **at the point of provisioning** — a hard gate, not a reminder | Provisioning cannot complete without them | Extended to vendor personnel individually as the workforce model changed; frequency of the review cycle raised to quarterly |
| **Access removal** | Departure and engagement-end triggers checked against actual revocation within 1 business day | In the offboarding workflow | Vendor-personnel departures added as a trigger after the workforce model changed |
| **Infrastructure operation** | Continuous configuration compliance scanning of the account, drift detection against the IaC baseline, and the entity's **own** compliance-evaluation service run against its own environment | Continuous, with findings raised as tickets automatically | New rules added as new resource types are adopted; the current open findings are outputs of this mechanism |
| **Monitoring and alerting** | Alert-to-human timing measured (MTTD); required-event coverage reviewed; alarm inventory reconciled to the required list | Continuous | Coverage list updated when a new component or event type appears; a missing-telemetry finding (recorder exclusions, SecurityHub) was raised by this mechanism |
| **Incident response** | Post-incident review mandatory for every Sev-1 and near miss, and the review **must state whether the risk register was wrong** | After every incident | Findings change controls; two control changes this period |
| **Assessment operation** (the product) | Completeness check that every scorable requirement received a conclusion; `unable to assess` surfaced rather than inferred; anomalous verdict-shift alerting; quality metrics on every run | Inside the pipeline, per assessment | Thresholds re-tuned from the quarterly expert-agreement sample |
| **Evidence handling** | Classification on receipt; retention conformance checked quarterly; upload-time prompts when personal data is detected | At receipt and continuously | The technical no-local-copy control was added after case D-2026-004 |
| **Backup** | Backup success monitored; **restore tested quarterly to a clean environment** — an untested backup path is zero-tolerance | Continuous monitoring; quarterly test | Test scope extended when a new data store is added |
| **Third-party operation** | Provider performance monitored against the requirements annex; named-individual attestations tracked; incident-notification timeliness recorded | Continuous, per provider | A late notification is recorded in the supplier record and changes the next assessment's scope |
| **Financial close** | Reconciliations, variance analysis against the accrual model, approval limits enforced in the system | Every close | Thresholds reviewed annually with materiality |
| **Control ownership** | **Quarterly control-owner attestation** — a personal written statement per control, not a checkbox | Quarterly, per owner | Attestation questions are revised when a failure mode shows the previous question was too easy to answer affirmatively |
| **Communication** | Acknowledgement tracking on required communications; the semi-annual hotline fail-safe test | Per communication; semi-annually | The acknowledgement requirement on control assignment became blocking after 3 owners were found unacknowledged |

## 6. Varying the scope and frequency of separate evaluations by risk

**The entity varies the scope and frequency of separate evaluations depending on risk.** The annual
monitoring plan is built from the risk register: scope and frequency are derived from residual risk,
criticality, rate of change, and prior findings, and the derivation is recorded so the plan can be
challenged.

| Risk / area | Residual risk and criticality | Separate-evaluation frequency and scope | Why this frequency |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Assessment accuracy and integrity** (R-001, R-038; PI-1…PI-5) | High residual; Critical component | Expert-agreement sampling **quarterly**, stratified with full coverage of high-impact verdicts; adversarial/red-team evaluation of the model and prompt layer **quarterly**; internal audit of the change-to-result chain **annually**; the independent AI assurance advisor **semi-annually** | Highest residual risk, highest criticality, and the highest rate of change |
| **Logical access and identity** (R-005, R-009; S-1, S-2, S-7) | High residual, with **open findings** on administrative access and MFA | Internal audit **semi-annually** rather than annually while the findings are open; penetration testing quarterly includes the identity path; **external review of the identity path** added specifically because R-041 is an accepted limitation | Elevated by open findings and by an accepted segregation limitation — an accepted risk gets more evaluation, not less |
| Network and boundary (S-3) | High residual; open findings on ingress and boundary protection | Quarterly external penetration test plus continuous scanning; internal audit annually | Internet-facing, continuously probed |
| Logging, monitoring and detection (R-039; S-5) | Medium residual; **open findings** on recorder coverage, SecurityHub and log immutability | Detection coverage review **quarterly**; internal audit annually; a purple-team detection validation exercise added this period | Detection failure is invisible by nature, so it needs deliberate testing |
| Confidentiality and tenant isolation (R-012; C-1…C-4) | Medium residual; Critical asset | **Tenant isolation tested every quarter** as part of penetration testing, with an explicit isolation objective in the rules of engagement; internal audit of classification and retention annually | The magnitude is severe even though likelihood is reduced |
| Availability, backup and recovery (R-022, R-030; A-1…A-4) | Medium residual, one Board-accepted risk | Restore test **quarterly**; recovery exercise quarterly; capacity review monthly; internal audit annually | The accepted single-region risk is conditional on the restore test actually passing |
| Privacy (P-1…P-7) | Low–Medium residual; high regulatory magnitude | DPIA per new or changed processing; annual privacy control audit; external counsel review of the notice and transfer mechanism annually; DSR timeliness sampled quarterly | Statutory deadlines have zero tolerance |
| Change management (R-015) | Low residual | Internal audit annually; change-record sampling monthly; every emergency change reviewed individually | Strong preventive controls reduce the need for frequent separate testing — the reduction is justified, not assumed |
| Third parties (R-006) | Medium residual | Provider assessment **annually per provider**, plus reassessment within 30 days of any change; the supplier with the secure-coding corrective action is reassessed **quarterly until closed** and remains scope-restricted | Frequency raised for a specific provider on evidence, then reduced when closed |
| Control environment, governance, fraud | Low residual, but severe if wrong | Internal audit of the control environment annually; fraud risk assessment annually to the Audit Committee; Board and committee self-evaluation annually; culture survey annually | Low rate of change, but a leadership change triggers immediate reassessment |
| Financial reporting | Low–Medium | External audit annually; internal review at each close; Audit Committee review of significant judgments quarterly | Statutory cycle plus monthly process |
| **Areas with no prior finding** | Varies | A sample is included each year regardless of score, and rotates | Deliberate defence against the plan only ever looking where problems were already found |

Adjustments made to the plan in the current period, each with a recorded reason: access-path evaluation
increased to semi-annual (open findings); one supplier moved to quarterly (corrective action);
detection validation added (R-039); change-management sampling reduced from weekly to monthly (verified
control effectiveness) — recorded so that a *reduction* in evaluation is as visible as an increase.

## 7. Periodic separate evaluations providing objective feedback

**The entity periodically performs separate evaluations to provide objective feedback on whether internal
controls are present and functioning.** Objectivity comes from who performs the evaluation and to whom
they report, not from the technique.

| Separate evaluation | Performed by | Independence basis | Frequency | Reports to |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Internal audit control testing** | Outsourced internal audit provider | Not part of management; engaged by and reporting to the Audit Committee; the audit plan is approved by the Committee, not by management | Quarterly engagements on an annual risk-based plan | **Audit Committee**, presented directly, with an in-camera portion |
| **SOC 2 examination** | Independent service auditor | Selected, retained and compensated by the Audit Committee; independence confirmed annually | Annual | Audit Committee then the Board; auditor presents directly, in camera for part |
| **ISO/IEC 42001 certification and surveillance audit** | Accredited certification body | External and accredited | Certification plus annual surveillance | AI Governance Oversight Committee and the Board, **with all findings including minor nonconformities** |
| **Penetration test and red team** | External security testing firm | External; scope and rules agreed in advance, including a critical-finding escalation path mid-test | Quarterly | Security & Privacy Committee, full report plus remediation status |
| **Adversarial / red-team evaluation of the model and prompt layer** | External advisor with the internal team | External challenge on the highest-risk area | Quarterly | AI Governance Oversight Committee |
| **Expert-agreement accuracy sampling** | Expert reviewers independent of the engineers who built the logic | Reviewers do not build the thing they sample | Quarterly, stratified | AI Governance Oversight Committee |
| **Second-line control testing** | Compliance Manager and analyst | Independent of the control owners, though inside management — which is why it is not the only separate evaluation | Continuous on a rolling plan; every control tested at least annually | CEO and the Audit Committee |
| **Independent AI assurance advisory review** | Retained independent advisor | Reports to the committee, not to management | Semi-annual | AI Governance Oversight Committee |
| **External privacy counsel review** | Retained counsel | Reports to the committee | Annual, plus per novel processing | Security & Privacy Committee |
| **Provider assessments** | Third-Party Risk Manager, using the provider's own independent assurance reports | Uses external assurance rather than provider self-assertion | Annual per provider; more often on evidence | Board, semi-annually |
| **Customer security assessments of the entity** | Customers' own security teams | Fully external, with a different perspective and no relationship to manage | As requested | Board in aggregate; adverse findings immediately — the boundary-protection finding arrived this way |
| **Board and committee self-evaluation** | Board, facilitated externally | Evaluates the oversight function itself | Annual (February) | Board |
| **Culture survey** | Anonymous, administered by a third party | Anonymity is the independence mechanism; asks directly whether personnel believe management would accept an unfavourable finding | Annual | Audit Committee |
| **Review of the monitoring function itself** | Internal audit provider | The monitor is monitored, and reports without management present | Annual | Audit Committee |

**Feedback is objective in substance as well as source:** every separate evaluation reports all findings
including those management disputes, with both positions stated (GOV-PRO-007 §3); findings are not
pre-reconciled with management before the Committee sees them; and the current period's results —
4 internal audit findings, 2 high penetration test findings, 2 minor nonconformities, 1 adverse customer
finding, and 2 residual scores corrected upward because a credited control failed its test — were all
reported as raised.

## 8. Management and the Board assessing the results of evaluations

**Management and the Board of Directors, as appropriate, assess the results of ongoing and separate
evaluations of internal controls.** Assessment means reaching and recording a conclusion, not receiving a
report.

| Forum | What it assesses | Cadence | Conclusion recorded |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Control owner** | Their own control's results; whether it operated as designed every time it was due | Quarterly attestation | A personal written statement per control, with exceptions listed rather than implied |
| **Monthly management control review** | Ongoing evaluation output: scan findings, alert and exception volumes, access review results, change failures, incident metrics, deficiency ageing | Monthly | Minutes with actions, owners and dates |
| **Quarterly management review** | The aggregate: all evaluation results in the quarter, deficiency severity and trend, CAP status, and **an explicit conclusion on whether each control component is present and functioning** | Quarterly | The conclusion is minuted per component, with any component qualified and why |
| **Audit Committee** | Internal audit results; the SOC 2 examination; second-line testing; the deficiency register in full; the fraud assessment; the culture survey; the monitoring function's own review | Quarterly, and on any immediate escalation | The Committee records whether it accepts management's conclusion, and it has not always done so |
| **Security & Privacy Committee** | Penetration test results, security posture, access review outcomes, privacy assessments, DSR timeliness | Quarterly | Minuted conclusions and required remediation with deadlines |
| **AI Governance Oversight Committee** | Accuracy sampling, adversarial evaluation, model and prompt change results, the AI assurance advisor's review | Quarterly | Minuted; can require remediation before further releases |
| **Full Board** | The aggregate internal control report; material deficiencies; external assessment results; the risk report; and the entity's conclusion on internal control | Quarterly; full annual assessment | The Board's own conclusion, minuted, including any disagreement with management |
| **Management assertion process** | Whether the description is fairly presented and the controls were suitably designed and operating effectively throughout the period | Annually | The signed assertion, supported by the evidence file |

**Evidence that assessment is real rather than procedural.** In the current period: the Board re-scored
two risks upward against management's proposed scores; the Audit Committee sent one proposed risk
acceptance (vendor personnel access) back for further reduction rather than accepting it; the Committee
rejected management's initial conclusion that a control component was fully functioning while the
identity-path findings were open, and required the qualification to be stated; and the Committee added
an external review of the identity path that management had not proposed. A Board that only ratifies is
not assessing, so these outcomes are recorded deliberately.

## 9. Communicating deficiencies to the parties responsible for corrective action

**The entity communicates identified internal control deficiencies to the parties responsible for taking
corrective action.** Every deficiency is assigned to a named individual with the authority to fix it, and
the assignment is acknowledged.

| Step | Rule | Timing |
|---|---|---|
| **Capture** | Every deficiency — from any source: ongoing scanning, self-testing, attestation exceptions, internal audit, the external auditor, the certification body, penetration testing, incidents, near misses, customer or researcher input, and any report through the channels in GOV-PRO-006 §3 — is entered in the single **deficiency register** with its source | Within **2 business days** of identification |
| **Classify** | Severity assigned using the criteria in §10; a deficiency affecting a zero-tolerance objective is classified Significant or Material by default | With capture |
| **Assign** | To the individual who owns the control and has the authority to remediate — not to a team mailbox. Where remediation requires authority the owner lacks, the deficiency is assigned jointly with the officer who holds it | Within 2 business days |
| **Communicate** | Written notice to the assignee stating the deficiency, its severity, the required remediation, the due date, and the interim compensating control required in the meantime; **acknowledgement is required and is tracked** | Same day as assignment |
| **Notify others affected** | The control owner, the process owner, and — where the deficiency affects a customer commitment, personal data or a provider's control — Customer Operations, the DPO, or Third-Party Risk respectively | Within 2 business days |
| **Communicate to the provider** where the deficiency is in an outsourced control | Formal notice under the requirements annex, with a corrective action plan required and a deadline; scope restriction applied where warranted | Within 5 business days |
| Interim risk decision | If remediation cannot be immediate, a compensating control is required and recorded; **if none is possible, the residual risk is escalated for an explicit acceptance at the right level** — it is never left implicit | With assignment |

Communication is bidirectional: the assignee must confirm the remediation plan and flag if the due date
is not achievable **before** it passes, and a due date can be changed only by the approver who set the
severity, with the reason recorded. This exists because silently slipping dates is how a tracked
deficiency becomes an untracked one.

## 10. Communicating deficiencies to senior management and the Board

**The entity communicates identified internal control deficiencies to senior management and the Board of
Directors, as appropriate.** "As appropriate" is defined in advance by severity, so escalation is not a
judgment made by the person with an interest in not escalating.

| Severity | Definition | Communicated to | Timing |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Material weakness** | A deficiency, or combination, such that there is a reasonable possibility that a material misstatement or a failure to meet a stated commitment would not be prevented or detected on a timely basis; or any deficiency causing unauthorized disclosure of customer or personal data; or a materially wrong verdict issued at scale | **Board Chair and Audit Committee Chair immediately; full Board and CEO within 24 hours**; disclosed in the assertion and to customers where required | **Within 24 hours, not held for the next meeting** |
| **Significant deficiency** | Less severe than a material weakness but important enough to merit attention by those responsible for oversight; includes any breach of a zero-tolerance objective, any deficiency in a Critical component, and any repeat of a previously remediated deficiency | CEO and the accountable officer within 2 business days; **Audit Committee at its next meeting, and immediately if it concerns management, fraud, or an assurance function** | 2 business days / next meeting |
| **Deficiency** | A control does not operate as designed, or a design gap exists, without rising to the above | Accountable officer within 2 business days; reported to management review monthly; to the Audit Committee in the quarterly aggregate | Monthly / quarterly aggregate |
| **Observation / improvement opportunity** | No control failure, but an opportunity to strengthen | Control owner; reported in the quarterly aggregate | Quarterly |
| **Any deficiency implicating management, or any pressure on an assurance function** | Regardless of severity | **Directly to the Audit Committee Chair**, bypassing management entirely; the reporter may use the anonymous channels | Immediately |
| **Aggregation rule** | Individually minor deficiencies that together indicate a pattern — same root cause, same owner, same component, or a repeat — are escalated **as a combination** at the higher severity | Audit Committee | Quarterly, with the aggregation analysis stated |

**What the Board receives quarterly**, in the internal control report: every open deficiency by severity
with its age; all new deficiencies in the quarter with their source; remediation status against the
agreed dates with overdue items named; the aggregation analysis; deficiencies remediated and how closure
was verified; deficiencies whose due dates were extended, with the reason and by whose authority; and
management's conclusion on each control component with any qualification. Management also confirms in
writing each quarter that it has reported every deficiency it is aware of — a positive assertion, so
silence is not taken as nothing to report (GOV-PRO-006 §2).

**Current period, reported as raised:** the open account findings (administrative access, account and
user MFA, unrestricted SSH ingress on three security groups, inconsistent transport-security enforcement,
audit-bucket versioning and log retention, the `Demo` table configuration, absent boundary protection,
and the configuration-recorder and SecurityHub telemetry gaps) are recorded in the register, classified,
assigned with dates, and reported to the Audit Committee with the compensating controls applied in the
interim. The identity-path items are classified **Significant** because they affect zero-tolerance
objectives, and the Committee's response was to add an external review rather than to accept the
remediation plan as proposed.

## 11. Monitoring corrective action to timely remediation

**Management tracks whether identified internal control deficiencies are remedied on a timely basis.**
The corrective action plan (CAP) register is the tracking mechanism, and timeliness is measured against
the date set at classification rather than against the date the work happened to finish.

| Element | Rule |
|---|---|
| **Remediation deadlines by severity** | Material weakness: interim compensating control **immediately**, permanent remediation within 30 days; Significant: 60 days; Deficiency: 90 days; Observation: next planning cycle. Deadlines for security findings follow the vulnerability SLAs where tighter (Critical 7 days, High 30, Medium 90) |
| **Owner and authority** | Named individual with the authority to remediate; joint assignment where the authority sits higher |
| **Interim compensating control** | Required from the moment of classification until closure, and recorded; a deficiency with neither remediation nor a compensating control is escalated as an unaccepted residual risk |
| **Progress tracking** | Status updated at least monthly by the owner; reviewed in the monthly management control review; the register is a single source, not per-team lists |
| **Overdue handling** | An item overdue by 1 day is flagged and reported; **overdue past the agreed date is a breach of the control-effectiveness tolerance (RSK-STD-008 §3) and escalates to the Audit Committee**; the owner must state the new date and the reason, and only the approver who set the severity may change it |
| **Escalation ladder** | Overdue → accountable officer; 30 days overdue or any overdue Significant/Material item → CEO and Audit Committee; a repeatedly overdue item → the Board, with the resource or authority obstacle named explicitly, since an overdue remediation is often a resourcing decision that has not been made openly |
| **Closure requires verification, not assertion** | Closure is approved by the Compliance Manager on evidence that the control now operates — for a configuration finding, a re-scan showing the corrected state; for a process finding, a test of the process operating; for a provider finding, provider evidence plus the entity's own verification. **The owner cannot close their own item** |
| **Re-testing after closure** | Closed items are re-tested at the next scheduled evaluation of that control; a deficiency that recurs is classified at the **higher** severity as a repeat, on the basis that the first remediation did not address the root cause |
| **Root cause required** | Closure requires a stated root cause and confirmation that the fix addresses it; where the root cause is systemic, the fix must be systemic, and a systemic root cause **reduces the individual sanction** rather than the remediation requirement (GOV-POL-005 §8) |
| **Metrics reported** | Open items by severity and age; percentage remediated within deadline; number of extensions and by whose authority; number of repeats; mean days to remediate by severity; items closed with verification evidence |
