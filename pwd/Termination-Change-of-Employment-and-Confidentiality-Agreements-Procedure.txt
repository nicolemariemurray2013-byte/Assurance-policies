# Termination, Change of Employment and Confidentiality Agreements Procedure

**Document ID:** HR-PRO-042
**Version:** 2.0 — **effective 2026-03-02**, superseding v1.2 of 2025-09-08
**Owner:** Head of People (process), jointly with the General Counsel (agreement terms and enforcement)
**Approved by:** General Counsel 2026-02-26, per the approval authority in GOV-POL-040 §3
**Next scheduled review:** 2026-12-31 (Annually, and on any change to employment law in a jurisdiction where personnel)
are located, to the entity's legal structure, or to the confidentiality agreement templates
**Scope:** All 48 personnel of both legal entities, all contractors, and the 11 named vendor individuals with
access to entity information. Covers what survives the end of an engagement, what is signed to make it
survive, and how a change of role is handled — which is treated as a termination and a new start, not as an
adjustment.

**Related:** GOV-POL-040 (policy framework), SEC-AST-041 (asset return and intellectual property rights),
SEC-PRO-013 (physical access removal), SEC-IAM-021 (logical access removal), HR-POL-004 (screening and
competence), GOV-POL-005 (code of conduct), TPR-PRO-016 (third-party risk), PRV-RET-036 (retention).

---

## 1. Why this is a separate procedure

Access removal is a control that happens on a date. What this procedure governs is the set of obligations
that **do not** end on that date — confidentiality, intellectual property, and the duty not to use knowledge
acquired here elsewhere — together with the knowledge and role transfer that must happen so that a departure
does not leave a control unowned. Those two things fail in different ways: the first fails silently years
later, the second fails immediately and invisibly. Both are handled here.

A change of role is in scope because the most common way an entitlement outlives its justification is a
promotion or a transfer, where nobody experiences the event as an ending.

## 2. Information security responsibilities that remain valid after termination or change (6.5-R1)

The following duties survive the end of employment, contract or engagement, for the durations stated, and are
enumerated in the individual's terms rather than left to general law:

| Surviving duty | What it requires | Duration | Basis |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Confidentiality of information** | Not to disclose, publish or use entity or customer Confidential information, including evidence, assessment results, control weaknesses, security architecture, incident details, pricing and customer identities | **Indefinite** for customer data, personal data and trade secrets; **5 years** for other Confidential business information | Employment agreement §12; contractor agreement §9; NDA (§4) |
| **Intellectual property** | Assignment of all work product created in the engagement; no retention of copies; no reuse of entity code, models, prompts, document templates or assessment methodology | **Indefinite** (assignment is permanent) | Employment agreement §13; SEC-AST-041 §3 |
| **Other knowledge obtained** | Not to use non-documented knowledge — how a control is bypassed, where a weakness sits, which customer is behind schedule, how the AI system is prompted — for personal benefit or the benefit of another party | **Indefinite** for security-relevant knowledge; this is stated explicitly because "it was in my head, not in a file" is the failure mode a confidentiality clause alone does not close | Employment agreement §12.3 |
| **Responsibilities contained in confidentiality agreements** | Every obligation in the signed NDA or confidentiality undertaking, including return/destruction certification and the duty to notify the entity of any compelled disclosure | Per the agreement's own survival clause (§4.2) | NDA §7 |
| **Non-solicitation of entity data** | Not to contact entity customers to obtain or use entity assessment content | 12 months | Employment agreement §14, jurisdiction-limited |
| **Duty to report** | To report to the CISO any subsequently discovered exposure of entity information that the individual becomes aware of | Indefinite, framed as a duty rather than a prohibition | GOV-POL-005 §8 |
| **Cooperation** | To cooperate with an investigation, regulatory enquiry or litigation concerning the period of the engagement | 3 years, reasonable-cost-reimbursed | Employment agreement §15 |

**A duty stated with its limits.** The entity does not assert a general non-compete; three of the five
jurisdictions in which personnel are located would not enforce one, and asserting an unenforceable term
weakens the terms that are enforceable. The General Counsel maintains a jurisdiction matrix recording, for
each of the five jurisdictions, which surviving clauses are enforceable, which are narrowed, and which are
omitted — reviewed annually and on legal change (6.6-R5). The EU subsidiary's population receives a locally
reviewed variant.

## 3. Where the surviving duties live: the terms of engagement (6.5-R2)

The duties in §2 are **contained in the individual's terms and conditions of employment, contract or
agreement** — not only in a policy the individual could later claim not to have read.

| Population | Instrument carrying the surviving duties | Signed when | Current state |
|---|---|---|---|
| Employee (both entities) | Employment agreement §§12–15, plus a standalone confidentiality undertaking | Before the start date; access is not provisioned without it (SEC-PRO-013 §2) | 48 of 48 |
| Contractor (individual) | Contractor agreement §§9–11 with an equivalent survival clause | Before engagement start | 4 of 4 |
| **Named vendor personnel** (11 individuals) | The vendor's master agreement requirements annex, **plus an individual confidentiality undertaking from each named person** — because a corporate NDA binds the company, not the engineer | Before access is granted | 11 of 11 |
| Business partner personnel (auditors, assessors) | Mutual NDA, purpose- and duration-scoped | Before the engagement | 6 current |
| Interviewees and candidates | Short-form NDA where a demo exposes non-public material | At interview | Used 9 times this period |

The template language is owned by the General Counsel, versioned in the document management system, and
changed only through legal review. A change to the surviving-duty clauses triggers re-signature for personnel
whose duties materially change; a change that only clarifies does not, and which of the two applies is a
recorded legal judgement rather than an administrative default.

## 4. Confidentiality and non-disclosure agreements (6.6)

### 4.1 The agreements the entity uses, identified and documented (6.6-R1)

| Agreement | Used for | Owner of the template | Register entry |
|---|---|---|---|
| Employee confidentiality undertaking | All employees | General Counsel | HR record |
| Contractor confidentiality clause + undertaking | Individual contractors | General Counsel | Contract register |
| **Individual undertaking for named vendor personnel** | The 11 vendor individuals | General Counsel | Vendor register, per named person |
| Mutual NDA (standard) | Prospects, partners, auditors, assessors | General Counsel | Contract register |
| One-way inbound NDA | Where a third party's material is received | General Counsel | Contract register |
| Candidate NDA (short form) | Interviews exposing non-public material | Head of People | Recruitment record |
| **Customer DPA and confidentiality terms** | Customers, covering entity obligations to them | General Counsel | Customer contract register |

Every executed agreement is recorded in the contract register with counterparty, date, version, term,
survival period and the business owner. The register is the population for the review in §4.4; an agreement
not in the register is treated as not existing, which is what makes the register worth maintaining.

### 4.2 Terms determined by information type, classification, use and permissible access (6.6-R2)

Terms are not uniform. The template selected and the clauses enabled are determined by what the counterparty
will actually handle:

| Element the agreement must contain | How it is set | Example of variation by classification |
|---|---|---|
| **A definition of the information to be protected** | Explicit and positive — not "all information disclosed". Names the categories: customer assessment content and evidence, personal data, security architecture and weaknesses, AI system prompts/models/evaluation data, source code, pricing, and the fact and content of incidents | A candidate NDA defines only "the product demonstration and roadmap"; a vendor annex defines evidence, logs and personal data by name |
| Expected duration | Fixed term for business information; **indefinite** for personal data and trade secrets | Candidate NDA 2 years; vendor annex indefinite for personal data |
| Required actions on termination of the agreement | Return or destruction, with **written certification** within 15 days | Required for every agreement that permits receipt of Confidential or above |
| Responsibilities and actions of signatories to avoid unauthorized disclosure | Need-to-know limitation, no onward disclosure without written consent, minimum protective measures matching the classification, subcontractor flow-down | Vendor annex mandates encryption at rest and in transit and named-individual access; a candidate NDA does not |
| Ownership of information, trade secrets and intellectual property | Stated, with no implied licence | Cross-referenced to SEC-AST-041 §3 |
| **Permitted use** of the protected information | Purpose-limited to the engagement's stated purpose; any other use is a breach, including training a model on it | Explicit AI clause added in template v4 (2025-11): no use of entity or customer material as training or fine-tuning input |
| Right to audit and monitor activities involving Confidential information | Present for vendors handling Confidential and above | Exercised twice this period |
| Process for notification and reporting of unauthorized disclosure or breach | **Notice to the entity without undue delay and in any case within 24 hours**, with cooperation in investigation and notification | Vendor annex; mirrored in PRV-TPD-038 |
| Terms for return or destruction of information at agreement cessation | Method appropriate to classification (secure deletion, certified destruction), plus certification | Cross-referenced to PRV-RET-036 |
| Expected actions in case of a breach | Injunctive relief, indemnity where negotiated, and the entity's right to suspend access immediately | Suspension right exercised once (§4.5) |
| **Applicable jurisdiction and governing law** | Chosen per counterparty location against the jurisdiction matrix; clauses unenforceable locally are narrowed rather than left as decoration (6.6-R5) | EU counterparties: EU-entity paper, local law, GDPR terms incorporated |

### 4.3 Signature (6.6-R3)

Confidentiality or non-disclosure agreements are **signed** — by personnel, contractors, named vendor
individuals and other relevant interested parties — before access is granted, and signature is a technical
gate, not an administrative expectation: identity provisioning in SEC-IAM-021 and badge encoding in
SEC-PRO-013 both require the signed undertaking as a prerequisite, and both have refused provisioning for a
missing undertaking this period (one contractor, recorded in SEC-PRO-013 §2). Signature is captured
electronically with signer identity, template version and timestamp, retained for the term plus the survival
period plus 7 years under PRV-RET-036.

**Current state:** 48 of 48 employees; 4 of 4 contractors; 11 of 11 named vendor individuals; 6 of 6 current
partner/auditor NDAs; 9 of 9 candidate NDAs where required. Two engagements were **blocked** at this gate
during the period rather than proceeding on a promise.

### 4.4 Review (6.6-R4)

| Review | Cadence | Reviewer | What it tests |
|---|---|---|---|
| Template review | **Annually**, and on legal change | General Counsel | Currency against law, the classification scheme, and the AI-use clause |
| Executed-agreement review | Annually against the contract register | General Counsel with the business owner | Whether the agreement still matches what the counterparty now handles — scope creep is the common failure |
| **Change-triggered review** | Within 30 days of the trigger | General Counsel | Triggers: a change in the information a counterparty handles; a classification change; a new jurisdiction; a change in law; an incident involving the counterparty; a change in the entity's structure; a material change to a vendor's subprocessors |
| Jurisdiction matrix review | Annually | General Counsel | Enforceability of each surviving clause in each of the five jurisdictions (6.6-R5) |
| Completeness reconciliation | Quarterly | Head of People and Third-Party Risk Manager | Every person with access has a current signed undertaking; an unmatched identity is suspended |

**Reviews performed this period, and what they changed.** The annual template review produced v4, adding the
no-training-input clause and tightening the breach-notice window from 72 to 24 hours. A change-triggered
review followed the managed SIEM provider's expansion into log retention, which moved it from Internal to
Confidential handling and required a re-executed annex with the audit right enabled. The jurisdiction review
narrowed the non-solicitation clause in two jurisdictions and removed it in one. The quarterly reconciliation
found **one named vendor individual whose individual undertaking had lapsed at contract renewal while the
corporate NDA remained in force** — recorded as deficiency **D-2026-014** (Moderate), access suspended the
same day, undertaking re-executed within 2 business days, and the correction is that individual undertakings
now carry the renewal date of the master agreement rather than their own, so renewal cannot desynchronise
them.

### 4.5 Enforcement in practice

Enforcement is recorded because an unenforced clause is not a control. This period: one contractor's access
was **suspended** under the immediate-suspension right after they forwarded an internal architecture diagram
to a personal address (handled as a security incident, no customer data involved, engagement terminated, the
return-and-certify obligation completed and verified); one former employee's post-employment request to
retain a copy of their own project documentation was **refused** in writing, with the specific clause cited,
and the refusal recorded; and one candidate NDA breach — a roadmap detail repeated publicly — resulted in a
cease-and-desist letter rather than litigation, a proportionality decision recorded by the General Counsel.

## 5. Enforcing surviving duties, and treating a role change as termination plus initiation (6.5-R3)

**Post-termination duties are enforced**, and a **change of responsibility or employment is managed as the
termination of the current responsibility combined with the initiation of the new one.** Operationally that
means a role change runs both halves of the process, in that order, and neither half may be skipped because
the person is staying.

| Step | On departure | On role change |
|---|---|---|
| Departure/change attestation signed, restating the surviving duties in §2 and listing what must be returned or destroyed | Required, before the last working day | **Required** — the individual attests to the duties attaching to the role being left, including confidentiality of material they will no longer be entitled to see |
| Exit or transition interview covering the surviving duties, the return obligations and the reporting duty | Held for all departures | Held for every change into or out of a privileged, control-owning or customer-data role |
| Logical access | Revoked on the last working day (SEC-IAM-021) | **Old entitlements revoked first, then new ones granted** — never additively. The identity system enforces this: a role change closes the prior entitlement set and opens a new request |
| Physical access | Deactivated on the last working day (SEC-PRO-013 §3) | Zones reassessed at the level of the new role |
| Assets | Returned per SEC-AST-041 | Reassessed; equipment no longer required for the new role is returned |
| Control ownership and security roles | Transferred per §6 | Transferred per §6 |
| Confidentiality undertaking | Survives; a copy is issued to the individual with the attestation so the terms are in their hands, not only in a file they no longer have access to | Re-signed where the new role changes the categories of information handled |
| Register update | Leaver register, asset register, control-owner register | Same three registers |

**Enforcement mechanics.** The surviving duties are enforceable because: the instrument is signed and
retained (§4.3); the attestation restates the duties at the moment of departure, removing the "I did not
know it continued" defence; the entity retains the access logs that would evidence a pre-departure bulk
export, and reviews them for every departure classified as involuntary or as a privileged-role exit — a
**mandatory pre-departure access-log review** covering the 30 days before notice, performed for 4 of 4
qualifying cases this period, finding one large but legitimate export (a customer's own report, to that
customer); and the General Counsel maintains the escalation path from reminder letter to cease-and-desist to
injunctive relief, which has been used at the first two levels this period (§4.5).

**Preventing pre-departure copying** is handled jointly with SEC-AST-041 §5 and SEC-DLP-023: individuals under
notice retain normal access unless a risk assessment says otherwise (removing access on notice creates its own
risks), but their exports are monitored more closely, bulk-download alerting thresholds are lowered for the
notice period, and involuntary terminations have access removed before notification.

## 6. Transferring the security roles and knowledge a departing person held (6.5-R4, 5.11-R4 cross-reference)

**Information security roles and responsibilities held by any individual who leaves or changes job roles are
identified and transferred to another individual** — before the departure date, not after.

| What is transferred | How it is identified | Deadline | Verification |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Named control ownership** | The control-owner register in CTL-POL-012 is filtered by the individual; every control they own appears on the offboarding checklist as a line item with a named successor | Successor named before the last working day; the checklist **cannot be closed** with an unassigned control | Register shows the new owner; MON-PRO-011's monthly check flags any control with no owner |
| Policy ownership (§3 of GOV-POL-040) | Policy register filtered by owner | Same | Policy register |
| Risk ownership | Risk register filtered by owner | Same | Risk register |
| On-call and incident-response roles | On-call roster and the incident role matrix in SEC-IRP-027 | Removed from the roster on the last working day; successor certified before removal | Roster and certification record |
| Vendor relationship ownership | Vendor register (TPR-PRO-016) | Same | Vendor register |
| Approval authority (change approver, access approver, expense) | Authority matrix in GOV-STR-003 | Reassigned before departure; the workflow tool's approver list is updated so an orphaned approval cannot route to a departed identity | Workflow configuration |
| Break-glass and key-custody roles | Named lists in SEC-CRY-022 and SEC-PRO-013 | Removed same day; **if a physical key cannot be recovered the lock is re-keyed** | Key-holder list |
| **Operational knowledge** | A documented handover covering current work, in-flight decisions, undocumented dependencies, known workarounds, contacts, and anything the individual believes only they know | Written handover reviewed by the receiving manager before the last working day; where the departure is abrupt, the manager records what could not be recovered rather than leaving the gap implicit | Handover document in the team space; the manager signs that it is sufficient |
| Customer and regulator contact ownership | CRM and the regulator contact register | Reassigned before departure; **external contacts are notified of the change** where the individual was a named contact (6.5-R5) | Notification record |

**This period:** 3 departures and 5 role changes. The departures transferred 11 named controls, 2 policy
ownerships, 1 vendor relationship and 3 on-call certifications. One case is recorded as a partial failure:
an abrupt departure left a **single undocumented dependency in the evidence-staging cleanup job** that was
discovered 6 days later during a routine failure; the handover had been signed as sufficient. This is
deficiency **D-2026-013** (Low, no customer impact, no data loss), and the correction is that the handover
template now requires the individual to list every scheduled job, alarm and manual step they own, verified
against the observability inventory rather than against memory.

## 7. Communicating the surviving duties, and to whom (6.5-R5)

| Audience | What is communicated | When | How |
|---|---|---|---|
| The departing or changing individual | The duties in §2, the return obligations, the reporting duty, and a copy of their signed undertaking | At notice and again at the exit/transition interview | Attestation pack, countersigned |
| Their manager and team | That the individual's access and control ownership have ended, and who holds them now | On the effective date | Team notification and the control-owner register |
| **Relevant contact persons and operating counterparties** — customers, the regulator contact, vendors, the external auditor, the managed SIEM provider | That a named contact has changed, and the new contact | Within 5 business days of the effective date, and immediately where the individual was an escalation contact | A defined **process for communicating changes and operating procedures to relevant contact persons**, owned by the relationship owner and tracked in the offboarding checklist |
| Reception and facilities | Badge deactivation and visitor-host changes | Same day | Badge system and reception list |
| Third-party risk | Vendor-side changes and any required annex update | Within 5 business days | Vendor register |
| Personnel generally | The existence and content of the surviving duties, so they are known before they matter | Annually, in the code-of-conduct acknowledgement cycle, and at onboarding | Awareness training module "what follows you out of the door" |

This period the contact-change process issued 7 notifications (3 departures, 4 relevant role changes),
including a change of the named incident escalation contact to the managed SIEM provider and a change of the
regulator contact recorded in the AI reporting obligations register.

## 8. External personnel: the same process, contractually (6.5-R6)

**The termination and change process is applied to external personnel — suppliers — when a person, contract or
job ends, or when the job changes within the organization.** It is applied through the contract, because the
entity cannot run a supplier's HR process:

1. The requirements annex obliges the provider to notify the entity of any change or departure affecting a
   named individual with entity access **within 1 business day**, and to confirm the individual's own return
   or destruction of entity information.
2. Entity-side access is removed on the same deadlines as for employees, and the badge carries a **hard expiry
   at the recorded engagement end date** so a missed notification fails closed rather than open (SEC-PRO-013
   §3).
3. The individual's confidentiality undertaking survives independently of the corporate relationship (§4.2),
   which is precisely why individual undertakings are required.
4. Entity assets held by vendor personnel are recovered per SEC-AST-041 §4, with the provider confirming the
   return in writing.
5. Control or role ownership held by vendor personnel — the managed SIEM provider holds two named
   monitoring roles — is transferred per §6, with the provider naming the successor and the entity
   acknowledging the name rather than accepting "the team".
6. A **change of the job within the provider** is treated as a role change: entitlements are reassessed at the
   level of the new role, and unescorted physical status is re-justified (SEC-PRO-013 §4).
7. Where the whole contract ends, the exit provisions run: access removal, asset return, information return
   or certified destruction, deletion confirmation for any entity data held, and a closure record signed by
   the Third-Party Risk Manager.

**This period:** 3 vendor departures and 1 vendor-side role change. All 3 departures were notified within the
1-business-day window and access was removed same-day; the role change reverted one individual from
unescorted to escorted physical status. One provider's initial closure record omitted the deletion
confirmation and was **not accepted** until the confirmation was produced 4 business days later — recorded in
the vendor register as a supplier performance observation rather than waived.
