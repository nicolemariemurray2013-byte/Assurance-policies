## 1. The board identifies and accepts its oversight responsibilities

The Board has **identified and formally accepted** its oversight responsibilities in relation to the
requirements and expectations established for the entity — the trust services criteria for security,
availability, processing integrity, confidentiality and privacy; the AI management obligations under
AIMS-POL-001; and applicable legal, regulatory and contractual commitments. Acceptance is recorded as
a Board resolution, not inferred from practice.

| Accepted oversight responsibility | Established requirement it relates to | How the Board discharges it | Cadence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Oversee the design and operating effectiveness of internal control | All trust services criteria; CC4 monitoring | Reviews the internal control report, control failures and remediation status | Quarterly |
| Oversee security of the system and data | Security criteria (CC6) | Reviews the security posture report, incidents, penetration test and vulnerability metrics | Quarterly |
| Oversee availability and resilience commitments | Availability criteria (A1) | Reviews SLA attainment, capacity headroom, backup and recovery-test results | Quarterly |
| Oversee processing integrity of assessment outputs | Processing integrity criteria (PI1) | Reviews output-accuracy metrics and the verdict-integrity sampling results | Quarterly |
| Oversee confidentiality of customer information | Confidentiality criteria (C1) | Reviews confidential-data inventory, disclosure register and disposal attestations | Semi-annually |
| Oversee privacy commitments and legal privacy obligations | Privacy criteria (P1-P8); applicable privacy law | Reviews the privacy program report, data-subject request metrics, DPIAs and privacy incidents | Quarterly |
| Oversee the AI management system and responsible-AI obligations | ISO/IEC 42001; AIMS-POL-001 | Receives the AI Governance Board report; approves the AI risk appetite | Quarterly |
| Oversee the integrity and ethical values of the entity | GOV-STD-001 | Standing ethics agenda item; receives every conduct case involving management | Quarterly |
| Oversee external audit and the assertion | SOC 2 examination | Appoints the service auditor; approves management's assertion and the system description | Annually |
| Oversee risk assessment, including fraud risk | CC3 | Approves the risk assessment and the fraud-risk assessment | Annually, and on material change |
| Oversee third-party and outsourced service provider risk | CC9 | Reviews the supplier risk register and concentration exposure | Semi-annually |

The Board minutes for 2026-02-19 record the resolution: *"The Board identifies and accepts the
oversight responsibilities set out in the Charter §1, acknowledges that these relate to the entity's
established trust services and privacy requirements, and directs that it be provided the information
necessary to discharge them."*

**Information rights.** The Board resolved that it receives management reporting on a defined
schedule, has unrestricted access to any record, may engage independent advisors at the entity's
expense without management approval, and holds an in-camera session without management present at
every meeting. Acceptance of responsibility without these rights would be nominal.

## 2. Independence from management

The Board has **sufficient members who are independent from management and objective in evaluations
and decision making**.

| Member | Role | Independent of management? | Basis |
|---|---|---|---|
| M-1 | Chair of the Board | **Yes** | Not an employee or officer; no consulting or commercial relationship; no family relationship with management; independent of the founding shareholders |
| M-2 | Audit Committee Chair | **Yes** | Non-executive; no other relationship with the entity; financially literate |
| M-3 | Security & Privacy Committee Chair | **Yes** | Non-executive; retained solely as a director |
| M-4 | Non-executive director | **Yes** | Investor-nominated but not an employee; recuses on matters affecting the nominating shareholder |
| M-5 | Chief Executive Officer | No — management | Executive director |

**Four of five directors are independent of management.** The Chair is independent and is not the CEO,
so the person leading the Board is not the person the Board evaluates.

Objectivity is maintained by defined mechanisms rather than by assertion:

- **Independent majority and independent chairs.** All three standing committees are chaired by
  independent directors, and management holds no committee seat.
- **In-camera sessions.** Every quarterly meeting includes a session with no member of management
  present. Held 4 of 4 quarters in the current period.
- **Recusal.** A director recuses from any matter in which they have an interest. Recorded in the
  minutes; three recusals in the current period, including M-4 on a matter concerning the nominating
  shareholder's portfolio company.
- **Annual independence determination.** Each director submits an independence and interests
  declaration; the Board determines independence in February and records the determination.
- **Direct access channel.** The Audit Committee Chair receives conduct reports directly, bypassing
  management (GOV-STD-001 §5, channel D-4).
- **Annual Board effectiveness review**, including whether management dominated the agenda. The 2026
  review found that management-prepared material crowded out independent discussion in two of four
  meetings, and the Board responded by reserving the first 30 minutes of each meeting for the
  independent directors.

## 3. Skills and expertise — defined, maintained, and periodically evaluated

The Board **defines, maintains, and periodically evaluates the skills and expertise needed among its
members** to enable it to ask probing questions of senior management and take commensurate action. The
instrument is a skills matrix, evaluated every February.

| Required skill | Why the Board needs it | Held by | Depth |
|---|---|---|---|
| Financial reporting and audit | Approve the assertion; oversee the examination | M-2, M-4 | Strong (M-2 is a former audit partner) |
| Information security | Probe the security posture report | M-3 | Strong (former CISO) |
| Privacy and data protection law | Probe privacy commitments and legal obligations | M-3, M-2 | Moderate — **supplemented**, see §4 |
| AI/ML governance and assurance | Probe model risk and assessment quality | M-1, M-5 | Moderate — **supplemented**, see §4 |
| Cloud infrastructure and availability engineering | Probe resilience and capacity claims | M-3 | Moderate |
| Regulated-industry compliance operations | Understand customer obligations | M-1, M-4 | Strong |
| Enterprise risk management | Approve the risk appetite | M-1, M-4 | Strong |

**Evaluation, and the action taken.** The February 2026 evaluation was performed against this matrix
by the Chair with input from each director and produced two identified gaps: privacy law depth
(assessed Moderate against a Strong requirement, given the expansion of the P-series commitments) and
AI assurance depth. The Board did not record the gaps and move on — it took **commensurate action**:
engaged the two consultants in §4, added privacy-law depth as a required attribute for the next
director appointment, and commissioned director education (four sessions delivered: privacy law,
threat detection, model evaluation methods, and the SOC 2 examination process).

The evaluation also asks a behavioral question, since expertise on paper is not the criterion: *did
each director in fact ask probing questions this year?* The 2026 review recorded that two directors
had asked few substantive questions in the security and privacy sessions, which is what prompted the
director education program.

## 4. Supplementing board expertise through subcommittees and consultants

The Board **supplements its expertise relevant to security, availability, processing integrity,
confidentiality and privacy through the use of subcommittees and consultants**, where its own members'
depth is insufficient.

**Standing subcommittees**

| Subcommittee | Chair (independent) | Members | Trust services scope | Cadence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Audit Committee** | M-2 | M-2, M-4 | Internal control over all five categories; external audit; conduct cases; the assertion | Quarterly, plus ad hoc |
| **Security & Privacy Committee** | M-3 | M-3, M-1, + external advisors (non-voting) | **Security, availability, processing integrity, confidentiality, privacy** | Quarterly |
| **AI Governance Oversight Committee** | M-1 | M-1, M-3, + the Chief AI Officer reporting in | AI-specific risk, model performance, responsible AI | Quarterly |

The Security & Privacy Committee exists specifically because the full Board lacks the depth to probe
these five areas unaided. Its charter gives it authority to commission independent testing, to demand
remediation with a deadline, and to escalate directly to the Board.

**Consultants engaged to supplement expertise**

| Consultant | Expertise supplied | Reports to | Engagement | Output in the current period |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| External privacy counsel | Privacy law and contractual privacy obligations (the identified gap) | Security & Privacy Committee | Retainer since 2025-10-01 | Legal-obligations register refresh; opinion on cross-border transfer; review of the privacy notice |
| Independent AI assurance advisor | Model evaluation, assessment-quality methodology (the identified gap) | AI Governance Oversight Committee | Retainer since 2025-11-15 | Independent review of the evaluation methodology; 3 recommendations, all accepted |
| Independent penetration testing firm | Adversarial security testing | Security & Privacy Committee | Annual + on major change | 2026 test report: 1 high, 4 medium, all remediated and retested |
| Service auditor | SOC 2 examination | Audit Committee | Annual | Examination in progress |
| Independent internal audit provider | Control testing independent of management | Audit Committee | Semi-annual | 2 reports; findings tracked in the CAP register |

Consultants report to the relevant committee, not to management, which is what makes the supplement
independent. The Security & Privacy Committee's 2026-Q1 minute records it rejecting management's
proposed risk rating for a finding after the penetration testers disagreed with it — the practical
demonstration that the supplement functions.

## 5. Meetings and records

Four scheduled quarterly meetings plus meetings on demand; the Audit Committee met 5 times and the
Security & Privacy Committee 4 times in the current period. Attendance in the current period was 100%
for the Audit Committee and 95% across all meetings. Minutes record attendance, materials received,
questions raised, decisions, recusals, in-camera sessions and actions with owners and dates. Open
Board actions are tracked to closure and re-reported at the next meeting; 11 of 12 actions in the
current period closed on time, and the overdue one (a policy refresh) was re-committed with a date.
