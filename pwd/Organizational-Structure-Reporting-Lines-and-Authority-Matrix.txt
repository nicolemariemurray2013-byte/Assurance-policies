## 1. All structures of the entity are addressed

Management and the Board **consider the multiple structures used to support the achievement of
objectives** — operating units, legal entities, geographic distribution and outsourced service
providers. Each is enumerated below with its accountable owner and how it is overseen; a structure
that is not on this list is not authorized to process entity or customer data.

### 1.1 Operating units

| Operating unit | Objective it supports | Head | Reports to |
|---|---|---|---|
| Platform Engineering | Build and run the evaluation service | VP Engineering | CEO |
| AI/Assessment Engineering | Model selection, prompts, evaluation quality | Chief AI Officer | CEO |
| Security & Infrastructure | Security, availability, infrastructure controls | AI Security Lead | VP Engineering, with a direct line to the Security & Privacy Committee |
| Compliance & Assurance | Control operation, evidence, examinations | Compliance Manager | General Counsel |
| Customer Operations | Onboarding, support, incident communication | Director, Customer Ops | CEO |
| Legal & Privacy | Legal, privacy, contracts | General Counsel / DPO | CEO, with a direct line to the Board |
| People Operations | Hiring, background screening, competence, conduct | Head of People | CEO |
| Finance & Procurement | Financial control, supplier onboarding | Head of Finance | CEO |

### 1.2 Legal entities

| Legal entity | Jurisdiction | Role | Controls applied |
|---|---|---|---|
| Parent operating company | United States (Delaware) | Contracting party; operates the service; controller/processor of record | Full control set; owns the SOC 2 assertion |
| EU subsidiary | Ireland | EU/EEA contracting party and EU representative for privacy purposes | Full control set; intra-group data processing agreement; SCCs where transfers occur |

The intra-group data processing agreement between the two entities is executed and reviewed annually
by the General Counsel; it defines controller/processor roles, transfer mechanism, sub-processor
consent and audit rights. The Board reviews the legal-entity structure annually because a structure
change alters who owes which obligation to whom.

### 1.3 Geographic distribution

| Location | Function | Data resident there | Control consideration |
|---|---|---|---|
| us-west-2 (AWS) | Production system of record | Customer scan evidence, documents, assessment results | Primary control boundary; all production controls |
| us-east-1 (AWS) | Reserved for planned cross-region backup replication (not yet configured; backup standard §6.5) | None today | Backup controls (A1); no compute, no access path for personnel |
| United States (remote workforce) | Engineering, operations | No local storage permitted; access via managed endpoints only | Endpoint controls, MDM, no-local-copy standard |
| Ireland (remote workforce) | EU customer-facing, privacy | No local storage permitted | Same, plus EU privacy oversight |
| India (contracted engineering, 1 supplier) | Non-production tooling only | No customer data — enforced by account separation | Vendor personnel controls; access limited to non-production accounts |

Geographic distribution is a control consideration in its own right: personnel located outside the US
create cross-border transfer obligations, which is why the vendor engineering location is denied
production access by account boundary rather than by policy alone.

### 1.4 Outsourced service providers

| Provider | Service outsourced | Data exposure | Owner | Oversight mechanism |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| AWS | Cloud infrastructure, Bedrock model inference | Processes customer evidence in-region | AI Security Lead | Annual SOC 2 report review; shared-responsibility matrix; CC9 supplier record |
| Identity provider (SSO/MFA) | Authentication | Employee identity data | AI Security Lead | Annual SOC 2 review; configuration reviewed quarterly |
| Managed SIEM/monitoring provider | Log analysis, alerting | Security telemetry, which may contain identifiers | AI Security Lead | Annual SOC 2 review; DPA; log-minimization requirement |
| Contracted engineering supplier | Non-production tooling | None (account-separated) | VP Engineering | Quarterly personnel review; no production access |
| Independent internal audit provider | Control testing | Read access to evidence under NDA | Audit Committee | Engagement letter; reports to the Committee, not management |

| Independent penetration testing firm | Security testing | Access to a non-production replica | AI Security Lead | Scoped engagement; results to the Security & Privacy Committee |

Every provider above is in the supplier register with a risk tier, a current DPA where personal data is
involved, and an annual review — the mechanism is defined in AIMS-PRO-010 and CC9. Outsourcing
transfers execution, never accountability: each provider has a named internal owner who remains
answerable for the control.

## 2. Reporting lines are designed and evaluated

Management **designs and evaluates lines of reporting for each entity structure** to enable execution
of authorities and responsibilities and the flow of information needed to manage the entity's
activities.

**Design principles.** Every operating unit has exactly one primary reporting line; every control
owner can reach a decision-maker with authority to stop work; assurance functions (Security,
Compliance, Legal/Privacy, Internal Audit) hold a **secondary reporting line that bypasses the
management chain they assure**, so a control concern cannot be suppressed by the person it concerns.

| Structure | Primary line | Secondary / escalation line | Why the secondary line exists |
|---|---|---|---|
| Platform Engineering | VP Engineering → CEO | — | — |
| Security & Infrastructure | AI Security Lead → VP Engineering | **→ Security & Privacy Committee directly** | Security must be able to report on the engineering organization it assures |
| Compliance & Assurance | Compliance Manager → General Counsel | **→ Audit Committee directly** | Control failures must reach the Board unfiltered |
| Legal & Privacy (DPO) | General Counsel → CEO | **→ Board directly**; the DPO cannot be instructed on the substance of privacy advice and cannot be dismissed for it | Privacy law requires an unconflicted advisory line |
| Internal audit provider | — | **→ Audit Committee only** | Independence from management |
| Any employee or contractor | Line manager | **→ Ethics channels, incl. the Audit Committee Chair** (GOV-STD-001 §5) | A report about management must not route through management |
| Incident reporting | On-call → Incident Commander | → CEO and Board for Severity 1 within 24h | Time-critical information must not queue behind hierarchy |
| Outsourced providers | Named internal owner | → Third-Party Risk → CC9 register | External activity must enter the entity's information flow |

**Evaluation of the reporting lines** is performed annually, and is a genuine evaluation rather than a
re-publication of the chart:

- 2025-09 evaluation found the AI Security Lead reported solely through VP Engineering, meaning
  security findings about Platform Engineering were filtered by the accountable manager. **Action
  taken:** the direct line to the Security & Privacy Committee was added (v3.0).
- 2026-02 evaluation found that Customer Operations learned of degraded assessment quality only after
  customers reported it, because the quality metric flowed up Engineering and not across. **Action
  taken:** a standing cross-functional quality review was added, and the processing-integrity metric
  is now distributed to Customer Ops directly (v3.3).
- The evaluation also tests information flow the other way — whether the Board's decisions reach the
  people who must act on them. Two of eleven Board actions in 2025 had not been communicated to the
  accountable owner within a week; a Board-action register with named owners now closes that loop.

## 3. Authority is delegated, responsibilities defined, and duties segregated

Management and the Board **delegate authority, define responsibilities, and use appropriate processes
and technology to assign responsibility and segregate duties** at the various levels of the entity.
Delegation is written and bounded; where an authority is enforced by technology, the technical control
is named, because a documented limit that the platform does not enforce is advisory only.

### 3.1 Authority matrix

| Decision / action | Delegated to | Limit | Enforced by (technology / process) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Accept a risk within appetite | Accountable Owner | Within the Board-approved appetite; above it → Board | Risk register requires the approver's identity and the appetite band |
| Accept a risk outside appetite | Board | None | Board minute required before the risk may be accepted |
| Approve a production change | Change approver ≠ author | Standard changes only; emergency path requires post-hoc review within 24h | **Branch protection: pull request + 1 approving review, author cannot self-approve; CI gate** |
| Deploy to production | CI/CD pipeline identity | Humans cannot deploy directly | **No human principal holds deploy permission; pipeline role is the only deployer** |
| Grant or change production access | AI Security Lead + resource owner | Time-bound; least privilege | **IAM permission boundary; access requests recorded; break-glass alarmed** |
| Use break-glass/elevated access | Two-person authorization | Session recorded and reviewed within 1 business day | **CloudTrail alarm on privileged action → SNS to Security; MFA required** |
| Approve a new supplier / sub-processor | Third-Party Risk + General Counsel | No production data access before DPA execution | Procurement gate; register entry required |
| Disclose confidential customer information | General Counsel | Contract or law only; logged in the disclosure register | Disclosure register; legal review recorded |
| Respond to a data-subject request | DPO | Statutory deadlines | Request tracker with a deadline clock |
| Publish or amend an assessment result | Assessment pipeline only | **No manual override of a verdict; corrections require a new assessment with evidence** | **No human write path to the result store; changes are appended and journalled** |
| Change model, prompt or evaluation logic | Chief AI Officer + Responsible AI Lead | Requires evaluation-set regression pass | CI evaluation gate blocks merge on regression |
| Declare a Severity 1 incident | Any on-call engineer | No approval needed to declare or to stop the service | Runbook authority; no gate by design |
| Approve expenditure | Head of Finance / CEO by threshold | Tiered thresholds; two signatures above the top tier | Purchasing system approval chain |
| Terminate access on departure | People Ops triggers, Security executes | Same business day for involuntary; ≤ 24h otherwise | Offboarding workflow with a verification step |

### 3.2 Segregation of duties

| Duties kept separate | Why | How the separation is enforced |
|---|---|---|
| Authoring a change ↔ approving it | Prevents unilateral change to the production system | Branch protection rejects self-approval |
| Approving a change ↔ deploying it | Prevents bypassing review | Only the pipeline identity can deploy |
| Requesting access ↔ granting access | Prevents self-provisioning of privilege | Access workflow requires a second party; IAM denies self-grant |
| Operating a control ↔ testing it | Preserves assurance validity | Internal audit provider reports to the Audit Committee |
| Producing an assessment result ↔ reviewing quality | Preserves processing integrity | Responsible AI Lead samples results and is outside the delivery line |
| Managing suppliers ↔ approving supplier payments | Reduces fraud opportunity | Procurement/Finance split; two signatures above threshold |
| Administering identity ↔ approving privileged roles | Prevents privilege self-escalation | Role changes require resource-owner approval; break-glass alarmed |
| Investigating a conduct case ↔ being its subject | Prevents self-exoneration | Conduct Panel; Audit Committee handles management cases |

**Where segregation is not achievable**, the compensating control is recorded rather than the gap
being left implicit. The entity is small enough that the AI Security Lead both administers identity
and holds break-glass. Compensating: every break-glass session raises a CloudTrail alarm to the
Security & Privacy Committee's mailbox (not only to Security), sessions are reviewed by the Compliance
Manager who is outside the reporting line, and quarterly the independent internal audit provider
re-performs the review. This is documented in the risk register as R-041 with the Board's acceptance.

## 4. Security, availability, processing integrity, confidentiality and privacy requirements are addressed when defining authorities and responsibilities

Management and the Board **consider requirements relevant to all five trust services categories when
defining authorities and responsibilities**. Each category has a named accountable owner at management
level, a Board-level overseer, and specific authorities allocated to make the responsibility
executable.

| Category | Accountable owner | Board overseer | Authorities specifically allocated to discharge it |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Security** | AI Security Lead | Security & Privacy Committee | Grant/revoke access; block a release on a security finding; require break-glass review; commission penetration testing |
| **Availability** | VP Engineering | Security & Privacy Committee | Declare an incident; allocate capacity; require a restore test; authorize failover |
| **Processing integrity** | Chief AI Officer, with the Responsible AI Lead | AI Governance Oversight Committee | Block a model/prompt change on regression; suspend a control's automated assessment; require re-assessment |
| **Confidentiality** | General Counsel | Audit Committee | Approve or refuse any disclosure; require disposal; classify information |
| **Privacy** | DPO (General Counsel) | Security & Privacy Committee | Decide data-subject requests; halt a processing activity that lacks a lawful basis; require a DPIA before launch |

Two of these are deliberately negative authorities — the right to **stop** something (block a release,
halt a processing activity). A responsibility for a trust services category without the authority to
prevent the thing that would breach it is not a control.

## 5. Interaction with, and monitoring of, external parties

Management and the Board **consider the need to interact with and monitor the activities of external
parties when establishing structures, reporting lines, authorities and responsibilities**. Every
external party category has an internal owner, a defined interaction channel, and a monitoring
mechanism.

| External party | Internal owner (structure) | Interaction channel | How their activities are monitored |
|---|---|---|---|
| Customers | Customer Ops; General Counsel for contracts | Support channel, status page, contractual notices | SLA reporting; complaint register; commitments tracked against contract terms |
| Outsourced service providers / sub-processors | Named owner per §1.4; Third-Party Risk coordinates | Contract, DPA, annual review meeting | Annual SOC 2 / attestation review; incident notification obligation; access reviewed quarterly; register entry |
| Vendor and contractor personnel | Engagement owner | Onboarding through the entity's own access process | Same conduct evaluation as employees (GOV-STD-001 §4); quarterly continue/remove decision |
| Business partners and integrators | VP Engineering | Partner agreement with a conduct and disclosure annex | Annual review; integration security review |
| Regulators and supervisory authorities | General Counsel / DPO — **sole authorized channel** | Formal correspondence only | Correspondence register; Board notified |
| Service auditor | Audit Committee (engaged), Compliance Manager (coordinates) | Examination process | Reports to the Audit Committee |
| Independent testers and advisors | Relevant Board committee | Engagement letter | Report directly to the committee, not to management |
| Data subjects | DPO | Privacy request channel published in the notice | Request tracker with statutory deadlines |
| Security researchers / external reporters | AI Security Lead | Vulnerability disclosure channel | Triage SLA; findings enter the remediation register |

Two structural choices follow from this analysis. First, the DPO and General Counsel are the **only**
authorized channel to regulators, so an engineer cannot inadvertently make a statement that binds the
entity. Second, independent testers report to a Board committee rather than to the management they
test, which is what makes their monitoring of external-facing controls meaningful.

## 6. Legal and contractual privacy requirements in the structure

Management **addresses legal and contractual privacy requirements and objectives when establishing
structures, reporting lines and authorities**. The privacy obligations drive specific structural
features, not merely a policy document.

| Legal / contractual privacy requirement | Structural consequence — deliberately adopted |
|---|---|
| An unconflicted privacy advisory function is required, and its advice must not be overridden by commercial pressure | The **DPO role is formally designated**, is held by the General Counsel, reports to the CEO **and directly to the Board**, cannot be instructed on the substance of privacy advice, and cannot be dismissed or penalized for performing the role |
| Controller/processor roles must be allocated and documented | Roles are allocated per legal entity in §1.2 and per customer contract; the DPA and intra-group agreement record them |
| An EU/EEA presence and representative may be required for EU customers | The **Ireland subsidiary** exists in part for this purpose; the EU representative function is assigned there |
| Cross-border transfers require a lawful transfer mechanism | Transfer mechanism (SCCs) and the transfer impact assessment are owned by the DPO; **no new region or offshore supplier may be added without the DPO's approval** — a hard gate in the procurement and infrastructure change processes |
| Sub-processors require customer notice/consent and flow-down obligations | Sub-processor list is maintained by the DPO and published; adding one requires DPA execution and customer notification before processing begins |
| Data-subject rights must be honored within statutory deadlines | DPO holds the authority to decide requests and to compel any operating unit to act; deadline clock in the request tracker |
| A DPIA is required before high-risk processing | The DPO holds a **launch veto**: no processing activity assessed as high risk proceeds without a completed DPIA |
| Privacy incidents require notification within statutory windows | Incident structure names the DPO as a mandatory Severity-1 participant with authority to start the notification clock, independent of management's view of severity |
| Sanctions must apply to privacy policy violations | Conduct framework covers privacy violations explicitly (GOV-STD-001 §6) |
| Records of processing must be maintained | ROPA owned by the DPO; each operating unit has a named contributor |

## 7. Board oversight of the structure

The Board reviews this structure annually and on material change: new legal entity, new region, new
outsourced provider with data access, a change to segregation of duties, or a change in legal or
contractual privacy obligations. The 2026-02-19 resolution approved v3.3, and specifically approved
the compensating control for the identity/break-glass segregation limitation in §3.2 as an accepted
risk (R-041) with quarterly independent re-performance.
