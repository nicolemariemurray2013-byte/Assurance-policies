**Version:** 3.2
**Owner:** AI Security Lead (physical security), jointly with the Head of People (badge lifecycle) and the
Director, IT Operations (device recovery)
**Approved by:** CEO 2026-02-19; v3.2 approved by the CEO 2026-08-14; reviewed by the Audit Committee 2026-02-19 and 2026-08-14

**Scope:** All entity-controlled facilities across five geographies and two legal entities; all entity

## 1. Facilities in scope, and the boundary of what the entity controls

The entity operates no data centre of its own. The production service runs entirely in AWS account
471885797689 in us-west-2, so **the physical protection of production information assets is inherited from
AWS**, and the entity's own physical controls protect offices, endpoint devices and the small amount of
physical media that exists.

| Facility / asset location | Control model | Who operates the physical controls | How the entity obtains assurance |
|---|---|---|---|
| Headquarters office (primary geography) — 34 desks, one server closet holding network equipment only, no production data at rest | **Entity-operated** | Facilities lead within IT Operations, reporting to the AI Security Lead | Direct testing: badge audit, review log review, tailgating observation, quarterly access review |
| Secondary office (EU subsidiary) — 9 desks, no server room | **Entity-operated**, landlord-provided building access with entity-controlled suite access | Facilities lead, with the EU subsidiary manager as on-site delegate | Direct testing on the same cadence; landlord's building access reviewed annually against the tenant list |
| Three co-working locations (three further geographies, 5 personnel) | **Provider-operated** | Co-working operator | Provider assessment; **no entity information asset above Internal classification may be stored on site**; no entity-issued badge is created by the entity for these sites, and the operator's badge list is reconciled quarterly against the entity's personnel roster |
| Remote/home working — 42 of 48 personnel work primarily remotely | **Individual accountability under a defined standard** | The individual, under the remote-working standard in §8 | Attestation, device posture enforcement, and the no-local-copy technical control |
| **AWS us-west-2 — all production information assets** | **Provider-operated; not entity-controlled** | AWS | Reviewed annually via the provider's own audit reports and the shared-responsibility allocation; the entity records this as an inherited control and does **not** assert it as its own (GOV-PRO-007 §5, and the CUEC communication in the customer-facing description) |
| Managed SIEM provider's facility (log copies) | **Provider-operated** | The provider | Annual provider assessment including physical controls; addressed in the requirements annex |
| Physical media | **Eliminated by design** — no removable media is used for entity or customer data; USB mass storage is blocked by device policy | — | Device-policy compliance report; the absence of an approved media register is itself the evidence |

**Protected information assets requiring physical protection, and where each sits:** production evidence
and assessment results (AWS only — never on an endpoint, enforced by the no-local-copy control introduced
after case D-2026-004); network equipment (HQ server closet); endpoint devices (with the individual, or in
the HQ secure store when spare); signed contracts and statutory records (locked cabinet in HQ, and the EU
subsidiary's statutory register in its own locked cabinet); HR and screening records (retained
electronically only, access-controlled — no paper personnel file exists); and printed material, which is
restricted to Internal classification or below by the print policy, with Confidential printing disabled at
the device.

## 2. Creating and modifying physical access — authorization required for every population

**Physical access is created and modified for employees, contractors, vendors and business partner
personnel based on appropriate authorization.** No badge is issued or modified without a named authorizer
who is not the requester, and the authorization requirement differs by population because the risk does.

| Population | Who must authorize | Prerequisites that gate issuance (all verified before the badge is encoded) | Zones granted by default |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Employee** | The hiring manager, and the Head of People confirms the employment record | Signed employment agreement; **screening completed and cleared** (HR-POL-004 §3); confidentiality undertaking signed; Code of Conduct acknowledged; start date reached — a badge is not encoded before the start date | General office areas of their assigned site |
| **Employee needing the server closet** | AI Security Lead **and** Director, IT Operations — dual authorization | The above, plus a role in the network/infrastructure role profile and a recorded business need | General office + server closet |
| **Contractor / individually named vendor personnel** (11 individuals) | The engagement owner **and** the Third-Party Risk Manager | Executed contract with the DPA and the requirements annex; **the provider's screening attestation naming that individual** — a hard gate, not a formality; confidentiality undertaking from the individual; a defined engagement end date recorded at issuance | Escorted-visitor status by default; unescorted access only on a separate, explicitly justified authorization, granted to 4 of 11 |
| **Business partner personnel** (auditors, assessors, integration partners) | The relationship owner **and** the General Counsel | Executed NDA; purpose and date range recorded; identity verified at reception against the pre-registration | Escorted only, day-scoped |
| **Visitors** (candidates, guests, service technicians) | The host employee, pre-registered at least one business day ahead where practicable | Photo identity checked at reception; sign-in recorded with host, purpose, time in and time out; visitor badge issued and **returned before departure** | Escorted only, reception and meeting rooms; **never the server closet without the dual authorization above** |
| **Landlord and building service staff** | Building management under the lease; the entity authorizes suite entry | Suite entry only during scheduled hours with entity notification, or escorted for unscheduled work; **the server closet is excluded from all landlord access** and the closet lock is entity-keyed, not part of the building master suite | Suite common areas, scheduled only |
| **Emergency services** | No prior authorization — access is never obstructed | The entry is recorded after the fact and reviewed | As required |

**Modification** — any change to a zone, to unescorted status or to an expiry date follows the same
authorization as initial issuance, at the level of the *new* access rather than the old: an employee moving
into an infrastructure role needs the dual authorization for the server closet, and a contractor moving
from escorted to unescorted needs the separate justification. Modifications are recorded in the badge
system with the authorizer's identity, and the quarterly review (§4) tests modifications specifically,
because an incremental modification is the most common way access accumulates beyond a role.

**Enforcement rather than policy:** the badge system will not encode a badge without a completed
authorization record and a linked identity record. Two issuances were **refused** this period — one where
the provider's screening attestation for a named vendor individual had not been received (the individual
started on escorted access nine days later, once it arrived) and one where a contractor's confidentiality
undertaking was outstanding. Both refusals are recorded, because a control that has never refused anything
has not been demonstrated to work.

## 3. Removing physical access when it is no longer required

**Physical access is removed when the individual no longer requires it.** Removal is triggered by an event,
not by someone remembering, and the trigger is the same HR/vendor event stream that drives logical access
removal — so the two cannot diverge.

| Trigger | Removal deadline | How the trigger reaches the badge system | Current-period instances |
|---|---|---|---|
| Employee departure (resignation or termination) | **Badge deactivated on the last working day**; for involuntary termination, deactivated **before the notification meeting** | The offboarding checklist is initiated by the Head of People and includes badge deactivation as a mandatory item with a timestamp; the checklist cannot be closed with the item open | 3 departures (6% attrition), all deactivated on the last working day; 1 involuntary case deactivated before notification |
| **Vendor or contractor engagement end** | **Within 1 business day**, and the badge carries a **hard expiry at the recorded engagement end date** so it fails closed if the trigger is missed | Expiry encoded at issuance; the engagement end date in the supplier record drives it | 3 vendor departures, all same-day; the hard expiry has never been the operative control, but 2 badges did reach expiry within the period and were re-authorized rather than silently extended |
| Role change removing the need for a zone | Within 1 business day of the effective date | The role-change workflow includes a physical-access reassessment step | 2 zone reductions, incl. one server-closet removal on a move out of infrastructure |
| **Screening lapse** (periodic re-screening not completed, or an adverse result) | Access suspended immediately on lapse, restored only on clearance | The screening record's status drives both logical and physical access | 2 individuals had **privileged logical access and server-closet access removed on a screening lapse**, restored after clearance — the same case recorded in HR-POL-004 §3 |
| Extended leave over 30 days | Suspended, not deleted, for the duration | Leave record | 1 case |
| Disciplinary suspension | Immediately, at the same time as logical access | Conduct case workflow | 1 case, coincident with the sanction issued to a member of management (2026-02-05) |
| Visitor departure | Badge collected at sign-out on the same day | Reception process; unreturned visitor badges are reported | 2 visitor badges not returned (both left in meeting rooms and recovered the same day); both recorded as exceptions and the sign-out prompt was changed at reception |
| Co-working location no longer used by any personnel | Operator asked to remove all entity personnel within 5 business days, and the removal confirmed in writing | Quarterly reconciliation against the roster | 1 location's list found to still contain a leaver **11 days after departure** — see below |

**A removal failure this period, recorded rather than omitted:** the quarterly reconciliation of the
co-working operators' access lists found one departed employee still listed at one location 11 days after
their departure date. The entity's own badge system had been correct; the gap was that the operator's
removal was requested by email and not confirmed. This is recorded as deficiency **D-2026-011** (Moderate,
no evidence of any access attempt after departure — verified against the operator's entry logs), and the
correction is that co-working removal is now a **checklist item with a required written confirmation**
before the offboarding checklist can be closed, and the reconciliation moved from quarterly to monthly for
provider-held lists. The provider's own access-removal obligation was added to its requirements annex at
the same time.

## 4. Periodic review of physical access against job responsibilities

**Physical access is reviewed periodically for consistency with job responsibilities.** The review runs on
the same quarterly cadence as the logical access review and is deliberately performed together with it, so
that an inconsistency between what a person can reach physically and what their role requires is visible
in one place.

| Review | Reviewer | Cadence | What is compared | Outcome required |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Quarterly badge entitlement review** | Each manager for their own reports; AI Security Lead for privileged zones; Head of People certifies the roster is complete | Quarterly (raised from semi-annual in 2025-12, at the same time as the logical review, because vendor-personnel churn changed the rate of change) | Every active badge and every zone against the individual's current role profile | Confirm, reduce, or revoke in writing; a manager who does not respond has the review escalated to their officer, and unreviewed badges are **suspended** at 10 business days |
| **Server-closet access review** | AI Security Lead and Director, IT Operations jointly | Quarterly, and after any infrastructure role change | The named list against the current infrastructure roster; target is the minimum number of people who can restore network service | Access is a named list, not a role grant; 6 individuals held it at the start of the period, 5 at the end |
| **Unescorted contractor status review** | Engagement owner and Third-Party Risk Manager | Quarterly, and on any engagement scope change | The 4 unescorted individuals against their current statements of work and screening currency | Revert to escorted where the justification no longer holds |
| **Provider-held access list reconciliation** | Facilities lead | **Monthly** (raised from quarterly after D-2026-011) | Co-working and landlord lists against the personnel roster | Written confirmation of every removal |
| **Badge-system-to-identity reconciliation** | Facilities lead with Security | Quarterly | Active badges against active identity records — catching a badge with no owner, or an owner with no employment record | Any orphan badge is deactivated the same day |
| Visitor log review | Facilities lead | Monthly | Entries against hosts; unreturned badges; out-of-hours entries | Investigate anomalies; unreturned badges deactivated |
| Physical access log review for privileged zones | AI Security Lead | Monthly, and event-driven on any out-of-hours entry | Server-closet entries against change records and incident records | Any entry without a corresponding work record is investigated |
| **Independent test** | Internal audit (second/third line) | Annually | Revocation timestamps against HR and supplier records; a sample of authorizations against their prerequisites; observation of tailgating and reception practice | Findings enter the deficiency register on the same terms as any other control |

**Results this period:** the reviews produced one server-closet removal, two zone reductions, one reversion
of a contractor from unescorted to escorted after a scope change, one orphan badge (a spare encoded during
a badge-printer test, never issued — deactivated the same day and the test procedure changed to use a
non-encoding test card), and the co-working finding at D-2026-011. Internal audit's observation pass found
that **reception did not challenge an unbadged individual on one of three attempts** during an announced
walkthrough; this is deficiency **D-2026-012** (Moderate), remediated by adding a challenge script to
reception onboarding, a standing "challenge, don't assume" item in the quarterly all-hands control briefing,
and a repeat unannounced test in 2026-Q3 whose result will be reported to the Audit Committee whether it
passes or not.

## 5. Restricting physical access to facilities and protected information assets

**Physical access to facilities and protected information assets is restricted to authorized personnel.**

| Layer | Control | Type | How it is enforced |
|---|---|---|---|
| Building perimeter (HQ) | Landlord-operated access control with 24/7 staffed reception during business hours; after-hours entry requires a building credential and is logged | Preventive + detective | Landlord; tenant list reviewed annually |
| **Suite entry** | Entity-controlled badge reader on every suite door; no mechanical override key held outside the entity; doors alarmed on prop-open beyond 60 seconds | Preventive + detective | Badge system, entity-administered |
| Reception | Visitors cannot pass reception unescorted; identity verified against pre-registration; visitor badge visually distinct from personnel badges; **standing instruction to challenge any unbadged individual** | Preventive | Procedure, with the observed weakness at D-2026-012 recorded |
| **Server closet** | Separate badge reader on a named-individual list, physically keyed independently of the building master suite; entry logged; **entries reviewed monthly against work records**; no landlord access | Preventive + detective | Badge system + a distinct lock; dual authorization to be added to the list |
| Locked storage — contracts, statutory registers, spare devices | Keyed cabinets; key custody with the General Counsel (records) and IT Operations (device store); key holders on a named list reviewed quarterly | Preventive | Physical key custody |
| **Protected information assets in production** | Not present in any entity facility. Evidence, assessment results and logs exist only in AWS; the no-local-copy control prevents an endpoint copy, and there is no path by which a production data set reaches a physical location the entity controls | **Preventive, by design** | Architecture + device policy — this is the strongest physical control the entity has, because it removes the asset from the physical attack surface rather than protecting it there |
| Endpoint devices | Full-disk encryption; screen lock at 5 minutes; MDM with remote wipe; USB mass storage blocked; cable locks available at HQ desks; devices not left in vehicles or checked baggage, per the remote standard | Preventive | MDM, enforced technically |
| Printing | Confidential printing disabled at the device; badge-release printing at HQ; **no printer at co-working or home locations is permitted for entity material above Internal** | Preventive | Print server configuration |
| Clear desk and clear screen | Required at all locations; tested during internal audit's walkthrough | Directive + detective | Procedure and observation |
| Environmental protection (HQ closet) | Landlord-provided cooling with a temperature alarm to IT Operations; smoke detection; no water-bearing services routed through the closet | Detective | Alarm to a monitored channel |
| Video recording | Building-operated at the perimeter and lobby, landlord-retained per its own schedule; **the entity operates no camera inside its suite** — a deliberate decision recorded on privacy grounds (DPIA reference), with the compensating controls being badge logging and the monthly log review | Detective (limited, disclosed) | Landlord; the limitation is stated rather than implied |
| Out-of-hours entry | Permitted, logged, and reviewed monthly; **an out-of-hours server-closet entry generates an alert** to the AI Security Lead rather than waiting for the monthly review | Detective | Badge system alerting |

**Limitations stated plainly.** Three of these controls are not fully within the entity's power and are
recorded as such rather than asserted: the building perimeter and its video retention are the landlord's;
the co-working locations are the operator's, which is why no asset above Internal may be held there; and
the physical security of every production information asset is AWS's, which the entity inherits and
monitors but does not operate. Where the entity has chosen *not* to implement a control — internal video
recording — the decision, the reason and the compensating controls are recorded, because an unstated
absence is indistinguishable from an oversight.

## 6. Recovering entity assets — badges, laptops and mobile devices

**The entity obtains or recovers its assets — including access devices, laptops and mobile devices — when
they are no longer needed.** Recovery is tracked per asset, not per person, so an item cannot be closed out
because the person left.

| Asset | Recovery requirement | Deadline | Verification | Current period |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Badge** | Physically collected, or deactivated and recorded as not returned | Deactivation on the last working day; physical collection on or before it | The badge system shows deactivation; the checklist records collected/not-returned | 3 departures: 2 badges physically collected, 1 remote employee's badge returned by courier and confirmed 4 days later — **deactivated on day one, so the physical delay carried no access risk**, and this is recorded as the reason deactivation and collection are tracked as separate items |
| **Laptop** | Returned to the HQ device store or the EU suite, wiped, and either re-imaged for reissue or securely disposed of with a certificate | Within 5 business days of departure for local staff; **prepaid return shipment issued on the last working day** for remote staff, with escalation at 10 business days | Asset register updated with the return date, the wipe confirmation and the disposition; **MDM remote-wipe is triggered on the last working day regardless of the physical return**, so the data is removed before the hardware comes back | 3 of 3 laptops recovered; one remote return took 12 business days and triggered the escalation — the device had been remotely wiped and locked on day one, and the case is recorded as an exception in the asset register |
| **Mobile device** — entity-issued to 12 on-call certified personnel | Returned and wiped; for BYOD, the entity work profile is removed remotely and the removal confirmed | On the last working day | MDM enrolment record shows removal | 1 entity-issued phone recovered; 2 BYOD work profiles removed remotely, both confirmed |
| Hardware security keys / MFA tokens | Collected or deactivated; **deactivation is authoritative** | Last working day | Identity system shows the authenticator removed | Handled as part of logical access removal |
| Cabinet keys, and the server-closet key where held | Physically collected and the key-holder list updated the same day; **if a key cannot be recovered the affected lock is re-keyed** | Last working day | Key-holder list | No server-closet key holder departed; one records-cabinet key recovered |
| Physical documents held by the individual | Returned or attested destroyed; the departure attestation covers this explicitly | Last working day | Signed departure attestation | 3 of 3 attestations signed |
| **Vendor-held entity devices** (2 of 11 vendor individuals hold entity laptops) | Returned per the contract, with the requirements annex naming the deadline and the provider's obligation to confirm the return | Within 5 business days of engagement end | Asset register plus the provider's written confirmation | 3 vendor departures; 1 held an entity laptop, returned in 3 business days |

**Rules that make recovery real:** the asset register is reconciled against the personnel roster quarterly,
so an unreturned device surfaces even if a checklist was closed prematurely; **remote wipe and access
deactivation are never dependent on physical recovery**, because the data risk must be closed on day one
while the hardware risk can take a week; an unrecovered asset past its escalation point is recorded as a
deficiency with an owner and a date rather than written off quietly; and disposal of any device that held
entity data requires a certificate of destruction retained with the asset record. The one 12-business-day
laptop return and the one 4-day badge return are both in the register — the control is credited for the
day-one wipe and deactivation, and the physical delays are recorded as the exceptions they are.

## 7. Physical access in incidents, changes and continuity

Physical access is treated as part of incident response and continuity, not as a separate discipline:

- **Loss or theft of a device** is a Sev-2 security incident by default and Sev-1 where the device may hold
  Confidential material. The runbook requires MDM lock and wipe immediately, credential revocation,
  assessment of whether personal data was involved (routed to the DPO immediately on suspicion, per the
  1-hour DPO response), and a post-incident review. **One device was reported lost this period** — a phone
  left in a taxi, reported by the individual within 40 minutes, remote-wiped within 12 minutes of the
  report, no Confidential material present, no notification required; the post-incident review changed the
  reporting guidance to name a single number to call rather than an email address.
- **Facility unavailability** is covered by the continuity plan: because production runs in AWS and 42 of 48
  personnel work remotely, loss of an office is a low-severity continuity event and the recovery exercise
  tests it annually. The plan does **not** claim resilience the entity lacks: the single-region dependency
  on us-west-2 remains the material availability limitation, Board-accepted as **R-022** and conditional on
  the quarterly restore test passing.
- **Tailgating and social engineering** are covered in annual security awareness training and tested by
  internal audit's observation pass; the failed challenge at D-2026-012 is the current open item.
- **Server-closet work** requires a change record; a closet entry with no corresponding change or incident
  record is investigated under §4's monthly log review.

## 8. Remote and home working standard

Because 42 of 48 personnel work primarily remotely, the physical control set extends to locations the
entity cannot inspect. The standard is enforced by what is technically prevented rather than by trust:

1. **No production evidence, assessment result or log may be stored on an endpoint.** This is prevented
   technically, not requested — the no-local-copy control introduced after case **D-2026-004**, where an
   individual downloaded sample data to a personal directory. That case is taught as a case study in
   awareness training with the individual's consent and identity removed.
2. Full-disk encryption, 5-minute screen lock, MDM enrolment and current OS patch level are conditions of
   access; a device out of posture loses access rather than receiving a reminder.
3. Work is not performed on personal or public computers; BYOD is limited to a managed work profile on
   mobile for on-call notification only.
4. Screens are not visible to others in shared or public spaces; calls involving Confidential material are
   not taken in public spaces — reinforced quarterly rather than assumed.
5. Devices are not left in vehicles or checked baggage, and loss is reported immediately to the single
   number in the runbook.
6. No printing of material above Internal classification; no removable media.
7. The standard is acknowledged annually alongside the Code of Conduct, and the acknowledgement rate is
   reported to the Audit Committee (48 of 48 for the current period).

**What this standard cannot achieve, stated as a limitation:** the entity cannot verify the physical
security of 42 home locations, and does not claim to. The compensating control is that there is nothing of
production value at those locations to protect — the asset is not there — combined with device encryption,
remote wipe, and access that depends on device posture rather than on physical possession. This limitation
and its compensating control are recorded in the risk register and disclosed in the customer-facing
description rather than left for a customer to discover.

## 9. Physical security perimeter — construction, openings and fire doors

**The perimeter of every entity-controlled area is a defined, physically sound boundary, not a notional
one.** The boundary is drawn at the suite door, because the entity is a tenant: the building envelope is
the landlord's, and the entity's perimeter is the suite it leases.

| Perimeter element | HQ suite | EU subsidiary suite | Verified how, and when |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Walls** | Slab-to-slab partition on all four sides — the demising walls run from the structural floor to the structural slab above, with no plenum bypass over the ceiling grid. Confirmed against the landlord's as-built drawing (drawing set A-201, rev C) and by physical inspection above two ceiling tiles | Slab-to-slab on the corridor and neighbour-facing walls; the window wall is the building curtain wall | Inspection at fit-out and re-inspected at the annual facility walkthrough (2026-02-11); **the drawing reference and the inspection note are retained with this procedure** |
| **Ceiling and floor voids** | No accessible void connects the suite to a neighbouring tenancy. The raised-floor void terminates at the demising wall with a fire-stopped closure | No raised floor; ceiling void closed at the demising wall | Annual walkthrough; re-checked after any landlord cabling work, which requires an escort under §2 |
| **External / suite doors** | Two doors, both solid-core with a rated frame, both on the entity badge reader, both alarmed on prop-open beyond 60 seconds, both fitted with a closer so the unattended state is *closed and locked*. **No mechanical override key exists outside entity custody** | One suite door, same specification, landlord building access outside it | Badge-system configuration export, reviewed quarterly with the access review; closer and latch operation tested at the annual walkthrough |
| **Windows** | 11 windows, all at the 4th floor with no accessible roof, ledge, adjacent structure or fire-escape landing below or beside them — **no window in the HQ suite is ground-level or otherwise reachable without equipment**. All are of the building's fixed and top-hung tilt type; every opening pane carries a **keyed restrictor limiting the opening to 100 mm**, key held with the facilities lead, and each is on the alarm zone in §12 | 4 windows, 2nd floor, facing an enclosed courtyard with no public access. Same keyed restrictors; **the two courtyard-facing panes additionally carry laminated security film** applied at fit-out | Restrictor presence and key custody checked at the annual walkthrough and after any window maintenance; **the fact that no opening is ground-level is the reason no bar or grille is fitted, and that reasoning is recorded here rather than left implicit** |
| **Ventilation and service penetrations** | HVAC is landlord-provided and ducted through the demising wall; every penetration over 150 mm on the entity boundary is fitted with a **welded anti-intrusion bar grille**, and smaller penetrations are fire-stopped and too small to pass a person or a hand-carried device. There is no independently accessible roof intake serving the suite alone | Same specification; the courtyard intake carries a grille and is within the alarmed zone | Penetration schedule held with the facility file; re-verified after any mechanical work, which is escorted |
| **Server closet within the suite** | An inner perimeter: slab-to-slab walls, no window, no ventilation penetration larger than 150 mm, its own badge reader on a named-individual list, its own lock keyed independently of the building master suite, and a motion detector in the alarm zone | Not applicable — no server room | Monthly log review (§4) plus the alarm test schedule in §12 |
| **Fire doors on the escape route** | Two: the suite's escape door onto the protected stair, and the stair door itself (landlord's). The suite escape door is **fire-rated to EN 1634-1 (30-minute integrity, FD30S with intumescent and cold-smoke seals)**, is **alarmed on any opening at all** — not merely on prop-open — reporting to the same monitored channel as the intrusion alarm, and is **fitted with a push-bar that fails safe to unlocked in the outward direction on power loss while remaining a locked, no-handle barrier from the stair side** | One escape door, same specification and rating | **Third-party inspection annually against EN 1634-1 and BS 8214 installation practice** — inspection certificate FD-2026-04 dated 2026-04-08 is retained in the facility file; door-closer force, seal condition, gap tolerance and self-closing operation are each recorded; the alarm on the escape door is included in the quarterly alarm test in §12 |

**The two rules that keep this a control rather than a description.** First, *every* opening on the entity
boundary — door, window, penetration — appears in exactly one row above and in exactly one alarm zone in
§12, and the annual walkthrough reconciles the two lists against each other; an opening present in the
building but absent from both lists is a finding, and reconciling in both directions is what makes an
omission visible. Second, an unattended perimeter is closed and locked by mechanism, not by intention:
closers on every door, restrictors on every opening pane, and a prop-open alarm on anything that can be
held open.

**Limitation, stated.** The building envelope, its ground-floor entrances, its loading bay and its roof are
the landlord's, and the entity does not assert them as its own controls. What the entity holds is the lease
schedule of the landlord's obligations, the annual tenant-list reconciliation, and the fact that its own
perimeter is a *second* boundary inside the landlord's — which is why the suite-level construction above is
specified at all.

## 10. Delivery, loading and incoming material

The entity receives little physical material — devices from the reseller, returned laptops, and legal post
— and it receives none of it at a loading bay it controls: HQ deliveries arrive at the building's ground-floor
goods entrance, which is the landlord's.

- **Access is separated from the rest of the suite.** Deliveries are received *at reception*, which is inside
  the suite door but is not a route to any working area, the server closet or any locked cabinet — a courier
  reaching reception has reached a dead end, and couriers are not admitted past it under §2.
- **Incoming material is inspected before it moves out of reception**, by the receiving facilities lead or
  the reception delegate. The inspection covers the outer packaging for damage, tampering or signs of
  concealment, and confirms the consignment against the expected-deliveries list before the packaging is
  opened. **Anything unexpected, unaddressed, leaking, unusually heavy for its size, or bearing wires,
  powder or an unexplained odour is not opened and not moved**: it is left in place, reception is cleared,
  and the runbook's single number is called — the same Sev-2 path as a device loss, escalating to building
  management and emergency services rather than to a locally improvised decision. Suspected explosive,
  chemical or otherwise hazardous material is **never** carried further into the suite to be examined
  somewhere quieter, which is the mistake this rule exists to prevent.
- **Registration.** Every incoming and outgoing consignment is recorded in the **delivery register** —
  date, carrier, tracking reference, sender or destination, description, whether it was inspected and by
  whom, and for anything that is or contains an entity asset, the asset-register identifier that ties it to
  §6's recovery tracking. 27 incoming and 9 outgoing consignments are recorded for the current period; the
  register is reviewed with the monthly access-log review, and a returned laptop with no register entry is
  the trigger that finds a recovery closed prematurely.
- **Incoming and outgoing are segregated.** Reception holds two separately labelled and physically distinct
  areas, and an item never occupies both: outbound consignments are staged on the outbound shelf only, with
  a same-day handover to the carrier where practicable, so an unopened inbound package cannot be handed to
  a courier and an outbound device cannot be mistaken for an unopened delivery.
- **The two doors are not open at once.** While the suite door is held open for a handover, the inner
  reception door to the working area stays closed — the 60-second prop-open alarm in §5 enforces this, and
  it is the reason the alarm threshold is 60 seconds rather than long enough to complete an unloading.
- **EU suite and co-working locations.** The EU suite follows the same rule at its own door, with the
  subsidiary manager as the receiving delegate. **No delivery of any entity asset to a co-working location
  is permitted** — devices are shipped to the individual's home or to HQ, never to a site operated by
  someone else, which is why the co-working rows in §1 need no delivery control at all.

**Neither the building nor the suite advertises what is inside it, and the entity does not publish where its
facilities are.** This is a decision the entity can make cheaply and has: the value it protects sits in AWS,
so there is nothing to be gained by having its offices findable.

## 12. Physical security monitoring — detection, alarms, protection and testing

**The premises are continuously monitored for unauthorized physical access**, and the monitoring is stated
here in full, including where it is deliberately thinner than a larger organisation's would be.

### 12.1 What monitors, at what times

**Continuity of coverage is the property being claimed**, and it is claimed precisely: every opening in §9
sits in an armed zone whenever the area behind it is unoccupied, the server closet is armed at all times,
and the badge log is continuous in both states. What is *not* claimed is continuous human observation or
continuous video of the interior — the entity has neither, says so, and compensates with detection at the
boundary plus reconciliation of every closet entry against an independently recorded reason.

### 12.2 Protecting the monitoring system itself

A monitoring system that can be read or switched off by an intruder is a description of a control, not a
control:

- **The design is Confidential.** Zone layout, detector positions and placements, arming schedules, panel
  location, engineer codes and the monitoring-centre account reference are classified Confidential under the
  data classification standard and held in the facility file, access-controlled to the AI Security Lead, the
  Director of IT Operations and the facilities lead — **three named people**. They are not in the staff wiki,
  not in a ticket, and not in this procedure, which is why the tables above name zones and mechanisms but no
  detector position, code or account reference.
- **The panel is inside a zone it protects.** The alarm control panel is mounted **inside the server closet**
  — behind the badge reader, in the always-armed motion zone — so reaching the panel requires first
  triggering the detector that reports to it and to the monitoring centre. The panel is not in reception, not
  in a corridor, and not in the landlord's riser.
- **Tamper protection is on the panel and on every detector.** The panel enclosure, every contact, every PIR
  and every glass-break unit carries a **tamper switch monitored on a 24-hour zone**, which reports whether
  the system is armed or disarmed; the cabling is monitored for open and short circuit; and enclosure
  opening, detector cover removal, cable cut and detector masking each raise the same alarm path as an
  intrusion.
- **It cannot be quietly disabled, locally or remotely.** Disarming requires an individual code — codes are
  named, never shared, and revoked on the last working day under §3 — and **every arm, disarm, bypass,
  zone-isolate and code change is logged at the panel and mirrored to the monitoring centre**, which is
  outside entity control and therefore cannot be edited by an entity insider. **Remote administration of the
  panel is disabled**; changes are made at the panel by an escorted engineer under a change record.
  Communication to the monitoring centre is **dual-path (IP with cellular failover) and supervised**, so
  cutting a line raises a path-fail alarm rather than producing silence, and the panel holds **a 12-hour
  standby battery** so a power cut is not a coverage gap.
- **Bypasses are events, not conveniences.** A zone isolation requires the AI Security Lead's approval, is
  time-boxed at the panel, appears in the monthly review, and is reconciled against a change or maintenance
  record — the same test §4 applies to closet entries.

### 12.4 The legal basis for monitoring, and the DPIA

Monitoring people's movements is regulated, and the applicable requirements are named rather than gestured
at. Badge logs, alarm records and the landlord's CCTV are **personal data**:

- **Applicable law.** **EU GDPR** for the EU subsidiary's suite and for EU personnel (Article 6(1)(f)
  legitimate interests as the lawful basis for badge logging and intrusion detection; Article 13 transparency;
  Article 35 DPIA where monitoring is systematic; Article 88 and **national employment-monitoring provisions**
  for workplace monitoring), the **UK GDPR and Data Protection Act 2018** for UK-based personnel, and in the
  primary geography, **state workplace-privacy and biometric statutes** — no biometric reader is used at any
  entity door, and that is a deliberate choice made to avoid processing biometric data for a control that a
  badge already delivers. Employment-law consultation obligations are met through the EU subsidiary's
  employee-representative consultation, completed at fit-out.
- **DPIA-2026-002** is the assessment covering physical monitoring: badge logging, intrusion detection, the
  always-armed closet zone, and reliance on landlord CCTV. It records the purpose, the necessity and
  proportionality test, the data minimisation applied, the retention periods, the DPO's opinion, and the
  residual risk with the Board's acceptance. **It is a controlled document in its own right, retained with
  the privacy record set and provided on request during an assessment** — it is referenced here rather than
  reproduced, and the reference is to a document that exists rather than to a decision someone remembers.
  **Its central conclusion is the reason there is no camera inside the suite**: for a 34-desk suite whose
  protected assets are not physically present, interior video was assessed as neither necessary nor
  proportionate when badge logging, an always-armed closet zone and monthly reconciliation achieve the
  detection objective with markedly less personal-data processing.
- **Transparency and minimisation.** Personnel are told in the privacy notice and at onboarding what is
  logged, why, how long it is kept and who sees it; **notices at the suite door and at reception** state that
  entry is access-controlled and logged and that the building operates CCTV, naming the controller and
  pointing to the notice. Badge and alarm records are retained **13 months** and are accessible only to the
  three named individuals above plus the DPO; they are used for security and safety purposes only, and
  **explicitly not for performance or attendance management** — a rule stated in the notice, in the Code of
  Conduct and in DPIA-2026-002 because it is the misuse a monitoring log most invites.
- **Landlord CCTV** is the landlord's processing under its own notice and retention schedule; the entity
  requests footage only by incident reference, records each request, and made one this period (D-2026-012).
