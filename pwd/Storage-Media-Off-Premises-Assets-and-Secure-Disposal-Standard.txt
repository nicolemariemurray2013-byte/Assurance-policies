# Storage Media, Off-Premises Assets and Secure Disposal Standard

**Document ID:** SEC-MED-046
**Version:** 2.0 — **effective 2026-03-09**, superseding v1.0 of 2025-05-06
**Owner:** Director, IT Operations
**Approved by:** CISO 2026-03-04, per the approval authority in GOV-POL-040 §3
**Next scheduled review:** 2026-12-31 (Annually, and on any change to the removable-media position, the endpoint fleet, the)
disposal supplier, the office footprint, or a lease approaching its end
**Scope:** All storage media in any form — fixed media inside entity equipment, removable media, cloud-held
volumes and snapshots, and paper as a storage medium where it carries classified information — across their full
life cycle of acquisition, use, transportation, reuse and disposal. All assets used or held outside the
entity's premises. All equipment disposal and re-use, and the end-of-lease removal of security controls.

**Related:** SEC-END-043 (endpoint devices and remote working), SEC-PHY-044 (physical threats and secure
areas), OPS-EQP-045 (equipment siting, utilities, cabling and maintenance), SEC-PRO-013 (physical access),
SEC-AST-020 (asset inventory and classification), SEC-AST-041 (asset return and IPR), GOV-CLS-018
(classification scheme and handling), SEC-CRY-024 (cryptography and key management), SEC-DLP-027 (information
transfer and monitoring), TPR-PRO-016 (supplier risk).

## 1. The media inventory, and what "storage media" means here

| Media class | Where it exists | Classification it may hold | Life-cycle owner |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Endpoint fixed media** — 52 laptop SSDs, 12 mobile devices | With individuals, five geographies | Up to **Internal**; Confidential only transiently in a brokered session; **Restricted never at rest** | IT Operations |
| **Network equipment flash** — firewall, switch, controller, access points, UPS management card | HQ closet, EU alcove | Configuration only (Internal), including credentials material | Platform Engineering |
| **Printer storage** — 2 multi-function devices with internal drives | HQ, EU suite | Up to Internal (spool) | IT Operations |
| **Cloud block and object storage, snapshots, backups** | AWS us-west-2 | Up to **Restricted** | Platform Engineering |
| **Removable media** | **None permitted, none held** — see §3 | — | — |
| **Paper** | Two keyed records cabinets; printed output | Up to Confidential; Restricted printing blocked | Facilities lead / record owner |
| **Backup media** | AWS only; **no tape, no removable backup media anywhere** | Up to Restricted | Platform Engineering |

**The material fact that shapes this standard:** the entity holds **no removable storage media at all**, and
production data lives in cloud storage the entity manages logically but does not physically hold. That makes
most of this standard's removable-media requirements a matter of *enforced prohibition plus a controlled
exception path*, rather than of handling procedures for media in circulation — and it makes the disposal
requirements bite on endpoints, network equipment and printers rather than on a media library. Every requirement
below is answered in those terms rather than by describing a media library the entity does not have.

## 2. Storage media through the life cycle, per the classification scheme (7.10-R1)

Media are managed at every life-cycle stage against the handling rules in GOV-CLS-018, and the stage-by-stage
position is recorded so that a gap in one stage is visible rather than hidden by strength in another:

| Stage | Control | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| **Acquisition** | Endpoints and network equipment are bought only from the approved supplier list under IT Operations' specification, which requires **hardware full-disk-encryption support and a firmware-level secure-erase capability** — a procurement requirement precisely so that disposal is possible later. Devices are **enrolled and registered in the asset register before issue**, with the storage serial captured; an unregistered device cannot obtain a device certificate and therefore cannot reach entity systems. Removable media is **not purchasable** — the category is blocked on the corporate card policy and there is no approved supplier | Asset register; procurement specification; purchase records |
| **Use** | Encryption enforced at rest on every endpoint (§6.2); classification-based handling rules per GOV-CLS-018; Restricted material rendered through a brokered session and never written locally, the **no-local-copy control from case D-2026-004**; printer spool cleared on job completion; cloud volumes and snapshots encrypted with entity-managed keys | MDM encryption compliance report (52/52); brokered-session configuration; key inventory |
| **Transportation** | Endpoints in transit are hand-carried, never in checked baggage, never left in a vehicle (§4.2); equipment moved for repair or disposal travels **wiped, by tracked courier, under a chain-of-custody record** (§4.3, §5.5); **no media is ever posted** | Chain-of-custody log; courier records |
| **Storage** | §4 for off-premises; **§5** for on-premises storage conditions | Inspection records |
| **Reuse** | §6.3 — cryptographic erase plus re-image from the golden baseline before any reissue | Reissue records |
| **Disposal** | §6 — verified media identification, destruction or erasure appropriate to the classification, certificate retained, register updated only after the certificate arrives | Certificates of destruction; asset register |

### 2.1 Topic-specific policy on removable storage media (7.10-R2)

A **topic-specific policy on the management of removable storage media** is established as part of GOV-POL-040's
register and is stated here in full, because the whole of it fits and paraphrase would obscure it:

| Rule | Detail | Enforced by |
|---|---|---|
| **Removable mass storage is prohibited** | No USB flash drive, external HDD/SSD, SD or microSD card, optical disc, or tape may be connected to any entity endpoint or server, whether entity-owned or personal | **MDM device-control policy blocking the USB mass-storage class and SD card slots on all 52 laptops**; not a request, a block |
| Non-storage USB is permitted | Keyboards, mice, headsets, docks, displays, hardware security keys and wired network adapters are allowed by device class | Same policy, class allowlist |
| **Hardware security keys are an intentional exception** | FIDO2 keys are removable devices but hold no information storage in the sense of this policy; they are issued, registered to an individual, and reported lost like any asset | Identity provider registration; asset register |
| **Exception path exists and is narrow** | Where a genuine business need arises — the only credible case being a customer or regulator who can accept evidence no other way — an exception requires **CISO approval**, is **time-bound to a single transfer**, uses an **entity-issued hardware-encrypted device**, and the transfer is **logged and monitored**. **Zero exceptions have been requested or granted since v1.0**, which is recorded rather than presented as a policy with no demand | Exception register (empty, and audited as empty) |
| Personal media is prohibited | A personally owned drive may not be connected even to a personal device holding the entity's mobile work profile; the work profile cannot write to external storage | MDM work-profile policy |
| Cloud storage is the sanctioned alternative | Approved services with entity identity and DLP inspection; the policy names them so the prohibition comes with a route rather than only a wall | SEC-DLP-027 |
| Found media is never connected | Any media found in an office or received unsolicited is handed to IT Operations and destroyed, **never inserted** — including to "see what's on it" | Awareness module; destruction log (3 items this period, 2 unsolicited vendor drives and 1 found in the HQ lift lobby) |
| Communication | The policy is issued to **all personnel and to the 11 named vendor individuals** at onboarding and annually, acknowledged in the awareness platform, and is stated in the vendor requirements annex so it binds people who are not employees | Acknowledgement records: **48/48 personnel, 11/11 vendor individuals** |

The policy is **communicated to anyone who uses or handles removable storage media** — which, given the block, is
nobody in normal operation; it is nonetheless communicated to everyone, because the population that needs the
rule is the population that might otherwise try, and because the found-media rule applies to people who handle
media they did not obtain.

## 3. The removable-media position, and how it is verified rather than assumed

A prohibition believed to be in force but never checked is the most dangerous kind of control, so the position
is verified on three independent paths:

1. **Configuration** — the MDM device-control policy is in the version-controlled baseline; drift is reported
   daily, and a device out of compliance is quarantined from entity access by the posture check (SEC-END-043 §2).
2. **Behaviour** — endpoint telemetry alerts on any mass-storage device *attempt*, whether or not it succeeds,
   because an attempt is a signal about intent or ignorance. **7 attempts this period**, all blocked, all
   followed up: 5 were personal phones being charged (benign, and the reason the policy explicitly permits
   charge-only), 1 was a personal drive at a home location (coaching, no data moved), 1 was a conference-supplied
   drive (destroyed, and cited in the awareness module).
3. **Physical** — the quarterly closet and office inspections check for unauthorised attached devices, and the
   device store is reconciled against the asset register (OPS-EQP-045 §4.4, §2.3).

**Servers:** production instances are cloud instances with no physical port to populate; block-device
attachment is an API action governed by IAM and change control, logged in CloudTrail and alarmed on
out-of-change attachment — which is the cloud-native equivalent of a port control and is claimed as such rather
than by analogy to a USB block.

## 4. Security of assets off-premises (7.9)

The entity is **remote-first — 42 of 48 personnel work primarily outside an entity office** — so off-premises is
the normal condition, not the exception. This section is therefore written as the primary control set, not as an
addendum to an office regime.

### 4.1 Management authorization for off-premises use, including BYOD (7.9-R1)

- **Standing authorization** for off-premises use of an entity-issued laptop and mobile device is granted by
  the individual's manager as part of the issue record at onboarding, and re-affirmed at each **annual access
  review** — so authorization is a live record with a date, not a permission granted once in 2023 and never
  revisited.
- The authorization is **specific**: it names the device (asset tag and storage serial), the individual, the
  approved work locations (home, an approved co-working location, entity offices, and travel), and the maximum
  classification permitted at that location. It is recorded in the asset register against the device and in the
  HR record against the person.
- **BYOD** is authorized only in the single form permitted by SEC-END-043 §2.4 — a **managed mobile work
  profile on a personal phone**, with **zero personal laptops authorized** and none present in the fleet.
  Authorization is per-device, requires acceptance of the selective-wipe terms, and is recorded in the MDM
  enrolment record. The count is reported at each access review (**31 personal phones enrolled with a work
  profile; 0 personal laptops**), so an unauthorized category would show up as a number rather than as a
  narrative.
- **Vendor personnel** are not authorized to hold entity assets off-premises at all, except for the 3 named
  individuals issued entity laptops under their individual undertakings (HR-PRO-042 §4.3); the other 8 access
  entity systems only from their employer's managed devices via the brokered session, which is authorization by
  architecture — nothing to leave unattended because nothing is held.
- Authorization is **withdrawn** on the terminate-or-change triggers in HR-PRO-042 §5, and withdrawal is
  enforced by device wipe and certificate revocation rather than by an expectation that the device stops being
  used.

### 4.2 Unattended devices, manufacturers' instructions, and shoulder surfing (7.9-R2)

| Requirement element | Rule and its enforcement |
|---|---|
| **Not left unattended in public or unsecured places** | Absolute rule: a device is either **on the person, locked in a room the individual controls, or in a locked container**. Specifically prohibited — left on a co-working desk while away, in an unattended vehicle (including a locked boot), in checked baggage, in a hotel room without the safe or a cable lock, or at a conference cloakroom. At **co-working locations nothing is left overnight at all**, which is why **D-2026-011** (unconfirmed removal of a co-working locker) was raised as a deficiency rather than tolerated: the control depends on the absence being verified |
| **Manufacturers' instructions observed** | The issue pack states the manufacturer's environmental limits and the practical consequences: no charging or storing in a vehicle in heat or freezing conditions; not left in direct sun; **no charging on soft furnishings** (a fire condition, and an insurer point per OPS-EQP-045 §5.6); kept dry, and a liquid event reported immediately rather than dried out and used; kept out of dusty environments; **no exposure to strong electromagnetic fields** — which in practice means devices are not placed on or beside magnetic-field sources, and is stated because the requirement names it. Lithium-battery handling and the designated charging bench at HQ apply on premises |
| **Shoulder surfing in public** | **Privacy filters standard-issue on all 52 laptops and mandatory in any public setting** — transit, aircraft, co-working, cafés, conferences; **5-minute automatic screen lock** and manual lock on stepping away, both MDM-enforced; a rule against working on Confidential material in a public setting at all (the brokered session for Restricted makes it technically impossible to hold, and policy forbids the display); **no speaker use** for calls in public and no dictation of classified content; positioning guidance to sit with the screen away from through-routes, reinforced in the awareness module with the location self-assessment asking whether the screen is visible from outside a home workspace |
| Verification | The location self-assessment (annual, and on any move) covers all of the above; **48 of 48 completed this period**, generating 6 privacy-filter issues and 2 desk-repositioning recommendations. **4 off-premises security events** were reported this period — 2 near-miss unattended-device reports, 1 attempted shoulder-surf at an airport lounge reported by the individual, 1 device left in a taxi and recovered within 40 minutes with no unlock attempt in the logs — all recorded, all with the reporting treated as the desired behaviour |

### 4.3 Chain of custody on transfer between individuals or parties (7.9-R3)

When off-premises equipment is transferred among different individuals or interested parties, a **chain-of-custody
log** is maintained, holding **at minimum the names and organizations of those responsible**:

| Field | Captured |
|---|---|
| Asset tag, model, storage serial | Yes, from the asset register |
| **Releasing individual and their organization** | Yes — name and organization, including "the entity" where internal |
| **Receiving individual and their organization** | Yes — for a courier, the carrier's organization and the consignment number stand as the responsible party for the transit leg |
| Date, time and reason for transfer | Yes |
| Classification of information the device held before transfer | Yes |
| **Secure-deletion action performed before transfer, and its verification** | Yes — and this is a required field, not optional |
| Condition on release and on receipt | Yes, with photographs for damaged items |
| Authorization reference | Yes (§4.4) |

**Information that does not need to be transferred with the asset is securely deleted before transfer.** In
practice this means the default is that *nothing* transfers with the asset: a device leaving an individual's
custody is **remote-wiped and the wipe confirmed before despatch**, so the receiving party — internal
reallocation, warranty repair, or disposal — receives a device with no entity information on it. The only
exception is an internal transfer where the device stays enrolled and moves directly between two authorized
individuals, and there the enrolment, not the wipe, is the control, and the log records that choice explicitly.

**Transfers this period: 17** — 9 refresh reallocations (all wiped and re-imaged), 3 warranty repairs (all wiped
and confirmed; one required disk retention and destruction per OPS-EQP-045 §5.5), 4 disposals (§6), 1
recovered-from-taxi device (returned to the individual after a posture check and a log review). Every one has a
complete custody record; the completeness of the *secure-deletion* field is sampled at internal assessment
because it is the field most likely to be left blank when someone is in a hurry.

### 4.4 Authorization and record for removal from the premises (7.9-R4, and 7.10-R4)

Both controls raise the same requirement — authorization plus an auditable record of removals — and the entity
answers it once, honestly, in terms of what "removal from the premises" means for a remote-first entity:

- **Endpoints are issued for off-premises use by design**, so a per-occasion removal permit would be a form
  nobody would complete truthfully. The authorization is therefore the **standing, device-specific,
  annually-reviewed issue authorization** in §4.1, and the record of "removals" is the **asset register's
  custody state**: every device has a named custodian, a location class and an authorization date, reconciled
  against the HR roster quarterly. This is a stronger audit trail than a sign-out sheet, because it is complete
  by construction rather than dependent on someone remembering to sign.
- **Removal of anything that is *not* routinely off-premises requires explicit, recorded authorization**, and
  this is where the per-occasion control lives:

| Item removed | Authorization | Record |
|---|---|---|
| **Network equipment from the HQ closet** (including a spare or a failed unit) | Director IT Operations, under a change record | Change record + custody log + asset register state |
| **Any storage media** | **CISO** (and there is none to remove — the register would show it) | Exception register |
| **Paper records above Internal** | Record owner, and only for a stated purpose with a return date | Records-cabinet removal log |
| A printer or its drive | Director IT Operations; the drive is treated as data-bearing | Custody log + service record |
| A device going to disposal | Director IT Operations | Custody log + certificate of destruction |
| Loaner device issued temporarily | IT Operations, with a return date | Loaner log; **4 of 4 loaners currently reconciled** |

- The records are retained 3 years and are sampled at internal assessment. The quarterly reconciliation is what
  makes the trail real: it found one unreturned loaner in 2025 (recovered) and is the mechanism that would
  surface a silent removal.

### 4.5 Location tracking and remote wiping (7.9-R5)

| Capability | Coverage | State and verification |
|---|---|---|
| **Remote wipe / remote disable** | **52/52 laptops, 12/12 entity mobiles, 31/31 personal phones (work profile — selective wipe of the profile only)** | MDM-enforced; **tested quarterly on a loaner device**, 4 of 4 successful this period, with the median time-to-wipe recorded. Exercised for real once (the taxi case, cancelled on recovery within the same hour) |
| **Location tracking** | Entity-owned laptops and entity mobiles: **enabled**. Personal phones: **not enabled** | This split is deliberate and is stated rather than glossed: enabling location tracking on a personally owned device is not proportionate and, in **two of the entity's five jurisdictions, employee-privacy law makes it legally problematic**. The compensating position is that a personal device holds only a work profile whose content is a rendered view, and the profile is selectively wipeable. The reasoning, the legal basis and the compensating control are recorded here because a bare "location tracking is implemented" would be false for a third of the fleet |
| **Purpose limitation, disclosed** | Both capabilities | Location and telemetry data are used **only** for loss, theft and security-incident investigation, are not used for performance or attendance monitoring, are access-restricted to IT Operations and the CISO, are retained 90 days, and this purpose limitation is **disclosed to personnel in the endpoint standard and the acceptable-use acknowledgement** — which is what makes the monitoring lawful in the relevant jurisdictions as well as trusted |
| Remote lockout and passcode reset | Full fleet | MDM; used 3 times this period on lock-screen lockouts |
| Trigger | — | The loss/theft procedure (SEC-END-043 §2.3) makes wipe the default within 4 hours of a confirmed loss, without waiting for certainty, on the reasoning that a recoverable device re-images cheaply and an unwiped lost device does not |

### 4.6 Permanently installed off-premises equipment (7.9-R6)

The entity has **no permanently installed equipment outside its premises** — no antenna, mast, kiosk, ATM,
sensor, camera, digital signage, or unattended appliance at any third-party site. This is a stated fact
verified against the asset register at each annual assessment, not an omission. The three provider-operated
co-working locations hold **no entity equipment whatsoever**, which is why they appear in this standard only as
locations where devices are used and never left, and why **D-2026-011** matters: the one thing that could have
become de-facto permanently installed equipment was a storage locker, and the control is that it was removed.

Were such equipment ever installed, the pre-installation assessment in SEC-PHY-044 §3.1 would be the gate, and
this section would need to specify the measures 7.9-R6 names — monitoring, physical and environmental threat
protection, physical access control and tamper-proofing, and logical access control. Recording the trigger is
the honest way to hold a requirement that is currently not applicable, because "not applicable" without a
trigger becomes wrong the day the facts change.

## 5. On-premises storage conditions and media handling (7.10-R3)

Storage media are stored in a **safe, secure environment according to classification and protected against
environmental threats, in accordance with manufacturers' specifications**:

| Media | Storage environment | Environmental protection | Manufacturer's specification |
|---|---|---|---|
| Spare and loaner laptops (4) | **Keyed device store** inside the badge-controlled suite, not on open shelving | Within the office's monitored range; not adjacent to plant or a water-bearing service | Stored charged to 50–80 % and rotated, per the battery manufacturer's storage guidance — a real specification that is easy to ignore and is therefore in the quarterly checklist |
| Failed devices awaiting destruction | **Locked cabinet in the closet, segregated and labelled "awaiting destruction"** so they cannot be mistaken for stock and reissued | Closet environmental monitoring (OPS-EQP-045 §2.5) | Not applicable beyond containment |
| Retained disks pending destruction | Same locked cabinet, in tamper-evident bags, itemised on the pending-destruction list | As above | As above |
| Network equipment flash | Inside equipment in the locked closet | Temperature, humidity, water, dust and vibration controls per OPS-EQP-045 §2.3 | Ambient limits monitored and alarmed |
| **Paper at Confidential** | Two keyed records cabinets in the internal corridor, not reception | Cabinets are not beneath a water-bearing service — re-verified after the 2026-01 pipe reroute (**D-2026-015**); fire detection in the suite | Not applicable |
| Cloud volumes, snapshots, backups | AWS us-west-2, multi-AZ, encrypted | **Inherited** facility environmentals; the entity's own control is multi-AZ placement and **quarterly restore testing**, which is the condition on which the Board accepted **R-022** | Provider specification |
| **Removable media** | **None held**; the pending-destruction cabinet is the only place media ever sits detached, and only briefly | — | — |

The pending-destruction cabinet is deliberately a *locked, labelled, itemised* holding place with a target of
**no item held longer than 30 days**: the failure mode this prevents is a box of dead disks accumulating in a
corner for two years, which is how organisations end up unable to say what they destroyed. Current contents: **1
item (a failed access point), held 11 days**; longest hold this period, 26 days.

## 6. Secure reuse and disposal (7.10-R7, 7.10-R8, 7.14)

### 6.1 Identification of items requiring secure disposal (7.14-R1, and 7.10-R7's identification element)

**Every item is verified for the presence of storage media before disposal or re-use**, and the verification is
a recorded step performed against a **standing media-bearing determination held on the asset type**, so the
question is answered from a specification rather than from an engineer's guess in the moment:

| Asset type | Media-bearing? | Verified how |
|---|---|---|
| Laptop | Yes — SSD | Asset record holds the storage serial; physically confirmed at teardown |
| Mobile device | Yes — internal flash | Asset record |
| **Multi-function printer** | **Yes — internal drive, and this is the one people forget**; it is flagged in bold on the asset record and in the disposal checklist | Asset record; service contract requires drive return on replacement |
| Firewall, switch, wireless controller, access point | Yes — configuration flash holding credentials material | Asset record |
| UPS management card | Yes — configuration flash | Asset record |
| Environmental sensor, leak detector | Yes — trivial config flash; treated as media-bearing anyway | Asset record |
| Monitor, dock, keyboard, mouse, cable, AV display | **No** — determination recorded so the checklist is not padded | Asset record |
| Hardware security key | Holds key material, not information storage; destroyed as a credential | Identity provider deregistration + destruction |

Where a determination cannot be made confidently, the item is **treated as media-bearing and physically
destroyed** — the asymmetry is deliberate, because the cost of shredding a monitor is trivial next to the cost
of releasing a drive.

### 6.2 Erasure and destruction methods, proportional to sensitivity (7.10-R7, 7.14-R2)

Sensitive data and licensed software are removed or **securely overwritten using techniques that render the
original information non-retrievable — never the standard delete function, and never a filesystem format
alone**:

| Classification held | Destination | Method | Why this method |
|---|---|---|---|
| Up to Internal | **Internal reuse** | **Cryptographic erase** — destroy the full-disk-encryption key in the device's secure element — followed by re-provisioning and a re-image from the golden baseline | The data was never stored unencrypted, so key destruction makes it non-retrievable; the re-image removes configuration and licensed software |
| Up to Internal | External disposal, resale or donation | **Cryptographic erase plus firmware secure-erase (ATA Sanitize / NVMe Format with secure-erase setting), verified**, then physical destruction by the certified vendor | Belt and braces where the entity loses custody |
| **Confidential or Restricted held at any time**, or classification uncertain, or the device failed | External disposal | **Physical destruction — shredding to the vendor's stated particle size — with a serialised certificate**; no erase-and-resell | Verification of an erase on a failed device is not possible, so destruction is the only defensible method |
| Network equipment | Any | **Configuration wiped to factory default, then credentials rotated on the assumption the flash may be recoverable**, then physical destruction of the flash where the device leaves entity custody | Configuration flash recovery is plausible and the contents are credentials material; rotating rather than trusting the wipe is the control |
| **Printer** | Any | Drive removed and **physically destroyed**; the chassis is disposed of separately | The drive is the risk, not the chassis |
| **Paper** | — | **Cross-cut shredding on site** for Internal; **Confidential shredded on site by the record owner**, never placed in a confidential-waste sack for later collection | On-site destruction removes the custody gap that a waste sack creates |
| **Cloud storage** | Decommission | **Cryptographic erasure by KMS key deletion** for the data set, plus deletion of volumes, snapshots and their copies, verified by an inventory query that the snapshot count is zero; the provider's media sanitisation is **inherited** and evidenced by its audit reports | The entity cannot shred a provider's disk; key destruction is the control it genuinely holds, and saying so is more accurate than claiming a sanitisation it does not perform |
| Licensed software | All routes | Licences deactivated and reclaimed before erasure, and the reclaim recorded — because an unreclaimed licence is both a cost and a residual identifier | Licence register |

**Logging of disposals to maintain an audit trail (7.10-R7):** every disposal produces a record holding asset
tag, storage serial, highest classification ever held, method, date, performer or vendor, the **serialised
certificate of destruction**, and the licence-reclaim reference. The asset register is moved to `DISPOSED`
**only after the certificate is received** — a sequence chosen because a register entry closed in anticipation
is how disposals become unevidenced. **4 disposals this period, 4 certificates on file**, reconciled at the
quarterly asset review.

### 6.3 Secure deletion or formatting before reuse (7.10-R7's reuse element)

Reuse is the common path — 9 devices were reallocated at this period's refresh — and it is controlled as
tightly as disposal: cryptographic erase, **re-image from the golden baseline rather than a cleanup of the
outgoing user's profile**, a new device certificate, re-enrolment in MDM, and a posture check that must pass
before access is granted. The outgoing user's profile is never merely deleted, and the device is never handed
directly from one individual to another without passing through IT Operations — because a device that has not
been through the pipeline cannot be asserted to be clean. Devices returned from external repair are treated as
**untrusted and re-imaged** on the same reasoning (OPS-EQP-045 §5.7).

### 6.4 Risk assessment on damaged devices holding sensitive data (7.10-R8)

A **risk assessment is performed on any damaged device containing sensitive data to decide whether it must be
physically destroyed rather than repaired or discarded.** The assessment is a short recorded determination, made
by IT Operations with CISO sign-off where the answer is not obvious, weighing:

1. the **highest classification the device ever held** (from the asset record, not from recollection);
2. whether an **erase can be executed and verified** — a device that will not power on cannot be verifiably
   erased, which alone usually decides it;
3. whether **repair requires the storage to leave entity custody**;
4. whether the vendor will accept the unit **without** its original disk;
5. the **replacement cost** against the repair cost, so the answer is not distorted by an attempt to save a
   trivial sum.

**Default: if an erase cannot be verified, the device is physically destroyed.** Applied this period: 2 laptops
were destroyed rather than repaired (one spill case that would not power on, one with SSD failure), and of the 3
warranty repairs, **1 proceeded only because the vendor accepted the unit with the original disk retained and
destroyed** — the determination is on file for each, including the reasoning, because the value of this control
is in the cases where destroying the asset was the more expensive choice and was made anyway.

### 6.5 Removal of labels and markings (7.14-R3)

Before any equipment leaves entity custody — **including resale and donation to charity, which the requirement
names and which is exactly where this step gets skipped** — the following are removed and the removal is a
signed-off checklist line:

- entity asset tags, barcodes and inventory labels;
- **classification labels and handling markings**;
- owner, system, hostname, network and rack-position labels, including handwritten port and patch labels
  (OPS-EQP-045 §4.5's labelling scheme is informative to an attacker: it names devices and topology);
- entity branding, stickers and engraved marks;
- configuration-embedded identifiers — hostname, network names, certificates, saved wireless profiles,
  management addresses, and the entity's own name in device descriptions;
- support-contract and warranty labels naming the entity to a third party.

The entity's current practice is **destruction rather than resale or donation for all data-bearing equipment**,
so in most cases the labels are destroyed with the asset; the checklist exists nonetheless because non-data-bearing
items (monitors, docks) **are** donated, and a donated monitor with an asset tag and a classification sticker
still discloses the entity's inventory scheme. **2 monitors and 3 docks were donated this period**, all
de-labelled and signed off.

### 6.6 Equipment leaving for maintenance (cross-reference)

Devices leaving for maintenance are handled under §4.3's chain of custody and OPS-EQP-045 §5.5: wiped and
verified before despatch, tracked courier, custody record naming the responsible individuals and organisations,
disk retained and destroyed where the repair requires disk replacement, and **re-imaged and re-enrolled on
return rather than trusted**. This section exists so that OPS-EQP-045's cross-reference resolves to a stated
rule rather than to an implication.

### 6.7 Removal of security controls at end of lease or on vacating premises (7.14-R4)

The entity **considers the removal of security controls such as access controls and surveillance equipment at
the end of a lease or on moving out**, and the consideration is documented with its three named inputs:

| Input the requirement names | The entity's position |
|---|---|
| **Lease agreements** | Both leases are reviewed for reinstatement and removal obligations, and the review is recorded against the lease. The HQ lease requires reinstatement of alterations, which **includes the server closet's entity-fitted lock, the badge reader, the containment and the sensors**; the EU lease requires the wall enclosure's removal and making good. Knowing this before the notice period is what makes an orderly removal possible |
| **Risk of leaving systems holding sensitive information for the next tenant** | The itemised list of entity-fitted controls that hold information is maintained now, not written at exit: the **badge reader and its access-control head-end (holding names, badge IDs and 12 months of entry logs)**, the **prop-open alarm**, the **environmental sensors and leak detector** (holding readings and the on-call webhook), the **network equipment** (holding configuration and credentials), the **printers** (spool), and the **as-built cabling drawing and labels** (topology). Each is on the vacating checklist with a required action: export the retained record, then **wipe and destroy or remove**. The badge system is the sharpest case — leaving it behind would hand the next tenant the entity's personnel roster and movement history — so it is marked **remove-and-destroy, never leave, never transfer to the landlord** |
| **Ability to reuse the controls at the next facility** | Assessed per item: the badge reader head-end and the environmental sensors are **reusable after a factory reset and credential rotation** and are earmarked for reuse; the cabling, containment and the closet lock are **not economically reusable** and are treated as reinstatement waste; the network equipment moves with the entity, wiped and reconfigured in transit under §4.4 authorization |

**Trigger and state:** neither lease is within its notice period (HQ break option 2028-06, EU 2027-11), so the
checklist is a prepared control rather than an executed one. It is **reviewed annually alongside this standard**
and becomes an owned project at notice service. The co-working locations have no entity-fitted control to remove
— **D-2026-011** was precisely the case of a fitted item at such a location, and its removal is the small-scale
rehearsal of this control.
