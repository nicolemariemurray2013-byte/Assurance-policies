# Data Masking, De-identification and Pseudonymization Standard

**Document ID:** SEC-MSK-043
**Version:** 2.1 — **effective 2026-08-14**, superseding v1.4 of 2025-09-30
**Owner:** Data Protection Officer (policy and adequacy); Head of Platform Engineering (implementation)
**Approved by:** CISO 2026-08-14; DPO 2026-08-14; General Counsel 2026-08-12 (legal and regulatory positions)

**Scope.** Every use of masking, obfuscation, pseudonymization, anonymization, tokenization, redaction and
aggregation applied to sensitive data held or processed by the entity — customer evidence documents and their
contents, assessment results, personal data of customer personnel and of the entity's own personnel, credentials
and secrets, and any extract of the above taken for development, testing, support, analytics, audit or
demonstration. The reference implementation is AWS account **471885797689**, region **us-west-2**.

Companion documents: the logical access control and authentication standard (the access control policy this
standard is aligned to); the information asset inventory and data classification standard (what is sensitive, and
at what level); the cryptographic standard and key management procedure (the key material tokenization and
deterministic pseudonymization depend on); the audit testing procedure (isolated copies for audit); the privacy
notice and the choice, consent and collection limitation procedure (the lawful basis and purpose limits masking
operates inside).

## 1. Policy, and what it is aligned to

**Masking is a means of enforcing the access control policy where access itself cannot be withheld.** Where a
person or process has no business need for a data element, the first control is to deny access to it; masking is
used when access to the surrounding record is legitimate but access to the element is not, or when a copy of data
must exist outside its original protective boundary. Masking is therefore never a substitute for access control —
it is applied **in addition** to it, and a masked dataset remains subject to the classification, retention and
access rules of the data it derives from unless it has been shown to be anonymous under §4.

Four requirements govern every masking decision, and each is recorded for each decision:

1. **The access control policy.** The logical access control and authentication standard defines least privilege,
   role-based entitlement, need-to-know and the joiner-mover-leaver process. Masking implements the same rule at
   field level: a role sees the minimum data elements its function requires, and no more. The role definitions in
   §3 are the same role definitions used for entitlement, not a parallel set — a divergence between the two would
   mean a role could see through one control what the other denied.
2. **Business requirements.** Masking must leave the data fit for the purpose it is being used for; an
   unusable dataset drives people to request the unmasked one, which is the failure mode this standard exists to
   prevent. Each masking rule in §3 records the purpose it serves and the utility it must preserve (format,
   referential integrity, distribution, or none).
3. **Applicable legislation and regulation.** Positions taken with the General Counsel and DPO, recorded in the
   legal, regulatory and contractual requirements register: **GDPR/UK GDPR** — pseudonymized data remains personal
   data (Article 4(5)) and stays in scope for rights, security and breach obligations, while pseudonymization is
   an explicit Article 32 security measure and supports Article 25 data-protection-by-design; genuinely anonymous
   data falls outside the Regulation, but only where re-identification is not reasonably possible, which is what
   §4 tests. **CCPA/CPRA** — de-identified data is excluded from "personal information" only where the business
   maintains the technical safeguards, business processes prohibiting re-identification, and the process
   preventing inadvertent release that §3 and §5 provide, so the exclusion is conditional on the controls being
   operated. **PCI DSS** — the entity does not store, process or transmit cardholder data; the position is
   maintained by the input controls in §3.6 and is re-tested annually rather than assumed. **HIPAA** — not
   applicable; no protected health information is in scope, and the classification standard's ingest controls are
   the mechanism that keeps it so. Where a customer's own regulator imposes a masking requirement on data the
   customer sends the entity, it is captured as a contractual requirement in the register and implemented as an
   entity obligation, not left to the customer.
4. **The classification of the data.** Restricted and Confidential elements are masked by default in every
   context other than the one that requires them; Internal and Public elements are not masked, because masking
   data that does not need it degrades utility for no gain and trains people to work around the control.

**Standing prohibitions.** Production customer data is not used in development or testing (see the change
management policy §3.4). Unmasked Restricted data is not exported to any endpoint, spreadsheet, ticket,
screenshot, chat message or third-party tool. Re-identification of de-identified data is prohibited except under
the narrow, approved path in §5. No person may build or hold a mapping table, cross-reference or "helpful export"
that would defeat a masking control.

## 2. Which data is sensitive, and where masking applies

| Data element | Classification | Where it exists | Default treatment outside its purpose |
|---|---|---|---|
| Customer evidence document **content** | Restricted (customer confidential; may contain the customer's personal data) | Object storage, encrypted with a customer-scoped key; processed in memory by the assessment runtime | Not exported. Where a fragment must be shown outside the service (a support investigation, a defect reproduction), it is **redacted** to the minimum span needed and the redaction is recorded |
| Customer personnel identifiers — names, email addresses, job titles, comment authorship | Confidential, personal data | Assessment records, comments, audit trail | **Pseudonymized** in any extract; displayed in the product only to entitled users of that customer's tenant |
| Assessment results, scores, control verdicts, identified gaps | Restricted (customer confidential; commercially sensitive) | Primary data store | Not exported outside the tenant boundary. Aggregate benchmark statistics are produced only under §3.5 |
| Cloud resource identifiers read from a customer environment (account ids, ARNs, resource names) | Confidential | Scan evidence records | **Tokenized** in any extract; account identifiers are treated as identifying the customer |
| Entity personnel data — HR records, training and screening records, disciplinary records | Confidential, personal data | HR system of record (a processor) | Access is role-restricted rather than masked, because the population entitled to it is small and defined; extracts for analysis are aggregated per §3.5 |
| Authentication secrets, API keys, tokens, private keys | Restricted | Secrets manager and KMS; never in application data | **Never displayed, never logged, never masked-but-present**: secrets are referenced, not copied, and any secret found in data is treated as a leaked credential and rotated |
| Log and telemetry records | Confidential | Central logging account | Structured logging **excludes** Restricted content by construction (§3.4); identifiers appear tokenized |
| Support and correspondence records | Confidential | Support system | Customers' evidence content is not attached to tickets; references are used instead |
| Cardholder data | **Out of scope** | Not held — payment processing is delegated entirely to a PCI-compliant processor | Blocked at input and monitored for (§3.6) |

The authoritative list is the information asset inventory; this table is its masking-relevant view and is
reconciled against the inventory at each annual review.

## 3. The masking techniques, and where each is used

Technique selection is a recorded decision, because the techniques are not interchangeable: they differ in
whether the original value can be recovered, whether equal inputs produce equal outputs, and how much utility
survives.

| Technique | What it does | Reversible? | Preserves | Used for |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Access denial** | The element is not returned at all | n/a | Nothing | The default whenever the purpose does not need the element. Preferred over every technique below |
| **Redaction / suppression** | The value is removed and its absence marked | No | Record structure | Evidence fragments shown outside the service; documents shared with a third party; screenshots in defect reports |
| **Partial masking (obfuscation)** | A defined portion is replaced with a fixed character, leaving a stated remainder | No | Format, and a limited ability to recognise a value one already knows | Support views: email as `j•••••@customer.example`, last 4 of an identifier, first octet of an IP |
| **Deterministic pseudonymization (keyed HMAC)** | The identifier is replaced by a stable surrogate derived under a secret key | Only by the key holder, which is a separate, restricted service | **Referential integrity** across tables and over time, and the ability to count and join | Analytics, defect reproduction, and any extract needing joins. The key lives in KMS with a policy denying the analytics roles, so a holder of the extract cannot reverse it |
| **Tokenization (random surrogate + vault)** | The value is replaced by an unrelated token; the mapping is held in a restricted vault | Only through the vault, whose use is logged and alarmed | Referential integrity | Cloud resource identifiers and account numbers in extracts, where reversal must be possible for a named operational purpose but must leave a trail |
| **Encryption of the field** | The value is present but unreadable without the key | Yes, by the key holder | Nothing about the value | At-rest protection of Restricted fields; not counted as masking, because the ciphertext is still the data |
| **Generalization / banding** | A precise value is replaced by a range or category | No | Distribution, analytic utility | Dates to month, ages to bands, counts to buckets, geography to country |
| **Perturbation / noise** | A small bounded random change | No | Aggregate statistics | Benchmark statistics where an exact value could single out a customer |
| **Synthesis** | Wholly artificial data generated to the real schema and distribution | n/a — no real value is present | Schema, distribution, volume | **The default for development and test data.** Nothing derived from real data is safer than data that never contained any |
| **Aggregation with a minimum cell size** | Only group results are released, and only above a threshold | No | Group-level insight | Benchmarks and management reporting (§3.5) |
| **Nulling** | The field is emptied | No | Schema only | Fields no downstream purpose needs, in extracts |

**Choosing between them, and choosing how strong.** The rule is: deny access first; if a copy must exist, prefer
**synthesis**; if real records are unavoidable, prefer **irreversible** techniques (redaction, generalization,
synthesis) over reversible ones; use **deterministic pseudonymization** only where joins are genuinely required
and **tokenization** only where reversal is genuinely required. Strength is set against the **assessed
re-identification risk and the recipient's context**, and the decision is recorded with its reasoning in the
masking register — including what was rejected. The recorded factors are: the sensitivity of the element and the
harm from disclosure; who will hold the output and what else they can already see (an internal engineer under
contract and monitoring is a different context from an external analyst); whether the output leaves the entity's
control; the size of the population, because small populations are easy to single out; and how much utility the
purpose truly requires, which is often less than requested. Where the assessment is finely balanced, the stronger
technique is used: the cost of over-masking is inconvenience and the cost of under-masking is a disclosure.

### 3.1 Minimum necessary access, enforced at field level

The product and internal tooling apply masking by role, and the role set is the entitlement role set:

| Role | Sees unmasked | Sees masked or not at all | Basis |
|---|---|---|---|
| Customer's own entitled users | Their tenant's evidence content, results, and their colleagues' names | Nothing of any other tenant — enforced by tenant isolation, not by masking | The customer's own data, under their own access decisions |
| Assessment runtime (machine) | Evidence content in memory for the duration of the assessment | Persists no evidence content outside the encrypted store; emits no content to logs | Purpose limitation: it needs the content to do the work |
| Customer Support (tier 1) | Tenant name, assessment status, timestamps, error codes | **Evidence content: not at all.** Customer personnel identifiers: partially masked | Support resolves process failures, which does not require reading the customer's evidence |
| Customer Support (tier 2 / escalation) | As tier 1, plus **specific, named** evidence metadata (file name, size, type, ingest result) | Evidence content still withheld; access to a content fragment requires the §5 path | Escalation needs the shape of the object, not its substance |
| Engineering (defect investigation) | Pseudonymized extracts and synthetic reproductions | Production customer content, unless the §5 path is followed | Almost all defects reproduce without real content; the exception is handled explicitly rather than by standing access |
| Data analysis and product analytics | Deterministically pseudonymized, generalized datasets | Direct identifiers, evidence content, free text | Joins and trends need surrogates, not identities |
| Security operations | Security telemetry with tokenized identifiers; unmasked identity data during a confirmed incident under the incident program | Evidence content | Proportionality: detection works on metadata; investigation may need more, and is authorized when it does |
| Compliance and internal audit | Control operation records; metadata; masked samples | Evidence content, unless sampling requires it, in which case the audit testing procedure's isolated-copy path applies | Testing a control needs the record of its operation, not the customer's substance |
| External auditor | Masked or de-identified samples in an isolated copy; on-behalf-of access to the live system where a real record is unavoidable | Bulk unmasked data; standing access of any kind | Audit testing procedure §5 |
| Third-party processors | Only the fields their service requires, per the DPA annex | Everything else | Data minimization in the contract, enforced by what is sent |

Masking is applied **server-side, before the response is formed**. A masked value is not sent to the client and
hidden there, because a hidden value in a payload is not masked — it is merely inconvenient to read.

### 3.2 Test, development and demonstration data

Lower environments hold **synthetic data by default**, generated from committed generators that reproduce the
production schema, referential structure, volume and distribution — including the awkward cases (very large
documents, unusual encodings, adversarial content) that make synthetic data a better test corpus than a copy of
production. Demonstration tenants use a curated synthetic dataset with a fictional company.

Copying production data into a lower environment requires **DPO and data-owner approval**, is time-bounded, is
recorded, and is de-identified before it lands unless the approval specifically records why it could not be.
In this period **0** such copies were made.

### 3.3 Extracts and analytics

Any dataset leaving the primary store for analysis goes through a single, reviewed extraction path that applies
the register's rules by classification — direct identifiers pseudonymized under the analytics key, free text
excluded (free text is where identifiers hide), dates generalized to month, and cell sizes enforced. Ad-hoc export
from the production store is not available to any human role; an analyst cannot construct a different extract by
choosing different SQL, because the extract, not the query, is the interface.

### 3.4 Logs, telemetry, errors and screens

Structured logging **excludes Restricted content by construction**: log helpers accept an allowlist of fields, so
including evidence content requires changing reviewed code rather than adding a variable. Identifiers are
tokenized in logs. Exception handlers emit types and stack traces, never payloads. A **secret and PII detection
pass** runs continuously over the log store, and a hit is an incident: the record is purged under the two-person
path and the emitting code is fixed, because a masking failure in logs is a masking failure everywhere the logs
go. Screens showing Restricted data may not be screenshotted or photographed except through the redacting support
workflow (see the awareness programme §5). In this period the log scanner raised **2** findings, both
non-sensitive false positives, and **0** confirmed content leaks.

### 3.5 Aggregate reporting and benchmarks

Cross-customer statistics are released only as aggregates with a **minimum cell size of 5 customers**, with
suppression of complements so a suppressed cell cannot be recovered by subtraction, and with bounded noise where
a value could still single out a customer. No benchmark identifies a customer, and no customer can infer another
customer's posture from one. Every benchmark definition is reviewed by the DPO before first release.

### 3.6 Payment card data: keeping the out-of-scope position true

The entity's position is that **no cardholder data is stored, processed or transmitted**: payment pages are
hosted by the processor, the entity receives only a processor token and the last four digits for display, and no
entity system is in the cardholder data path. The position is maintained rather than assumed — input validation
rejects card-number patterns in evidence uploads and in support fields, the log and data-store detection pass
includes a card-number detector with Luhn validation, and the position is re-tested annually with the processor's
current attestation on file. If cardholder data were ever found, PCI DSS masking rules (display of no more than
the first six and last four digits, and only to personnel with a business need) would apply to it, and the finding
would be an incident. In this period the detectors raised **0** hits.

## 4. Verifying that de-identification is adequate

A masking rule that has been implemented is not the same as a masking rule that works. Adequacy is **tested**, by
someone other than the implementer, before an output is relied on and periodically thereafter.

**The test asked.** For each de-identified output: could a reasonably resourced recipient, using what they hold
and what is publicly or commercially available, single out an individual or a customer, link records about them,
or infer a sensitive attribute about them? The three failure modes are named separately because they need
different defences — **singling out**, **linkability**, and **inference**.

**How indirect re-identification is prevented.** Removing direct identifiers is the beginning, not the end. The
recorded controls are:

- **Quasi-identifier analysis.** Every field remaining in the output is assessed for its contribution to
  uniqueness in combination with the others — role, job title, organization size, industry, region, joining date,
  assessment dates, control counts, unusual configuration facts. A combination is not evaluated field by field,
  because uniqueness is a property of the combination.
- **A k-anonymity floor of k ≥ 5** on released aggregate and analytic datasets, on the quasi-identifier set as a
  whole, achieved by generalization and suppression, and **measured** rather than intended. Where a sensitive
  attribute is present, l-diversity is checked so that a k-anonymous group does not have a single value of that
  attribute — a group of five who all share one outcome discloses that outcome.
- **Removal of free text and of high-cardinality fields.** Free-text fields, file names, resource names and
  verbatim quotations are excluded from de-identified outputs; they are the most common re-identification route
  and they resist generalization.
- **Rare-value handling.** Outliers — the very large customer, the single-employee tenant, the one entity in a
  region — are suppressed or generalized to a level where they are not unique. Rarity is a re-identification
  route regardless of the field it appears in.
- **Attention to auxiliary data.** The assessment explicitly asks what the recipient already has: another extract
  from the same system, a customer list, a public filing, an employee directory, a data-breach corpus. Two
  separately safe releases can be unsafe together, so releases are considered cumulatively, and the register
  records what has previously gone to each recipient.
- **No collation.** Datasets released under different pseudonymization keys use **different keys per purpose**, so
  they cannot be joined by surrogate. Combining de-identified datasets, or joining one against another source, is
  prohibited without a fresh assessment (§5).
- **Key separation for pseudonymization.** Because pseudonymized data is still personal data, the recovery key is
  held in KMS under a policy that denies the roles holding the data, key use is logged and alarmed, and the key
  is rotated annually. The surrogate's irreversibility to its holder is a property of the key policy, not of the
  algorithm, so the key policy is tested in the access review.

**A motivated-intruder test** is performed **annually** and before any first release to a new recipient class: a
named person other than the dataset's author, given the output and normal open-source resources and a fixed
time-box, attempts re-identification and records the method and result. A successful or partially successful test
blocks release until the output is strengthened, and the test is repeated. The most recent test (2026-06-18, run
by the AI Security Lead against the benchmark dataset and the analytics extract, 8 hours) **did not re-identify
any customer or individual**; it did note that a rare industry-plus-region combination reduced a cell to 3
customers, which was suppressed before release and recorded as **D-2026-026**, closed.

**When "anonymous" may be claimed.** Only where the quasi-identifier analysis, the k ≥ 5 floor, the exclusion of
free text, and the motivated-intruder test are all documented and passed, **and** the General Counsel and DPO
concur in writing. Absent all of that, the output is treated as **pseudonymized — that is, as personal data** —
and keeps the classification, access controls, retention limit and breach obligations of its source. The default
is the conservative one, so an untested output is never treated as out of scope by inaction.

**Ongoing re-verification.** Adequacy decays as auxiliary data grows and as the underlying population changes, so
every de-identified output in continuing use is re-verified **annually**, and immediately on a material change to
the data model, the customer population, the recipient set, or the state of public re-identification research.
Outputs no longer needed are deleted rather than left in place, since an unused dataset accumulates risk without
producing value.

## 5. Usage agreements, restrictions, and tracking

Masking survives release only if the recipient's obligations are stated and the release is tracked.

**Written usage terms are a condition of receiving any masked, pseudonymized or de-identified dataset**, internal
or external. Every recipient agrees, in a signed data use agreement for external recipients and in an
acknowledged internal record for staff:

1. **No re-identification**, and no attempt at it, by any means including combination with other data.
2. **No collation.** The dataset will not be joined, matched or cross-referenced with any other dataset, extract,
   or external source without a fresh approval under this section — because collation is the mechanism by which
   two adequate releases become one inadequate one.
3. **Purpose limitation** to the stated purpose only. A new purpose is a new request.
4. **No onward disclosure**, no copying outside the named storage location, no export to personal tooling or
   endpoints, no upload to any third-party service including AI services.
5. **Stated security controls** at least equal to the classification of the source data — encryption, access
   control, a named custodian.
6. **A stated expiry and mandatory deletion**, with a deletion confirmation returned to the DPO.
7. **A duty to report** any suspected re-identification, whether accidental or not, immediately — an accidental
   re-identification is a personal data breach and is handled as one, and reporting it carries no penalty for
   the reporter, which is stated because a concealed re-identification is far worse than a reported one.
8. **Acceptance of audit** of compliance with these terms.

**Prohibited uses**, stated absolutely: re-identification; any automated decision about an individual made from a
de-identified dataset; marketing or sales use of customer assessment data; any use that would exceed the purpose
the data was collected for under the privacy notice; and building or retaining a re-identification mapping.

**Approved re-identification path.** Re-identification is permitted only where it is necessary for a purpose the
data subject or customer would expect — investigating a defect affecting a specific customer, responding to a
data subject request, or a security investigation. It requires a recorded request naming the purpose and the
minimum records needed, **DPO approval** (plus **General Counsel** approval where the purpose is legal or
disciplinary), execution by the tokenization or key service by someone other than the requester, a scope limited
to the named records rather than the dataset, logging of the vault or key use, and notification to the data owner.
In this period **2** re-identifications were approved, both for defect investigations affecting a named customer,
both scoped to fewer than 10 records; **1** was refused as achievable from metadata alone.

**Tracking of personally identifiable information — the masking register.** The DPO maintains a register recording,
for every masked, pseudonymized, de-identified or aggregated output in existence:

| Recorded | Why it is recorded |
|---|---|
| Dataset name, owner and custodian | Someone must be accountable for it |
| Source data and its classification, and **which PII elements it derives from** | So that a change to the source, or a breach of it, can be traced to every downstream copy |
| Purpose, and the lawful basis it inherits | Purpose limitation is enforceable only if the purpose is written down |
| Technique applied per field, the strength chosen, and **the reasoning including what was rejected** | The adequacy decision must be reviewable |
| Whether it is reversible, and by whom (key or vault, and its policy) | Reversibility determines whether it is still personal data |
| The adequacy verification: quasi-identifier analysis, k value achieved, motivated-intruder test date and result | Adequacy claimed without evidence is not adequacy |
| Recipients, dates, and **prior releases to the same recipient** | Cumulative-release risk is invisible without this |
| Storage location and applied controls | To confirm the terms are being met |
| Retention period, expiry date and deletion evidence | An indefinite extract is an indefinite risk |
| Whether it is treated as personal data or as anonymous, and the GC/DPO concurrence for the latter | The classification decision must be attributable |
| Re-verification due date and last outcome | Adequacy decays |

The register is reconciled **quarterly against the data inventory and against actual storage** — a discovered
extract that is not in the register is a deficiency, because the register's value is its completeness. Personal
data appearing in any output is tracked through the register into the records of processing activity, so a data
subject request or an erasure request reaches derived datasets and not only the primary store. In this period the
register held **9** active outputs, **3** were deleted at expiry with deletion evidence, **1** unregistered
extract was found in a team's analysis bucket in the quarterly reconciliation, registered and then deleted as
unnecessary, and recorded as **D-2026-027**, closed.

**What this standard does not do.** It does not make masked data safe to publish — publication is a separate
decision with its own approval. It does not exempt pseudonymized data from privacy obligations; only §4's tested
anonymity does that, and rarely. And it does not replace access control: every masking rule here sits behind an
entitlement decision that was already made on need-to-know grounds.
