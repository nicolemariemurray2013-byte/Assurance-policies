# Asset Return and Intellectual Property Rights Standard

**Document ID:** SEC-AST-041
**Version:** 2.0 — **effective 2026-03-16**, superseding v1.2 of 2025-07-21
**Owner:** General Counsel, jointly with the Director IT Operations for asset recovery execution
**Approved by:** CISO 2026-03-11
**Next scheduled review:** Annually (next by 2026-12-31), and on any change to the endpoint fleet platform, to the
software catalogue, or to a licence agreement material to the service

**Scope.** Two closely related obligations: recovering **every asset** the entity has issued or entrusted to a
person when their employment, contract or agreement changes or ends; and protecting **intellectual property
rights** — the entity's own and other parties' — in the software and information the entity uses and produces.
Applies to all 48 personnel, the **11 named vendor individuals** with access to entity information, and all
contractors. The production environment is AWS account **471885797689**, region **us-west-2**.

Companion documents: HR-PRO-042 (the termination and change-of-employment process this standard plugs into, and
the confidentiality agreements that survive it); SEC-AST-020 (the asset inventory and classification scheme this
standard depends on); SEC-END-043 (endpoint and remote-working controls, including remote wipe); SEC-MED-046
(destruction methods for recovered media and equipment); SEC-IAM-021 (revocation of logical access);
SEC-MAL-024 (application control, which is also the enforcement mechanism for licensed-software compliance).

## Part A — Return of assets (5.11)

## 1. The complete asset set that must be returned (5.11-R6)

The obligation cannot be met if the set is left implicit. Every issuable asset class is identified in
SEC-AST-020, is recorded against a named custodian at issue, and appears as a line on the return checklist. A
class with no instance for a given person is marked *not issued* rather than left blank, so a blank is always a
missing return.

| Asset class | Instances in the entity | Recorded at issue | Return / recovery method |
|---|---|---|---|
| User endpoint device (laptop) | One per person, 48 in the fleet, all centrally enrolled | Serial, model, assignee, encryption key escrow ref | Physical return; wipe and re-image before reissue |
| Mobile device (entity-owned) | 9 issued to on-call and executive roles | IMEI, assignee | Physical return; factory reset verified in the platform |
| Personally owned device with a managed container (BYOD) | 31 registered, email and chat only | Device ID, container ID, user consent record | **Container wipe** (business data only); attestation of no local copy |
| Portable storage device | **None issued.** Removable media is prohibited for entity data and USB mass storage is blocked by device policy | n/a | Line marked *not issued*; a device found in use is an incident |
| Authentication hardware (FIDO2 security key) | 2 per person, 96 in the register | Serial, assignee, credential IDs | Physical return; credentials deleted from the identity provider on receipt |
| Break-glass credential material | 1 set for the `mac` break-glass IAM user, held in a sealed envelope in the HQ safe | Custodian, seal number | Custody transferred and the credential rotated, not merely returned |
| Physical access badge | One per person; 3 co-working site passes | Badge ID, assignee | Physical return or, where the pass is site-issued, cancellation with the site operator |
| HQ / EU office keys and safe combinations | 6 key holders, 2 safe combination holders | Holder register | Physical return; combination changed on any holder change |
| Specialist equipment | Monitors, docks, headsets, 2 hardware test devices | Serial, assignee | Physical return or written off with approval |
| Physical copies of information | Rare by design: Confidential printing is disabled at the device and there is no paper personnel file | Print exception record, if any | Return for shredding in the locked console; attestation of none held |

| Corporate card and expense credentials | 11 holders | Holder register | Cancellation with the issuer |
| Credentials and secrets | Federated identities, repository access, vendor portal logins | Identity inventory | Revoked, not returned — see §3 |

## 2. The formalized change-and-termination process (5.11-R1, 5.11-R2)

Return is a step in the single offboarding-and-change workflow in HR-PRO-042, not a side conversation. The
workflow is initiated by Head of People on notice, or by the engagement owner for a contractor or vendor
individual, and it does not close until every line reconciles.

1. **T-minus.** On notice, the workflow generates the person's asset list **from the inventory**, not from
   memory, covering both physical and electronic assets owned by or entrusted to the entity.
2. **Return instructions.** Because **42 of 48 personnel work primarily remotely**, the default return path is a
   pre-paid tracked courier with a prepared packing list; the tracking number is recorded in the workflow. HQ
   and EU office staff return in person to IT Operations against the same checklist.
3. **Last working day.** Devices, security keys, badges, keys and specialist equipment are due **by the last
   working day**. Logical access is revoked on the last working day at the recorded end time regardless of
   whether physical assets have arrived (§3).
4. **Receipt and verification.** IT Operations records receipt per line, verifies serials against the inventory,
   confirms full-disk encryption state, and moves the record to *returned — pending sanitization*.
5. **Sanitization or reissue.** Recovered equipment is wiped and re-imaged before reissue, or disposed of, under
   SEC-MED-046. The inventory record is closed with the method, date and verifier.
6. **Reconciliation and escalation.** An outstanding line at last working day +2 working days escalates to the
   manager; at +5 to the department head and the CISO; at +10 it becomes a recorded deficiency, and where the
   asset held Confidential or Restricted data it is treated as a potential incident under SEC-IRP-027 with a
   remote wipe issued immediately.
7. **Closure.** Head of People closes the workflow only on a fully reconciled checklist. The completed checklist,
   courier records, wipe verifications and any exception approvals are retained for **seven years**.

**Change of employment** runs the same workflow with a different endpoint: assets required in the new role are
re-recorded against it, assets not required are returned on the effective date, and access is re-granted from
zero rather than added to (SEC-IAM-021). A change is treated as a termination of the old responsibilities plus an
initiation of the new ones — the same construction used in HR-PRO-042 §4.

## 3. Assets that cannot be handed back: information on personal or purchased equipment (5.11-R3)

Three cases arise, and each has a defined procedure because "return the laptop" does not cover them.

**(a) Personally owned equipment used for entity work (BYOD).** Permitted only for email and chat through a
managed container with its own encryption and independent wipe. On change or termination the container is wiped
from the management console and the wipe is confirmed in the platform log; the individual additionally attests in
the offboarding record that no entity information remains outside the container. **Customer data is never
downloaded to endpoints** (a requirement hardened after case **D-2026-004**), so the exposure surface of a BYOD
device is limited by design, not only by the wipe.

**(b) An individual purchases their entity device.** Permitted by exception with Director IT Operations approval
and only after: all relevant entity information has been **traced and transferred** to the entity (§4); the
drive has been **cryptographically erased and then sanitized with verification**, or physically destroyed and
replaced, under SEC-MED-046; entity labels, asset tags, certificates, licensed software and configuration
profiles have been removed; the device has been un-enrolled from management; and the sale is recorded with the
inventory record closed as *sold — sanitized, verified*. Purchase of a device that held Restricted data is
refused rather than sanitized.

**(c) A contractor's or vendor individual's own equipment.** Where an engagement permits work on
vendor-owned equipment, the contract imposes a **wipe-and-attest obligation at engagement end**, verified before
the final invoice is approved, and tracked by the Third-Party Risk Manager in the vendor register. Where entity
information may have been held, a certificate of destruction is required within 30 days. Non-return or
non-attestation is a contractual breach and a recorded deficiency.

In all three cases the trace-and-delete step is evidenced: the data set concerned, the transfer destination, the
deletion method, the verification, the date and the verifier.

## 4. Knowledge important to ongoing operations (5.11-R4)

Knowledge does not travel in a courier box, so it is captured deliberately:

- **Handover record.** Every leaver and every person changing role completes a handover record naming: the
  systems, controls and processes they own; runbook and documentation locations; in-flight work and known risks;
  vendor and customer contacts they hold; undocumented operational knowledge; and any credential, key or
  break-glass custody they hold.
- **Named successor.** Each item is assigned to a named successor with a date; unassigned items escalate to the
  department head. Control and policy ownership transfers are reflected in GOV-STR-003 and in the control
  register, so no control is left ownerless (this is the same mechanism HR-PRO-042 §5 relies on).
- **Documentation-before-departure.** Where knowledge is undocumented, documenting it is a deliverable of the
  notice period, reviewed by the successor and the manager. A leaver in a single-point-of-failure role triggers a
  documented overlap period where the notice period allows.
- **Verification.** The successor confirms in writing that they can operate the handed-over items. Head of People
  will not close the workflow without that confirmation for any item on the critical list.

## 5. Preventing unauthorized copying during and after notice (5.11-R5)

Notice is the period of highest data-exfiltration risk and is treated as such. On notice being given or received:

| Measure | What happens | Enforcement point |
|---|---|---|
| Heightened monitoring | The individual's access is flagged for enhanced review for the notice period and 30 days after | SEC-MON-026; DLP and access-log review, proportionate and disclosed in the privacy notice for personnel |
| Access right-sizing | Access beyond what the remaining work needs is removed on notice, not on the last day; production and customer-data entitlements are removed first | SEC-IAM-021 |
| Egress paths | No bulk export capability exists; removable media is prohibited and USB mass storage is blocked; outbound mail and file-share DLP blocks Restricted content and requires justification for Confidential | SEC-DLP-023 |
| Repository controls | Cloning and download activity is logged; secret scanning and large-file detection run on push; personal repository forks are not permitted | ENG-SDL-048 |
| Printing | Confidential printing is disabled at the device; a print exception during notice requires manager approval and is logged | SEC-PHY-044 |
| Reminder of duties | The individual is reminded in writing, at notice and at exit, of the surviving confidentiality and IP obligations in their agreement and of the consequences of breach | HR-PRO-042 §3 |
| Post-departure detection | Access attempts by a revoked identity raise an alert (`AIGov-UnauthorizedApiCalls`); repository and SaaS audit logs are reviewed once at 30 days post-departure | SEC-MON-026 |
| Legal remedy | Confirmed misappropriation of intellectual property is pursued under the agreement and, where applicable, referred; the entity does not rely on goodwill alone | General Counsel |

The measures apply symmetrically to a resignation, a dismissal and a contract non-renewal, and to external
personnel. They are proportionate, disclosed in the personnel privacy notice, and are not a substitute for the
preventive controls — they are the detective layer over them.

## Part B — Intellectual property rights (5.32)

## 6. Topic-specific policy and compliance procedures (5.32-R1, 5.32-R2, 5.32-R8)

**Policy statement.** The entity respects the intellectual property rights of others and protects its own. Every
person may use only software and information products the entity has acquired the right to use, only within the
terms of that right, and only for entity purposes. Infringement — of copyright, licence terms, trade marks,
patents or database rights — is a disciplinary matter and, for the entity, a legal and contractual risk to the
service.

The policy is issued under GOV-POL-040, published on the policy portal, communicated at onboarding, in the
quarterly briefing and on any material change, and **acknowledged** by every person under GOV-POL-040 §5.2.
Vendors and contractors receive it as a contractual annex.

**Compliance procedures — what compliant use means (5.32-R2):**

1. **Use only from the managed catalogue.** Software is installed only from the entity's managed catalogue by
   application control (SEC-MAL-024); users hold no local administrator rights by default and elevation is
   brokered, time-bound and logged. An unlisted product requires a request, which triggers the licence and
   security review in §7.
2. **One person, one seat.** Named-user licences are not shared, and shared accounts are prohibited by
   SEC-IAM-021.
3. **No unlicensed copies, no circumvention.** Copying, key-sharing, keygen use, licence-server tampering and
   use of cracked or unlicensed builds are prohibited absolutely.
4. **Respect the field of use.** A licence granted for evaluation, development, education, personal or
   non-commercial use is not used in production or for commercial purposes. Free-for-personal-use tools are not
   used for entity work.
5. **Open source is licensed software.** Its terms are honoured — attribution, notice files, source
   availability, and copyleft reciprocity. Licence classes are pre-classified as allowed, allowed-with-conditions
   or prohibited in ENG-SDL-048; a prohibited class fails the build.
6. **Content, not just code.** Documents, standards, articles, images, fonts, datasets, model weights and
   training data are all subject to this standard (§10, §11).
7. **AI-assisted development** is permitted only with approved tooling configured so that entity code is not
   used for provider training, and output is reviewed for licence provenance before merge (AIMS-POL-003).
8. **Maintaining licence conditions (5.32-R8).** Each licence in the register has a named owner responsible for
   renewal dates, seat counts, notice files, attribution obligations and any audit clause. Renewals are calendared
   with 60 days' notice; a licence approaching expiry without a renewal decision escalates to the General
   Counsel. Conditions that require an ongoing action — publishing notices, providing source, reporting usage
   metrics — are recorded as scheduled control activities with evidence, not as one-off tasks at acquisition.

## 7. Acquisition only through known and reputable sources (5.32-R3)

- **Single procurement path.** All software, whether paid, free or open source, is acquired through the
  procurement and vendor-risk process in TPR-PRO-016. Direct purchase by an individual, download from an
  unofficial mirror, or use of a personal licence for entity work is prohibited.
- **Source verification.** Acquisition is from the publisher, an authorized reseller, or the canonical package
  registry for the ecosystem. Artefacts are verified by **checksum or signature**, and container images by
  **digest, never by mutable tag** (ENG-SDL-048). A package whose provenance cannot be verified is not used.
- **Registry discipline.** Dependencies resolve through the entity's proxy registry with pinned versions and a
  lockfile; typosquat and dependency-confusion detection runs on resolution; a new external dependency requires
  review of its licence, maintenance history and security record before first use.
- **Records retained at acquisition.** Purchase order, invoice, licence agreement or terms accepted, the version
  and edition, the entitlement quantity, the download location and its checksum, and the accepting approver.
- **Second-hand and transferred licences** are acquired only where the publisher confirms transferability in
  writing; without it the licence is treated as absent.

## 8. Asset registers identifying IP-protected assets (5.32-R4)

Two registers are maintained inside the inventory framework of SEC-AST-020, and both are reconciled rather than
merely held:

**(a) The software and licence register** — every software asset with: product, publisher, version, licence type
(perpetual, subscription, named-user, per-CPU, per-resource, open source class), entitlement quantity and metric,
seats or resources consumed, licence owner, cost centre, renewal or expiry date, proof-of-entitlement reference,
audit clause, attribution or notice obligations, and the environments in which it may be used.

**(b) The entity's own intellectual property register** — the service source code, the assessment methodology and
control mappings, the evidence-analysis prompts and templates, documentation, brand and marks, and datasets the
entity has created. Each entry records the owner, its classification under SEC-AST-020 (all of it Confidential or
Restricted), where it is held, who may access it, and any third-party rights embedded in it.

Additionally, the **software bill of materials** for the service is generated on every build and retained with
the release record, so the open-source components in any shipped artefact are enumerable after the fact. Third-
party components embedded in the entity's own products are cross-referenced between (a) and (b) so that an
obligation attached to a component is visible from the product that ships it.

## 9. Proof of entitlement (5.32-R5)

Evidence of ownership is stored centrally in the licence repository, not on the purchaser's laptop or in a
mailbox: licence agreements and accepted terms, purchase orders and invoices, licence keys and entitlement
certificates, publisher portal entitlement exports, manuals and any media, open-source licence texts and notice
files, and the SBOMs. Each item is linked from its register entry. Records are retained for the **licence term
plus seven years**, because an audit or a dispute can arrive after the product is retired. Access to the
repository is restricted to the General Counsel, the Director IT Operations and named licence owners.

## 10. Not exceeding permitted users or resources (5.32-R6)

- **Reconciliation.** Consumption is reconciled against entitlement **quarterly** for every licence in the
  register, and **monthly** for the ten highest-risk licences (by cost, by audit clause, or by proximity to the
  entitlement limit). Sources are the identity provider group memberships, the vendor portal seat reports and,
  for resource-metered licences, the infrastructure inventory in account **471885797689**.
- **Resource-metered licences** — per-CPU, per-vCPU, per-core, per-instance, per-host — are reconciled against
  actual deployed resources. The current production footprint is small and fully enumerated (no running EC2
  instances, no NAT gateways, managed serverless compute only), so resource-metered exposure is low; that fact is
  re-established at each reconciliation rather than assumed, because a change of architecture could change it
  overnight.
- **Prevention before detection.** Seats are provisioned through the identity provider on a joiner-mover-leaver
  basis, so a seat is released automatically on departure or role change (§2); where the vendor supports a hard
  entitlement cap, it is enabled.
- **Over-consumption handling.** An overage is remediated within **10 working days** by reclaiming seats or
  purchasing entitlement, is recorded as a deficiency, and its cause is addressed. Deliberate over-deployment to
  avoid cost is a disciplinary matter.
- **Under-consumption** is also acted on: unused seats are reclaimed at renewal, which keeps the register
  credible rather than generous.

## 11. Reviews for authorized and licensed software only (5.32-R7)

| Review | Frequency | Method | Output |
|---|---|---|---|
| Endpoint installed-software inventory vs the register | Continuous collection, **monthly** review | Device management inventory across the 48-device fleet | Unauthorized installations list; removal within 5 working days |
| Application-control block report | Monthly | SEC-MAL-024 allowlist denials | Attempted unauthorized installs; user follow-up or catalogue addition |
| Production dependency scan vs licence classes | Every build, blocking | SCA on the SBOM; prohibited class fails the build | Build pass/fail; exception record if any |
| Cloud and SaaS discovery vs the vendor register | Quarterly | Egress destination review, expense analysis, SSO app inventory | Shadow-IT findings; onboarded or blocked |
| Licence entitlement reconciliation | Quarterly (monthly for the top ten) | §10 | Reconciliation record; overage remediation |
| Register completeness and owner currency | Annually | Register vs procurement records vs SBOMs | Corrections; ownerless entries reassigned |
| Attribution and notice-file verification for shipped artefacts | Per release | Notice file generated from the SBOM and compared to the prior release | Notice file published with the release |

Findings are recorded in the deficiency register with an owner and due date and reported to the Audit Committee
quarterly. Repeat findings against the same individual route to the disciplinary process.

## 12. Disposal or transfer of software (5.32-R9)

- **Retiring software.** The licence owner initiates retirement: the product is removed from the managed
  catalogue and from every device (verified by the next inventory cycle), any licence server or key is
  decommissioned, entitlement is cancelled or allowed to lapse with a recorded decision, and the register entry
  is **closed rather than deleted**, retaining the entitlement history and the proof of entitlement for the
  retention period in §9.
- **Data first.** Where the software held data, the data is exported or destroyed under PRV-RET-036 and
  SEC-MED-046 **before** the licence lapses, so retention obligations do not outlive the means to read the data.
- **Transferring software to another party.** Permitted only where the licence is transferable, and then only
  with: written publisher confirmation; a written transfer agreement; removal of the entity's data,
  configuration and credentials; removal of the entity's labels and identifiers; and a record of what was
  transferred, to whom, when and under what authority. Media and keys are handed over or destroyed under
  SEC-MED-046. Where transferability cannot be confirmed, the software is destroyed rather than transferred.
- **Never bundled with hardware.** Licensed software is removed from any equipment being sold, donated or
  recycled (§3(b) and SEC-MED-046); a device leaves the entity re-imaged, with no entity-licensed software on it.
- **Entity-owned IP is not transferred** — licensed, contributed to open source, or assigned — without General
  Counsel approval, an IP clearance check against the register in §8(b), and, for open-source contribution,
  confirmation that no third-party or customer-derived material is included.

## 13. Third-party and commercial material (5.32-R10, 5.32-R11, 5.32-R12)

- **Material obtained from public networks and outside sources (5.32-R10).** Anything downloaded — code,
  libraries, container images, datasets, model weights, fonts, images, templates, standards, research — is used
  only within its stated terms and conditions, which are read and recorded before first use. Where terms are
  absent or unclear, the material is treated as **all rights reserved** and is not used. Provenance and the
  applicable terms are recorded in the register entry; publicly available does not mean freely usable, and
  scraped or unlicensed datasets are prohibited as inputs to the AI system under AIMS-POL-003 and the data
  acquisition record (AIMS-REC-005).
- **Commercial recordings (5.32-R11).** Commercial video and audio recordings are **not duplicated, converted to
  another format, or extracted from** other than as expressly permitted by copyright law or the applicable
  licence. Purchased or licensed training video is consumed through the provider's platform; it is not recorded,
  re-hosted, screen-captured or re-distributed internally. Where the entity wants internal reuse, it licenses
  that right explicitly, and the licence is registered under §8(a).
- **Standards, books, articles and reports (5.32-R12).** These are **not copied in full or in part** other than
  as permitted by law or licence. The entity's ISO and other standards documents are held under **named-user
  licences with the seat count reconciled under §10**; they are not shared beyond their seats, posted to internal
  wikis, or attached to email. Quotation for internal analysis stays within the licence's permitted extent with
  attribution; where a control mapping needs to reproduce requirement text, the entity uses licensed material
  within its terms or paraphrases. Research reports and subscription analyst material are shared only per their
  redistribution terms.
- **Awareness.** These three rules are the most commonly breached in good faith, so they are called out
  explicitly in onboarding and in the annual refresher, with concrete examples, rather than left to a general
  "respect copyright" statement.
