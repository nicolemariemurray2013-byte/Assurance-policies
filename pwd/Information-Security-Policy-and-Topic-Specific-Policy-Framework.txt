# Information Security Policy and Topic-Specific Policy Framework

**Document ID:** GOV-POL-040
**Version:** 4.0 — **effective 2026-03-02**, superseding v3.1 of 2025-04-14
**Owner:** CISO
**Approved by:** Chief Executive Officer on behalf of top management, 2026-02-26; noted by the Audit Committee 2026-03-05
**Next scheduled review:** Annually (next by 2026-12-31), and on any significant change (see §6.2)

**Scope.** The information security policy of the entity, the register of topic-specific policies subordinate to
it, and the process by which both are approved, published, acknowledged and reviewed. Applies to all 48
personnel, to all contractors and vendor personnel with access to entity information, and to all information and
information processing facilities including the production environment in AWS account **471885797689**, region
**us-west-2**.

This document is the apex of the documentation set. Every other standard, policy and procedure referenced in §3
derives its authority from §2 of this document and is bound by the approval, communication, acknowledgement and
review rules in §4 to §6.

---

## 1. Why a two-tier structure

A single monolithic policy cannot be both short enough for every employee to read and specific enough to direct
an engineer. The entity therefore maintains:

- **One information security policy** (§2) — short, approved by top management, addressed to everyone, stating
  what information security means here, what the entity intends to achieve, who is accountable, and what is
  required of every person.
- **A register of topic-specific policies** (§3) — each addressed to the population that performs the work, each
  with a named owner competent in the topic, each approved at the level of authority appropriate to its subject
  matter rather than escalated to the CEO for form's sake.

The relationship is strictly hierarchical: a topic-specific policy may be more restrictive than this policy,
never less, and a conflict is resolved in favour of this policy until the conflict is corrected.

## 2. The information security policy (5.1-R1)

### 2.1 Definition of information security

**Information security** is the preservation of the **confidentiality**, **integrity** and **availability** of
information, together with the related properties of **authenticity**, **accountability**, **non-repudiation**
and **reliability**. It covers information in every form and at every stage — created, transmitted, processed,
stored, printed, spoken and destroyed — and it covers the systems, facilities, people and suppliers that handle
it. It is not a synonym for IT security: a control failure in hiring, in a supplier contract, or at a desk is an
information security failure.

### 2.2 The entity's approach to managing its information security

1. **Risk-based.** Controls exist because a risk assessment (RSK-STD-008) identified a risk to a defined
   objective. Controls are selected, not accumulated, and are retired when the risk that justified them is gone.
2. **Objectives.** The entity's information security objectives are: protect the confidentiality of customer
   assessment data and evidence; preserve the integrity and demonstrability of compliance conclusions; keep the
   service available to the availability commitment; meet legal, regulatory and contractual obligations
   including privacy law in the jurisdictions where personnel and customers are located; and support the safe
   and responsible operation of the AI system under AIMS-POL-001.
3. **Secure by design and by default.** Security requirements are identified before a system is built or bought
   (ENG-SDL-048), and the default state of a control is the restrictive state.
4. **Least privilege and no standing access.** No human holds standing access to customer data; access is
   federated, MFA-authenticated, time-bound and purpose-limited (SEC-IAM-021).
5. **Defence in depth.** No single control is relied on alone; where a preventive control cannot be applied, a
   detective control and a documented compensating measure are.
6. **Evidence.** A control that produces no record is treated as a control that does not operate. Every control
   in the register names its evidence.
7. **Continual improvement.** Deficiencies, incidents, audit findings and exceptions feed the improvement
   backlog and the management review (MON-PRO-011).
8. **Cloud-native and vendor-dependent by design.** The entity operates no data centre. Physical and
   environmental protection of production infrastructure is the responsibility of the infrastructure provider,
   and the entity's reliance on it is a managed supplier relationship (TPR-PRO-016), not an assumption.

### 2.3 Requirements binding on every person

Every person with access to entity information must: use only the access granted to them and only for entity
purposes; protect their credentials and never share them; use only managed, enrolled devices for entity work
(SEC-END-043); classify and handle information per SEC-AST-020; report a suspected incident, weakness or policy
breach immediately (SEC-IRP-027); complete assigned security and privacy training; and comply with the
topic-specific policies applicable to their role.

### 2.4 Accountability

Top management is accountable for the information security of the entity and demonstrates this by approving this
policy, funding the programme, receiving quarterly reporting, and holding owners to account for deficiencies.
The **CISO** owns the programme and this policy. **Every named policy owner in §3** is accountable for the
currency and effectiveness of their policy. **Every manager** is accountable for their team's compliance.
**Every individual** is accountable for their own compliance. Deliberate or negligent breach is handled under
the disciplinary provisions of the code of conduct (GOV-POL-005).

### 2.5 Consequences and exceptions

Non-compliance is a deficiency, recorded and tracked under MON-PRO-011. An exception to any policy in §3 requires
a written risk acceptance with a named accepting owner at or above the policy's approval level, a compensating
control, an expiry date of no more than 12 months, and entry in the exception register; exceptions are reviewed
monthly and expire automatically.

## 4. Approval (5.1-R2)

- **This policy** is approved by **top management** — the CEO, as the accountable executive, with the Audit
  Committee noting the approval. Approval is recorded in the document header, in the CEO's approval record, and
  in the committee minutes. **Any change** to this policy, however small, re-enters the approval path; there is
  no "editorial change" bypass, because the classification of a change as editorial is itself a judgement the
  approver must make.
- **Topic-specific policies** are developed by their owner, **reviewed** by the CISO for consistency with this
  policy and by the General Counsel where legal or privacy obligations are engaged, and **approved** by the
  approver named in §3 — selected because that role holds authority over the subject matter and the technical
  competency to judge it. A policy whose subject matter spans two owners names both, and both must review.
- **Approval evidence.** Approval is recorded in the document management system with approver identity, role,
  date and version; the record is immutable and versioned. An unapproved draft is watermarked and cannot be
  published to the policy portal — publication requires the approval record.
- **Delegation.** An approver may not delegate approval of a policy they own. During absence, approval waits or
  is escalated to the next level, and the escalation is recorded.

## 5. Publication, communication and acknowledgement

### 5.1 Publication and communication (5.1-R3)

- Every approved policy is published to the **policy portal**, which is the single authoritative source. It is
  reachable from any managed device, on the corporate network or remotely — which matters because **42 of 48
  personnel work primarily remotely**, so a policy pinned to a wall at HQ would not reach them.
- Policies are written in plain English, in the same structure (purpose, scope, requirements, roles, evidence),
  with defined terms in a shared glossary. Length is a design constraint: this policy is deliberately short.
- **Relevant, accessible and understandable to the intended reader** is tested, not assumed: each new or
  materially revised policy is read by two members of its intended audience before approval, and their comments
  on clarity are resolved or recorded.
- **Accessibility.** The portal meets the entity's accessibility standard; alternative formats are provided on
  request; the EU office population receives a locally reviewed translation of this policy and of GOV-POL-005.
- **Communication events.** Publication triggers: an all-hands announcement for this policy and for any policy
  affecting all personnel; a targeted notification to the audience in §3 for narrower policies; inclusion in the
  onboarding curriculum within 5 working days; and coverage in the quarterly security briefing.
- **Relevant interested parties.** Customers receive the security overview derived from this policy through the
  trust portal and, under NDA, the full policy on request. Vendors and contractors receive the policy set
  applicable to their engagement as a contractual annex — the **11 named vendor individuals** with access to
  entity information are each issued the applicable set at engagement start, and re-issued on material change.
  The managed SIEM provider, which holds copies of entity logs, additionally receives SEC-MON-026 and
  SEC-IRP-027. Regulators and the external auditor receive policies on request.

### 5.2 Acknowledgement (5.1-R4)

- **Acknowledgement is required, tracked and enforced.** Every person with access to entity information
  acknowledges, in the policy portal, that they have read, understand and agree to comply with this policy and
  with each topic-specific policy applicable to their role. The acknowledgement records identity, policy
  version, timestamp and the applicable-policy list, and is retained for the duration of employment plus seven
  years.
- **Triggers.** On hire, before first access to production or customer data; on any material revision, within
  **10 working days** of publication; on a change of role, for the newly applicable policies; and annually for
  this policy and GOV-POL-005 regardless of change.
- **Enforcement.** Outstanding acknowledgements are reported weekly to the individual and their manager at day
  5, escalated to the department head at day 10, and at **day 15 access is suspended** until the acknowledgement
  is recorded. Completion is reported to the Audit Committee quarterly; the target and current state is 100% for
  in-force policies.
- **Contractors and vendor personnel** acknowledge through the same portal where they hold entity identities, or
  by countersigned contractual annex where they do not; the Third-Party Risk Manager tracks the latter in the
  vendor register.
- **Understanding, not just receipt.** For the policies with the highest consequence — this policy,
  SEC-IAM-021, SEC-END-043, AIMS-POL-003 and SEC-IRP-027 — acknowledgement is paired with a short assessment,
  and a failed assessment routes to remedial training rather than to a second click.

## 6. Review (5.1-R5)

### 6.1 Planned intervals

| Review | Interval | Performed by | Output |
|---|---|---|---|
| This information security policy | Annually, by end of February | CISO, approved by CEO | Approved version or recorded no-change decision |
| Each topic-specific policy | Annually, on the owner's schedule in §3 | Policy owner, reviewed by CISO | Approved version or recorded no-change decision |
| Register completeness against the statement of applicability | Annually | CISO | Coverage reconciliation; findings |
| Acknowledgement completeness | Quarterly | Head of People | Completion report; escalations |
| Exception register | Monthly | CISO | Expired and renewed exceptions |
| Management review of the whole system | Annually, with quarterly interim reporting | Top management | Minuted decisions and actions |

A review that concludes "no change required" is recorded with that conclusion, the reviewer, the date and the
inputs considered. Silence is not a review, and an unreviewed policy past its due date is a deficiency reported
to the Audit Committee.

### 6.2 Significant-change triggers

A review is performed out of cycle, within **30 days** of the trigger, when any of the following occurs:

1. **A change to the entity's business strategy** — a new product line, a new customer segment, a new market, a
   material change to the service's purpose, or a change to the AI system's intended use.
2. A change to the **technical or operating environment** — a new region, a new class of infrastructure, a new
   model provider, a significant architecture change, or the adoption of a new material vendor.
3. A change to **legal, statutory, regulatory or contractual requirements**, including privacy law in a
   jurisdiction where personnel or customers are located, and including new AI-specific obligations.
4. A **material change to the risk landscape** — a new threat relevant to the entity, a change in risk appetite,
   or a risk assessment result that a current control no longer addresses.
5. A **significant incident, near miss, or repeated deficiency**, including case **D-2026-004**, whose
   post-incident review produced the no-local-copy requirement now in SEC-END-043 and a corresponding change to
   §2.3 of this policy.
6. An **audit or certification finding**, internal or external, touching policy adequacy.
7. A **significant organizational change** — restructure, acquisition, a change in the accountable executive, or
   a change in the location or size of a facility.
8. A **change to the entity's own control set** that alters what personnel are required to do.

### 6.3 What the review assesses

Each review explicitly assesses and records: **opportunities for improvement** in the management of information
security; whether the policy still responds to changes in the **entity's business strategy**, technical
environment, legal and contractual obligations, risk landscape, threat environment, and organizational
structure; the **effectiveness** evidence — incident volume and cause, deficiency ageing, exception volume and
age, acknowledgement completion, training completion, audit findings; whether any topic-specific policy has
drifted out of alignment with §2; and whether the register in §3 still covers the applicable control set. The
assessment feeds the improvement backlog with named owners and dates.
