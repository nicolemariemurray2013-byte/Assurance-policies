**Privacy Notice — version 4.1, effective 20 January 2026.**
This notice is written to be read. If anything here is unclear, contact us at the address in §10 and we will
explain it — and if the notice itself was unclear, we will change it.

## A1. Who we are and what this notice covers

**Objective description of the entities and activities covered.** We are a provider of an automated compliance
evaluation service. This notice is given by two legal entities: the parent company, which provides the service
worldwide, and its European subsidiary, which is the controller for personal information of individuals in the
European Economic Area and the United Kingdom. Both are covered by this notice, and the notice states which
entity is responsible where that differs.

**This notice covers:**

| Activity covered | Our role |
|---|---|
| Our public website, product documentation and status page | Controller |
| Creating and administering a customer account, and providing the compliance evaluation service to a customer organization | Controller for the **account and user** information we hold about the individuals who use it |
| Personal information that happens to be **inside evidence documents or configuration data a customer uploads or exposes to us** for assessment | **Processor** — the customer is the controller, and the customer's own privacy notice governs. We process only on their documented instructions under a data processing agreement |
| Sales, marketing and events | Controller |
| Recruitment | Controller |
| Employment, contracting and vendor personnel administration | Controller |
| Support, complaints and disputes | Controller |
| Security monitoring and logging of our own systems | Controller |

**This notice does not cover:** our customers' own privacy practices, including how they collect the personal
information they may place in evidence they submit to us — for that, ask the customer organization; other
companies' websites we link to; and personal information a customer's own privacy notice governs where we act
as processor. **Where we act as processor and you contact us about your information, we will tell you so and
route your request to the customer organization** — see §6.

## A6. How we use, retain and dispose of personal information

**Use.** We use your information **only for the purposes in §A2**, for which it was collected. If we need it
for something else, §A3 applies — new purpose documented, notice given, consent obtained first. Internally,
access is limited to the people who need it for their role, under least-privilege access that is reviewed
quarterly.

**Retention.** We keep personal information only as long as necessary for the purpose, unless the law requires
longer. Our schedule:

| Information | How long we keep it | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Account and user information | For the life of the account, then **deleted within 90 days** of account closure | No purpose survives the account |
| Assessment results and evidence (including any personal information inside them) | **Per the customer's contract and instruction**, since we are processor; deleted or returned on their instruction and at the end of the contract | The customer decides; we execute and evidence it |
| Access and security logs | 12 months, then deleted | Security investigation and legal need |
| Support and complaint records | 3 years from closure | To handle disputes and to review themes |
| Billing and financial records | As required by tax and company law in each jurisdiction (typically 7 years) | Legal obligation |
| Marketing contact details | Until you unsubscribe or object, and then only a suppression record so we do not contact you again | To honour your choice |
| Unsuccessful job applications | 12 months, or longer only with your explicit talent-pool consent | Recruitment record and legal defence |
| Employment records | For the duration of employment and then per statutory limitation periods in your jurisdiction | Legal obligation |
| Screening outcomes | The outcome and its date only — **not the underlying detail**, which we do not retain | Minimization |
| Cookies | Session cookies expire when you close your browser; persistent cookies have the lifetimes stated in "Cookie settings" | Transparency |
| Prior versions of this notice | Indefinitely | So you can see what you were told, and when (§B4) |

**Disposal.** When a retention period ends, or when you ask us to delete information we hold as controller and
we have no overriding obligation to keep it, we delete or irreversibly anonymize it — including **in our
backups as those backups age out on their own schedule, and at our providers and their sub-processors, from
whom we obtain written confirmation.** Devices holding personal information are wiped, and disposal of any
device is evidenced by a certificate. Paper is not used for personnel files at all.

**We check this rather than assume it.** A quarterly retention conformance check tests what we actually hold
against this schedule. In the current period that check **found personal information held past its period.** We
deleted it, obtained evidence of the deletion including from a sub-processor, tightened the technical
enforcement so it does not recur, and recorded the finding in our compliance register. We tell you this because
a retention statement that has never been tested is not worth much.

## A7. Access to your information, and your other rights

**You can find out whether we hold personal information about you, and get a copy of it.** Depending on where
you are and which law applies, you have some or all of these rights — and where a right exists in your
jurisdiction we honour it, without charge, in a form you can understand:

| Right | What it means in practice |
|---|---|
| **Confirmation and access** | We tell you whether we hold personal information about you, and give you a copy, in a **commonly used electronic format that a person can read** (or paper if you ask). We also tell you the categories, the purposes, the sources, the recipients, and the retention period |
| **Correction** | We correct information that is inaccurate, and complete information that is incomplete — see §A8 |
| **Deletion** | We delete information where we have no continuing lawful reason to keep it, and tell you if an obligation requires us to keep some of it and which |
| **Portability** | Where applicable, a machine-readable copy of information you gave us |
| **Objection and restriction** | You can object to processing based on legitimate interests — including marketing, which we always honour — and ask us to restrict processing while a dispute is resolved |
| **Withdraw consent** | At any time, as described in §A3 |
| **No discrimination** | We do not treat you worse for exercising a right |
| **Complain** | To us (§A10), and to your data protection authority — you do not have to come to us first |

**How to exercise a right:** contact us at the address in §A10. **How we handle it:**

1. We **verify your identity** proportionately to the sensitivity of what you are asking for, using information
   we already hold. We do not ask for more information than we need to verify you, and we never ask for a new
   identity document where an existing account authentication will do.
2. We respond **within the period your law requires**, and our internal target is **60% of that period**. Our
   system escalates a request to the DPO automatically at **75% of the period elapsed**, so a deadline is not
   missed by inattention. In the current period **every request was completed within the statutory period, and
   none reached the escalation threshold**.
3. **There is no charge**, except where a law allows a fee for a manifestly excessive or repetitive request —
   in which case we tell you the amount and the basis before doing the work.
4. We give you the information **in an understandable form** — plain field labels rather than internal codes,
   with an explanation of anything that needs it.
5. **If we deny a request, in whole or in part, we tell you in writing:** what we denied, **the reason**, the
   **legal provision we rely on** to deny it, and **how to challenge the denial** — including your right to
   complain to your data protection authority and any statutory appeal route. The same applies if we deny a
   correction request: we give the reason and how to appeal it.
6. **If we hold the information as processor for a customer** — the case for personal information inside
   evidence documents — we cannot decide the request ourselves. We tell you that promptly, tell you who the
   controller is if we may lawfully do so, and forward your request to them, and we are contractually obliged
   to assist them in time for them to meet their own deadline.

**Accounting of what we hold and to whom we disclosed it.** On request we provide an accounting of the personal
information we hold about you, the categories of recipients we disclosed it to, and the purposes — drawn from
our data inventory and our disclosure record (§A9), not assembled from memory.

## A8. Quality of information — and your part in it

We keep personal information **accurate, complete, up to date and relevant** for the purpose we use it for,
because a decision or a communication based on wrong information harms you as well as us. We do this by
collecting only what we need; taking most information **directly from you**; validating formats at entry;
letting you see and edit your own profile; re-confirming information at defined points (annually for personnel
records, at renewal for billing contacts, and when a message to you bounces); correcting promptly when told;
and reviewing at each retention cycle whether the information is still **relevant** — deleting what is no
longer needed rather than keeping it because we have it.

**Your responsibilities for quality.** You know your information better than we do. Please:

- Keep your profile and contact details current, and update them yourself where you can;
- Tell us promptly if anything we hold about you is wrong, out of date or incomplete — using §A10;
- Give us accurate information when you provide it, and tell us if something changes (a new work email, a
  changed name, a new billing contact);
- If your organization's administrator manages your account, tell **them** as well, since their record feeds
  ours.

If you tell us something we hold is wrong, we correct it, and **we pass the correction (or a deletion) on to
any third party we previously gave that information to**, so a mistake does not live on somewhere else.

## A10. Security, monitoring and enforcement, and how to contact us

**Security for privacy.** We protect personal information with encryption in transit and at rest;
least-privilege access reviewed quarterly; separation of each customer's data from every other's; logging of
access, including denied attempts; monitoring and alerting for unusual access; secure development with
automated code, dependency and credential scanning; vulnerability management with defined remediation times;
staff screening, training and confidentiality obligations; and an incident response procedure with defined
severities and notification timers. Security is not perfect anywhere, and we say so: our own testing has
identified weaknesses in our environment — including multi-factor authentication not yet enforced on all
administrative identities, and network access rules that are broader than they should be. **Each has an
identified owner, a date, and interim compensating controls, and each is reported to our Audit Committee until
it is closed.** We would rather tell you that than imply a clean state.

**If a breach happens.** We have a defined process for deciding whether a breach requires notification, who
must be told, by what method and by when. Where the legal threshold is met we notify the relevant authority
**within 72 hours of becoming aware**, and affected individuals without undue delay, in clear language — what
happened, what information was involved, what we are doing, and what you can do. Where we hold the information
as processor, we notify our customer so that they can meet their own obligations. **We record every decision,
including a decision that notification is not required, with our reasons.**

**Monitoring and enforcement — how we hold ourselves to this notice.**

| Mechanism | What it does |
|---|---|
| **A Data Protection Officer** who reports to a **Board committee, not to management**, and who can **veto a launch** on privacy grounds — and has exercised that veto | Independent internal accountability |
| Annual privacy compliance review, with the results documented and reported to management and the Audit Committee | Verifies practice against this notice |
| Quarterly retention conformance checks, quarterly access reviews, annual data subject rights tests, and privacy impact assessments before new or changed processing | Ongoing monitoring of the controls that protect your information |
| Independent internal audit, and external audit of our control environment | Checks us, not our self-assessment |
| A register of privacy non-compliances, with owners, dates and remediation tracked to verified closure — **including items that are overdue** | Turns a finding into a fix |
| Disciplinary consequences for misuse of personal information, applied to **anyone, including management** | In the current period we sanctioned a member of management under our conduct process, and we sanctioned retaliation against a person who reported a concern |
| Assessment of our providers' privacy compliance, with corrective action, scope restriction or termination | Extends enforcement past our own boundary |

**Contact us — for any question, request, complaint or dispute about privacy:**

- **Email:** the privacy address published with this notice on our website (monitored by the DPO's team)
- **Post:** the registered address of the relevant entity, published with this notice, marked "Data Protection
  Officer"
- **In the EEA/UK:** the European subsidiary's DPO contact, published with this notice

**We acknowledge every inquiry, complaint or dispute, tell you who is handling it, resolve it within the

| **Before a new purpose** | Notice **and consent** before the new use begins; the new purpose is documented first | The DPIA trigger criteria route any new purpose to the DPO, who holds a **launch veto** |
| Personnel and contractors | At hire, at engagement start, and annually with the Code of Conduct acknowledgement | Acknowledgement tracked — 48 of 48 for the current period |
| Marketing contacts sourced from third parties | The source is disclosed in the first message, with a one-click objection | Template control |

## B3. Periodic review of the notice

**A defined process reviews the notice's content periodically and implements identified updates.**

| Review | Owner | Cadence | What is checked | Result this period |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Annual notice review** | DPO with the General Counsel | Annually | Every row of the B1 checklist; accuracy against the **actual** processing record and data map, not against the previous notice; readability | Produced v4.0 (restructure) and v4.1 |
| **Processing-change trigger review** | DPO | Within 30 days of any new or changed processing, purpose, category, recipient, provider, retention period or transfer | Whether the notice must change, and whether consent is needed | Triggered 4 times; 2 resulted in notice changes, 1 in a DPIA, 1 in the decision **not to implement** an analytics purpose |
| **Legal-change review** | General Counsel with external privacy counsel | On any change to applicable law, tracked in the obligation inventory across two legal entities and five geographies (**R-047**) | Whether new disclosures or rights statements are required | One review triggered and completed |
| **Readability testing** | Customer Operations with the DPO | At each material revision | Plain language, structure, findability; tested with people who did not draft it | Drove the v4.0 restructure |
| **Findability check** | Customer Operations | Quarterly | That the notice is reachable in one click from every page, from the sign-in page, and from every collection form | A form was found without a link → corrected, and a **build-time check** added so it cannot recur |
| **Complaint- and request-driven review** | DPO | On any complaint or request that reveals an unclear or missing statement | The specific wording | An inquiry about sources led directly to the §A5 table in v4.1 |
| **Independent check** | Internal audit | Annually | The notice against the processing record and the data inventory | Reviewed; no discrepancy found in the current period |

**Reviews produce dated updates or a recorded conclusion that no update is needed** — a review with no output
and no recorded conclusion is treated as not performed.

## B4. Communicating changes, and retaining prior versions

**Communicating changes:** every revision is accompanied by a **summary of changes in plain language** (the
table in A12), published with the notice; direct email notification to all account users; an in-product notice
for material changes; **at least 30 days' advance notice for material changes**; and, where a change involves a
new purpose for information already collected, **consent obtained before the new use** rather than notice
alone. Personnel are notified through the internal channel and re-acknowledge annually. The notification
itself is recorded — recipient set, date, method — so that "we told people" is evidenced.

**Retaining prior versions:** every version of the notice is retained **indefinitely** in a public dated
archive linked from the current notice, and internally with its approval record, the summary of changes, the
list of B1 checklist confirmations, the DPO's sign-off, the notification record and the readability-test
result. This is a deliberate internal requirement: to answer a question or a complaint about what someone was
told in the past, the entity must be able to produce the exact text that was in force at that date. Five
versions are currently archived (3.0, 3.1, 3.2, 4.0, 4.1) with their effective dates and change summaries.
